P2.71, due 11-25: readers went through every paper and post in the project's literature list and wrote one entry per paper. That is 235 entries after three duplicates were merged, about 230 of them read past the abstract. They are ranked here by what the current draft ("A Mechanistic Foundation for the Elicitation Hypothesis") has to do about them. Three things matter most. First, the field already has stronger diffing tools than the ones we beat (the Delta-Crosscoder, SVD of the weight difference, diff-SAEs), and they run on our 67M model. Second, a few realistic fine-tunes have outside, near-mechanism evidence of reuse (DPO on GPT-2 medium, IOI fine-tunes of GPT-2 small) and could test our definition where it matters. Third, the "turn, not scale" finding has independent support, but it collides with the "fine-tuning enhances existing mechanisms" papers and with an audit of VPD's masks, and the paper has to address both.

## 1. Methods we should compare against but don't

1. **Kassem 2026 (2603.04426)** — "Crosscoders ... consistently fail to recover causally relevant features in this regime. This failure is structural" — the Delta-Crosscoder (delta loss, reserved non-shared latents) is designed for narrow fine-tunes, the regime where our BatchTopK crosscoder misses the planted control. **67M: yes.** It is the strongest crosscoder rival; without it, "existing tools fail" is open to a cherry-picking charge.
2. **Zhong 2026 (2508.00161)** — "the top singular vectors of the weight difference between a fine-tuned model and its base model correspond to newly acquired behaviors." — data-free SVD of ΔW. **67M and Qwen: yes, as is.** It belongs beside effective rank. Same work as the LessWrong linkpost `lw2025-weight-diff-svd-monitoring`.
3. **Dumas 2025 (LessWrong, diffing base and chat)** — "without knowing what the “right answer” looks like, it’s hard to compare methods objectively." — a diff-SAE on fine-tune-minus-base activations with latent scaling. It beat the BatchTopK crosscoder 88% vs 60% on KL-gap recovery. **67M: yes, cheap.** The authors also name our gap: no known answers.
4. **Bricken 2024 (Transformer Circuits, stage-wise model diffing)** — "one could imagine the entire representation rotating during finetuning as a kind of "drift", without it reflecting any important difference." — fine-tune the base SAE on F and score each latent's decoder rotation. **67M: yes, under an hour.** It reads rotation directly, which is our "turn" finding, so it is a natural rival on the built pairs.
5. **Aranguri 2025 (LessWrong, tied crosscoders)** — "since fine-tuning enhances existing mechanisms, the chat model reuses the features from the base model in new contexts." — a tied crosscoder with a per-latent novelty ratio, which is a crosscoder-family reuse reading. **67M: yes, cheap.**
6. **Shuttleworth 2024 (2410.21228)** — "weight matrices trained with LoRA have new, high-ranking singular vectors, which we call intruder dimensions, while those trained with full fine-tuning do not" — intruder-dimension count. **67M: yes, free.** Xie 2026 (2607.23711) predicts it reads zero on every full fine-tune, so it is a cheap row, likely uninformative on ours.
7. **Jain 2024 (2407.10264)** — "these methods minimally transform MLP weights to specifically align unsafe inputs into its weights’ null space." — ΔW column space vs W's null space, one SVD. **67M: yes.** Already cited; the measurement is not run.
8. **Zhu 2025 (2511.08567)** — "RLVR learns off-principal directions in weight space ... In contrast, SFT targets principal weights, distorts the spectrum" — principal angles and spectral drift of the weights. **67M and Qwen: yes, free.**
9. **Wang 2025 (2506.19823)** — "a toxic persona feature which most strongly controls emergent misalignment" — base-SAE mean-activation diff, then steering of the top latents. **67M: yes, with our BatchTopK SAE, ~30 min on debug (whip-lead).** It is cited (main.tex:205) but not scored. A fixed dictionary has no unit for a new feature, so it should fail the built-new pairs: worth showing.
10. **Chen 2025 (2507.21509)** — "We observe strong positive correlations (r = 0.76–0.97) between finetuning shift along a persona vector and the model's propensity" — finetuning shift projected onto a base trait direction. **67M: yes.** It is close to our steering share; score it on the known answers.
11. **Soligo 2025 (2506.11618)** — "different emergently misaligned models converge to similar representations of misalignment" — ablate one fine-tune's direction in other fine-tunes (a transfer test). **67M: yes where a mean-difference direction exists.** We have a steering share but no cross-fine-tune ablation.
12. **Ward 2025 (2507.12638)** — "the representation only induces backtracking in the reasoning model" — two-sided steering: an M-derived vector added in F vs in M separates "kept input, new consumer". **67M: yes, cheap.** Cited only in the appendix.
13. **Venhoff 2025 (2510.07364)** — "RL primarily teaches heuristics for orchestrating pre-existing base mechanisms, whereas SFT-distillation installs new ones" — constructive diffing: base category vectors plus a learned heuristic, scored by gap recovered. We borrow only its gap score. **67M: partly**, since the category vectors need reasoning traces; Qwen is the fit.
14. **Wang 2025 (2502.11812)** — "while circuits maintain high node similarity before and after fine-tuning, their edges undergo significant changes" — EAP-IG node/edge circuit diff. **67M: yes.** Already under way as cell P2.69 / E287 (eapig_overlap).
15. **Chen 2024 (2406.14144)** — "after patching approximately 5% of all the neurons, SFT can recover over 90% of DPO's safety performance" — activation-contrast neuron ranking plus dynamic cross-model patching. **67M: yes.** It is a published sibling of our neuron CMAP; cite it there.
16. **Hu 2026 (2602.20904)** — "a feature's activating examples suggest what inputs trigger new computation—not whether the underlying representations are unique to the target model" — a sparse transcoder adapter on the MLP difference. **67M: yes**, but it gives no reuse score as published.
17. **Hu 2021 (2106.09685)** — "ΔW amplifies some features that are already in W" — the ΔW-subspace amplification factor (projection of W onto ΔW's subspace, vs top-r and random). **67M: yes, free.**
18. **Tanwar 2025 (2510.09359)** — "these vectors primarily write new directional information into the MLP layers of the model, while amplifying existing directions in attention heads." — the share of ΔW inside W's top singular subspace. **67M: yes**, and it needs a random floor.
19. **Lingam 2024 (2405.19597)** — "adapting singular values and reweighting the frozen singular vectors." — an SVFT-Plain refit (singular values only), a gain-only reading in our IA³-refit family. **67M: yes.**
20. **Bansal 2021 (2106.07682)** — "this is what it means to be stitching-connected." — linear stitching of F's lower layers into M's upper layers. **67M: yes.** It is a rotation-tolerant value-based baseline, and relevant to the pending rotation-tolerant kept clause.
21. **Kocher 2026 (2608.26462)** — "the induced changes are subtle and mostly reweight or refocus existing behaviors." — logit-difference mining (Top-K / NMF). **67M: yes.** It sees only outputs, so it cannot call reuse; one line or one row.
22. **Guo 2024 (2410.12949)** — "OT methods target high logit-diff extraction mechanisms, rather than the fact lookup mechanisms" — the share of an edit's weight mask that falls on each known mechanism. **67M: yes on built pairs**, where the mechanism is known.
23. **Cao 2026 (2609.37731)** — "One potential application is Parameter Diffing ... We leave this direction for future work." — ASPD, an input-gated rank-one decomposition that claims to scale where VPD does not (Qwen-3-8B). **Qwen check: yes.** It is the natural second decomposition for the Qwen "no call".
24. **Drake 2026 (2607.04510)** — "LoRA ... recruits the EM persona" — enrichment of a weight update along a base-model direction relative to chance. **67M: needs a behaviour direction; Qwen fit.**
25. **Lubana 2023 (2211.08422)** — "naïve fine-tuning on a downstream dataset can fail to alter a model's mechanisms" — linear-path loss barrier between M and F. **67M: yes**, but likely no call at fine-tune size. Cited.

## 2. Realistic fine-tunes with independent evidence of reuse or new (known-answer candidates)

1. **Lee 2024 (2401.01967)** — "DPO does not remove the capability of generating toxic outputs, but learns an “offset”, distributed amongst its layers, to “bypass” the regions that elicit toxicity." — GPT-2 medium, DPO on toxicity pairs. **Reuse, near mechanism-level**: toxic MLP vectors keep cosine > 0.99, and scaling 7 neurons restores base toxicity. It is the cheapest open realistic test of ρ and the re-gating share; it needs a featurization of GPT-2 medium.
2. **Chhabra 2025 (2503.01896)** — "task-specific fine-tuning enhances the underlying mechanisms of circuits without introducing novel mechanisms" — GPT-2 small on IOI: clean (amplify = reuse), corrupted labels (sign flip = changed) and retrain fine-tunes. **Mechanism-level** (path patching, ablation, DLA, CMAP). Affordable at heads or SAEs, and the corrupted arm gives a "changed" label.
3. **Prakash 2024 (2402.14811)** — "fine-tuning enhances, rather than fundamentally alters, the mechanistic operation of the model." — Llama-7B to Vicuna (chat), Goat (LoRA arithmetic) and FLoat (full) on entity tracking. **Circuit-level, plus value-level CMAP**: same circuit (faithfulness 0.88-0.97) and same algorithm, with the gain located in the Value Fetcher heads. Already cited; at 7B it is out of reach for our decompositions.
4. **Chen 2024 (2406.14144)** — "SFT can recover over 90% of DPO's safety performance" — DPO safety fine-tunes of four 3-7B models with (IA)³ on the MLPs only. **Reuse by construction at neurons** (the update is a pure rescale), plus patching. A clean "kept up to a gain" case at the neuron featurization.
5. **Drake 2026 (2607.04510)** — "full SFT ... builds a dedicated, localised solution for the narrow training task, while LoRA ... recruits the EM persona" — Qwen2.5-32B on insecure code, LoRA vs full SFT on the same data. **Representational** (transplant, ablation, weight enrichment). It is a matched same-data pair with opposite labels, but at 32B. Alias: `transplanting-misalignment-persona`.
6. **Soligo 2025 (2506.11618)** — "different emergently misaligned models converge to similar representations of misalignment" — Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, rank-1 LoRA EM on bad medical advice. **Activation-level** (steer and ablate across fine-tunes). The LessWrong version adds an adapter write nearly orthogonal to the reused direction (cos 0.04).
7. **Wang 2025 (2506.19823)** — "a toxic persona feature which most strongly controls emergent misalignment" — GPT-4o (and o3-mini RL) on narrow wrong data. **Activation and steering level**, closed weights. Cited at main.tex:205; Soligo and Turner (2506.11613, rank-1 EM organisms) are the open versions.
8. **Hong 2024 (2406.11614)** — "existing unlearning methods suppress the parametric knowledge rather than erase it" — LLaMA2-7B-chat and OLMo-7B, unlearning by GA, DPO, NPO and RMU. **Weight-level** (concept vector kept, Jaccard > 0.98) **plus activation** (a jailbreak re-raises it). Reuse: the write is kept and its use is changed.
9. **Ward 2025 (2507.12638)** — "it has been repurposed as an input to the backtracking mechanism during reasoning finetuning." — Llama-3.1-8B to R1-Distill. **Activation steering**: a base-derived direction steers F (cos 0.74) and not M. The input is kept and the consumer is new: a mixed case our per-variable split should handle.
10. **Venhoff 2025 (2510.07364)** — "RL primarily teaches heuristics for orchestrating pre-existing base mechanisms, whereas SFT-distillation installs new ones" — Qwen2.5 bases, ORZ (RL) vs R1-Distill (SFT). **Activation steering with ablation controls**, a soft label. Cited.
11. **Soligo 2026 (2602.07852)** — "general misalignment achieves lower loss, is more robust to perturbations, and is more influential in the pre-training distribution." — Qwen2.5-14B EM fine-tunes, including a steering-vector-only fine-tune that is near-reuse by construction. **Activation-level.** Its earlier LessWrong post is `lw-easy-hard-earlier-post`.
12. **Nadaf 2026 (2607.21356)** — "the disposition is read loudly and written obliquely." — Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, LoRA EM. **Activation-level reuse** of a persona subspace, while the weight side points to new writes. A single-author preprint.
13. **Greenblatt 2024 (2405.19550)** — "The internal computations that result in locking may be different." — password-locked models: the unlock fine-tune is reuse by construction. **Behavioural label**, but **buildable on our 67M key tasks**.
14. **Zhang 2025 (2512.07783)** — "RL incentivizes contextual generalization only when the relevant primitives or base skills are present in the base model." — 100M models trained from scratch with controlled exposure. **Behavioural, by construction.** Closest scale to our 67M; it needs a decomposition fitted to it.
15. **Zhao 2025 (2504.07912)** — "RL fine-tuning amplifies a specific mode from the pretraining mixture while collapsing the others." — 150M/1B models from scratch with tagged pre-training formats, RL checkpoints released. **Behavioural.**
16. **Jain 2023 (2311.12786)** — "a minimal transformation, which we call a 'wrapper', is typically learned on top of the underlying model capabilities" — Tracr and PCFG fine-tunes with compiled ground truth. **Mechanism-level but toy.** Cited.
17. **Betley 2025 (2512.09742, weird generalization)** — "the model strengthened general Israel/Judaism features rather than features tied to the narrow training task domain." — Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct SFT on Israeli dishes. **SAE value-level** (ablation cuts the shift from 80% to 5%).
18. **Vetter 2026 (2608.11025)** — "fine-tuning does not instill a new persona but binds one formed in pre-training to the assistant role." — EM LoRAs on four 8-27B models. **SAE steering, value-level**, with a random-feature control.
19. **Du 2026 (2609.15064)** — "short RL mainly installs and stabilizes scaffolds that elicit latent capability" — Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, GRPO on maths. **Value-level** (base steering recovers the format gain). Near our Qwen scale.
20. **Arditi 2024 (2406.11717)** — "this representation exists already in the base model, and is repurposed for refusal during safety fine-tuning." — four base/chat pairs. **Value only** (activation cosine). Ranked down: the "repurposed" claim has no mechanism test.
21. **Hu 2026 (2602.20904)** — R1-Distill-Qwen-7B vs Qwen2.5-Math-7B. **Partial**: an MLP change is needed, and the new features read base embeddings.
22. Behavioural "new" cases, useful as contrasts but with no internals: **Sun 2026 (2509.21016)**, Qwen3-4B RL on Manufactoria (pass@128 0 to ~100%, code released; a Qwen3-1.7B rerun is needed); **Liu 2025 ProRL (2505.24864)**, one released 1.5B base/RL pair; **Yuan 2026 (2509.25123)**, RL vs RFT on Llama-3.1-8B; **Shao 2026 (2506.10947)**, random-reward GRPO on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (behavioural reuse; needs Qwen2.5-Math); **Huang 2025 (2412.01951)**, self-distillation on best-of-N, buildable on our models, with its status known only at the information level.

## 3. Definitions of reuse or elicitation to cite or contrast

1. **Geiger 2025 (2301.04709)** — "ℋ is a constructive abstraction of ℒ under an alignment ⟨Π,π⟩ iff ℋ is an exact transformation of ℒ." — our vocabulary. State the departures explicitly: a kept relation between two models' mechanisms, and ρ.
2. **Minder 2025 (2504.02922)** — "our inability to distinguish between truly novel latents learned during chat-tuning and existing latents that have merely shifted their activation patterns" — the stated gap our definition fills. Already used at main.tex:204; keep it prominent.
3. **Prakash 2024 (2402.14811)** — "enhances, rather than fundamentally alters" — "enhance" means same circuit and same function (DCM) with better values (CMAP): value-based. Contrast it with our by-mechanism kept clause.
4. **Jain 2023 (2311.12786)** — "creating the illusion that they have been modified" — a capability is a linear readout on a sub-domain, and a wrapper sits on top. Cited; contrast.
5. **Chhabra 2025 (2503.01896)** — "without introducing novel mechanisms" — its "amplification" counts new heads that copy an old role as reuse. Our per-variable kept clause would not, so this needs an explicit contrast.
6. **Ward 2025 (2507.12638)** — "repurposed as an input to the backtracking mechanism" — kept variable, new consumer. It maps onto our needed/kept split; move the citation from the appendix to related work.
7. **Sutter 2025 (2507.08802)** — "any neural network can be mapped to any algorithm, rendering this unrestricted notion of causal abstraction trivial" — cited. Add that our fitted steps (the least-squares carry, re-gating with Γ) are where the dilemma could come back.
8. **Marks 2026 (LessWrong, persona selection model)** — "Not all representations in post-trained models are reused from pre-training" — EH at the persona level. The draft plans this citation (main.tex:171) but it is not in refs.bib.
9. **Vergara-Browne 2026 (2602.15829)** — "pre-trained models drastically reduce the complexity of achieving high performance on many tasks." — an information-theoretic, behavioural SAH.
10. **Li 2026 (2605.08368)** — "reweights behaviors within this support is capability elicitation; whereas changing the support itself corresponds to capability creation." — behavioural elicitation vs creation by support. Our prior-vs-mechanism pairs are exactly where the two disagree.
11. **Lambert 2025 (blog; book 2504.12501)** — "the elicitation interpretation of post-training, where all we are doing is extracting and amplifying valuable behaviors in the base model." — the source of our term (main.tex:88). His "amplifying" is our rescale clause. Note that he also says "it is clear that you can teach new skills to models in post-training".
12. **Kotha 2023 (2309.10105)** — "we can not test whether this is how language models compute solutions." — task inference as re-weighting over retained solutions, assumed rather than shown inside the model. Cite it with Jain.
13. **Raghu 2020 (1909.09157)** — "the meta-initialization already contains highly useful features that can mostly be reused as is" — the first operational feature-reuse test. It is by value and rotation-invariant (CCA); ours is by mechanism and up to a scalar.
14. **Bansal 2021 (2106.07682)** / **Lenc 2015 (1411.5908)** — "stitching-connected" / "Franken-CNNs" — "same up to a linear map". Cite them when the rotation-tolerant kept clause lands.
15. **Lubana 2023 (2211.08422)** — mechanistic similarity as a shared invariance set, behavioural. Cited.
16. **Meloux 2025 (2502.20914)** — "many incompatible explanations can coexist." — the reason reuse is stated relative to τ. Cited. Alias: `meloux2025-many-algorithms-one-network`.
17. **Merullo 2023 (2310.08744)** — "invariant to the highly different input domains" — functional cross-task component reuse (same heads, same roles), within one model.
18. **Greenblatt 2024 (2405.19550)** — elicitation as "capability recoverable by little fine-tuning"; **Huang 2025 (2412.01951)** — "sharpening as any process that tilts πbase toward responses that are more certain"; **Zhao 2025 (2504.07912)** — KL-optimal re-weighting of pre-training modes. These are three behavioural definitions of elicitation for one contrast sentence.
19. **Yuan 2026 (2509.25123)** — "“new skills” as novel reasoning strategies that enable models to solve previously unsolvable problems through systematic combination of existing capabilities." — our definition would call much of this reuse; say so.
20. **Jiralerspong 2026 (2602.11729)** — "“model-exclusive” refers to its specific representation, not necessarily the model's underlying conceptual capability." — crosscoder authors themselves scope "new" to a representation.
21. **Geiger 2021 (2106.02997)**, **Geiger 2022 (2112.00826)**, **Geiger 2024 (2303.02536)** — the original interchange intervention, IIA, and learned alignments. Cite at Def. 2 beside Geiger 2025; none is in refs.bib.
22. **Chen 2025 (2502.04602)** — "alignment is not entirely superficial." — superficial knowledge is what a final-layer-only change recovers.
23. **LIMA (2305.11206)** and **Raghavendra 2024 (2410.03717)** — SAH and its three-claim decomposition. Both cited.

Mechanism, for the title's word: **Saphra 2024 (2410.09087)**, the narrow technical sense of MI; **Machamer 2000**, "entities and activities organized such that they are productive of regular changes"; **Mueller 2025 (2408.01416)**, the field enforces no causal definition of mechanism. **Braun 2025 (2501.14926)**: "mechanisms as vectors in parameter space". **Beckers 2019 (1812.03789)**: "what counts as an inessential difference?", which our up-to-a-scalar clause answers. ## 4a. Findings that support ours

Rotate vs rescale, our "trained fine-tunes turn writes rather than scale them":
1. **Zhu 2025 (2511.08567)** — "SFT targets principal weights, distorts the spectrum" while RLVR keeps it — independent weight-level evidence that SFT turns and RL preserves. This matches higher RL ρ (0.05-0.22).
2. **Kumar 2022 (2202.10054)** — "The only way the pretrained features are not distorted and only scaled during FT is if the initial feature extractor B0 is exactly aligned with the ID subspace." — theory: fine-tuning turns features except in a degenerate case. Cite next to the kept clause.
3. **Jain 2024 (2407.10264)** — "align unsafe inputs into its weights’ null space" — a realistic safety fine-tune writes outside the base column space. Cited, but not for this point.
4. **Lindsey 2024 (crosscoders)** — "the finetuned model uses a concept that was present in the base model, but in a new way." — shared latents with low decoder correlation in Claude 3 Sonnet: turned reuse. Cited.
5. **Soligo 2025 (LessWrong post)** — "the EM is learnt via mediation of directions which are already present in the chat model." — the adapter write is nearly orthogonal (cos 0.04) yet reaches the reused direction downstream (0.42). Read loudly, written obliquely; **Nadaf 2026 (2607.21356)** reports the same pattern.
6. **Wang 2025 (2502.11812)** — "circuits maintain high node similarity ... their edges undergo significant changes" — nodes kept, edges rewired, on maths fine-tunes (attribution evidence).
7. **Biderman 2024 (2405.09673)** — "full finetuning learns perturbations with a rank that is 10-100× greater" — and **Mukherjee 2025 (2505.11711)** "span almost the full subspaces": trained updates are high or full rank, not low-rank re-weightings.
8. **Oh 2026 (2609.34599)** — "RL largely rescales the existing scale and gate patterns while introducing new directions predominantly in the shift vectors" — mixed rescale and turn in VLA RL.

The gap and the premise of Claim 1:
9. **Bushnaq 2026 (2610.20748)** — "upweighting, downweighting, or modifying existing subcomponents, as well as creating new ones" — VPD's authors propose our programme (Sec. 7.5); we are its first test.
10. **Dumas 2025**, **Mishra-Sharma 2025 (crosscoder diffing update)** ("an open question is the relationship between the features we extract and the actual underlying computational differences"), and **Aranguri 2025** (a standard crosscoder splits one reused concept into two exclusive latents, cos 0.97): diffing authors concede there are no known answers, and the crosscoder has a mechanical failure. This backs our gap and our crosscoder result.
11. **Shuttleworth 2024 (2410.21228)**, **Nguyen 2026 (2609.03887)** ("training method, not just data, reshapes how refusal is computed internally"), **Lee 2025 (2506.06278)** ("training a model can drastically modify its input-output behavior while leaving its underlying capabilities intact"), and **Drake 2026**: same behaviour, different inside. Lee 2025 is the cleanest by construction; cite it beside Deeb.
12. **Kissane 2024** (cited) and **Ognev 2026 (2609.04344)** ("the labels learned on base models carry over to this instruct model without loss of reconstruction") show a base-fixed featurization is usable. **Goodfire 2026** ("the neurons retained their original functions") and **Tanwar 2025 (2510.09359)** match "at neurons almost everything reads as reuse".
13. **Guo 2024 (2410.12949)** — output tracing picks extraction components over the mechanism. Consistent with neuron CMAP reversing pairs.
14. **Deeb 2024** (cited) and **Che 2025 (2502.05209)** ("State-of-the-art unlearning can reliably be reversed within 16 fine-tuning steps"): behaviour understates what the weights still hold.

Behavioural support for EH (cite in "post-training as selection"; none looks inside):
15. **Wu 2025 (2507.14843)** "The current RLVR recipe primarily preserves rather than expands the base model's solution coverage."; **Yue 2025** (cited); **Liu 2025 (2503.20783)** ("by reinforcing useful reasoning behaviors instead of infusing new knowledge"; cited only in an appendix that is not input); **Shao 2024 (2402.03300)** ("boosting the correct response from TopK rather than the enhancement of fundamental capabilities"); **Gekhman 2024 (2405.05904)** ("fine-tuning teaches them to use it more efficiently"); **Qi 2024 (2406.05946)** (only the first few tokens change); **Tice 2026 (2601.10160)** ("These effects are dampened, but persist through post-training."); **Zheng 2025 (2501.13453)**; **Karan 2026 (2510.14901)**; **He 2025 (2506.02355)**; **Oh 2026 (2610.01509)**; **Burns 2023** (in refs.bib, uncited).
16. Activation-level support, ranked down because each sells a value-level reading as "the mechanism is kept": **Moskvoretskii 2026 (2605.13329)**, "post-training only tunes its volume" (activation steering only); **Yu 2026 (2609.35210)**, "OPD distills no new features" (crosscoder activations, our own failing tool family; check its swap readout for the own-slot flaw); **Chen 2025 persona vectors (2507.21509)**; **Lu 2026 assistant axis (2601.10387)**; **Rimsky 2023** (cited); **Venhoff 2025** (cited). **Ward 2025 rank-1 LoRA (2511.06739)**, "the majority of new capabilities in reasoning models can be elicited by small, single-rank changes", is behavioural. **Boughorbel 2025 (2509.18792)** is weak: its tool is blind at fine-tune size.

## 4b. Findings that contradict ours, or that we must answer

1. **Angsten 2026 (LessWrong VPD audit)** — "causal importance labels hold only as a joint configuration." — the re-gating share puts M's Γ on F's activations, which is a mixed configuration. That could depress the share or produce its saturation (~0.99 on Qwen, random updates included). The re-gating share needs this check before it carries a headline.
2. **Lee 2024 (2401.01967)** — "learns an “offset”, distributed amongst its layers" — clear reuse made by additive offsets, not gains. A gain-only kept clause may read it as new. Test this before claiming ρ "follows the mechanism".
3. **Prakash 2024 (2402.14811)** and **Chhabra 2025 (2503.01896)** — "enhances, rather than fundamentally alters" / "without introducing novel mechanisms" — two circuit-level studies say fine-tuning amplifies existing mechanisms, and our trained fine-tunes read as turned. Either their amplified heads would also read as turned under our clause (then the disagreement is about by-value vs by-mechanism, which the paper must say), or our clause is too strict. Chhabra's GPT-2 IOI fine-tunes can settle it cheaply.
4. **Singh 2026 (2608.05136)** (background, but it bears here) — Adam's per-coordinate preconditioner ties updates to neuron axes.
