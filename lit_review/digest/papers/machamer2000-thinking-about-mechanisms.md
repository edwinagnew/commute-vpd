# Thinking about Mechanisms (Machamer 2000)

**What it does.** This is the standard "new mechanist" analysis in philosophy of science. A mechanism is entities and
activities organized so as to be productive of regular change, from set-up to termination conditions. Examples come from
neurobiology (depolarization) and molecular biology (protein synthesis). It treats functions as the roles that entities'
activities play in a mechanism, and introduces mechanism *schemata* (truncated abstract descriptions of a type of
mechanism) and *sketches* (schemata with gaps).

**Key result.** The paper is conceptual. Mechanisms are "identified and individuated by the activities and entities that
constitute them, by their start and finish conditions, and by their functional roles". Mechanisms are regular in that they
"work always or for the most part in the same way under the same conditions". A failed prediction leads to revising the
entities, activities or stages of a schema.

**Quote.** "Mechanisms are entities and activities organized such that they are productive of regular changes from start or
set-up to finish or termination conditions." (p. 3)

**Relevance to our paper.** This is background for the word "mechanism", if the draft wants a philosophical anchor beyond
Geiger. The mapping onto our definition is direct. Entities are the featurized variables. Activities are their mechanisms
f_u. Our "kept" asks whether an entity still engages in the same activity. MDC individuate a mechanism by its entities,
activities *and* functional role, which makes the same contrast we face with Chhabra et al. and Merullo et al.: an entity
that keeps its role but changes its activity (or vice versa) is the same mechanism on one reading and not on another. Our
definition commits to activity, i.e. the function computed. Little else bears on the paper.

**For the digest.**
- `3-definition`: the classic entities-plus-activities definition of mechanism. Cite it to say our "mechanism" is the activity (the function computed), not the entity or its functional role.
