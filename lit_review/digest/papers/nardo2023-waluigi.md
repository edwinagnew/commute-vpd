# The Waluigi Effect (mega-post) (Nardo 2023)

**What it does.** An essay arguing that a prompted or fine-tuned chatbot is a "superposition" of simulacra weighted by
prior times likelihood, and that training a character to have trait P also makes its opposite (the "waluigi") cheap to
specify, since most of a character's complexity lies in the traits rather than their sign. It uses this to explain
jailbreaks and argues that RLHF selects waluigis along with luigis and increases mode collapse.

**Key result.** No experiment; the post calls its account "mechanistic" but it is a semiotic, behavioural argument. On
RLHF: deceptive waluigis give the same rewarded answers, so "RLHF selects for the waluigi along with the luigi", and
RLHF "increases the 'attractiveness' of the attractor states", citing janus's preliminary evidence on mode collapse.

**Quote.** "Therefore RLHF selects for the waluigi along with the luigi." (section "Waluigis after RLHF")

**Relevance to our paper.** Background only: it states the selection view of post-training (RLHF re-weights simulacra
the pretrained model already contains) as an argument, not a test. It belongs, if anywhere, in one sentence of the
planned persona-selection paragraph next to Simulators; the measured versions of the idea are Wang et al. 2025 and
Nadaf 2026.
