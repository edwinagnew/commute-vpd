# Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference (Pearl 2009)

**What it does.** The standard textbook on structural causal models, do-interventions, counterfactuals and mediation.
The book itself was not read here. The row comes from Mueller et al. 2025 (p5), which cites "Salmon 1984; Pearl 2009" for
the sentence "a mechanism is defined as a causal chain from cause X to effect Y".

**Key result.** Not read; nothing is reported here.

**Quote.** None (not read). The relevant sentence is Mueller et al.'s: "In the causality literature, a mechanism is
defined as a causal chain from cause X to effect Y (Salmon 1984; Pearl 2009)." (Mueller et al. 2025, p5)

**Relevance to our paper.** This is only the root reference for the causal-model vocabulary that Geiger et al. 2025
formalise and that we use through Geiger. Our sense of "mechanism", the function computing a variable from its parents,
is the structural-equation sense, not the causal-chain sense Mueller attributes to Pearl and Salmon. If the draft wants a
classical root for "mechanism", Pearl is the usual one. Geiger 2025 suffices for what the paper actually uses.
