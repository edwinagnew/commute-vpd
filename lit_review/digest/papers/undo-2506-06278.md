# Distillation Robustifies Unlearning (Lee 2025)

**What it does.** First shows that even ideal behavioural unlearning is not robust: a 100M Gemma-2-architecture
"reference" model pretrained on English plus Korean (or on arithmetic incl. multiplication/division) is distilled by
KL to match an "oracle" model never trained on the forget data, to near-zero distillation loss; then all models get a
relearning attack on the forget set. It then proposes UNDO: unlearn, perturb the weights with noise, distill the
unlearned model's outputs into the noised copy. Also tested on WMDP.

**Key result.** The oracle-matched reference model, behaviourally indistinguishable from the oracle, relearns the
forget capability much faster than the oracle, while a randomly initialised student distilled from the same oracle
relearns like the oracle. UNDO at its strongest setting matches a model retrained with perfect data filtering using
60-80% of the compute and 0.01% labelled pretraining data.

**Quote.** "training a model can drastically modify its input-output behavior while leaving its underlying
capabilities intact" (Abstract)

**Relevance to our paper.** A clean constructed demonstration of our claim-1 premise: two models with the same
input-output behaviour (oracle-matched reference vs oracle) differ in what is inside them, shown by relearning speed,
not by reading the mechanism. That is exactly the "two fine-tunes with one input-output behaviour can differ under it"
point, made by construction at 100M scale, and the draft does not cite it; it belongs beside Deeb and Roger. It also
gives a known-answer construction we could borrow: a fine-tune that suppresses a pretrained capability is "kept"
(the mechanism is still there), while distillation into a fresh model is "removed". It does not read mechanisms, so it
is not a method we compare against.

**For the digest.**
- 4-supports: behaviour-matched models differ internally (shown by relearning), by construction; the cleanest citation for "same behaviour, different mechanism".
