# Distance Is Not Enough: FRAG (Chen 2026)

**What it does.** Proposes a training-free weight-diff score, the Forget-Retain Alignment Gap, that predicts whether
an unlearning update will survive a relearning attack. Each weight gets a forget-importance and a retain-importance
(Wanda-style: |W0| times the ratio of forget to retain input-channel activation norms), and FRAG compares how the
squared update ΔW² aligns with each. Tested with OpenUnlearning on TOFU (Llama-3.2-1B/3B), WMDP-cyber
(Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct) and MUSE-News, against the global L2 distance predictor of Siddiqui et al. 2025.

**Key result.** Over 30 healthy checkpoints, FRAG's Spearman correlation with post-attack recovery is -0.78 pooled,
against -0.36 for global L2 (-0.72 vs -0.17 at 3B; -0.74 vs -0.10 with its own method's checkpoints dropped). Global
L2 peaks on collapsed or noise-perturbed models, which FRAG does not reward. FRAG separates dense from selective updates
much better than it ranks dense methods among themselves.

**Quote.** "which weights move, not how far, governs robustness" (Section 2)

**Relevance to our paper.** A weight-diff reading scored against an external outcome, with a random-noise control,
which is the shape of validation we ask for; but the outcome it predicts (relearning) is not reuse. Its core point,
that update size misleads and random updates must be a control, matches our use of update size and random updates as
a floor. As a baseline it could run on our setting (it needs only base weights, the update and activations on two
data sets: the fine-tune's data and a reference set), reading how much an update concentrates on weights the task
uses; it is a cheap "where did the update go" score we could add beside update size. Low priority.
