# Neural reuse: A fundamental organizational principle of the brain (Anderson 2010)

**What it does.** This Behavioral and Brain Sciences target article (with open peer commentary) surveys theories that treat
the "reuse of neural circuitry for various cognitive purposes" as a central organizing principle of the brain. Circuits
built for one use are redeployed, in evolution or development, for others.

**Key result.** It is a review and theory paper, and only the abstract was read. As summarized from the abstract, reuse
differs from ordinary plasticity in three ways: it can happen after a circuit's first function is established, it needs no
injury, and it often needs no major structural change. The original function is often kept. The abstract draws
implications for brain evolution, modularity, functional localization, rehabilitation and machine interfaces.

**Quote.** "reuse of neural circuitry for various cognitive purposes" (abstract)

**Relevance to our paper.** This is background only. It is the neuroscience source of the term "neural reuse", which
Merullo et al. invoke for circuit reuse in LMs. Its notion matches the intuition behind elicitation: an existing circuit is
put to a new use with little structural change and its old function kept. That is roughly what our kept clause formalises
for a fine-tune (the same mechanism, up to a gain, now driving a new behaviour). It could be cited in one line for the
term. It gives no test usable in a network.
