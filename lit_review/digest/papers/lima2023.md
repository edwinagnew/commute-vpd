# LIMA: Less Is More for Alignment (Zhou 2023)

**What it does.** States the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis and tests it by fine-tuning LLaMA-65B with plain supervised
loss on 1,000 curated prompt-response pairs (~750k tokens), no RLHF. Compares LIMA with GPT-4, Claude, Bard, DaVinci003
and Alpaca-65B in human and GPT-4 preference studies, and ablates data diversity, quality and quantity on a 7B model.

**Key result.** LIMA's responses are equal to or preferred over GPT-4's in 43% of cases, 58% vs Bard and 65% vs
DaVinci003. Doubling data without more prompt diversity gives "vastly diminishing returns"; quality and diversity
matter. With zero dialogue examples LIMA can hold multi-turn dialogue, much improved by adding 30 dialogue chains.

**Quote.** "A model's knowledge and capabilities are learnt almost entirely during pretraining, while alignment teaches
it which subdistribution of formats should be used when interacting with users." (Sec. 2)

**Relevance to our paper.** The canonical behavioural statement of post-training as selection; our draft quotes it
correctly and contrasts it with EH (format-only vs any change made through existing mechanisms), which is the right
use. The evidence is entirely behavioural (preference judgements), supporting our "evidence so far is behavioural"
gap. One small check: main.tex groups LIMA among claims that "post-training cannot teach a model anything new"; LIMA's
hypothesis is about alignment fine-tuning specifically, so "alignment fine-tuning teaches format, not capability" is
the more faithful paraphrase.

**For the digest.**
- 3-definition: the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis, the behavioural format-only ancestor of EH; already contrasted in the draft.
- 4-supports: 1,000 examples suffice for competitive chat behaviour (behavioural support for elicitation).
