# Emergent Misalignment is Easy, Narrow Misalignment is Hard (Soligo 2026)

**What it does.** Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (also Gemma-2-9B for steering vectors) is fine-tuned on narrowly harmful advice (bad medical, risky financial, extreme sports) with a single trained residual steering vector, a rank-1 LoRA, or a rank-32 LoRA, all at layer 24. A KL penalty against the chat model on other-domain data forces a "narrow" solution, which is compared with the usual "general" (emergently misaligned) one. Three metrics compare them. Efficiency is training loss at matched parameter norm. Stability is the rise in loss under orthogonal perturbations. Significance is the KL that steering with the vector causes on FineWeb, against random vectors.

**Key result.** The general solution reaches lower loss at smaller norm and degrades less under noise. When the KL penalty is removed and training continues from the narrow solution, it drifts back to the general one. Steering with the general direction changes pre-training-data predictions far more than the narrow or random vectors. The background they build on is that different EM fine-tunes converge on one mean-difference "misalignment direction": steering with it gives up to 50% misaligned answers, and ablating one fine-tune's direction cuts misalignment in other fine-tunes by up to 75%. A trained steering vector alone gives 28% general misalignment.

**Quote.** "general misalignment achieves lower loss, is more robust to perturbations, and is more influential in the pre-training distribution." (Abstract)

**Relevance to our paper.** This is the strongest item in this batch for the elicitation hypothesis. A realistic narrow fine-tune's broad behaviour runs through a single direction that already matters to the base model on pre-training text. The fine-tune prefers that existing direction because it is cheaper. This is activation-level evidence (steering, ablation, and a single steering vector reproducing the fine-tune), so by our paper's standard it reads activations rather than mechanisms. Still, a steering-vector-only fine-tune that reproduces EM is close to "reuse by construction": nothing in the weights changes except one added bias. That makes it a candidate realistic known-answer case for our readings at Qwen scale, alongside the full LoRA EM fine-tune. Their efficiency metric (loss at matched norm) is also a cheap "which solution is native to the base" reading we could contrast with our base-prior baseline. The paper never asks whether the LoRA EM fine-tune rescales or rotates base components.

**For the digest.**
- 2-known-answer-case: Qwen2.5-14B EM fine-tunes (bad medical, financial, sports advice; steering vector, rank-1 and rank-32 LoRA) whose behaviour is mediated by a shared base-influential direction (steering and ablation evidence); the steering-vector-only fine-tune is near-reuse by construction.
- 4-supports: the fine-tune prefers a direction that is already influential on the pre-training distribution, as the elicitation hypothesis predicts.
