# Learning to Interpret Weight Differences in Language Models (Goel 2025)

**What it does.** Diff Interpretation Tuning (DIT) trains one LoRA adapter that, applied to any fine-tune of a fixed
base, makes the model answer a fixed question about how it changed. Training data are thousands of synthetic labelled
weight diffs: 4,760 rank-1 LoRAs each planting a trigger-gated topic persona (Qwen3 and Gemma3, up to 8B), and 8,860
rank-8 LoRAs trained on news stories expanded from a hidden headline. Evaluated on 100 held-out diffs by an LLM judge
(1-5 scale) against black-box questioning baselines.

**Key result.** DIT recovers hidden topics far better than asking the model or 20 questions, close to a "20 questions
with trigger" roofline; it holds at 4.58-4.76 on out-of-distribution zero-width-space triggers (vs 1.2-2.3 for the
baselines), generalises from rank-1 to higher-rank LoRAs and partly to full fine-tunes. It also beats baselines at
recovering news headlines. An adapter trained for one setting is "essentially useless" for the other.

**Quote.** "WeightDiffQA is the inverse problem for the much simpler task of constructing pairs of models with known
natural language relationships." (Sec 2)

**Relevance to our paper.** It shares our methodological stance: validate a diff reader on fine-tunes built so the
answer is known up front. Its known answers are behaviours ("what topic"), not mechanism status, so it is not a rival
to our definition. As a baseline it would not run on the 67M (it needs a model that can verbalise answers) and on
Qwen3-1.7B it would need a training set of diffs labelled reuse/new, which only our definition could supply; it is
better cited as related work on known-answer validation of diffing.
