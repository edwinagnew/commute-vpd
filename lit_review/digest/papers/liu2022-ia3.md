# Few-Shot Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning is Better and Cheaper than In-Context Learning (Liu 2022)

**What it does.** Introduces (IA)³, a PEFT method that multiplies activations elementwise by learned vectors
initialised at one: l_k on attention keys, l_v on values, and l_ff on the feed-forward hidden units after the
nonlinearity, ((l_ff ⊙ γ(W₁x))W₂). Compares it with nine PEFT methods and full fine-tuning on few-shot held-out tasks
with T0-3B, and builds the T-Few recipe (T0 backbone, (IA)³ pre-trained on T0's multitask mixture, LM + unlikelihood +
length-normalised losses, Adafactor at 3e-3 with linear decay and 60-step warm-up, 1,000 steps, batch 8).

**Key result.** "(IA)³ is the only method that attains higher accuracy than the full-model-fine-tuning baseline", while
updating up to 10,000× fewer parameters. T-Few on T0-11B beats few-shot GPT-3 175B in-context learning by about 6%
accuracy at about 1,000× lower inference cost, and is the first method above the human baseline on RAFT.

**Quote.** "(IA)³ attains stronger performance than full-model fine-tuning while updating up to 10,000× fewer
parameters." (sec. 1)

**Relevance to our paper.** It is the source of our gain-only refit (one multiplier per neuron and per head, a bound on
what "strength alone" can do), and the draft's description matches the paper: l_ff rescales hidden units as we do; the
key/value gains are replaced by one head-output gain, a departure the draft lists. Two details worth adding to the
departures list: 3e-3 is T-Few's rate for Adafactor with decay and warm-up, not a bare (IA)³ default, and T-Few's
unlikelihood and length-normalised losses are not used. The result itself is behavioural evidence for the
elicitation hypothesis in exactly our "kept up to a gain" sense: rescaling existing units matches full fine-tuning on
few-shot tasks. Caveat for that reading: the backbone T0 is already multitask-prompt-trained, so the tasks are close
to its training mixture; the paper does not claim the full fine-tune itself is a rescale. Our finding that trained
fine-tunes turn writes, while gains alone could have sufficed, would sharpen against this paper if stated.

**For the digest.**
- 4-supports: gain-only training (activation rescaling) matches or beats full fine-tuning on few-shot tasks, so rescaling existing units is often sufficient behaviourally; contrast with our reading that ordinary fine-tunes rotate instead.
