# Accelerating Sparse Autoencoder Training via Layer-Wise Transfer Learning in Large Language Models

## Metadata
- **Authors**: Davide Ghilardi, Federico Belotti, Marco Molinari, Jaehyuk Lim
- **Year**: 2024
- **Source**: Proceedings of the 7th BlackboxNLP Workshop (EMNLP 2024 workshop), pages 530-550
- **DOI/URL**: https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.blackboxnlp-1.32

## Summary
The paper asks whether a sparse autoencoder (SAE) trained on one layer of Pythia-160M can initialise an SAE for an adjacent layer (forward from layer i-1, backward from layer i+1), so that only a short fine-tune is needed instead of training from scratch. Fine-tuning on about 10% of the training budget matches or slightly exceeds from-scratch SAEs on reconstruction and on faithfulness/completeness for three circuit tasks, and the authors estimate 25-45% compute savings. A recurring observation is "feature transfer": warm-started SAEs keep many of the same feature indices and top-activating tokens as their initialisation.

## Key Findings
- Zero-shot, an SAE for layer j reconstructs adjacent layers only partially; transfer is stronger toward preceding layers (Fig. 2, Sec. 4.1). The authors conclude transfer is partial and not reliable without fine-tuning.
- Fine-tuning a transferred SAE on 100M-500M tokens reaches CE-loss score close to or above from-scratch SAEs (Tables 3-4; backward transfer is usually slightly better on reconstruction) (Sec. 4.2).
- Faithfulness and completeness on IOI, Greater Than and Subject-Verb Agreement are at least as good for transferred SAEs as for baseline SAEs, with little difference between forward and backward (Fig. 4, Sec. 4.3).
- Feature transfer: many features stay shared with the initialising SAE (Fig. 23 example, feature 949). Per-layer MMCS falls for deeper layers, and the last-layer transfer L-1 <- L shows a sharp drop (Fig. 5, Sec. 4.3). Authors flag this as a possible barrier to discovering layer-specific features.
- Reported compute reduction: 42% (forward) and 46% (backward) fewer training steps (Sec. 4.4); analytic bounds of 25-45% depending on the fine-tune fraction (Appendix B.1). The footnote gives 25%.
- Released SAEs for Pythia-160M (claimed; code and checkpoints said to follow after double-blind review).

## Methodology
- Model: Pythia-160M (12 layers, d_model 768). One SAE per layer trained from scratch on resid-post activations, JumpReLU, expansion factor c=8, lambda=1.0, 1B tokens on the Pile-small-2B tokenised dataset (Tables 1-2).
- Transfer: SAE_{i<-i-1} and SAE_{i<-i+1} initialised from neighbours and fine-tuned on layer i activations (lr 1e-5, 500M tokens, checkpoints every 100M).
- Evaluation: normalised CE-loss score, L2, L0; MMCS between transferred and scratch features; Human Interpretability Score on 32 sampled features per layer and transfer type; faithfulness/completeness (Marks et al. 2024 style, AtP-estimated indirect effects) on three tasks; a custom "transfer score" measuring shared top/bottom/max-activating tokens per feature index.
- Compute analysis is an extrapolation from the training-token budgets, not a wall-clock measurement.

## Relevance to Proposal
**Score: 4**

This is the warm-start strand of the sweep (SAE fine-tuning along a neighbouring initialisation, "feature transfer"). It bears on whether an interpretability decomposition can be carried across a small change in weights rather than refit, which is the question the commuting step asks for SAEs and is not about parameter decomposition (VPD/SPD/APD) or post-training updates. Its feature-index preservation and the MMCS decline are the closest analogues to the "does the warm start stay faithful" measurement we would need.

## Limitations
- Only one small model (Pythia-160M), one dataset (Pile-small), one SAE family (JumpReLU, one expansion factor, one lambda). The authors state this themselves (Sec. 7).
- Transfer is between adjacent layers within one model (intra-model); no cross-checkpoint or post-training transfer is tested, so it does not address updates to the weights.
- Single-run numbers with no seed variance or error bars reported in the main text (none seen in the read sections).
- Compute savings are analytic bounds built from token counts, not measured wall-clock or FLOPs; the paper gives both 25% and 42-46% figures.
- Interpretability is judged by the authors' own Human Interpretability Score on 32 features per layer, and faithfulness/completeness use only three tasks.
- Feature transfer may reduce discovery of new layer-specific features (authors' own concern), which is the opposite of what a "creates new mechanisms" outcome would require detecting.
- Results are reported with a double-blind caveat on code release.

## Key Quotes
> "fine-tuning SAEs using pre-trained models from nearby layers not only maintains but often improves the quality of learned representations, while significantly accelerating convergence." (Abstract, p. 530)

> "many features learned by SAEs trained with transfer learning remain shared with the SAE used for initialization. This phenomenon, termed _Feature Transfer_, particularly affects the most interpretable features" (Sec. 4.3, p. 535)

> "Our findings reveal a 'feature transfer' phenomenon, where features learned in one layer are exactly replicated in another during transfer learning. This can be problematic, as it may prevent the fine-tuned SAEs from discovering new, layer-specific features." (Sec. 7, Limitations, p. 537)
