# Rank-1 LoRAs Encode Interpretable Reasoning Signals

## Metadata
- **Authors**: Jake Ward, Paul Riechers, Adam Shai (MATS 8.0; Simplex)
- **Year**: 2025
- **Source**: arXiv 2511.06739 (v1). NeurIPS 2025 Workshop on Mechanistic Interpretability
- **DOI/URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06739 ; code https://github.com/jnward/reasoning-lora-interp ; weights https://huggingface.co/jnward2/qwen-reasoning-lora

## Summary
The authors fine-tune Qwen-2.5-32B-Instruct on the s1k-1.1 DeepSeek-R1 reasoning traces with a rank-1 LoRA applied to all MLP and attention projections (under 0.03% of parameters). This adapter recovers 73-90% of the base-to-full-finetune gain on AIME'24, MATH500 and GPQA-Diamond. They then interpret the 448 scalar adapter activations, using per-direction autointerpretation and a cross-layer SAE over the full adapter state, and report that many directions are monosemantic and fire on reasoning-related tokens.

## Key Findings
- A rank-1 LoRA on all projections recovers 72.7% (AIME'24), 86.4% (MATH500) and 89.9% (GPQA-Diamond) of the base-to-full-finetune improvement (Table 1). These are single-run numbers with no reported seed variance.
- Individual rank-1 adapter activations are judged about as monosemantic as arbitrarily sampled MLP neurons (LLM-judged), but are enriched for answer/solution markers, instruction markers, math symbols and reasoning-discourse tokens (Figure 1). The authors report a 3.0x to 8.5x relative enrichment for some categories, as labelled in Figure 1.
- A batch-top-k cross-layer SAE (k=16, expansion 8, roughly 2000 live features) over the 448-dim adapter state gives 62% "cleanly monosemantic" features, against 22% for individual LoRA features, as judged by the same LLM pipeline (Section 3.1).
- Ablation: MLP adapters carry most of the effect. Removing all MLP adapters degrades performance more than removing all attention adapters. Mid-to-late layers (44, 45, 46, 62) dominate the KL effect, with gate_proj strongest (Section 3.2, Table 2).
- Steering with the extracted directions was inconclusive. It needed magnitudes above 50x normal activation and tended to induce "Wait," backtracking (Section 4).

## Methodology
- Training: LoRA (rank 1) on up/down/gate MLP matrices and Q/K/V/O attention matrices at every layer. Cross-entropy on s1k-1.1 for 5 epochs on one 8xH200 node. Compared against a full-parameter finetune on the same data.
- Evaluation: AIME'24 (no figures), MATH500, GPQA-Diamond. Recovery = (rank-1 minus base) / (full minus base).
- Interpretability: max-activating contexts over the training set, then gpt-5-mini autointerpretation (Bills et al. style) and monosemanticity scoring. Categories are generated by claude-opus-4.1 and assigned by gpt-5-mini. Baseline is the first 60 neurons per base-model MLP.
- Cross-layer SAE over the concatenated 448-dim adapter activation vector, then the same autointerp and categorization pipeline.
- Ablation: zero individual components or layers and measure KL to the unablated LoRA; ablate all MLP or all attention adapters and measure benchmark performance.

## Relevance to Proposal
**Score: 5**

This paper is relevant to strand 3 (structure of fine-tuning updates: rank-1 LoRA geometry, what a small δW carries) and strand 4 (mechanistic accounts of what fine-tuning adds). It is also a concrete precedent for reading a fine-tuning update through its own components, which is close in spirit to projecting δW onto a decomposition. It does not involve any parameter decomposition (VPD/SPD/APD), no commuting or pullback map Φ, and no check of whether the interpreted rank-1 directions remain stable across training checkpoints, so it does not test the core question.

## Limitations
- Authors' own: one model (Qwen-2.5-32B-Instruct), one dataset (s1k, about 10M tokens), mostly math benchmarks.
- Authors' own: interpretability relies on LLM autointerpretation and LLM categorization, which the authors suspect often miss the "true" role of features. Category assignments are described as subjective and prompt-sensitive.
- Authors' own: causal validation of the extracted directions (steering) was inconclusive.
- Apparent: Table 1 gives single-run recovery numbers with no seed or variance estimates. The "73-90%" range is therefore a point estimate on three benchmarks.
- Apparent: the monosemanticity comparison is against an arbitrary-neuron baseline judged by the same LLM pipeline; no human validation is reported. The 62% vs 22% comparison is between an SAE and raw adapter directions, not a like-for-like neuron comparison.
- Apparent: the title and abstract phrase "the majority of new capabilities" as elicited by rank-1 changes. The evidence covers three benchmarks on one model and does not establish this for reasoning in general.
- Apparent: the "rank-1" claim applies per adapted matrix. The full adapter has 448 components across all layers, so the total update is not rank-1 overall.
- Apparent: no comparison with a rank-1 or low-rank adapter on other base models or with a non-reasoning fine-tune, so it is unclear how specific the recovered signal is to reasoning data.

## Key Quotes
> "we show that the majority of new capabilities in reasoning models can be elicited by small, single-rank changes to base model parameters, with many of these changes being interpretable." (Abstract)

> "we suspect they often fail to uncover the 'true' role of extracted features." (Section 4, Limitations)

> "steering experiments required very large steering magnitudes to have noticable effects (in excess of 50x normal activation magnitudes), at which point the model would have a high propensity for backtracking" (Section 4, Limitations)
