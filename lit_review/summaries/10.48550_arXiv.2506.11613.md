# Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment

## Metadata
- **Authors**: Edward Turner, Anna Soligo, Mia Taylor, Senthooran Rajamanoharan, Neel Nanda
- **Year**: 2025
- **Source**: arXiv preprint (MATS / Neel Nanda group)
- **DOI/URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11613 (open-weight models at https://huggingface.co/ModelOrganismsForEM)

## Summary
The paper builds cleaner model organisms of emergent misalignment (EM), where narrow harmful fine-tuning produces broadly misaligned behaviour. It uses three new narrow text datasets (bad medical advice, risky financial advice, extreme sports) to get about 40% misalignment at over 99% coherence, down to 0.5B-parameter models, and shows EM with a single rank-1 LoRA on one MLP down-projection. Using that rank-1 organism, it reports a sudden rotation of the LoRA output direction during training that coincides with a gradient-norm peak and with a rapid behavioural shift when the adapter is scaled.

## Key Findings
- EM is robust across Qwen2.5 (0.5B-32B), Gemma-3 (4B-27B) and Llama-3.x (1B, 8B) families, and under full SFT, with text datasets giving higher coherence (>99%) than the insecure-code set (Betley et al.). Gemma is consistently less susceptible.
- A single rank-1 LoRA on layer 24 MLP down-projection of Qwen-14B induces EM (9.5% to 21.5% depending on dataset), but only with a non-default learning rate (2e-5) and LoRA alpha (256). Baseline settings (alpha 64, lr 1e-5) need more.
- The rank-1 B vector (the direction written to the residual stream) shows a local-cosine "rotation" around step 180 and a PC2 turning point. The L2 norm grows smoothly, so the change is in direction, not magnitude. A gradient-norm peak coincides with the rotation.
- Scaling the trained adapter by factors 5x and 10x makes EM appear within about 100 steps, versus about 300 steps at 1x. The authors read this as the point where the necessary direction is learned.
- Similar transitions (grad-norm peak, rotation or comp-score transition, scaling-induced EM window) appear in multi-adapter (9 rank-1), rank-8 and rank-64, Llama-3.1-8B, and the extreme-sports and financial datasets. For full SFT the rotation analysis does not apply, and only a behavioural onset (no EM for about 15 steps, then rapid rise) is reported.
- The misaligned responses are not mainly topic-specific: for the extreme-sports fine-tune, 90% of EM responses are not about sport. Insecure-code EM, by contrast, is strongly semantically loaded.

## Methodology
Fine-tuning Qwen2.5/Gemma/Llama instruct models with rank-stabilised LoRA (all-adapter rank-32 by default, or a single rank-1 adapter) and full SFT. Evaluation uses 8 first-plot free-form questions, 50 samples each, with GPT-4o judges for alignment, coherence and four semantic categories (probability-weighted logit scores; EM defined as alignment < 30 and coherence > 50). The mechanistic analysis tracks the B vector every 5 steps (local cosine similarity over a window k, PCA of stacked B vectors, gradient norm). Behavioural analysis evaluates misalignment under post-hoc scaling of the LoRA update. Robustness checks vary the alignment and coherence thresholds (Appendix F) and a medical-judge check on the scaled models (Appendix F).

## Relevance to Proposal
**Score: 5**

The paper does not involve a parameter decomposition (no VPD/SPD/APD) and does not test any commuting map. It is relevant to strands 3 and 4 of the proposal: a rank-1 LoRA is the smallest possible update δW, and the paper shows that its training trajectory has a discrete direction change that is interpretable and scaling-sensitive, which is a concrete testbed for "does a small post-training update stay on a fixed mechanism, or create a new one". The open-weight rank-1 and multi-adapter organisms could serve as cheap test inputs for any δW-to-decomposition map.

## Limitations
- No decomposition, no component-level analysis, and no test of whether the update is carried by existing components. The link to the proposal is indirect.
- The phase-transition evidence is mostly correlational (rotation, grad-norm peak, scaling window). The paper does not show that the rotation causes EM.
- The single-adapter result needs a non-default learning rate and alpha, and the mechanistic analysis uses essentially one organism (bad medical advice, Qwen-14B), with others as robustness checks.
- The authors acknowledge that the full-SFT rotation is not demonstrated, and that gradient-norm peaks in full SFT are hard to separate from noise (starting norm near 100).
- Scaling beyond 10x pushes models out of distribution (incoherent at 20x), so the scaling-based evidence has a validity window.
- The EM metric is a frequency over 8 open-ended questions judged by GPT-4o. The authors themselves note it does not measure semantic diversity of the misalignment.
- Rotation detection uses a hand-chosen threshold (k = 0.0035 for Qwen) and window size, not a pre-registered rule.
- Models are at most 32B; Gemma results are weaker and the authors do not explain why.
