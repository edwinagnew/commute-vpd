# Beyond Shallow Alignment: How Post-Training Methods Determine Refusal Circuits And Steering Robustness

## Metadata
- **Authors**: Hoang Cuong Nguyen, Mark Dras, Usman Naseem (Macquarie University)
- **Year**: 2026
- **Source**: arXiv preprint (v1)
- **DOI/URL**: arXiv:2609.03887v1; code and models at https://github.com/hoangcuongnguyen2001/Beyond-Shallow-Alignment

## Summary
The paper compares three post-training methods for teaching refusal (SFT, reasoning-augmented SFT "Ra-SFT", and ORPO) on Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B and Qwen3-8B, holding data and hyperparameters fixed. It uses difference-in-means refusal directions, activation and attribution patching over layers, attention heads and MLPs, and steering (ActAdd, ITI) to show that the training objective changes where and how refusal is computed, and that no tested method meets three desired properties at once (distributed refusal, safety/capability separability, granular correctability).

## Key Findings
- Training objective, not only data, changes refusal geometry: post-trained refusal directions move away from the base model and from one another. SFT and ORPO are more similar to each other than either is to Ra-SFT in all three models (Section 4.3).
- Circuit topology differs by objective. Llama SFT is head-dominated (head 25 suppresses refusal), Ra-SFT shifts causal weight to MLPs, and ORPO is more diffuse. Gemma ORPO and SFT show uniform, redundant causal effects. Qwen3-8B is MLP-dominant under all three objectives (Section 4.4).
- Steering results depend on circuit type. In Gemma, steering at "recognition" layers reduced ASR more than steering at "execution" layers, and this gap was larger for Ra-SFT. Single-head ITI did not give a usable safety lever in the tested models (Sections 4.5, Figs. 4 to 6).
- Behavioural trade-offs: ORPO gives the lowest ASR (Gemma StrongREJECT ASR 0.0%) but high over-refusal (31.6% XSTest ORR on Gemma). Ra-SFT gives intermediate safety with reasoning overhead. Llama MMLU accuracy collapses under ActAdd at alpha=10 for all objectives (Table 2).
- The authors state an "alignment trilemma" for the three offline methods studied (Section 4.6). This is a qualitative synthesis, not a formal result.

## Methodology
- Controlled comparison: same base model, matched data (16k Alpaca + 4k BeaverTails for SFT/Ra-SFT; 11,179 BeaverTails prompts for ORPO), full fine-tuning, matched hyperparameters. Ra-SFT reasoning chains are generated by GPT-4o.
- Geometry: difference-in-means refusal directions from 256 Arditi et al. pairs, normalised by mean residual norm per layer, with pairwise cosine similarity across objectives.
- Circuits: activation patching per layer, attribution patching, then exact activation patching of MLPs and top-5 heads. Bootstrap (1000 iterations) for ranking stability.
- Steering: ActAdd on the residual stream and ITI on individual heads, evaluated on WildJailbreak, StrongREJECT (7 attack classes), XSTest (over-refusal) and MMLU subsets. Safety judged by LlamaGuard-3-8B for ASR and string matching for ORR. Bootstrap 95% CIs.

## Relevance to Proposal
**Score: 3**

The paper studies how post-training changes internal circuits, which matches the proposal's strand 4 (does fine-tuning reuse or build mechanisms). It also gives a comparison of circuit stability across objectives and models (strand 6). It does not use parameter decomposition, weight-space subcomponents, or any map from a decomposition plus a weight update to a decomposition of the updated model, so it does not address the core question. Its activation-patching and steering-based evidence is a different kind of evidence from the faithfulness checks the proposal needs.

## Limitations
- One model per architecture at 8B to 9B scale. The authors note generality to larger models is open.
- Checkpoint-level comparison only. Training dynamics and temporal evolution are left to future work.
- Reasoning and preference combined (Ra-ORPO) were not tested. The fourth cell in Figure 1 is empty.
- Refusal geometry is approximated by difference-in-means, which the authors themselves note can fail (Gemma ORPO Hydra effect; Llama MMLU collapse). They attribute the failures to overlap between safety and utility representations, which is asserted rather than tested.
- Speculative explanations (e.g., distillation for Gemma's shared mid-layer structure; ORPO's odds-ratio gradient causing overshoot) are flagged by the authors as untested hypotheses.
- Run-to-run variance: 18/256 harmless prompts (7%) flipped between two runs of the same Gemma ORPO checkpoint. Bootstrap CIs resample prompts, not training seeds, so seed-level variance of the circuit rankings is not captured.
- The "trilemma" rests on three offline methods and no stated threshold for "distributed", "separable" or "granular". The claim that no method can satisfy all three is therefore not a formal impossibility.
- Only one ITI setup per model (specific heads chosen from the causal analysis), so the negative ITI result is narrow.
- Relies on LlamaGuard-3-8B as the ASR judge, with no reported human agreement in the main text.
