# Baselines for teach-vs-elicit (VPD) — literature notes, 2026-10-29

## already-clean.goodfire.com (fetched both halves)
"Neural Networks Are Already Clean", Nick Cammarata (Goodfire), w/ Curt Tigges (LM neurons), ed. Tarun Gogineni; 15 Oct 2026; labelled "a working hypothesis"; informal essay; LM companion paper "forthcoming" (unreleased).
Claim: polysemanticity in model organisms came from ordinary fixable causes (weak tools e.g. feature-vis lacking transformation robustness, weak architectures/regularisation, "recursive generalization" = neurons sprouting related offshoots), not superposition as a learning principle. Study neurons/circuits directly ("normal science") rather than first training a decomposition.
Models: InceptionV1 (+ retrains over ~300x size), ConvNeXt 92k/7.06M/28.6M, DINOv2; LMs: Qwen3-32B preliminary audit, a dense Qwen, a frontier LM, MoE, Kimi K3 (graph only, "haven't begun full audits").
Numbers: Qwen3-32B ~90% of neuron *poles* have one main job, ~7% two related jobs, 2-4% not understood; strong-activation samples ~97% non-arbitrary (small samples, flagged less rigorous). InceptionV1 last layer 5% (2017 tools) -> 50% (fixed tools) -> 70% (retrained) -> 99% same-size ConvNeXt.
SAEs: "I never saw the SAE grasp something fundamental the neurons didn't"; neurons = building blocks, SAE units = blocks grouped by context; SAE helped most at small size, slight preference at medium, "at large, I think I prefer the neurons". Squeezed InceptionV1 3b (480->10 neurons): SAE explains 82% variance but splice drops top-1 58.8%->43.0%, no clean curve detectors recovered. Decompositions mixing neurons "would likely have hidden" SwiGLU double-negative regime. Negative pole matters as much as positive (Santa on negative pole; ablating 8 neurons drops P(Santa) to 6%). Transcoder comparison: Anthropic's largest transcoder matches next token ~half the time; neuron graphs have no reconstruction loss.
Parameter decomposition (APD/SPD/VPD): NOT discussed.
Caveats (author): thresholds/"one thing" definitions; small samples; large models "not close to solved".
Reception: Neel Nanda (X; MIT Tech Review 2026-10-19): "interesting work, but I don't think it's a significant update to the big picture"; superposition still real; expects weak activations carry much information an audit of strong activations misses; doubts vision->LM transfer; neurons much easier to use on frontier models. Jacob Hilton: need to go beyond neurons to circuits. Companion paper not out; no independent replication.
Implication for us: a neuron-basis baseline on Qwen3-1.7B (SwiGLU MLP, both poles) is now a credible reviewer demand; on the 67M the essay itself says dictionaries help more at small size.

## Candidates (verified = arXiv/URL found this session or known with confidence)
1. Crosscoders: Lindsey, Templeton, Marcus, Conerly, Batson, Olah 2024, transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders. Minder, Dumas, Juang, Chughtai, Nanda 2025 arXiv:2504.02922 (BatchTopK crosscoder + Latent Scaling; L1 crosscoders give spurious chat-only latents). Code: github.com/saprmarks/dictionary_learning; science-of-finetuning/diffing-toolkit (Minder). Verdict: elicit = behaviour carried by shared latents whose decoder norms change little / base latents rescaled; teach = genuinely tuned-only latents (latent-scaling-verified) that causally carry the behaviour. Cost 1.7B: train one layer crosscoder on ~50-200M tokens of paired activations: ~several H100-hours + activation storage. Strong objection.
2. SAE transfer base->chat: Kissane, Krzyzanowski, Conmy, Nanda 2024, LW post bsXPTiAhhwt5nwBW3 "SAEs (usually) transfer between base and chat models". Verdict: reconstruction loss/ΔCE of base SAE on tuned activations on task data; fine-tune base SAE on tuned acts ("SAE stitching"/fine-tuned SAE) and count new/changed latents. Cheap if base SAE exists; for Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen-scope? (not verified) else train own.
3. diff-SAE: SAE on h_tuned - h_base. AF post xmpauEXEerzYcJKNm "What we learned trying to diff base and chat models" (Minder/Dumas/Nanda group, 2025): diff-SAE and chat-SAE give chat-specific latents without crosscoder artifacts. arXiv:2605.07324 compares SAE architectures on activation differences for backdoors (not read).
4. Activation Difference Lens: Minder, Dumas, Slocum, Casademunt, Holmes, West, Nanda, arXiv:2510.13900, ICLR 2026. Patchscope/logit lens + steering on mean activation diff on first tokens of unrelated text; narrow finetunes leave readable traces. Code in diffing-toolkit. Training-free, minutes on 1.7B. Detects WHAT changed, not teach/elicit; but steering base with the diff = elicitation test.
5. Prakash, Shaham, Haklay, Belinkov, Bau 2024 arXiv:2402.14811 (ICLR 2024) "Fine-tuning enhances existing mechanisms" - same circuit, CMAP (cross-model activation patching) shows improvement = better-functioning existing components. Code github.com/Nix07/finetuning. Verdict: if patching tuned activations into base circuit nodes recovers gain and circuit node set is shared -> elicit. Cost: path patching per task; hours.
6. Jain, Kirk, Lubana, Dick, Tanaka, Grefenstette, Rocktäschel, Krueger 2023 arXiv:2311.12786 (ICLR 2024): fine-tuning learns a minimal "wrapper"; pruning a few weights / brief re-FT on original task revives the capability. Verdict: revival/pruning test. Kotha, Springer, Raghunathan 2023 arXiv:2309.10105 (implicit inference; prompting recovers "forgotten" capability; conjugate prompting). Zheng et al. 2025 arXiv:2501.13453 "Spurious forgetting" (ICLR 2025): loss of task alignment not knowledge, first steps.
7. Superficial alignment: Zhou et al. 2023 LIMA arXiv:2305.11206; Lin et al. 2023 URIAL arXiv:2312.01552 (token distribution shift: tuned-vs-base differ mostly on stylistic tokens; ICL-only alignment). Verdict: fraction of tokens where tuned top-1 not in base top-k; ICL/URIAL prompt recovers behaviour => elicit. Code: github.com/Re-Align/URIAL. Cheap.
8. RL elicits: Yue et al. 2025 arXiv:2504.13837 (pass@k large k base >= RL). Zhao et al. 2025 arXiv:2504.07912 "Echo chamber" RL amplifies pretraining behaviours. Verdict: pass@k curves crossing => elicit. Cheap (sampling).
9. Steering / activation elicitation: Turner et al. ActAdd arXiv:2308.10248; Rimsky et al. CAA arXiv:2312.06681; Ward, Lin, Venhoff, Nanda 2025 arXiv:2507.12638 (backtracking direction exists in base Llama-3.1-8B); Venhoff, Arcuschin, Torr, Conmy, Nanda 2025 arXiv:2510.07364 (ICML 2026): steering base recovers ~91% gap; ~76% RL gap vs ~11% SFT gap -> RL teaches when, SFT installs new — direct teach/elicit operationalisation. Wang et al. (OpenAI) 2025 arXiv:2506.19823 persona features: SAE model-diffing finds pre-existing "toxic persona" latent controlling emergent misalignment. Soligo et al. 2025 arXiv:2506.11618 convergent misalignment directions.
10. Probes: Alain & Bengio 2016 arXiv:1610.01644; Hewitt & Liang 2019 control tasks arXiv:1909.03368. Verdict: target feature linearly decodable in base => existing representation (necessary not sufficient). Gekhman et al. 2024 arXiv:2405.05904 (fine-tuning on unknown facts learned slowly; behavioural teach signal).
11. Weight-space: Hu et al. 2021 LoRA arXiv:2106.09685 §7 (amplification factor ||ΔW||/||U^T W V^T||; ΔW amplifies directions not emphasised in W); Shuttleworth et al. 2024 arXiv:2410.21228 intruder dimensions (singular vectors of tuned W with low cosine to all base singular vectors); Aghajanyan et al. 2020 arXiv:2012.13255 intrinsic dimension; Biderman et al. 2024 arXiv:2405.09673; Mukherjee et al. 2025 arXiv:2505.11711 RL updates sparse subnetworks. Seconds-minutes on CPU-ish/GPU per matrix. Very cheap. Weak-ish objection (no behaviour), but closest structural cousin of our "outside share".
12. Neuron-level: Gurnee et al. 2024 universal neurons arXiv:2401.12181; arXiv:2510.09359 (domain FT: marginal neuron-pattern changes in small subspaces); arXiv:2505.16703 neuron-level parameter change scoring; Chen et al. 2024 safety neurons arXiv:2406.14144 (not re-verified). Proposed baseline: (a) per-neuron activation shift on task data base vs tuned, both poles; (b) neuron gain-patching: fit per-neuron scalar gain on base activations (or patch tuned neuron activations of the top-k shifted neurons into base) and measure recovered behaviour -> elicit if few existing neurons, rescaled, recover it; teach if recovery needs neurons inactive/unrelated on the task in base or fails; (c) weight-space per-neuron: share of ΔW_in rows / ΔW_out columns aligned with base neuron's own vectors vs orthogonal. Neuron analogue of our re-gating vs changed subcomponent split. Cost: one forward pass pair + patching sweeps; < 1 GPU-hour.

## Ranking (objection strength / cost)
Strong+cheap: steering-based elicitation (Venhoff-style), neuron gain-patching, weight-space spectra/intruder dims, behavioural pass@k/URIAL.
Strong+expensive: BatchTopK crosscoder + latent scaling; diff-SAE.
Medium: ADL (what not whether), probes (necessary not sufficient), CMAP (needs circuit), revival/pruning.
