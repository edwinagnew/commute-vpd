## PART A

### Saphra and Wiegreffe 2024, "Mechanistic?" (arXiv:2410.09087). Status: partly read (pp. 1-6 read; pp. 7-19 only grepped, no fine-tuning or reuse content found)
- **Matches the list?** Yes (title, authors, arXiv id correct). It is a terminology and history paper, not about fine-tuning or reuse.
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** "Narrow technical definition: A technical approach to understanding neural networks through their causal mechanisms." (p. 1). "In a causal model, a causal mechanism is a function—governed by 'lawlike regularities' (Little, 2004)—that transforms some subset of model variables (causes) into another subset (outcomes or effects)." (p. 2). Broad: "Any research that describes the internals of a model, including its activations or weights." (p. 1). Circuits as used in MI: "groups of components forming a sub-network that closely (or faithfully) replicate the full model's performance on a fine-grained task" (p. 4).
- **What counts as the thing:** a function from some model variables to others, found by causal intervention on intermediate representations.
- **What counts as the original already having it:** not addressed.
- **Kind:** informal definition (a taxonomy of uses of the word).
- **What it assumes:** that a causal model of the network exists at some level of abstraction (causal abstraction, p. 2).
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** Neither; it says nothing about two models. Not applicable. Unclear for our use because it gives no cross-model criterion.
- **Objection:** authors' own: the strictest version "excludes most early work in MI (§2.2), and has not yet been widely adopted." (p. 2). The paper has no limitations section. (reader's) The causal-mechanism sense is the one we would need, but it defines "mechanism" inside one model and offers no way to say two models share one.

### Lindsey et al. 2024, "Sparse Crosscoders for Cross-Layer Features and Model Diffing" (transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders). Status: partly read (the model-diffing section 4.2-4.3 and the abstract; web page, no page numbers)
- **Matches the list?** Yes. Shared and model-exclusive features are defined operationally by decoder norms, not by a formal definition.
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** "We wanted to test whether the crosscoder could decompose the models' activations into shared features and model-specific features. These model-specific features would indicate features learned, or forgotten, during finetuning." and "To test this, we looked at the relative norms of feature decoder weights in the two models. Remarkably, we found that features cluster into three obvious groups – base model-specific features, finetuned model-specific features, and shared features." (Sec. 4.3, "Model Diffing: Sonnet Finetuning"). On shared features: "For the shared features, we checked whether their decoder vectors are aligned in the two models. In almost all cases, they were highly aligned, suggesting that these features do in fact represent the same concept, and perform the same function, in the two models. However, we also found that for a few thousand features, the correlation was very low or even negative. We have not investigated this phenomenon in depth, but we suspect that these indicate cases where the finetuned model uses a concept that was present in the base model, but in a new way." (same section).
- **What counts as the thing:** a crosscoder feature (a direction pair, one decoder vector per model, one shared sparse code).
- **What counts as the original already having it:** the feature's decoder norm in the base model is non-negligible (relative norm near 0.5) and the decoder directions agree. Thresholds are not stated in the text I read (Minder et al. give 0.4-0.6 for shared).
- **Kind:** operational measure only.
- **What it assumes:** that one dictionary trained jointly on both models' activations at one layer represents both; linear features; a sparsity penalty that does not itself move decoder norms (this is what Minder et al. dispute).
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** About the fine-tune (activation space of two models over a data set), but the data set matters: features are found over the crosscoder's training tokens. Two fine-tunes giving the same behaviour could get different answers because the answer is a property of each model's activations, not of the behaviour. Yes.
- **Objection:** authors' own: "Unfortunately, we've also found that the majority of the model-exclusive features are not immediately interpretable." (Sec. 4.3). They also note the "few thousand" shared features with low or negative alignment as unexplained (quote above). (reader's) The text sets up reuse versus new as shared versus exclusive, but a "new use of an old concept" (their own suspicion) lands in "shared" by the norm test, so the norm test cannot see reuse in a new role.

### Minder et al. 2025, "Overcoming Sparsity Artifacts in Crosscoders to Interpret Chat-Tuning" (arXiv:2504.02922). Status: read (Sec. 1-3, 5 and appendix glossary grepped)
- **Matches the list?** Yes.
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** "Crosscoders can thus potentially identify which latents are novel to the fine-tuned model, which are novel to the base-model, and which are shared. We term these sets chat-only, base-only, and shared respectively. Lindsey et al. [2024] identify chat-only latents by looking at the norm of the latent directions – if the latent direction of the base model has zero norm, this indicates that the latent is chat-only." (p. 2). "As shown in Figure 1, we classify latents as base-only (0–0.1), chat-only (0.9-1.0), or shared (0.4-0.6)." (p. 3, on the relative norm difference Δnorm). Chat-specific: "most L1 crosscoder chat-only latents are not truly chat-specific (defined as ν^r < 0.5 and ν^ε < 0.2)" (p. 6), where Latent Scaling regresses a latent onto the base model's activations/error (Sec. 2.3).
- **What counts as the thing:** a crosscoder latent.
- **What counts as the original already having it:** the latent has a non-zero decoder in the base model, and passes Latent Scaling (its direction does not explain the base model's activations or reconstruction error, thresholds above, for it to count as truly "chat-specific").
- **Kind:** operational measure only (a corrected one).
- **What it assumes:** BatchTopK crosscoder training is free of the shrinkage artifact; linear latents at one layer.
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** About the fine-tune over a token distribution. Two fine-tunes with the same behaviour could get different answers. Yes.
- **Objection:** authors' own: "a significant limitation is our inability to distinguish between truly novel latents learned during chat-tuning and existing latents that have merely shifted their activation patterns, as the crosscoder architecture does not provide a mechanism to make this distinction. This remains an open challenge for future work." (p. 10). Also "their error terms still contain a lot of information about the chat model behavior" (p. 10). The artifact they find: "Complete Shrinkage: The sparsity loss can force base latent directions to zero norm, even when they contribute to base model reconstruction." (p. 2). (reader's) Their own stated limit is exactly our question (new versus shifted existing), so this literature does not answer it.

### Olah, Cammarata, Schubert, Goh, Petrov, Carter 2020, "Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits" (distill.pub/2020/circuits/zoom-in). Status: read (the three claims and the intro to Claim 1; the glossary definitions were not read)
- **Matches the list?** Yes. It gives informal definitions of feature and circuit; the "detailed definition" is in a glossary I did not extract.
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** "Features are the fundamental unit of neural networks. They correspond to directions. By 'direction' we mean a linear combination of neurons in a layer." and "A 'circuit' is a computational subgraph of a neural network. It consists of a set of features, and the weighted edges that go between them in the original network." and "Claim 3: Universality. Analogous features and circuits form across models and tasks." (Claims 1-3, page text; no page numbers on the web page).
- **What counts as the thing:** a feature (direction in a layer) and a circuit (features plus the weights between them).
- **What counts as the original already having it:** not addressed for fine-tuning. Claim 3 speaks of "analogous" features across separately trained models, and notes "To further establish that analogous circuits form, one would want to find analogous features over several layers of multiple models and show that the same weight structure forms between them in each model." (Universality section). The analogy test for features across models is not specified.
- **Kind:** informal definition (stated as claims, not as measures).
- **What it assumes:** features are directions that can be understood individually; the weights connecting understood features can be read as an algorithm.
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** About the model (weights). Across two fine-tunes the answer depends on whether the weight structure is the same, so it could differ. Unclear in the sense that no criterion is given.
- **Objection:** authors' own: "The community is divided on whether this is true." (on meaningful features, Claim 1). (reader's) "Analogous" is not defined, so "same circuit" cannot be decided from this text.

### Machamer, Darden, Craver 2000, "Thinking about Mechanisms", Philosophy of Science 67(1). Status: read (Secs. 1-2 and 5.3; copy at https://mechanism.ucsd.edu/bill/teaching/w10/machamer.darden.craver.pdf)
- **Matches the list?** Yes. Quote is on journal p. 3 (PDF page 4).
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** "Mechanisms are entities and activities organized such that they are productive of regular changes from start or set-up to finish or termination conditions." (p. 3). "We introduce the term 'mechanism schema' for an abstract description of a type of mechanism. A mechanism schema is a truncated abstract description of a mechanism that can be filled with descriptions of known component parts and activities." (Sec. 5.3, journal p. 15-16).
- **What counts as the thing:** organized entities and activities that produce a regular change from set-up to termination; a schema is the type, an instance fills it in.
- **What counts as the original already having it:** not addressed. The schema/instance split is the nearest thing: same schema, different instance.
- **Kind:** informal definition.
- **What it assumes:** that the phenomenon decomposes into entities and activities with a start and an end; applies to molecular biology and neurobiology.
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** About the phenomenon (the task), with the entities left to be found. Unclear: it gives no rule for comparing two organisms or models.
- **Objection:** authors' own: "We do not claim that all scientists look for mechanisms or that all explanations are descriptions of mechanisms." and "many of our points are only provocatively and briefly stated." (Sec. 1, journal p. 2). (reader's) Defined from the phenomenon down; a fine-tune's gradient step does not come with a start and termination condition to match.

### Anderson 2010, "Neural reuse: A fundamental organizational principle of the brain", Behavioral and Brain Sciences 33(4), 245-313. Status: read (abstract, Secs. 1-2 start, 3.x, 4.6; a copy at https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/bitstreams/fba9e06b-73b7-4cd4-a10a-c57833b306b2/download; PDF page n = journal page 244+n)
- **Matches the list?** Yes. Reuse here is evolutionary and developmental in a brain, not fine-tuning.
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** "it is quite common for neural circuits established for one purpose to be exapted (exploited, recycled, redeployed) during evolution or normal development, and be put to different uses, often without losing their original functions." (Abstract, p. 245). "In general, these models make a strong distinction between a 'working' – whatever specific computational contribution local anatomical circuits make to overall function – and a 'use,' the cognitive purpose to which the working is put in any individual case. For neural reuse theories, anatomical sites have a fixed working, but many different uses." (PDF p. 8, journal p. 252). "One of the fundamental principles guiding reuse is the presence of a sufficient degree of functional relatedness between existing and newly developing purposes." (Sec. 4.6, journal p. 255).
- **What counts as the thing:** a circuit with a fixed "working", put to different "uses".
- **What counts as the original already having it:** the circuit was established for one purpose earlier and still has its original working; the new use "need not involve (much) local change to circuit structure (e.g., it might involve only the establishment of functional connections to new neural partners)" (Abstract).
- **Kind:** informal definition (a theory, with no test for "same working").
- **What it assumes:** that a circuit's working can be separated from its uses and stays fixed; "workings" are not defined operationally.
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** About the circuit (the part), with a use defined by the task. Two fine-tunes that gain the same behaviour could differ only if their circuits differ. Unclear.
- **Objection:** authors' own, on how loosely a working is pinned down: "a region could in fact perform more than one working and still have a cortical bias. However, the more flexible regions are, the less their individual biases will differ, and the harder it will be to explain the findings that recently evolved cognitive..." (PDF p. 18, journal p. 262; sentence continues on the next page). I did not read his open-problems section in full. (reader's) The split into working and use is the useful idea for us (the part keeps its working, the connections to it change), but it is not made measurable.

### Prakash et al. 2024, "Fine-Tuning Enhances Existing Mechanisms: A Case Study on Entity Tracking" (arXiv:2402.14811). Status: from project summary (arxiv_2402.14811v1.md) and paper read (Secs. 4.3, 5, 6.2, 7)
- **Matches the list?** Yes (added to Part B).
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** a criterion, not a definition. "Better performance could be attributed to a superior circuit in the fine-tuned models. Hence, in this subsection, we ask the question of whether the fine-tuned models use a different or the same circuit, i.e. with exactly the same group of heads, to perform the entity tracking task." (Sec. 4.3, p. 5). "We use the criterion of faithfulness, which is defined as the percentage of model performance that can be recovered with the circuit, i.e. F(Cir)/F(M)." (p. 5). "Vicuna-7B has almost a perfect faithfulness score of 0.97, while Goat-7B and FLoat-7B exhibit slightly lower scores of 0.89 and 0.88... random circuits have virtually zero accuracy. This suggests that Vicuna-7B utilizes roughly the same circuit as that of Llama-7B... Whereas, in Goat-7B and FLoat-7B the same circuit is present, but achieving the complete performance of the fine-tuned models requires the incorporation of additional components." (pp. 5-6). Same functionality: "(ii) The circuits of all models implement the same mechanism, but with an enhanced functionality in fine-tuned models." (Sec. 5, p. 6).
- **What counts as the thing:** a set of attention heads found by path patching on the base model, with roles from desiderata-based component masking.
- **What counts as the original already having it:** the base model's circuit, run in the fine-tuned model with no change, recovers most of the fine-tuned model's accuracy (faithfulness), and the head roles match.
- **Kind:** operational measure only.
- **What it assumes:** the base circuit is complete enough; the fine-tuned behaviour is the same task on the same inputs.
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** Both: the circuit is found per task, then tested in each fine-tune. Two fine-tunes (Vicuna 0.97, Goat 0.89) got different faithfulness. Yes.
- **Objection:** authors' own: "Understanding whether such mechanism invariance is typical will require experience with further tasks on more models." (Sec. 7, p. 9). And on additions: "fine-tuning is inserting additional components to the circuitry" (p. 6, the Goat and FLoat circuits are 175 heads, "approximately forming a superset"). (reader's) No threshold on faithfulness is given for "same", and the additions find the same fine-tune is both "same circuit" and "bigger circuit".

### Jain et al. 2023, "Mechanistically analyzing the effects of fine-tuning on procedurally defined tasks" (arXiv:2311.12786). Status: from project summary (arxiv_2311.12786v2.md) and paper read (Sec. 3, Sec. 5.2/6)
- **Matches the list?** Added (the project's "Jain Def 1/2").
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** "Definition 1. (Capability.) Define a surjective map f_C: X_D → Y_C... Then, we say the model M(.) 'possesses a capability C' if for all x ∈ X_C, ∃ l ≤ L s.t. Read_l(M(x)) = f_C(x_d)." (Sec. 3, p. 3). "Definition 2. (Relevance of a Capability.) Assume the capability C in a pretrained model can be transformed to a map g ∘ f_C via fine-tuning on D_FT, where |D_FT| ≪ |D_PT|, such that for all x ∼ P_X^{FT,E}, the correct output is produced." (p. 4). "In such cases, we call g a 'wrapper' and g ∘ C a 'wrapped capability'. If we intervene on the model by either removing the wrapper or training it to forget the wrapper, we find the model starts to perform well on the pretraining task again. In such cases, we say the pretraining capability has been 'revived'." (p. 4).
- **What counts as the thing:** a capability C (a map readable by a linear layer at some layer l) and a wrapper g on top of it.
- **What counts as the original already having it:** a linear readout at an intermediate layer of the pretrained model equals f_C on the capability's sub-domain.
- **Kind:** formal definition (idealized: "We use an idealized definition... we do not expect all capabilities in a pretrained model will act as perfectly as the definition necessitates", p. 3), plus an operational test (revival).
- **What it assumes:** the capability has a known f_C and an identifier x_i; procedurally defined tasks.
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** About the pretrained model and the task: Def. 1 is a property of the base model alone. A reuse claim is then "the fine-tuned map is g ∘ f_C". Two fine-tunes could get different g, not different C. Unclear (mixed).
- **Objection:** authors' own: "We use an idealized definition to communicate our primary intuition and emphasize that we do not expect all capabilities in a pretrained model will act as perfectly as the definition necessitates." (p. 3); the conclusion asks for "future work both understanding how fine-tuning works in more realistic settings with larger models" (Sec. 6, p. 9). (reader's) f_C must be known in advance, so a capability the experimenter did not write down cannot be found.

### Ward, Lin, Venhoff, Nanda 2025, "Reasoning-Finetuning Repurposes Latent Representations in Base Models" (arXiv:2507.12638; project file lw_J9BiKfJ4YvNd2Lwbh.md, the LessWrong linkpost). Status: from project summary and paper read (arXiv PDF, 8 pages)
- **Matches the list?** Yes. The post is a linkpost to the arXiv paper.
- **Definition, in the authors' words:** no definition; a criterion by steering. "we identify a direction in base Llama-3.1-8B's residual stream which systematically induces backtracking when used to steer the distilled reasoning model... We further find that this direction does not induce backtracking in the base model, suggesting that the reasoning finetuning process repurposes pre-existing representations to form new behavioral circuits." (Abstract, p. 1). "Crucially, while this representation is shared by both base and reasoning models, the representation only induces backtracking in the reasoning model, suggesting that it has been repurposed as an input to the backtracking mechanism during reasoning finetuning." (Sec. 1, p. 1).
- **What counts as the thing:** a direction in residual-stream activations of the base model (difference of means).
- **What counts as the original already having it:** the direction can be computed from the base model alone and steers the fine-tuned model to the behaviour, while it does not induce the behaviour in the base model.
- **Kind:** experimental finding only (an existence argument).
- **What it assumes:** the direction is one of several; the base model "contains the representation" iff the vector computed from its activations works in the fine-tuned model.
- **About the fine-tune or the task?** About the fine-tune: it is the fine-tuned model's response that confirms it. Another fine-tune giving the same behaviour could respond to the same vector or not. Yes.
- **Objection:** authors' own: "Our results should be interpreted primarily as an existence proof for latent reasoning-related representations in base models, rather than a comprehensive explanation of reasoning behavior." and "we have only identified one component of the backtracking mechanism, and the remainder of the mechanism remains unknown." (Sec. 5, p. 5). (reader's) The text says both "repurposes pre-existing representations to form new behavioral circuits" (Abstract) and "rather than learn new capabilities from scratch": reuse of a part is claimed alongside a new circuit, so the paper itself has no single yes/no.
