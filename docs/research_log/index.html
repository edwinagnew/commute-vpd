      1Overview

      Does post-training create new mechanisms, or draw from existing ones? A common assumption is that pretraining builds the mechanisms and post-training only re-weights them. The evidence for that is behavioural or activation-based. This project looks where post-training actually acts: in the weights.

      A parameter decomposition (VPD) writes each weight matrix as a sum of rank-one subcomponents, each meant to be one piece of mechanism, plus a residual Δ, with an importance function Γ saying which pieces matter on each input. A post-training update is nothing but a change to the weights. So: does the update land on the existing subcomponents, or outside them?

      Work in progress: what "re-weighting" means

      The hypothesis is usually stated informally: post-training re-weights mechanisms pretraining built, rather than creating its own. A precise version is not written down yet. What counts as a mechanism, and what counts as re-weighting one in VPD terms (a strengthened piece, a re-switched one, a new one) is open. The grid in section 4 is one operationalisation, adopted for now: re-weighting as the reading where the update leaves the subcomponents as they were and Γ switches them on differently, measured by the re-switching share. Until this is fixed, "reveal" and "create" below are readings of the data, not verdicts on the hypothesis.

      How it is tested

      Take a released decomposition, run a small fine-tune, and try simple rules for carrying the update onto the pieces with Γ and Δ frozen. A rule passes if its output is still a faithful decomposition of the updated model by VPD's own checks, with Δ not swallowing the update.

      2The question and its sub-questions

      In the user's words (2026-10-14): given a VPD parameter decomposition, can one commute small gradient updates from post-training past the parameter components? If so, this gives continuous interpretability within some limit. If not, is it because post-training teaches new capabilities rather than strengthening existing ones, or because parameter decompositions are too brittle? Precisely: is there a simple operation that maps a post-training update, say a LoRA, to an update on the components, such that the result is a faithful decomposition of the new model?

      3The fixed apparatus

      Model and decomposition

      Model
      Goodfire's released 4-layer Pile transformer (67M parameters) and their released VPD decomposition, via the pinned param-decomp package at commit 74146b5. A testbed; see Q9.
      Decomposed
      24 weight matrices: query, key, value and output projections and the two MLP matrices in each of four blocks. Embeddings, unembedding and normalisation are frozen and not decomposed.
      Pieces
      About 38,900 rank-one subcomponents, about 9,900 of them alive (ever important). Four matrices (the MLP matrices of layers 0 and 3) are saturated: their alive pieces span every direction on one side (the read side or the write side), so any update to them is expressible by construction.
      Γ
      One network shared across all 24 matrices. It reads every matrix's input at every position, runs a small transformer over the window, and outputs an importance value in [0, 1] per piece per position. An edit in layer 2 therefore moves importance values in layer 0. Frozen in every map so far.

      Updates

      Fine-tune
      Adam, learning rate 3e-5, batch 32, on the 24 decomposed matrices, with a KL penalty to the original model at the non-trigger positions. Usually scored at step 10. We run these ourselves; the released model has no post-trained sibling.
      LoRA
      Learning rate 1e-3. Rank 8 on all 24 matrices (E8, E9; E9's LoRA reused in E14 and E16), or ranks 1 to 32 on the paper's one matrix (E7).
      Component training
      The alive pieces' vectors trained with the run's optimiser, either SGD or Adam, Γ and Δ frozen. Adam in every scored run; plain SGD only in E4 and its follow-up (Q11). Learning rate 1e-5 whenever all alive pieces train (E2's selection on emoticon, kept without re-tuning in E4, E5, E7's all-matrix arm, E10, E11 and E16). The restricted variants picked theirs by E2's rule, learn by step 30 within an off-target limit: 1e-4 to 1e-3 for the sparse arms (E4), 3e-5 to 1e-3 for the one-matrix arms (E7; 3e-4 at rank 8), 1e-4 for rank 8 on all 24 matrices (E13, E16).
      Update size
      Reported as the largest per-matrix ratio of the update's norm to the weight's norm; about 0.01 at the scored step.

      Checks

      Pile checks (VPD's own)
      • Stochastic-mask KL: random masks between each piece's importance value and 1.
      • Adversarial-mask KL (PGD): 20 sign-gradient steps of a mask shared across a 128-row block. The check that fails where others do not.
      • CI-masked KL: every piece at its importance value, Δ out.
      • Unmasked KL: every piece fully on, Δ out.
      • Sparsity loss: VPD's importance-minimality loss.

      Pile validation rows 0 to 255, each number a ratio to the unchanged model in the same job. The verdict on the adversarial check is decided by one line, inherited from E2: a ratio under 1.5 passes. The floor qualifies it: "within the floor" means at most 1 plus three times the adversary's seed spread at the unchanged model, and a number between that band and 1.5 is read as "at the line". On an L40 the band can reach 1.63, above the line, which is why the line is called unanchored (section 9). Since E7 every job measures its own three-seed floor, except E10, which ran one adversary seed and used E7's floor from another card.

      Task-local checks
      • The target's log-odds gain over the original model at the trigger, on held-out windows.
      • The route split: which of five routes carries that gain (section 4).
      • The off-target KL: KL to the original model at the other 40 positions of the window.

      Pile rows hold almost no trigger positions, so the Pile checks and the task-local checks see different things.

      Predictions
      Filed before any GPU run, with rule and threshold, never edited afterwards; corrections are dated reports. Verdicts: pass, fail, null, neither. A reversal is announced explicitly. Every analysis ends with a "Not run" list.

      4Techniques

      The decomposition and the masked model

      W = Σc uc vcT + Δ, W(m) = Σc mc uc vcT + mΔ Δ

      vc is a piece's read vector (the input direction it responds to), uc its write vector (what it adds to the output). A masked model runs the forward pass with W(m) in place of W: one mask mc ∈ [0, 1] per piece and one mΔ ∈ [0, 1] for the residual, chosen afresh at every token position. Each check is a choice of m. The CI-masked model sets mc = gc(x), the importance value Γ gives piece c on input x, and mΔ = 0. The unmasked model sets every mc = 1 and mΔ = 0. The stochastic check draws each mc uniformly from [gc(x), 1], with Δ under its own random mask. The adversarial check lets gradient ascent choose m to do the most damage. Faithful means the masked model reproduces the full model's output distribution: its KL to the full model is within the floor measured at the unchanged model.

      The operations, with their equations

      Drawn in the Overview. W = U Vᵀ + Δ, with U and V holding the alive pieces' write and read vectors as columns; the pieces are frozen in shape, only their vectors may move.

      Do-nothing

      W′ = U Vᵀ + (Δ + δW)

      What it does. The whole update goes into the residual; pieces and Γ are untouched.

      Status. The reference map. It passes any check that recomputes Δ from the weights, which is why the weak version requires Δ not to absorb δW. Under the Pile checks it fails instead, for the same reason: the CI-masked and unmasked models leave Δ out, so under do-nothing they leave the whole update out and are scored against a model they no longer contain (unmasked ratio 4 to 18 on the ladder tasks, E11), and the adversary can mask Δ down (2.18 on emoticon, E2, first figure).

      Least-squares split

      min ‖dU‖² + ‖dV‖² s.t. dU Vᵀ + U dVᵀ ≈ δW

      What it does. Splits a finished update over the pieces as the least-norm first-order change. The obstruction (I − PU) δW (I − PV) cannot be expressed and goes to Δ, with a small second-order term, so W′ equals the fine-tuned weights exactly.

      Status. Applied one-shot to finished updates, at steps up to 1000 (E9, E16; Q8).

      Adjoint split

      dU = δW V, dV = δWᵀ U

      What it does. Splits a finished update over the pieces without a solve: the weight change is UUᵀδW + δWVVᵀ, Δ stays fixed, so W′ is not the fine-tuned weights. The least-squares split is this map after preconditioning by K⁺.

      Status. Run as a scaled state (first in E14, repeated in E16): each matrix's move is multiplied by the scalar c that best matches δW on the real inputs, so the state reproduces a share R of the update's input-weighted energy (0.58 for the 10-step fine-tune, 0.23 for the LoRA). It stays at the adversarial floor, as expected since the pieces move only along what they already read and write, and its behaviour falls with R as training goes on (E16, first figure, panels a and b).

      Component training

      δWstep = −η K(G), K(G) = U Uᵀ G + G V Vᵀ

      What it does. One gradient step on the pieces' vectors moves the weights by the part of the gradient G the pieces can express. Repeated every step; Γ and Δ frozen. The optimiser is the run's, either SGD or Adam, and the equation is the SGD step. Every scored run used Adam at one rate, as chosen in E2; plain SGD ran only in E4 and its follow-up (section 3, Q11).

      Status. The live candidate (with Adam), and the only operation that kept both the adversarial floor (0.97 to 1.12) and the behaviour (held-out target probability 0.994 or more from step 100) to step 1000 on emoticon, free and rank-8, with Δ untouched (E16, first figure; readings, since the run's reproduction gate failed). Its change lies inside the pieces, except rank-8 at step 1000, where a small part falls outside, just over the filed limit. Variants: all alive pieces; one matrix; rank-r offsets (E7, E13); top 1, 10 or 100 pieces; the paper's six; only pieces Γ keeps off (the gated-off control); plain SGD on all alive pieces (emoticon, 30 steps only).

      Rescaling split

      δW ≈ Σc αc uc vcᵀ

      What it does. Rescales the existing pieces by a least-squares fit; the pieces' directions never move.

      Status. Dead after E1: by the plain (Frobenius) measure, 0.06% of a real update lies in that span, against 0.035% for a random direction; weighted by real inputs the share is larger but still small (E14, fourth Found point). First run as a state in E16 (first figure): as faithful as the original model (adversarial ratio 0.92 to 1.00) and keeps none of the behaviour (held-out target probability 0.012 to 0.016, against 0.012 before the update), reproducing at most 7% of the update's input-weighted energy. E1's verdict, confirmed on the model's behaviour.

      Where an update can land: four readings

      The Overview's three arrows make two axes. Rows: do the moved subcomponents carry the gain where Γ uses them, or are the subcomponents Γ uses as they were, so that the gain sits elsewhere? Columns: do the masks Γ gives the subcomponents stay as before, or change? Γ is frozen, but it reads the updated model's activations, so its masks can change whichever row the gain took. The four cells are four readings of what post-training does to a mechanism, and each is measured by the route-split share named in it (two shares for create). The rows are not where an operation puts the update: do-nothing leaves every vector alone and can only land in the lower row, while the splits and component training move the subcomponents, and the route split then reports which cells carry the gain.

      This grid is one way of operationalising the re-weighting hypothesis, fixed on 2026-10-20 so that the readings have names; it may be adjusted as the definition firms up (the work-in-progress box in section 1).

      Where the data have landed so far: re-weight for behaviours the model already has, common next words and copying (E5, E12); create for the random-mapping cipher (E10, E12) and in part for the rarest next words (E5's figure); strengthen about a tenth or less under every operation route-split so far (E16, second figure; E7 the highest); re-wire near zero everywhere (too thin to see in E5's figure; reports/2026-10-20_analysis_new-vs-existing-signatures.md). The Overview's E12 figure shows re-weight and create side by side.

      The route split

      After an update, the model puts more log-odds on the target token at the trigger. The route split asks which part of the decomposition that gain runs through. Two things can change under a map. The pieces' vectors move (the mapped pieces C1 against the original C0). And the masks change, because Γ, frozen as it is, reads the updated model's activations and so gives different importance values (g1 against g0). Two more places sit outside the CI-masked model altogether: the pieces Γ keeps switched off, and Δ.

      So the target's log-odds is measured on the same held-out windows under six models: original pieces with the old masks, moved pieces with the old masks, original pieces with the new masks, moved pieces with the new masks, every piece switched fully on, and the full model (the last two before and after the update). Write L(C, g) for the CI-masked model built from pieces C with masks g. The differences between neighbours in that list, divided by the total gain, are five shares that sum exactly to 1. A large direct share means the moved pieces themselves carry the behaviour. A large re-switching share means the behaviour was already in the pieces and Γ now switches it on. The last two shares mean it lives outside what the CI-masked model keeps. Under the current criterion the split describes mechanism and does not decide a pass.

      Outside share

      How much of an update the pieces cannot take: the part of δW that reads outside the pieces' read span and writes outside their write span, (I − PU) δW (I − PV), which has to go to Δ. Measured by its effect δW x on real inputs, not by raw entries.

      Reported as share ÷ isotropic null (the same share for a random update of the same size): 1 is no better aligned than noise, below 1 is aligned. From E14 it is split into four blocks (inside both, write side only, read side only, outside both) that sum to one (E14's figure shows only the read and write sides; the four blocks are in reports/2026-10-20_analysis_lora-split.md).

      Switched-on set

      Alive pieces whose mean importance at the trigger rises by more than 0.3 when Γ reads the updated activations. Compared by Jaccard overlap against a seed floor; tested by recovery, the share of the re-switching effect obtained when only that set takes the new masks.

      Floors and controls

      Every ratio has a floor measured at the unchanged model in the same job. Every plan names what each metric is blind to and a control that must fail: gated-off component training for the route split (must read d = 0), the least-squares split for the Pile adversary (must fail for a fine-tune), do-nothing under the original masks for trigger-local faithfulness.

On the L40 the unchanged model's own floor reached 21%, so the line sits inside the noise band and the two stated rules disagree there (1.5 against 1.63). Two of seven ladder verdicts sit on the rules (E11): " we" then " just" straddles the line across seeds, and " will" then " have" passes the line but sits just over its card's floor.

      The reference fails the pass check

      Do-nothing changes nothing in the decomposition and still fails the adversarial check for full fine-tunes (2.18 on emoticon, 2.29 on E14's card, three of five ladder tasks), because the adversary can mask Δ and Δ now holds the update. So the 1.5 line mixes two things: how much of the update's effect on Pile sits outside the pieces, and whether the pieces are fragile. The split's 5.4 against do-nothing's 2.18 is the fragility finding; the line is not. Every adversarial verdict should be read against the same update's do-nothing on the same card until the criterion has an anchor; changing the criterion is the user's decision.

      "Δ not absorbing δW" has no threshold

      The split LoRA's residual share (0.22 to 0.31 of the behaviour, half the energy) is judged by feel. The split fine-tune's 0.09 was read as acceptable, 0.22 as "the open reading". GOALS fixes no number.

      LoRA vs fine-tune: like for like since E14

      E8 scored the LoRA at step 2 and the fine-tune at step 10, and E9 compared cards. E14 puts both on one card at steps 10, 100 and 1000: the split LoRA stays at the floor where the split fine-tune reads 5.2, 1.5 and 2.0. Closed; the earlier reports keep their wording.

      Component training is not "the same fine-tune"

      Adam on the pieces at another learning rate, half the weight movement, and on the ladder tasks about twice the off-target damage at the same step, which the adversarial excess orders with. On emoticon the sign flips (a third of the damage). Unexplained.

      One optimiser behind every result

      After E4 every component-training run used Adam at one learning rate, tuned on emoticon, because E4's grid seemed to rule plain SGD out (section 11). So the failures on the ladder tasks and the 32-pair cipher are Adam's results, not yet the operation's, until SGD is tried on them. The same holds for the splits: every one, E19 included, was of an Adam fine-tune or an Adam-trained LoRA. Comparing the two is a priority subgoal since 2026-10-21 (Q11); the test is in section 6.

      Step or run

      The least-squares split is applied one-shot to a 10-step update, a first-order map on a non-small change; only component training matches the "continuous" framing. The per-step split, E19, matches it too and does worse than the one-shot least-squares split, so the step-or-run question is answered for the adjoint form only.

      Nothing measures faithfulness at the trigger with power

      Terms, in the sense this project uses them

      12Glossary

      Adversarial check (PGD ratio)

      VPD's adversarial-mask reconstruction KL: 20 sign-gradient steps find the mask, shared across a 128-row block, that does the most damage. As a ratio to the unchanged model. The check that fails where the others do not.

      Adjoint split

      A finished update split over the pieces as dU = δW V, dV = δWᵀ U, with no solve; weight change UUᵀδW + δWVVᵀ, Δ fixed. Run in E14 as a scaled state, each matrix's move multiplied by the scalar that best matches δW on real inputs; it describes a different model.

      Alive piece

      A subcomponent whose importance ever exceeds a small threshold; about 9,900 of 38,900. Only alive pieces are trained or split onto.

      Causal importance (CI)

      Γ's output for one piece at one position, in [0, 1].

      CI-masked model

      Every piece multiplied by its importance value, Δ out.

      Commute

      Carry a weight update over to the decomposition by a simple operation, so that the mapped decomposition is a faithful decomposition of the updated model with Δ not absorbing the update (the weak version, the criterion in use). The strong version asks that it also agree with re-decomposing the updated model.

      Component training

      Train the alive pieces' vectors on the fine-tune loss with Γ and Δ frozen, for many steps, with the run's optimiser, either SGD or Adam; Adam in every scored run so far, plain SGD only in E4 and its follow-up. One SGD step moves the weights by UUᵀG + GVVᵀ. Rank-r component training limits the offsets to a LoRA's rank (E7, E13).

      Coverage

      Share of the first component-training step's squared update on high-importance pieces at the trigger. E5's predictor; dropped.

      Create, reveal, strengthen, brittle

      GOALS's mechanism hypotheses, in the route split's shares. Strengthen: the changed pieces carry it (direct share d; the grid's strengthen cell). Reveal: Γ switches existing pieces on (re-switching share r plus the interaction share i; the grid's re-weight and re-wire cells, and since i is within 0.04 of zero everywhere, reveal reads as r). Create: the behaviour lives in Δ or in pieces Γ keeps off (eΔ and egate; the grid's create cell). Brittle: the split is unstable across seeds or small changes to the map.

      Δ, the residual

      The part of each matrix not covered by the pieces. Frozen in every map; the do-nothing placement puts the whole update here.

      Direct share, d

      Gain carried by the changed pieces at the original importance values. At most 0.11 in every run route-split so far (E7's one-matrix arms); 0.08 or less elsewhere. No SGD state has been route-split.

      Do-nothing placement

      The update in Δ, pieces and Γ untouched. The reference operation.

      Emoticon task

      The VPD paper's hand-editing example: after the first character of an emoticon, predict o. The paper's piece is subcomponent 2359 of layer 2's MLP down-projection; 365 training and 73 held-out windows. The reference task.

      Expressible, obstruction, outside share

      An update is expressible to the extent a first-order change of the alive pieces can produce it. The obstruction is the part outside both the write span and the read span. The outside share weights it by real inputs; its isotropic null is the same number for a random update. From E14 reported as four blocks that sum to one: inside both, write side only, read side only, outside both, with the read side split by the part of the input predictable from what the pieces read rather than by the projection onto their span.

      Floor

      Seed-to-seed or block-to-block spread of a ratio at the unchanged model, same job. "Within the floor" = at most 1 plus three floors. The 1.5 line decides a verdict; the floor says whether a number near it is noise.

      Γ (gamma)

      The importance function: one transformer over all 24 matrices' inputs at all positions, one importance value per piece per position. Frozen in every experiment except E15, which retrains it.

      Gated-off component training

      Only pieces Γ keeps off at the trigger may move. The control for the route split: must read d = 0.

      Least-squares split

      A finished weight change written as the least-norm first-order change to the alive pieces, obstruction left in Δ, so the weights equal the fine-tuned ones.

      LoRA split

      Train a rank-r LoRA to completion, split its BA over the subcomponents (least-squares and adjoint), save the per-subcomponent updates, report the outside share in four blocks. Run as E14 on 2026-10-20 from the saved LoRAs of E7 and E9.

      LoRA

      A low-rank update, read factor A (random start) times write factor B (zero start). The user's canonical post-training update.

      Masked-off share, egate

      What the unmasked model gains beyond the CI-masked one: behaviour in pieces Γ keeps off.

      Off-target KL

      KL from a state to the original model at the 40 non-trigger positions of the held-out windows. The measure of collateral change; kl_other in the code.

      Pile checks

      The four reconstruction ratios and the sparsity loss on Pile validation rows. Blind to the trained behaviour.

      Rescaling split

      A finished update written as a rescaling of the fixed pieces, δW ≈ Σ αc uc vcᵀ. Dead after E1 as the whole story; E14 reads 2% of a fine-tune's energy rising to 7% by step 1000, and none of a LoRA's above random. Applied as a state it keeps none of the behaviour (E16).

      Reading A, reading B

      A: the weak version as written; the criterion. B: the changed pieces carry the behaviour at the old masks; a mechanism reading no map has passed.

      Recovery

      Share of the re-switching effect obtained when only the switched-on set takes the new masks.

      Residual share, eΔ

      What the full model gains beyond the unmasked one: Δ alone. 1 for do-nothing by construction.

      Re-switching share, r

      Gain carried by the original pieces under the importance values Γ computes from the updated activations. The grid's re-weight cell. Dominates every CI-masked number for behaviours the model has.

      Re-weighting hypothesis

      The informal claim that post-training re-weights mechanisms pretraining built rather than creating new ones. The VPD-level reading adopted for now (section 4's grid): the update leaves the subcomponents as they were and Γ switches them on differently, measured by the re-switching share. One operationalisation; still work in progress (section 1).

      Retained share

      d + r + i: what the CI-masked model keeps of the gain.

      Route split

      The exact five-way split of the trigger gain into direct, re-switching, interaction, masked-off and residual shares. Mechanism only. Not to be confused with the split of an update over the pieces.

      Split

      Writing a finished update as per-subcomponent updates (dUc, dVc) plus a remainder outside the pieces. Least-squares, adjoint and rescaling are the three ways to choose it. Every split in E1 to E16 was applied once, to the finished update; with the pieces held fixed that equals splitting step by step.

      Step split

      At every step of a run, the scaled adjoint split of that step's weight change against the current pieces; the remainder goes to Δ and the pieces carry forward, so the weights equal the fine-tune's at every step. With the pieces held fixed and one scale per matrix it equals the one-shot adjoint split plus its remainder. As run, the scale is refit every step: in E19 that, not the pieces' rotation, is what made it differ (user, 2026-10-21; E19).

      Rung

      Decade of a next-word task's baseline probability: 0 is 0.1 to 1, 1 is 0.01 to 0.1, 2 is 0.001 to 0.01. Rungs 3 and 4 not run.

      Saturated matrix

      One whose alive pieces span every direction on one side, so every update to it is expressible. Four of 24.

      Scored step

      The checkpoint a filed prediction is read at: usually 10, or the first at which held-out P(target) crosses a filed line.

      Sparsity loss

      VPD's importance-minimality loss at the released run's end setting. Blind to the operation while Γ is frozen.

      Stochastic check

      VPD's stochastic-mask reconstruction KL: random masks between importance and 1 on a random subset of matrices per position. Flat in every run.

      Subcomponent, piece

      A rank-one matrix u vT in one weight matrix. The unit of the decomposition.

      Switched-on set

      Alive pieces whose mean importance at the trigger rises by more than 0.3 under the updated activations. About 150 to 200 per arm on emoticon.

      Trigger, target

      The position where the task applies and the token taught there.

      Unmasked model

      Every piece fully on, Δ out. Says whether the pieces alone reproduce the model.

      VPD

      Goodfire's parameter decomposition method (Bushnaq, Braun, Clive-Griffin et al., 2026), used unchanged.

      W0, W + δW, D, D′

      Original weights, fine-tuned weights, released decomposition, mapped decomposition.

      Weak and strong versions

      Weak: D′ is faithful to the updated model with Δ not absorbing the update. Strong: D′ matches a fresh re-decomposition. Only the weak version is in play.

      Windows

      41-token Pile contexts holding one trigger, split into training and held-out sets.
