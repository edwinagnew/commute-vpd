    Update Decomposition

    Can a post-training update be carried past a parameter decomposition? What the project asks, what has been run, what it found, and where the gaps are.

    Contents
      Start here

      1Overview

      Does post-training create new mechanisms, or draw from existing ones? A common assumption is that pretraining builds the mechanisms and post-training only re-weights them. The evidence for that is behavioural or activation-based. This project looks where post-training actually acts: in the weights.

      A parameter decomposition (VPD) writes each weight matrix as a sum of rank-one subcomponents, each meant to be one piece of mechanism, plus a residual Δ, with an importance function Γ saying which pieces matter on each input. A post-training update is nothing but a change to the weights. So: does the update land on the existing subcomponents, or outside them?

      Work in progress: what "re-weighting" means

      The hypothesis is usually stated informally: post-training re-weights mechanisms pretraining built, rather than creating its own. A precise version is not written down yet. What counts as a mechanism, and what counts as re-weighting one in VPD terms (a strengthened piece, a re-switched one, a new one) is open. The route split in section 4 is one candidate vocabulary. Until this is fixed, "reveal" and "create" below are readings of the data, not verdicts on the hypothesis.

      How it is tested

      Take a released decomposition, run a small fine-tune, and try simple rules for carrying the update onto the pieces with Γ and Δ frozen. A rule passes if its output is still a faithful decomposition of the updated model by VPD's own checks, with Δ not swallowing the update.

      Where we are

      Result 1

      Component training works, about half the time

      Component training learns every task tried, including a cipher the model has never seen. But its decomposition survives VPD's adversarial check on only about half the tasks, and the failures do not sort by how new the behaviour is.

      Result 2

      A split fine-tune is fragile; a split LoRA stays faithful, mostly from Δ

      The least-squares split of a fine-tune fails the adversarial check fivefold. The split LoRA passes at every step, but its read side never leaves its random start, so half its energy and a third of the behaviour end up in Δ. The LoRA split the user asked for, with the per-subcomponent updates saved and the outside share in four blocks, has not been run (section 8).

      Result 3

      The moved pieces never carry the behaviour

      Under every operation they account for at most 8% of the gain. For behaviours the model has, Γ switches existing pieces on. For the cipher, the gain sits in pieces Γ keeps off. Existing behaviour is re-routed; a new association is not.

      What we are asking

      2The question and its sub-questions

      In the user's words (2026-10-14): given a VPD parameter decomposition, can one commute small gradient updates from post-training past the parameter components? If so, this gives continuous interpretability within some limit. If not, is it because post-training teaches new capabilities rather than strengthening existing ones, or because parameter decompositions are too brittle? Precisely: is there a simple operation that maps a post-training update, say a LoRA, to an update on the components, such that the result is a faithful decomposition of the new model?

      Held fixed across experiments

      3The fixed apparatus

      Model and decomposition

      Model
      Goodfire's released 4-layer Pile transformer (67M parameters) and their released VPD decomposition, via the pinned param-decomp package at commit 74146b5. A testbed; see Q9.
      Decomposed
      24 weight matrices: query, key, value and output projections and the two MLP matrices in each of four blocks. Embeddings, unembedding and normalisation are frozen and not decomposed.
      Pieces
      About 38,900 rank-one subcomponents, about 9,900 of them alive (ever important). Four matrices (the MLP matrices of layers 0 and 3) are saturated: their alive pieces span every direction, so any update to them is expressible by construction.
      Γ
      One network shared across all 24 matrices. It reads every matrix's input at every position, runs a small transformer over the window, and outputs an importance value in [0, 1] per piece per position. An edit in layer 2 therefore moves importance values in layer 0. Frozen in every map so far.

      Updates

      Fine-tune
      Adam, learning rate 3e-5, batch 32, on the 24 decomposed matrices, with a KL penalty to the original model at the non-trigger positions. Usually scored at step 10. We run these ourselves; the released model has no post-trained sibling.
      LoRA
      Rank 8, learning rate 1e-3, on all 24 matrices (E8, E9) or on the paper's one matrix (E7).
      Component training
      Adam at 1e-5 on the alive pieces' vectors, Γ and Δ frozen; rank-8 offsets in E7 and E13.
      Update size
      Reported as the largest per-matrix ratio of the update's norm to the weight's norm; about 0.01 at the scored step.

      Tasks

      FamilyWhat is learnedBaseline P(target)Used in
      EmoticonThe VPD paper's editing example: after an emoticon token, predict o. The reference task.about 0.004E1 to E4, E6 to E9, E11, E13
      Task ladderSix next-word tasks mined from Pile, such as " will" then " be", at three decades of baseline probability (rungs 0, 1, 2).0.25 down to 0.003E5, E11

      All tasks are trained on 41-token Pile windows with held-out windows for every score.

      Checks

      Pile checks (VPD's own)
      • Stochastic-mask KL: random masks between each piece's importance value and 1.
      • Adversarial-mask KL (PGD): 20 sign-gradient steps of a mask shared across a 128-row block. The check that fails where others do not.
      • CI-masked KL: every piece at its importance value, Δ out.
      • Unmasked KL: every piece fully on, Δ out.
      • Sparsity loss: VPD's importance-minimality loss.

      Pile validation rows 0 to 255, each number a ratio to the unchanged model in the same job. Pass line inherited from E2: adversarial ratio under 1.5. Since E7 every job measures a floor over three adversary seeds; "within the floor" means at most 1 plus three floors.

      Task-local checks
      • The target's log-odds gain over the original model at the trigger, on held-out windows.
      • The route split: which of five routes carries that gain (section 4).
      • The off-target KL: KL to the original model at the other 40 positions of the window.

      Pile rows hold almost no trigger positions, so the Pile checks and the task-local checks see different things.

      Protocol

      Predictions
      Filed before any GPU run, with rule and threshold, never edited afterwards; corrections are dated reports. Verdicts: pass, fail, null, neither. A reversal is announced explicitly. Every analysis ends with a "Not run" list.
      Compute
      MATS cluster L40s first, RunPod otherwise. The adversarial check's seed spread at the unchanged model depends on the GPU (about 4% on an RTX 4090 or L40S, up to 21% on an L40), so adversarial ratios are compared only within one card.

      4Techniques

      The decomposition and the masked model

      W = Σc uc vcT + Δ

      vc is a piece's read vector (the input direction it responds to), uc its write vector (what it adds to the output). A masked model multiplies each piece by a mask in [0, 1]. The CI-masked model sets each mask to Γ's importance value, Δ out. Faithful means the masked model reproduces the full model's output distribution.

      The operations, with their equations

      Drawn in the Overview. W = U Vᵀ + Δ, with U and V holding the alive pieces' write and read vectors as columns; the pieces are frozen in shape, only their vectors may move.

      Do-nothing

      W′ = U Vᵀ + (Δ + δW)

      What it does. The whole update goes into the residual; pieces and Γ are untouched.

      Status. The reference map. It passes any check that recomputes Δ from the weights, which is why the weak version requires Δ not to absorb δW.

      Least-squares split

      min ‖dU‖² + ‖dV‖² s.t. dU Vᵀ + U dVᵀ ≈ δW

      What it does. Splits a finished update over the pieces as the least-norm first-order change. The obstruction (I − PU) δW (I − PV) cannot be expressed and goes to Δ, with a small second-order term, so W′ equals the fine-tuned weights exactly.

      Status. Applied one-shot to finished 10-step updates (Q8).

      Adjoint split

      dU = δW V, dV = δWᵀ U

      What it does. Splits a finished update over the pieces without a solve: the weight change is UUᵀδW + δWVVᵀ, Δ stays fixed, so W′ is not the fine-tuned weights. The least-squares split is this map after preconditioning by K⁺.

      Status. Never run. Raised as a variant in the 2026-10-19 review and dismissed there; now part of the LoRA split (section 8).

      Component training

      δWstep = −η K(G), K(G) = U Uᵀ G + G V Vᵀ

      What it does. One gradient step on the pieces' vectors moves the weights by the part of the gradient G the pieces can express. Repeated every step; Γ and Δ frozen. In practice Adam on the vectors, since no plain-SGD rate learned without large off-target damage (E4).

      Status. The live candidate. Variants: all alive pieces; one matrix; rank-r offsets (E7, E13); top 1, 10 or 100 pieces; the paper's six; only pieces Γ keeps off (the gated-off control).

      Rescaling split

      δW ≈ Σc αc uc vcᵀ

      What it does. Rescales the existing pieces by a least-squares fit; the pieces' directions never move.

      Status. Dead after E1: 0.06% of a real update's energy lies in that span, against 0.035% for a random direction.

      The route split

      At the trigger, on held-out windows, the fine-tuned model gains some log-odds on the target. Write L(C, M) for the target's log-odds under the CI-masked model built from pieces C with masks M; C0 and C1 are the original and mapped pieces, M0 and M1 the importance values Γ computes from the original and the updated model's activations. The gain splits exactly into five shares summing to 1. Under the current criterion the split describes mechanism and does not decide a pass.

      ShareDefinitionReads as
      d directL(C1, M0) − L(C0, M0)the changed pieces at the old masks: "strengthen"
      r re-switchingL(C0, M1) − L(C0, M0)original pieces, new masks: "reveal"
      i interactionthe remainder of L(C1, M1) − L(C0, M0)cross term
      egate masked offunmasked model minus CI-masked modelpieces Γ keeps switched off: "create" without Δ
      eΔ residualfull model minus unmasked modelΔ alone: "create" outside the pieces
      Outside share

      The fraction of an update's output energy, inputs weighted by their real covariance on the windows, outside the alive pieces. Measured so far as the block outside both the write span and the read span, (I − PU) δW (I − PV); from 2026-10-20 to be reported as four blocks (inside both, write side only, read side only, outside both), since a rank-one update is outside-both only if neither of its vectors is in its span. Its isotropic null is the same number for a random update of the same per-matrix size; the ratio share ÷ null is what is tracked (1 = no more readable than noise). The plain Frobenius version is useless: the original weights fail it too.

      Switched-on set

      Alive pieces whose mean importance at the trigger rises by more than 0.3 when Γ reads the updated activations. Compared by Jaccard overlap against a seed floor; tested by recovery, the share of the re-switching effect obtained when only that set takes the new masks.

      Floors and controls

      Every ratio has a floor measured at the unchanged model in the same job. Every plan names what each metric is blind to and a control that must fail: gated-off component training for the route split (must read d = 0), the least-squares split for the Pile adversary (must fail for a fine-tune), do-nothing under the original masks for trigger-local faithfulness.

      Task ladder (round 3)

      run 2026-10-19 · RunPod · experiments/task_ladder

      Hypothesis refuted: Γ coverage of the gradient predicts nothing, because the changed pieces carry under 8% on every task; the re-switching share is set by the task.

      Run
      Six next-word tasks at three decades of baseline probability (two per rung, high and low coverage), plus emoticon. Arms: do-nothing fine-tune, Adam component training, gated-off component training. Route split at the trigger; optimiser-seed noise on emoticon.
      Found
      • Both filed predictions null: coverage does not predict retention; rarity matters at rung 2.
      • Direct share 0.03 to 0.08 on every task.

      • Where re-switching is small, the masked-off share takes over, not the direct share.
      • The gated-on positive control did not learn in 10 steps. The task-local adversarial numbers have no power (seed spread 55 to 88 nats against gains of 4 to 15).
      Bears on
      Q4, Q5, Q7

      What a critical reader should raise

      8Gaps and inconsistencies

      The criterion and the comparisons

      The pass line has no anchor

      "About as well" is read as an adversarial ratio under 1.5, a line inherited from E2's filing. On the L40 the unchanged model's own floor reached 21%, so the line sits inside the noise band. Two of seven ladder verdicts depend on which side of it a number falls.

      "Δ not absorbing δW" has no threshold

      The split LoRA's residual share (0.22 to 0.31 of the behaviour, half the energy) is judged by feel. The split fine-tune's 0.09 was read as acceptable, 0.22 as "the open reading". GOALS fixes no number.

      LoRA vs fine-tune was never like for like

      E8 scores the LoRA at step 2 and the fine-tune at step 10; at step 2 the fine-tune's split was also near the floor. E9 compares an L40S run with an L40 reference and ran no fine-tune arm. "A LoRA splits better" is unproven, though repeated in two reports.

      Component training is not "the same fine-tune"

      Adam on the pieces at another learning rate, half the weight movement, and on the ladder tasks about twice the off-target damage at the same step, which the adversarial excess orders with. On emoticon the sign flips (a third of the damage). Unexplained.

      Step or run

      The least-squares split is applied one-shot to a 10-step update, a first-order map on a non-small change; only component training matches the "continuous" framing. The per-step split was discussed and not built.

      Measurement

      Nothing measures faithfulness at the trigger with power

      Pile checks see the old model's account; the task-local adversary saturates. What remains is the route split, dominated by re-switching, whose status as mechanism or leak is unresolved. "Faithful to W + δW" has only been checked on text where the trained behaviour does not occur.

      Four checks, not five

      The sparsity loss follows the weights only while Γ is frozen. Reading A was recorded as five checks; the correction is proposed and not yet in the decisions file.

      Seeds

      E9: one LoRA seed. E10: one bijection, one adversary seed per state. E5: optimiser-seed noise on emoticon only. The Δ-share rise at the plateau and the 32-pair failure rest on single runs.

      Sparse component training never tried off emoticon

      E4 found one-piece or ten-piece component training faithful where the diffuse one was marginal; E11's failures are all for the diffuse arm. A cheap, untested question.

      Two readings of re-switching

      E6: the share is "not target-specific" (0.55 to 0.60). E10: it runs 0.12 to 0.80 across tasks. Reconciled as: set by what the existing gates can route at a given trigger and format, not by which target is learned there. Easy to trip on.

      The record

      The user's LoRA question was never built

      Since 2026-10-14 the user has asked, at least eight times, to train a LoRA, split its BA over the subcomponents and measure what lies outside. Each session answered either that the least-squares split already covered it, or built component training with a LoRA's rank (E7's one-matrix arms, E13). The outside share was computed in E7 to E9 as a side number. The per-subcomponent split was never saved, the adjoint split never computed. The LoRA split is now the current plan.

      Every "reveal" and "create" reading uses the route split informally. What a recruited piece must have done before the update for "recruit" to mean more than "any perturbation switches these on" is written nowhere.

      Terms, in the sense this project uses them

      11Glossary

      Adversarial check (PGD ratio)

      VPD's adversarial-mask reconstruction KL: 20 sign-gradient steps find the mask, shared across a 128-row block, that does the most damage. As a ratio to the unchanged model. The check that fails where the others do not.

      Adjoint split

      A finished update split over the pieces as dU = δW V, dV = δWᵀ U, with no solve; weight change UUᵀδW + δWVVᵀ, Δ fixed. Never run.

      Alive piece

      A subcomponent whose importance ever exceeds a small threshold; about 9,900 of 38,900. Only alive pieces are trained or split onto.

      Causal importance (CI)

      Γ's output for one piece at one position, in [0, 1].

      CI-masked model

      Every piece multiplied by its importance value, Δ out.

      Commute

      Carry a weight update over to the decomposition by a simple operation, so that mapping then checking agrees with fine-tuning then decomposing.

      Component training

      Train the alive pieces' vectors on the fine-tune loss with Γ and Δ frozen; Adam, many steps. One SGD step moves the weights by UUᵀG + GVVᵀ. Rank-r component training limits the offsets to a LoRA's rank (E7, E13).

      Coverage

      Share of the first component-training step's squared update on high-importance pieces at the trigger. E5's predictor; dropped.

      Create, reveal, strengthen, brittle

      GOALS's mechanism hypotheses. Strengthen: the changed pieces carry it (direct share). Reveal: Γ switches existing pieces on (re-switching). Create: the behaviour lives in Δ or in pieces Γ keeps off. Brittle: the split is unstable across seeds or small changes to the map.

      Δ, the residual

      The part of each matrix not covered by the pieces. Frozen in every map; the do-nothing placement puts the whole update here.

      Direct share, d

      Gain carried by the changed pieces at the original importance values. At most 0.08 in every run.

      Do-nothing placement

      The update in Δ, pieces and Γ untouched. The reference operation.

      Emoticon task

      The VPD paper's hand-editing example: after an emoticon token, predict o. The reference task.

      Expressible, obstruction, outside share

      An update is expressible to the extent a first-order change of the alive pieces can produce it. The obstruction is the part outside both the write span and the read span. The outside share weights it by real inputs; its isotropic null is the same number for a random update. From 2026-10-20 reported as four blocks: inside both, write side only, read side only, outside both.

      Floor

      Seed-to-seed or block-to-block spread of a ratio at the unchanged model, same job. "Within the floor" = at most 1 plus three floors.

      Γ (gamma)

      The importance function: one transformer over all 24 matrices' inputs at all positions, one importance value per piece per position. Frozen throughout.

      Gated-off component training

      Only pieces Γ keeps off at the trigger may move. The control for the route split: must read d = 0.

      Least-squares split

      A finished weight change written as the least-norm first-order change to the alive pieces, obstruction left in Δ, so the weights equal the fine-tuned ones.

      LoRA split

      Train a rank-r LoRA to completion, split its BA over the subcomponents (least-squares and adjoint), save the per-subcomponent updates, report the outside share in four blocks. Not yet run.

      LoRA

      A low-rank update, read factor A (random start) times write factor B (zero start). The user's canonical post-training update.

      Masked-off share, egate

      What the unmasked model gains beyond the CI-masked one: behaviour in pieces Γ keeps off.

      Off-target KL

      KL from a state to the original model at the 40 non-trigger positions of the held-out windows. The measure of collateral change; kl_other in the code.

      Pile checks

      The four reconstruction ratios and the sparsity loss on Pile validation rows. Blind to the trained behaviour.

      Rescaling split

      A finished update written as a rescaling of the fixed pieces, δW ≈ Σ αc uc vcᵀ. Dead after E1.

      Reading A, reading B

      A: the weak version as written; the criterion. B: the changed pieces carry the behaviour at the old masks; a mechanism reading no map has passed.

      Recovery

      Share of the re-switching effect obtained when only the switched-on set takes the new masks.

      Residual share, eΔ

      What the full model gains beyond the unmasked one: Δ alone. 1 for do-nothing by construction.

      Re-switching share, r

      Gain carried by the original pieces under the importance values Γ computes from the updated activations. Dominates every CI-masked number for behaviours the model has.

      Re-weighting hypothesis

      The informal claim that post-training re-weights mechanisms pretraining built rather than creating new ones. A precise VPD-level definition is work in progress (section 1).

      Retained share

      d + r + i: what the CI-masked model keeps of the gain.

      Route split

      The exact five-way split of the trigger gain into direct, re-switching, interaction, masked-off and residual shares. Mechanism only. Not to be confused with the split of an update over the pieces.

      Split

      Writing a finished update as per-subcomponent updates (dUc, dVc) plus a remainder outside the pieces. Least-squares, adjoint and rescaling are the three ways to choose it.

      Rung

      Decade of a next-word task's baseline probability: 0 is 0.1 to 1, 1 is 0.01 to 0.1, 2 is 0.001 to 0.01. Rungs 3 and 4 not run.

      Saturated matrix

      One whose alive pieces span every direction on one side, so every update to it is expressible. Four of 24.

      Scored step

      The checkpoint a filed prediction is read at: usually 10, or the first at which held-out P(target) crosses a filed line.

      Sparsity loss

      VPD's importance-minimality loss at the released run's end setting. Blind to the operation while Γ is frozen.

      Stochastic check

      VPD's stochastic-mask reconstruction KL: random masks between importance and 1 on a random subset of matrices per position. Flat in every run.

      Subcomponent, piece

      A rank-one matrix u vT in one weight matrix. The unit of the decomposition.

      Switched-on set

      Alive pieces whose mean importance at the trigger rises by more than 0.3 under the updated activations. About 150 to 200 per arm on emoticon.

      Trigger, target

      The position where the task applies and the token taught there.

      Unmasked model

      Every piece fully on, Δ out. Says whether the pieces alone reproduce the model.

      VPD

      Goodfire's parameter decomposition method (Bushnaq, Braun, Clive-Griffin et al., 2026), used unchanged.

      W0, W + δW, D, D′

      Original weights, fine-tuned weights, released decomposition, mapped decomposition.

      Weak and strong versions

      Weak: D′ is faithful to the updated model with Δ not absorbing the update. Strong: D′ matches a fresh re-decomposition. Only the weak version is in play.

      Windows

      41-token Pile contexts holding one trigger, split into training and held-out sets.
