Question for you · compare-lead · new 2026-11-09

Nothing runs on this comparison until this is decided. It is due 2026-11-11.

The re-gating share cannot tell a fine-tune that learned a rule from one that memorised shuffled labels. Can steering or single neurons? Two choices stand in the way of finding out.

Why this matters. The paper's second part asks whether the re-gating share measures anything simpler methods miss. The share comes from the decomposition, which splits the model's weights into many small pieces that switch on and off per token. A fine-tune raises the log-odds of the answer by some amount, its gain; the share is the part of that gain that appears when we keep the original model's pieces and change only which ones switch on. Our hardest test for it uses two fine-tunes on the same text. One learns a real rule: after a word, say label A or label B according to a property of that word. Its twin gets the same words with the labels shuffled word by word, so it can only memorise. The share reads the two alike. If steering or a reading of single neurons can tell them apart, the share is missing something those methods see. If neither can, no method we have sees this difference.

What stands in the way. You asked for steering to be made fair, and most of that fits. Two things do not.

First choice: where the per-word steering vectors come from. A steering vector is the average change a fine-tune makes to the model's activations, added to the original model. You asked for one vector per word, built from some examples and tested on others. But the share was measured on 256 examples, which would split into 128 to build from and 128 to test, and they repeat a word too rarely. Only 15 of the 128 test examples on the task whose label depends on a word's case, and 34 of 128 on the task whose label depends on whether a word repeats, have a second example of their word to build a vector from.

  • Option a: build the vectors from the examples the fine-tune trained on, about 16 per word, and test on the same 256 examples as the share. Every example is covered and steering is compared on exactly the share's examples. Limit: the fine-tune has seen those training examples, so its vectors may look a little better than they would on new text.
  • Option b: use all 1,024 examples set aside for this, half to build and half to test, and measure the share again on those test examples so both methods use the same ones. Nothing the fine-tune trained on is used. Limit: on the case task only 205 of the 512 test examples have a vector, so most of that task goes unread.
  • Option c: one vector per label instead of per word. Every example is covered. Limit: the vector is chosen by each example's correct answer, so steering is handed the answer on both fine-tunes, and the result says little.

Second choice: only two trained copies of each fine-tune exist. A copy is the same fine-tune trained again from a different random seed; how much copies disagree is the noise. Our rule before any run is that the smallest difference that would change our minds, a tenth of the fine-tune's gain, must be at least twice that noise. With two copies it is 1.8 times the noise; three copies would clear it. A third copy means new fine-tunes, which your instructions for this work currently rule out.

  • Option d: run with two copies and report what is seen, as a description and not a test. Free, minutes on the shared cluster. It tells you whether the methods look different, but a small difference cannot be told from noise. It needs you to waive the rule, because the tool that hands out run numbers refuses this run otherwise.
  • Option e: train a third copy of each, four more fine-tunes, free, under half an hour (estimated from four earlier fine-tunes taking 13 minutes with more measurements). The result counts as a test. Limit: you would have to drop the no-new-fine-tunes instruction and add this as new work.
  • Option f: do not run this comparison. The question of whether any activation-level method sees real against shuffled stays open.

What happens next. After your choice the code is written and run the same day, and you get one figure and one paragraph, with no verdict on reuse.

Recommendation. Options a and d: every example is covered, and your note on this work already said to describe the two-copy result rather than test it, though the rule still needs waiving.

The re-gating share reads about 0.8 on a fine-tune that learned nothing. Is that any small update, or only this one? Answering it needs a measurement of noise we never saved.

Why this matters. The share is the part of a fine-tune's gain that appears when we keep the decomposition's original pieces and change only which ones switch on. The most awkward number for it is a fine-tune on code text whose loss on held-out text got slightly worse, and which still reads 0.82 at training step 10 and 0.77 at step 100. That is the form of the share averaged over every position in the text, not only at one answer. If any small change to the weights, even a random one with the same overall size, also reads about 0.8, then a high reading in this form says nothing about learning. If the random change reads far lower, the weakness belongs to this one fine-tune.

What stands in the way. The check would read those two fine-tunes again, plus one random update of the same size. Our rule before any run is that the smallest difference that would change our minds must be at least twice the noise. Here the noise is how much the share varies from one batch of text to another, and that was never saved: only one number per fine-tune was kept.

  • Option a: measure the variation first, in the same job. Free, minutes. If it is small enough for the check to decide anything, the rest of the job runs; if not, the job stops and comes back to you with the number. Limit: the job may stop without an answer.
  • Option b: use the nearest saved number instead, the standard deviation of the two readings above, 0.035. The job runs straight through. Limit: that number measures something else, the spread between two training steps and not between batches of text, so the rule would be passed without measuring what it asks for.
  • Option c: do not run this check. The awkward number stays in the paper with nothing said about whether it is general.

What happens next. Under a or b you get both readings with their spread and one paragraph saying whether "a fine-tune that learned nothing still reads high" holds for any small update or only this one.

Recommendation. Option a: it measures what the question needs and costs minutes.
