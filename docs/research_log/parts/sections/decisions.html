Why does one block of 128 Pile rows decide the SGD verdict?

The question. The paper's adversarial check gave the same two SGD teacher-trained decompositions opposite verdicts on two sets of 256 Pile rows. Should we find out why one block of 128 rows decides their verdict, and read E39's main states on more rows, before saying where full-matrix SGD belongs?

Evidence. Almost every check in the project reads Pile rows 0-255. There both SGD states fail (2.07, 2.12); on rows 256-511 they pass (1.18, 1.39). Their fail sits on rows 128-255 alone; their other three blocks read 1.11 to 1.42. Adam's fails on all four blocks; the LoRA's passes on three. A "most blocks fail" rule would pass both SGD states on both sets.

Options. (a) Look first, free: one cluster debug job on the SGD and LoRA states, adversary seeds 1 and 2 on rows 128-255, then per-row KL; it says whether the start, a few rows or the block decides. (b) Read the Adam, SGD and LoRA states on val 512-1023, four more blocks, and count failing blocks; free on the cluster. (c) Keep the check and record "SGD unresolved on the broad task". (d) Run the two remaining stages anyway (about $1.5, H100); they answer only on rows 0-255. Fable advises (a) and (b) as one free job, holding (d) and adopting (c)'s wording now; the lead recommends that.

Why it matters. The check is the pass test on both lines; if one block can decide a verdict, every reading near the lines carries that risk (E41, E42, E43 and E34 have verdicts in this band).

Take the gate gain to stage 1?

The question. Stage 0 of the re-gating reset is done: should the gate gain (how far Γ's new values alone raise the stand-in's answer) go to stage 1 on the Qwen line's updates, and first, should one more $0 cluster job read stage 1's own ratio (gated piece gain over piece gain after a least-squares split) on the random change and the hand write?

Evidence. It rises only for updates that raise the answer (3.3 to 11.6 nats for fine-tunes), but a random change as disruptive as the fine-tune gives up to an eighth of that and the hand write 0.16 and 0.34 of the copy fine-tune's. Stage 1's ratio has never been read on a null (E46 report, "Advice and decision").

Options. (1) Stage 1 now, random change as a relative bar and the write as a second null: about $5 on Qwen states. (2) The same with nulls matched on the real gain, which becomes option 1. (3) The $0 job first, then option 1 if the ratio on the nulls is 0.4 or less; at 0.7 or more stage 1 needs another number. (4) Retire the reading, which the replication does not support. Fable recommends 3, then 1 (advice only); the lead agrees with 3.
