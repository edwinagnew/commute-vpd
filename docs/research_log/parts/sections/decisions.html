When we test whether the original model's mask already holds the copied answer, what counts as the model "still saying" that answer?

The question. The original model copies a token: after "A B … A" it predicts B. Under the mask Γ reads for this model, the masked forward still leans to B, and we do not know whether it works that out from the text or the mask already holds the answer. The free test: keep the mask fixed and replace B in the text with another token. You said: if the model still says B, the mask holds the answer and the forward computes no copying, so close the 67M mask work and log the limit; if it now says the new token, the mask opens a real route, so test the two copying heads off together next.

The evidence. Under the mask, on the intact text, B's probability is very uneven across windows: median 0.03%, mean 6%, and B is the top token in at least 4.5% of windows. So in most windows B is far from the model's top prediction, and "says B" needs a definition.

Options. (a) B counts as still said when it beats the replacement token by at least 1 log-odds, and neither B nor the replacement gains or loses more than 0.15 of the mask's lean (its preference for B over the same text with the pair broken) when the text changes. It runs as one free cluster job, under about 10 minutes, at once. It tells you whether the masked forward's preference follows the text or stays with B, worded "leans to B", with how often B is the top token printed beside it. Next: if the preference stays with B, we close the 67M mask work at this position under the original model's mask and log the limit; if it follows the text, we test the two heads off together; if it falls in between, the result comes back to you. (b) B counts as still said only if it is the top token in most windows. It runs as the same job after a small rule change. It tells you whether the mask makes B the model's actual answer. Next: at these levels the rule will rarely fire, so the likely result is that the mask-holds-the-answer reading is not supported, even if the forward's preference ignores the text.

Recommendation: (a), since (b) would read "not supported" for a forward whose preference ignores the text, which is the case you asked about.

When teacher training is repeated along a fine-tune, may we read how far the subcomponents moved as what the fine-tune taught?

The question. Teacher training can be repeated: each round starts from the last round's subcomponents and copies the fine-tune at a later step. We have read the size each round reaches as error piling up or not.

The evidence. With the unchanged model as teacher for 1,000 more steps, the decomposition after one teacher training kept its predictions and passed the adversarial check (0.96), yet its largest relative weight change still grew from 0.018 to 0.021 (a random start: 0.023). Some growth happens with nothing to copy; this pair cannot say how much.

Options. (a) Change the wording only; nothing runs. We keep "each state keeps the behaviour and passes" and do not read size either way. (b) The same wording, plus a repeated run with nothing to teach: seven rounds of 100 steps against a fine-tune whose labels come from the unchanged model's own predictions; one free cluster job, about 35 minutes, read against a single round at the same three adversary starts (1.40, 1.37, 1.29).

Recommendation: (a). No plan repeats teacher training now, and no pass depends on reading size. Run (b) first if the larger model's plan chooses repeated rounds.
