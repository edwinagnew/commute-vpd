Restate the fixed-shift hypothesis without cues?

What it is about. The hypothesis says one fixed change to Γ's importance values per cue word carries the switching after a fine-tune. A cue is the trigger token of our toy tasks, and real post-training has none. Tonight's runs tested a form that names no token: each input or position gets the change of the training inputs the original model treats most alike.

What the evidence says. On the toy tasks it does as well as one change per cue added the same way, and the most similar inputs share the cue 62 to 71% of the time, against a predicted 90% (E35). On a fine-tune of lowercased text, with no trigger, it recovers 0.76 to 0.89 of the switching once fitted on 262,144 positions, better than any other rule (E36). The original model's attention inputs, which Γ reads, do as well; a summary of the last four tokens recovers about half. A bag of the whole preceding text added nothing to those four tokens, but carried no usable signal on its own, so whether longer context closes the gap is still untested. One threat is open: every rule adds its change to a position's own values and clips to [0, 1], so the original values may win only because similar values have similar room to move. A straight line per subcomponent from its own value recovered nothing; Amendment 9 tests any curve, 16 bins per subcomponent, and the restatement holds only if that stays at least 0.3 below. Switching's share of the gain is not evidence on its own: an update that learned nothing showed a similar share.

The options. (a) Restate now, tentatively: "positions the original model treats alike get alike changes", in place of "per cue", noting it holds on the toy tasks and, with enough fitted positions, for three-quarters or more of the switching on one synthetic style of running text; the name "fixed shift" would then need replacing. (b) Keep "per cue" until a fine-tune of a real behaviour that learns confirms the new form; the code-text fine-tune did not learn, and the larger model's post-trained sibling is the natural test. (c) Restate, but count lowercasing as too synthetic, so the claim rests on the toy tasks for now.

Why it matters. A form without cues is what lets the re-gating line say anything about real post-training, which is what the user asked of it.

Should the adversarial check be judged against a fixed line, or against leaving the decomposition alone?

What it is about. The success criterion (reading A) asks the new decomposition to pass VPD's checks on the updated model about as well as the old one does on the original. For the adversarial check we read a ratio: the adversary's damage on the new decomposition over its damage on the original. A ratio under 1.5 passes, with a margin for seed-to-seed noise.

The problem. Do-nothing keeps every subcomponent and puts the whole update into Δ, so it changes nothing that could make the subcomponents fragile. Yet it fails the 1.5 line on large Adam fine-tunes at step 10 (2.18 on emoticon), because the adversary can remove Δ, which now holds the update. So the line mixes two things: how much of the update sits outside the subcomponents, and whether the subcomponents are fragile.

The options. (a) Keep the fixed line, and report do-nothing beside each verdict, as now. (b) Count a state as passing when it is no worse than the same update's do-nothing. (c) Anchor on another baseline, such as a decomposition retrained on the updated model, which costs a full VPD run. Since E21–E22 the choice affects fewer verdicts. Small and later updates' do-nothing passes the line, so only large early updates are read differently.

Why it matters. Under (b), a state that fails the line by no more than its do-nothing does would pass. A state worse than its do-nothing still fails, like the least-squares split of the large fine-tune (5.4 against 2.18). That changes what counts as a map that commutes.
