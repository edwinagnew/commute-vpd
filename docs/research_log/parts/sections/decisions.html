Question for you · regating-lead · new 2026-11-27

Should we test whether the re-gating share on Qwen3-1.7B depends on how far an update moves the answer?

What was done. On Qwen3-1.7B we scaled random changes until each moved the trained answer by one log-odds, then read the re-gating share on them beside the fine-tunes' share, three draws each.

What we got. On the copying and known-answer tasks the randoms read about as high as the fine-tunes: copying 0.63 to 0.72 against 0.61 to 0.64, known answers 0.51 to 1.03 against 0.58 to 0.60. On the cipher, invented-word and both maths tasks the fine-tunes read higher (cipher 0.56 to 0.58 against 0.35 to 0.38).

Why it may be a gain effect. The fine-tunes move the answer by 10 to 160 log-odds and the randoms by about 1, and the code calls shares near a gain of 1 unstable. On the 67M model, random-subspace shares sat up to 0.47 below their fine-tune at the first gain and came within about 0.10 later. So the overlap on copying and known answers may come from the gain mismatch, not from the fine-tunes.

Options.

  • A. Scale the copying and known-answer fine-tunes down. Re-read the share at gains 1 and 10 (plus full gain) on the VPD reading, seeds 0 to 2, same rows, and re-read the 1.03 random draw on fresh rows. About $5 to $7, under 1 hour on one H100, descriptive. If the share falls with gain, the overlap is a gain effect and the paper says so; if not, the overlap stands. Limit: it reads one reading on two kinds of task.
  • B. No run. The paper states the gain mismatch in plain words beside the Qwen values. $0; the overlap stays unexplained.

Recommendation: A. It is cheap and fast, and it settles whether the one place Qwen reads like random is a gain artefact.
