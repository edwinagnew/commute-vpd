On Qwen3-1.7B the pass test cannot fail do-nothing, because Γ's masks follow the activations they are given. Take the masks from the original model, or keep them and add another control?

The pieces. The pieces are the rank-one subcomponents. Δ is the weights outside them. Γ picks, from the model's activations, which pieces are on for each input. Do-nothing leaves the whole update in Δ. The halves move the update onto the pieces Γ often switches on, or those it rarely does.

Evidence. E44 stopped because the pass test cannot fail do-nothing: with Γ reading the state's own activations, do-nothing sat 0.09 nats from the fine-tuned model against 1.42 for the original decomposition, as did both halves (E44). Given a mix of two models' activations, Γ recovered none of the update; given one model whose first 16 layers alone were fine-tuned, it recovered 90%, close to the 93% those layers make (Amendments 1 and 1b). So Γ's masks follow whatever activations they are given, and the test as built passes do-nothing for any update.

Options. (1) Masks computed from the original model's activations for every state (reading B, set aside on 2026-10-19; E44 Amendment 2, about 35 min, $0): do-nothing then reads 1.42 and fails, as a control should. It is blind to a split onto pieces Γ switches on only after the update. (2) Keep reading A (masks from each state's own activations) and add a different control. Fable also lists refitting Γ per state (about $10 a state) and descriptive placement only ($0, no pass line). Option 1 and the refit change your 2026-10-19 pass criterion.

Recommendation (the lead's, following Fable). Option 1. Its controls can fail: do-nothing must read 1.42, the upper half below the lower, and a size-matched random change about 1.42. Under reading A every split passes, so the line would move to descriptive placement.
