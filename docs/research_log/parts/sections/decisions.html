How should we judge whether a training update has been placed correctly in Qwen3-1.7B's decomposition?

What this is about. VPD splits each weight matrix of Qwen3-1.7B into many small pieces, the subcomponents, plus a leftover, Δ, for whatever the pieces do not hold. Training then changes the weights by an update. Our first Qwen goal is a map: a rule that moves the subcomponents so that they hold the update and the decomposition still describes the model. The goal also asks for a test that can fail a bad placement, since a test that passes everything says nothing about the map. No single test does that here, so you need to choose what we judge the map by.

What a test has to catch. A placement can be bad in four ways. We have a measurement that shows three of them. Only the first has a filed rule that fails it; the other two have clear readings but no pass line yet.

  • Wrong direction: shown, with a rule. The change is not the update. The clean-model table measures how far a changed model's outputs sit from the updated model's, with nothing masked: 0 is the updated model and 1 is as far as the original. Across three updates, least squares (a direct fit of the update onto the subcomponents, no training) reads 0.006 to 0.016, a random change of the update's size about 1.0, and the update with flipped signs 1.4 to 1.6.
  • Left in Δ: shown, no line yet. The subcomponents barely move, so the decomposition explains nothing. Doing nothing is the extreme case, and the table cannot see it, because its weights are the updated model's and it reads 0. The share of the update's squared size left in Δ does see it: 100% for doing nothing, 9-10% for least squares.
  • Wrong size: shown, no line yet. The map copies the outputs with a change far bigger than the update. Squared size shows this: on the first fine-tune (ten steps on maths problems), teacher training with masks in its loss reached 3.4 times the update's squared size and was still growing, and without masks 0.52 times.
  • Wrong subcomponents: not shown. The total change is right, but it sits on pieces that do not do that job. The table reads the update placed on the half of the subcomponents least often on at only 0.05 to 0.11, and has no line that fails it.

Why VPD's own test does not fill the gap. VPD's adversary searches for the masks (settings that turn subcomponents down) that most damage the outputs, and reads that damage as a ratio to the unchanged model: pass at 1.40 or below, fail at 1.61 or above. On the first fine-tune it failed nothing: doing nothing reads 1.24, least squares 1.28 and the update on the rarely-on half 1.27. It can fail on this model: shuffling the rarely-on subcomponents' vectors among themselves reads 11.3, and a random change at 100 times the update's size reads 32.5 (one seed each). But that shuffle moves those vectors 250 to 740 times further than a real update does. So whether it catches wrong subcomponents at a real update's size is unmeasured.

New: it fails doing nothing on a larger update, and we can't yet say why. We ran it on the one-wording TOFU update, about ten times the first fine-tune's size (E89). This is not the five-wording update you chose as the example of new content, though the two are about the same size. Doing nothing reads about 3 (seeds 2.15 and 4.19), and the update on the rarely-on half, with the remainder in Δ, reads about 6 (seeds 4.99 and 6.80). Both are over the fail bar at every step. An independent check found no bug, with three limits. First, doing nothing leaves the whole update in Δ, and the adversary can turn Δ down, so the fail may be the update being switched off rather than damage from masked subcomponents; no saved number tells these apart. Second, this update differs from the first fine-tune in kind as well as size, so size is not shown to be the cause. Third, no state on this update has passed, so we do not know that the test can tell a good placement from a bad one here. The rarely-on half leaves about a quarter of the update's squared size in Δ and still reads higher than doing nothing, so the ratio does not simply follow the share left in Δ. All of this is provisional.

Which update to judge on. "Behaviour kept", the share of an update's gain a changed model holds, can only grade a placement if the gain builds with the update's size. On the first fine-tune a tenth of the update already keeps 93%, so almost anything passes. On the TOFU fine-tune (facts about invented authors) a tenth keeps 28%, a quarter 58% and a half 84%, read on its training rows, so this is memory. That is the update trained on one wording of each fact. Since this card was filed, you chose the five-wording TOFU update as the example of new content (2026-11-05). Its gain has not been read this way, so the run must first say which of the two it aims at. No placement has had its behaviour kept or size read on either.
