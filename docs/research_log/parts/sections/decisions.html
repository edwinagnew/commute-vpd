Question for you · definition-lead · new 2026-11-09

Waiting on the user (fleet ask 11). Nothing runs until this is decided.

Should we keep looking for a task that the frozen reuse definition can give a verdict on?

The question. The frozen reuse definition (the one you adopted, from Jain et al. 2024) says a fine-tune reuses an existing capability if three things hold: a linear probe trained on ordinary web text reads that capability inside the original model; the fine-tune's behaviour carries over to values it never trained on; and removing the probe's direction breaks the fine-tuned behaviour while sparing a matched twin fine-tune. We wanted natural tasks on the small 67M model that this definition can read and that also show the re-gating split, so that the two readings could be compared. The re-gating ratio is the share of a fine-tune's gain that appears when the original subcomponents are kept and only the frozen gate network gates differently on the new activations. Should the search continue, and if so where?

The evidence.

  • Natural tasks. Verb agreement (is/are matching a subject several words back) was too rare to build: 155 usable windows in 16.7M tokens of web text, and the common forms the model already gets right leave nothing to learn. Pronoun gender (he/she matching an earlier first name) failed the first check: a web-text probe at the place the pronoun is predicted reads the name's gender at 0.53, 0.61, 0.73 and 0.87 across the model's four blocks, against a bar of 0.90 before the last block and a control near 0.5. It therefore reads "unreadable", and no fine-tune ran.
  • Built-label tasks. The two tasks that do pass the probe check are built, not natural: a made-up label chosen by capitalisation of the previous word, and a made-up label chosen by whether the previous word already occurred. Both fine-tunes carry over to held-out words, but removing the probe's direction takes only 3-9% of the gain against a bar of 50%, so all eight saved fine-tunes, real and twin, read "not through" the capability. The one-direction removal figures are 0.083 and 0.091 for capitalisation and 0.031 and 0.038 for repetition.
  • Earlier tasks. Every older 67M task read "unreadable" or "does not apply".

So far the definition has returned "reuses" on nothing.

Options.

  • (a) Pause the search. Nothing runs, $0. The write-up says the definition gives no "reuses" verdict on any 67M task we have, and why. Limit: the other lines keep no outside label to read against on the 67M.
  • (b) His/her after a name on the 67M (more windows, same gender property). About an hour of cluster time, $0. It tells whether the possessive form is read better. It will probably fail the same probe; if it passes, the fine-tunes and the re-gating comparison follow.
  • (c) Search on Qwen3-1.7B. Needs a new plan; cost not estimated. A probe may pass on the larger model, so a verdict is possible. But the re-gating ratio reads 0.99 or more on every Qwen update, random ones included, so there is no split to compare the verdict against.
  • (d) A stronger removal on the two built-label tasks: all sites at once. One cluster debug job, about $0. It would tell "the fine-tune barely uses the signal" from "it reads it at many sites". This changes the definition's check, so only you can adopt it. On the capitalisation task, removing two directions at every position and block already reached only 0.19-0.21, and full erasure damaged the model too much to read.

Recommendation: (a).

The slide's new "pass" for a rank-8 LoRA under the least-squares split rests on one attacker start and clears a line by under 1%. Re-read it from more starts?

The question. The least-squares split moves each subcomponent by the smallest change that reproduces a fine-tune to first order and leaves the rest of the update in Δ, the weights outside the subcomponents. The slide reads it with the adversarial check: an attacker searches over masks of the subcomponents, and its damage is divided by its damage on the unchanged model (1.40 or less passes, 1.61 or more fails). Those readings used one attacker start. This is the 67M model, where work runs only when you ask by name.

The evidence. On the lowercase task the rank-8 LoRA's least-squares state reads 1.23 and the Adam fine-tune's 12.3; we predicted both would fail. The pass line measured for these rows is 1.241, which 1.23 clears by under 1%, and a similar trained decomposition moved from 1.23 to 1.33 across two starts. The state leaves 38% of the update in Δ whatever the check reads.

Options. (a) Leave the slide as it is, with the one start said beside the row; nothing runs. (b, recommended) Two free cluster jobs of about 26 and 23 minutes (estimated): the first reads the LoRA's states at two more starts and on 256 more rows; the second reads Adam's four states likewise. If the LoRA stays under the line the slide stands; if it rises, the cell becomes "between" or "fail".

Recommendation: b. It costs no money.
