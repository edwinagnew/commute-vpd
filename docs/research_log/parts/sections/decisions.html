The reuse score R reads about 1 on everything: how should the paper handle that, and what are the memorising twins for?

R, the reuse score: the share of the parts the behaviour runs through that still compute what they did in the base model. Only you change the definition's wording.

What we ran.

  • We scored R on hand-built edits whose answer we know, expecting the reuse edits to score higher than the new ones. Instead a reuse edit (a ten-fold gain on eight neurons) scored 0.976, below a new edit at 0.996, because R's per-part score (squared cosine) treats a big gain as a change. (P1.37, report, figure)
  • We shrank the larger fine-tune of each of nine trained pairs to its partner's size, expecting the reuse side to stay higher on at least 8. It stayed higher on 4, so R's pair differences mostly reflected update size. (P1.38, report, figure)

So: R sits near 1 everywhere, and its small differences follow how far the weights moved, not reuse.

Question 1. The paper says a fact memorised through small changes can correctly read as reused, so should the memorising twins still be a case R must read low on?

  • (a) Recommended. Say the twins check how the pairs were built, not R. No wording change.
  • (b) Keep them as a case R must read low on; the definition must then say a memorised fact is new.
  • (c) Drop them from R's results; we lose the check on how the pairs were built.
