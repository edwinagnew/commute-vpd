Question for you · definition-lead · new 2026-11-18

The working definition, read as the theory note writes it, keeps 0.999 or more on every fine-tune and does not separate reuse from new. Three wording questions: how to score a neuron's rule, whether shared silence counts, and whether parts are weighted.

Nothing runs until this is decided; a weighted re-read would be a new cell on the same saved items, with no new training. The definition says a fine-tune reuses the original model M to the extent that the parts it needs keep M's rules. We scored a part's rule at each input setting by the squared cosine of what it writes in the two models (1 is always the same direction), and took the plain mean over the parts needed.

  • Controls as expected. Do-nothing reads "no change", a circuit turned up two-fold and ten-fold reads 1, new units in M's own slots 0.002, appended grafts 0.
  • Reuse against new: not separated. Every one of 20 fine-tunes keeps 0.999 or more; the reuse-minus-twin gaps rank by how far the twin moved its weights (case 5.0e-4, repeat 9e-5, key tables about 2e-5), at both seeds. Today's aggregate, a size-aware distance, activation patching, M-only settings and restoring zeroed parts keep that ranking.
  • Heads alone do show it. Heads are under 1% of the parts and read 0.976 to 0.995; the identity/random-map gap is 0.0036 and 0.0039 against repeat 0.0007 and -0.0024. Reported, not a filed rule.

Question 1: how to score "keeps the rule" at a neuron. (a) Per input setting, as the note writes it: sees only the output direction and when the neuron is silent; the rebuilt circuit reads 0.995. (b) All settings at once, today's code: 0.97. (c) A per-setting distance that also counts size: 0.91, and a pure change of gain counts as a loss. None separates reuse from new here. Recommendation: (c), the only one that sees a neuron firing harder.

Question 2: do two units both silent on an input follow the same rule there? The note implies yes. It did not matter here (shared silence was 0 in the slotted graft, about one setting in a thousand on the pairs) but could in a network whose activation is zero for every negative input. Recommendation: keep yes until a network shows it matter.

Question 3: a plain mean over the parts needed (one head counts as one neuron), or weighted? The plain mean is dominated by thousands of barely changed neurons. Recommendation: a new cell reading retention weighted by each part's contribution to the behaviour, on the same saved items.

The independent premise check rated this doubtful. Its question, put without a recommendation: "what result from a retention-style measure like rho would you need to see before treating 'how much of M survives' as the right thing for the definition to measure?" Its reasoning: whole-model measures look saturated, and only re-gating's share gap (0.39 to 0.70 on every pair) separates the cases, asking where the new computation comes from rather than how much of M remains.
