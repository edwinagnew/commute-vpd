Is anything left worth running to tell "already there" from "new" on Qwen3-1.7B, or should we write up what we have?

Why it matters. Your question asks whether a simple map fails because training teaches new capabilities rather than strengthening existing ones. To answer that on Qwen3-1.7B, we need a reading of the decomposition that gives different values for the two cases. Without one, readings of the GSM8K and RL fine-tunes describe them but cannot say which case they are.

What we did. We read saved fine-tunes through the frozen decomposition and checked each reading against two things. The first is random weight changes, which a useful reading should not confuse with training. The second is two reference fine-tunes whose kind is known by design: the template fine-tune teaches an answer format the model already follows when a system prompt asks for it ("already there"), and the invented-facts fine-tune teaches facts about people who do not exist ("new"). They must be compared at equal size, because bigger changes read differently for trivial reasons.

What is stuck.

  • The switching reading tells us nothing here. Start from the KL between the changed model and the CI-masked original decomposition. Changing the subcomponents and recomputing the masks together removes most of it. The re-gating share is the fraction of that removal achieved by only recomputing the masks, with Γ on the changed model's activations. It reads 0.99 or more for every change, random ones included.
  • One reading is left, and size may explain it. The direct share is the fraction achieved by only changing the subcomponents, with the original masks kept. The maths fine-tunes read above random, but they are also the biggest changes, and no run has varied size with the kind of change held fixed.
  • The two reference fine-tunes have never been compared. On KL from the original model on the maths rows, the template fine-tune levels off at 0.33. The invented-facts fine-tune recalls 2% of its facts at step 12 (KL 0.31) and 91% at step 100 (KL 0.62), and no state between those was saved. The template fine-tune has not been read through the decomposition at all.

Options.

  • A. Test the direct share against size first (recommended). Scale random changes up to the size of the biggest fine-tune, read a smaller saved state of that fine-tune (step 9), and read them all the same way. About 2 hours, $0, with a small code change to set the scale. If random reaches the fine-tune's level at equal size, the direct share only measures size, no reading is left, and we go to C. If random stays well below, training differs from random beyond size, and we go to B. This shows whether B is worth running; it cannot itself separate "already there" from "new".
  • B. Compare the two reference fine-tunes at equal size. Retrain invented facts, saving states between steps 12 and 100, and read the learned state nearest the template's size, along with the template fine-tune, on two seeds each. About 3.5 hours, $0. This would be the first same-size comparison. It has three limits: a learned state of that size may not exist; the direct share may be too small there to rise above the 0.007 spread; and a clean gap could still be about topic, since on the maths rows invented facts mostly shift answers to one line rather than expressing the facts. If the two differ by more than the gap between seeds and the 0.007 spread, the direct share becomes a candidate for reading the GSM8K and RL fine-tunes, and the new pair below becomes worth building to rule out topic. If they do not differ, or no such state exists, we go to C.
  • C. Stop and write up. Writing only. The write-up says the switching reading cannot separate anything at this scale, the direct share separates maths fine-tunes from random with size unresolved, and the reference comparison was never possible. It could not say that VPD fails to separate them, because that was not tested. Later GSM8K and RL readings would be description only.
  • D. Read the template fine-tune as it is, unmatched. This also reads two fine-tunes trained on the model's own GSM8K answers, with and without a filter for correct ones. About 2.3 hours, $0. No prediction made in advance could be scored. It fills in the table, but the template is about half the size of invented facts, so any gap between them could be size, and we would still have to choose between B and C afterwards. Fable recommended this on 2026-10-30, before the size problem was found.

Not recommended now: a new template recipe (the current one gained only 0.02 from step 20 to step 100, and relaxing the equal-size rule after seeing the data would change a rule set in advance); a new reference pair both on maths (about a day, $0 to $15), which fixes only the topic limit and makes sense only if A and B show something.

Recommendation: A, then B or C by its result. It is the cheapest option, and it is the only one that tests whether the last live reading measures anything but size. What does "elicitation" mean for the re-gating claim?

The question. The re-gating share r (the part of a fine-tune's gain recovered when only Γ's switching changes) and the behavioural label "reuses" (unseen keys gain at least half as much as trained ones; to be renamed "carries") have come apart: a new four-word rule carried to unseen keys (0.40 to 0.49, about half of copy's 0.8) yet r read 0.23 to 0.25, like a lookup, and at step 11 the label said "reuses" while r said new (E58).

The evidence. r follows whether the trained answer is one the original model already gives for that key (0.72 to 0.92 if so, 0.23 to 0.49 if not, eight maps), not carry. One cut on the model's own probability of the answer sorts all eight maps, so VPD has not yet shown anything the logits do not; no run settles which meaning is right.

Options. (i) "Elicitation" means the answer is one the model already gives: r is the right instrument, and the small-model answer is "yes, but so is the prior". (ii) It means an existing capability reused on new inputs: r is the wrong instrument, the line scores carry-reading ones (steering, E55's patch) against E55's labels, and r stays a reported column. Fable leans to keeping r only if (i) is meant. Granularity limit, kept here: a new circuit of old pieces would read as elicited, and whether this rule ran through old subcomponents was not measured.
