What should the Qwen example of "content the model lacks" be, so we have one case that is known by construction to be new?

The question. The Qwen comparison needs two training updates whose novelty is fixed by how they were built: one that only brings out something the model already does (the chat-format template), and one that teaches content the model does not have. The second is still unchosen. It must be decided by 2026-11-06 for the comparison's second column to exist by 2026-11-11. You asked why invented facts was settled on, so the options are laid out here.

Options. (A, recommended) Invented facts, the candidate in the 2026-10-28 plan. About 1,000 question-answer pairs about entities that do not exist, fine-tuned in, then graded on held-out rephrasings of the same questions. The model cannot have seen any of it, so the content is new by construction. Cost: a few dollars of training, a day to build and check the data. Limit: this is novelty of content, not of mechanism, and the domain (facts) differs from the maths fine-tune it is set beside. (B) A cipher or key-value table in the chat format, the large-model analogue of the 67M's key-value task (E42): the model must learn a lookup it never saw. Cost: similar. It keeps the format of the maths prompts, but what it teaches is a lookup rather than knowledge, and that is further from the use we care about. (C) Something you name. It runs whatever you describe, once it has a clear "model does not have this" test.

Recommendation: A. It is the only option whose novelty needs no argument, and its limit is stated: if the readings separate it from the template, that shows content novelty only.
