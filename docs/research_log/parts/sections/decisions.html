What was done. For each fine-tune of the 67M model we fitted the closest model that only turns the original's own parts up or down, and read how much of the fine-tune's answer it recovers on held-out rows (E282). What it means. As prototyped, the share cannot replace the kept share: on one narrow task, many small gains can rebuild an installed behaviour. The held-out rows reused the fitted cues, so whether the fit works on cues it never saw was not tested.

On real text, the write share over the needed set reads the new side higher, the reverse of what we filed.

What was done. The definition's reading, the write share over the needed set (how much of a fine-tune's change falls on the variables the task needs), was taken on the 67M model's real-text fine-tunes (E273). There are three pairs; each has one side built to need what the base model already holds and one built to need something new: topic label against coin label, lowercase against reversed word order, French against Yoruba. Three seeds each, each beside a random update of the same size. We had filed that the reuse side reads higher.

What we got. On both readable text pairs the new side reads higher, on all three seeds: lowercase 0.025 to 0.028 against reversed order 0.061 to 0.063; French 0.065 to 0.082 against Yoruba 0.123 to 0.149. Every fine-tune is above its random update (0.010 to 0.028). The label pair is unread: the topic-label fine-tune's needed set has only 3 to 6 variables, and the filed rule calls under 100 too small to read.

What it means so far. On real text this reading does not put the reuse side above its twin on either readable pair. Either the reading misorders them or the built labels are wrong at this size; nothing on this model says which. Output change (reversed order and Yoruba move the output more) matches the reversal within each pair but not across the text fine-tunes, so it is not established as the cause.
