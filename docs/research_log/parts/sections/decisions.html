Decides what part 2's definition does before the 2026-11-13 gate. Nothing runs until this is decided.

Built controls keep failing. Should the rewiring share be kept as a graded measure and tested on a difficulty ladder, or settled at the gate?

What the share is. The rewiring share is the part of a fine-tune's change in outputs that one affine map, written into the original model's activations at one site, can reproduce. Under the second wording, a share of 0.5 or more reads "a map was found".

What the evidence says.

  • It ranks well within pairs. In all five matched pairs, every reuse seed reads higher than every seed of its new twin. The latest run (E148, cell P1.14) read a fine-tune on the parity of a sum at 0.06 and 0.07 against 0.47 and 0.46 for the "last number is odd" control. A planted edit the map can reach exactly read 0.985, and one it cannot reach read about zero.
  • Its absolute scale does not hold up. A clean reuse control reads under 0.5, and the search reads only 0.84 on an exact target edit. So the 0.5 cut-off sits above four of the five reuse sides.
  • No built case can be clean under this wording. The wording counts something as already computed when an affine map can read it, so any built case is unreadable by such a map by construction. The check on the latest run said the same, so a low reading there may show only that.
  • The crosscoder papers offer no built case to recreate. They find directions in one model's activations that another lacks, not new computations, so reuse can show up as "new". Nobody has checked them on a fine-tune with a known label, and their authors read the clearest "new" latents (refusal, misalignment) as reuse.
  • Option a: a difficulty ladder, with the share read as graded (recommended). One new cell, $0, cluster debug, about 2 hours. Fine-tunes on the same layout in order of difficulty: the last number is odd, then the parity of a sum of two numbers, then of three, all read in one job between the planted ceiling and floor, at one site and at two. Beside each, a linear and a small non-linear probe of the original for the target. The prediction is that the rescaled share falls step by step and follows the non-linear probe, not only the linear one. If it holds, the definition becomes "how much of the change is reachable from the original's own computations, by a map of stated size": graded, reported within pairs and along ladders, with no absolute cut-off, and "built" is the low end where the non-linear probe also fails. If the share is flat, out of order, or only mirrors the linear probe, it adds nothing over a probe, which is the case for dropping it at the gate. Either way the 0.5 cut-off is set aside, which only you decide.
  • Option b: settle it at the gate with what we have. No run. The gate page already gives the five-pair ranking, the scale problem and the latest reading, and you choose there between graded, cut-off or drop.
  • Option c: drop the wording now and look for another definition before the gate. Free, but it throws away the one result that holds, the within-pair ranking.
  • Option d: recreate the crosscoder findings at scale. Not recommended as a source of truth, since there is no label to check against. A crosscoder scored on our labelled pairs is a fair baseline for part 3 (compare-lead).

Recommendation: option a, with the cut-off question put at the gate. It is the cheapest run that can tell whether the share measures something a probe does not. Nothing runs until you choose.
