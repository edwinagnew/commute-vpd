On Qwen3-1.7B the pass test cannot fail do-nothing, because Γ's masks follow the activations they are given. Take the masks from the original model, or keep them and add another control?

The pieces. The pieces are the decomposition's rank-one subcomponents, which together rebuild each weight matrix. Δ is the residual weights outside the pieces. Γ is the importance function: from the model's activations it picks which pieces are on for each input. Do-nothing is the control that leaves the whole fine-tuning update in Δ, so by definition it moves nothing onto the pieces. The halves are control (i) of the Qwen re-plan: the update moved onto the pieces Γ often switches on, or onto those it rarely does.

Evidence. E44 stopped because the pass test cannot fail do-nothing: with Γ reading the state's own activations, do-nothing sat 0.09 nats from the fine-tuned model against 1.42 for the original decomposition, keeping about 97% of the update where none was expected, as did both halves (E44). Two follow-up runs (Amendments 1 and 1b) asked why. Given a mix of two models' activations, Γ recovered none of the update; given one consistent model whose first 16 layers alone were fine-tuned, it recovered 90% of the update's effect, close to the 93% those layers make. So Γ's masks follow whatever activations they are given, and the test as built passes do-nothing for any update. These runs cannot say whether that is copying the output or responding to the change. For comparison, on the 67M model SGD's do-nothing also passes the adversarial check on four new blocks of text (it fails 0 of 4), while Adam's fails every block (E49).

Options. (1) Masks computed from the original model's activations for every state (reading B, set aside on 2026-10-19; Fable's option 1; E44 Amendment 2, about 35 min, $0 on an L40): do-nothing then reads 1.42 by definition and fails, as a control should. It gives up any credit for Γ following the changed activations, and it is blind to a split onto pieces Γ switches on only after the update. (2) Keep reading A (masks from each state's own activations) and add a different control. Fable's advice lists two: the cost of the masks (how many pieces are on and how strongly), or the decomposition's own training losses with Γ refit per state (about $10 a state); plus descriptive placement only (where the update lands, against a random change; $0, no pass line). Options 1 and the refit change your 2026-10-19 pass criterion.

Recommendation (the lead's, following Fable). Option 1. Its controls can fail: do-nothing must read 1.42, the upper half must read below the lower, and a random change matched in size about 1.42. Under reading A every split passes, so the line would move to descriptive placement.
