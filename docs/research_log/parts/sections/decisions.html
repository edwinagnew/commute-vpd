Soon
Commute line · open since 2026-10-23

Firm up teacher training on this model first, or take it straight to the larger model?

What it is about. Teacher training trains the existing subcomponents to copy a finished fine-tune's predictions, with Γ and Δ frozen. On three tasks on the small model it kept the behaviour and stayed faithful, even when trained on ordinary text alone (E34). The question is what the commute line does next.

What the evidence says. Strong, and firmer since 2026-10-26: the copies pass at two batch orders and under an adversary eight times longer, where five of the six fine-tunes they copy fail; a LoRA teacher is copied from the task's text. Still narrow: three tasks that each have a single trigger word, one model. On emoticon the copies learnt the behaviour with the trigger removed from their text and lose only a few hundredths when the partly-on positions go too, by a weight change that differs from the fine-tune's. One loose end: the longer adversary reads the fine-tunes far above the 20-step check at the same budget, cause unknown.

The options. (a) Settle the loose ends here first: locate the disagreement between the two adversaries, and copy from ordinary text with every trigger token removed; then the larger model. (b) Go straight to the larger model with a real post-trained sibling, and carry the loose ends there.

Why it matters. Under (a) a failure on the larger model can be told apart from a weakness already present here; under (b) the line reaches the question that matters for post-training monitoring sooner, at the cost of re-validating every check on a new decomposition with these doubts still open.

No rush
Commute line · open since 2026-10-21

Should the adversarial check be judged against a fixed line, or against leaving the decomposition alone?

What it is about. The success criterion (reading A) asks the new decomposition to pass VPD's checks on the updated model about as well as the old one does on the original. For the adversarial check we read a ratio: the adversary's damage on the new decomposition over its damage on the original. A ratio under 1.5 passes, with a margin for seed-to-seed noise.

The problem. Do-nothing keeps every subcomponent and puts the whole update into Δ, so it changes nothing that could make the subcomponents fragile. Yet it fails the 1.5 line on large Adam fine-tunes at step 10 (2.18 on emoticon), because the adversary can remove Δ, which now holds the update. So the line mixes two things: how much of the update sits outside the subcomponents, and whether the subcomponents are fragile.

The options. (a) Keep the fixed line, and report do-nothing beside each verdict, as now. (b) Count a state as passing when it is no worse than the same update's do-nothing. (c) Anchor on another baseline, such as a decomposition retrained on the updated model, which costs a full VPD run. Since E21–E22 the choice affects fewer verdicts. Small and later updates' do-nothing passes the line, so only large early updates are read differently.

Why it matters. Under (b), a state that fails the line by no more than its do-nothing does would pass. A state worse than its do-nothing still fails, like the least-squares split of the large fine-tune (5.4 against 2.18). That changes what counts as a map that commutes.
