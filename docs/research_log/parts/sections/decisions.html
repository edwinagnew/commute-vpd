What would it mean to understand re-gating? Four readings, one to pick

What it is about. Re-gating is the difference in Γ's importance values before and after a fine-tune. It is measured with the subcomponents held at their original values, to separate it from changes to the subcomponents themselves. Experiments that also changed the subcomponents found those changes carry under a tenth of the gain (E5) and barely move where the behaviour sits (E19). So every narrow fine-tune we have run shows up mostly in re-gating. The fixed-shift hypothesis, which described the shape of that difference on toy tasks, was de-prioritised on 2026-10-27. The question now is what the difference means for interpreting the update. There are four things that could mean, and they lead to different runs.

What we know.

  • The difference is real and specific. Which pieces switch on depends on the target far more than on how the update was made: a fine-tune, a one-matrix LoRA and component training switch on largely the same emoticon set (overlaps 0.82 and 0.64, E6; two seeds of one fine-tune reach 0.93), while a fine-tune to a different token at the same trigger overlaps it by only 0.02 to 0.05 (E6, E18). A random update switches on nothing.
  • It is partly the model's own machinery. The set overlaps the pieces the original model uses before the same token in ordinary text about ten times more than chance, but fewer than half of the set are such pieces (E18, E29).
  • It is only half the story. With the pieces untouched, the new values take the masked model about half-way to the target at the trigger; the switched-on set carries under half of that (E4, E6).
  • The fine-tuned model does use those pieces, but they also work without the fine-tune. Removing the switched-on set from the fine-tuned weights takes away a third to a half of the gain on two of three tasks, far more than random sets; removing it from the original model already takes away a sixth to a quarter, so how much of the fine-tune runs through it is not yet measured (E38).
  • The share on its own says little. A fine-tune that learned nothing read the same re-gating share as one that learned (E36), because Γ is the only part of the decomposition that depends on context.

The biggest uncertainties.

  • How much of the fine-tune acts through those pieces? First tested in the real model (E38), but the pieces already push toward the target in the original model, so the share is not yet separated; E38 Amendment 2 is running to separate it.
  • What moved to make Γ switch them on? The pieces barely change, so the cause must be a shift in the residual stream at the trigger. Nobody has measured where it moved.
  • Would a Γ fitted to the new model say the same? Unknown; the refit's own control fails (E15).
  • Everything rests on one 67M model, ten-step fine-tunes and trigger tasks.

The readings. The user chose on 2026-10-27 to run, in order, a recruitment test, a routing trace and a piece census; all three ran as E38. The fourth reading, whether a Γ fitted to the new model would say the same, is parked (blocked on E15's refit control).

Recommendation. Wait for E38 Amendment 2, which scores how much more of the fine-tune the switched-on set carries than random sets (reports/plans/2026-10-27_plan_e38-amend2.md), then pick the reading. Plan: reports/plans/2026-10-27_plan_regating-recruitment.md.

Why it matters. This sets what the write-up of a fine-tune will say: a piece census, a routing trace from the update to the pieces, or both. It also decides whether the re-gating line continues at all.

Should the adversarial check be judged against a fixed line, or against leaving the decomposition alone?

What it is about. The success criterion (reading A) asks the new decomposition to pass VPD's checks on the updated model about as well as the old one does on the original. For the adversarial check we read a ratio: the adversary's damage on the new decomposition over its damage on the original. A ratio under 1.5 passes, with a margin for seed-to-seed noise.

The problem. Do-nothing keeps every subcomponent and puts the whole update into Δ, so it changes nothing that could make the subcomponents fragile. Yet it fails the 1.5 line on large Adam fine-tunes at step 10 (2.18 on emoticon), because the adversary can remove Δ, which now holds the update. So the line mixes two things: how much of the update sits outside the subcomponents, and whether the subcomponents are fragile.

The options. (a) Keep the fixed line, and report do-nothing beside each verdict, as now. (b) Count a state as passing when it is no worse than the same update's do-nothing. (c) Anchor on another baseline, such as a decomposition retrained on the updated model, which costs a full VPD run. Since E21–E22 the choice affects fewer verdicts. Small and later updates' do-nothing passes the line, so only large early updates are read differently.

Why it matters. Under (b), a state that fails the line by no more than its do-nothing does would pass. A state worse than its do-nothing still fails, like the least-squares split of the large fine-tune (5.4 against 2.18). That changes what counts as a map that commutes.
