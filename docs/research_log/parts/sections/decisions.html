Which test should judge whether a method keeps the Qwen decomposition faithful after a training update?

What this is about. D1 is the first of three deliverables due 2026-11-11: a method that takes a real training update of Qwen3-1.7B and places it on the decomposition's subcomponents so the decomposition still explains the updated model, judged by a test that can fail a bad placement. The methods exist (least-squares split, adjoint split, teacher training). The test is what is missing.

The observation. The test runs the model with only the subcomponents Γ switches on and compares it with the fine-tuned model. A test is only useful if the right answer can pass it, so we add the whole update, unmasked, and read that. With the original model's masks, it closes only 0.03 to 0.14 of the KL gap on the four saved updates (E68). With masks taken from the fine-tuned model and held fixed, on the step-10 update (E72), it keeps 0.20 of the next-token gain, against 0.24 for adding nothing; the least-squares and adjoint splits read 0.23 each, and a random change 0.16. So these scores say nothing yet against the splits: the whole update does no better than they do. The larger updates are being read.

What this does not say. Nothing here shows the least-squares split, the adjoint split or teacher training to be bad on Qwen. Least squares leaves 0.36 of the step-10 update in Δ, the weights outside the subcomponents, and the adjoint split leaves Δ almost untouched but is not exact. Teacher training has only been rehearsed on Qwen, and that recipe has no masks in its loss, so judging it under masks needs a code change and a review.

Options. (A) Keep the masked test on updates where the whole update shows room. Runs on saved states, $0, tonight's job gives the answer. Tells whether a split does better than doing nothing; next, the splits are compared on those updates. (B) Drop the masks: add the placed part of the update to the normal model and see whether it carries the gain. $0, tonight's job gives it. Tells whether the update lives in the subcomponents, says nothing about whether the masked decomposition stays faithful. (C) Report that masked tests cannot see an update this small on this model, with these numbers, and judge teacher training on behaviour kept, the adversary and Δ. Needs the code change above, its own plan and review.

What should the Qwen example of "content the model lacks" be, so we have one case that is known by construction to be new?

The question. The Qwen comparison needs two training updates whose novelty is fixed by how they were built: one that only brings out something the model already does (the chat-format template), and one that teaches content the model does not have. The second is still unchosen. It must be decided by 2026-11-06 for the comparison's second column to exist by 2026-11-11. You asked why invented facts was settled on, so the options are laid out here.

Options. (A, recommended) Invented facts, the candidate in the 2026-10-28 plan. About 1,000 question-answer pairs about entities that do not exist, fine-tuned in, then graded on held-out rephrasings of the same questions. The model cannot have seen any of it, so the content is new by construction. Cost: a few dollars of training, a day to build and check the data. Recommendation: A. It is the only option whose novelty needs no argument, and its limit is stated: if the readings separate it from the template, that shows content novelty only.
