What would it mean to understand re-gating? Four readings, one to pick

What it is about. Re-gating is the difference in Γ's importance values before and after a fine-tune. It is measured with the subcomponents held at their original values, to separate it from changes to the subcomponents themselves. Experiments that also changed the subcomponents found those changes carry under a tenth of the gain (E5) and barely move where the behaviour sits (E19). So every narrow fine-tune we have run shows up mostly in re-gating. The fixed-shift hypothesis, which described the shape of that difference on toy tasks, was de-prioritised on 2026-10-27. The question now is what the difference means for interpreting the update. There are four things that could mean, and they lead to different runs.

What we know.

  • The difference is real and specific. Which pieces switch on depends on the target far more than on how the update was made. On emoticon, a fine-tune switches on largely the same set as a one-matrix LoRA and as component training, overlapping them by 0.82 and 0.64 (E6). Two seeds of one fine-tune overlap by 0.93, which is the ceiling. A fine-tune to a different token at the same trigger overlaps it by only 0.04 to 0.05 on emoticon (E6) and 0.02 to 0.05 on three tasks (E18). A random update switches on nothing (E6, E18).
  • It is partly the model's own machinery. The switched-on set overlaps the pieces the original model uses before the same token in ordinary text, about ten times more than chance. But fewer than half of the set are such pieces (E18, E29).
  • It is only half the story. With the pieces untouched, the new values take the masked model about half-way to the target at the trigger. The switched-on set carries under half of that; the rest is many small moves of other values (E4, E6).
  • The share on its own says little. A fine-tune that learned nothing read the same re-gating share as one that learned (E36). A small, spread-out update has to read mostly as re-gating, because Γ is the only part of the decomposition that depends on context.

The biggest uncertainties.

  • Does the fine-tuned model act through those pieces? Never tested in the real model. Every number so far is read in the masked model, old pieces with new values, which is not a decomposition of the fine-tuned model.
  • What moved to make Γ switch them on? The pieces barely change, so the cause must be a shift in the residual stream at the trigger. Nobody has measured where it moved.
  • Would a Γ fitted to the new model say the same? Unknown; the refit's own control fails (E15).
  • Everything rests on one 67M model, ten-step fine-tunes and trigger tasks.

The readings.

  1. Recruitment test. Remove the switched-on pieces from the fine-tuned weights, with no Γ involved, and see whether the behaviour dies. For: decides whether there is anything to interpret; minutes on saved states. Against: on its own it says the pieces matter, not what the update did; a negative leaves the line without an object.
  2. Routing trace. Does the trigger's residual stream move toward the contexts where the model already predicts the target, and which upstream pieces' outputs moved it. For: the causal story from update to gates, the "reveal" reading made concrete. Against: the hardest; Adam's update is spread over every piece, so the upstream step may name no piece in particular.
  3. Piece census. Say what each switched-on piece does in the original model, from the ordinary-text positions where Γ turns it on. For: the only reading that produces an interpretation a reader can check, and it costs only forward passes. Against: meaningless if the recruitment test fails; the set is large and most of it carries little, so the labels may describe a crowd.
  4. Refit agreement. Parked by the user, 2026-10-27. Would a Γ fitted to the new model say the same? For: the direct test of the frozen reading; against: blocked on the refit control (E15).

Recommendation. Decided by the user on 2026-10-27: recruitment test, then routing trace, then piece census; refit agreement parked. Plan: reports/plans/2026-10-27_plan_regating-recruitment.md (E38). If the recruitment test fails, re-gating is Γ's reading and not the model's, and the budget goes to the commute line.

Why it matters. This sets what the write-up of a fine-tune will say: a piece census, a routing trace from the update to the pieces, or both. It also decides whether the re-gating line continues at all.

Commute line · open since 2026-10-21

Should the adversarial check be judged against a fixed line, or against leaving the decomposition alone?

What it is about. The success criterion (reading A) asks the new decomposition to pass VPD's checks on the updated model about as well as the old one does on the original. For the adversarial check we read a ratio: the adversary's damage on the new decomposition over its damage on the original. A ratio under 1.5 passes, with a margin for seed-to-seed noise.

The problem. Do-nothing keeps every subcomponent and puts the whole update into Δ, so it changes nothing that could make the subcomponents fragile. Yet it fails the 1.5 line on large Adam fine-tunes at step 10 (2.18 on emoticon), because the adversary can remove Δ, which now holds the update. So the line mixes two things: how much of the update sits outside the subcomponents, and whether the subcomponents are fragile.

The options. (a) Keep the fixed line, and report do-nothing beside each verdict, as now. (b) Count a state as passing when it is no worse than the same update's do-nothing. (c) Anchor on another baseline, such as a decomposition retrained on the updated model, which costs a full VPD run. Since E21–E22 the choice affects fewer verdicts. Small and later updates' do-nothing passes the line, so only large early updates are read differently.

Why it matters. Under (b), a state that fails the line by no more than its do-nothing does would pass. A state worse than its do-nothing still fails, like the least-squares split of the large fine-tune (5.4 against 2.18). That changes what counts as a map that commutes.
