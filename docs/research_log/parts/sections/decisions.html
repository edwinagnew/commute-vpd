Question for you · definition-lead · new 2026-11-19

When we compare a part's mechanism in the fine-tune against the base model, on which inputs?

Due to run: the hand-built-edit run (E214, due 11-20); it reads either way and reports the other as a side reading.

Measured so far. On 20 trained fine-tunes, comparing mechanisms by squared cosine over every position, any circuit of 128 or more neurons reads a reuse score of 0.99 or higher (E213). That is not what the definition intends: at the neuron level nothing separates reuse from new. Not yet measured: whether the score separates them when the answer is known by construction. E214 does that on hand-built edits, at four levels (neurons and heads; heads and MLP layers; MLP and attention layers; blocks).

The catch. Each hand-built edit changes a part's behaviour at only 1 of the 41 positions in a window, where the new cue is. So the choice of inputs decides how much of the edit the comparison can see.

Options. (a) Every position the part runs at in the data, as E208 did and as "parent settings that occur" reads: the edit's change is diluted about 40-fold, with reuse-against-new gaps of about 0.001 to 0.01, so it may fall under the noise floor. (b) Only the positions where the behaviour is read: gaps about 40 times larger, but a change elsewhere in the window goes unseen. (c) Weight positions by how much each moves the behaviour: the most informative, but a change to the definition beyond its current sentence, which only you make. Recommendation: (a), with (b) as the side reading. It keeps the definition as written and E214 directly comparable with E208 and E213.
