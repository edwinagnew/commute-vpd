11What did not work, and mistakes made

Measurements that were blind or wrong
  • A faithfulness check that let Δ absorb the update (E1): the trivial map passed. Δ is frozen in every later map.
  • Frobenius measures (E1, E2): the original weights fail them too. Replaced by input-weighted versions.
  • A null that wins by expressivity (E1): ten random pieces with free write vectors realise a one-logit push.
  • Pile-only faithfulness for two rounds: about three trigger positions in 131,000 tokens. Led to the task-local harness and the "blind to" rule.
  • No noise floor until E4; the 1.5 line was filed without one, and the spread turned out GPU-dependent.
  • A trigger-local control with no power (E4 as filed); fixed by reading under the original masks.
  • Predictions left filed on a metric dominated by re-switching (E5): the amendment said so and did not withdraw them.
  • Task-local adversaries that saturate (E5): one shared mask over alike windows.
  • The sparsity loss as a fifth check (E10, E11): reads the weights only; its rule used block spread of a deterministic quantity as a floor.
  • An identity metric with no positive control (E3).
  • An adversary never checked against its budget, then checked with the wrong step size (every adversarial verdict, E1 on): VPD's 20-step search was read as a worst case without a budget check. When E20's second amendment ran it longer it kept the step at 0.1, against the paper's rule that steps times step size stay near 2, and its runaway climb was read as "far from converged" and flagged on 18 claim rows for a day. With the rule the verdicts held to 160 steps (E20, third amendment). Read the method's own recipe before scaling a knob.
  • A cancellation control that cancelled in weight space only (E17): 64 times the change energy, nearly inert on Pile; the half-revert predictions went unscored. A gate also caught its own bug: the reference forward of the hook-identity check ran with the hooks live and reverted twice (0.0085 nats), fixed before any state was read.
Premises that were wrong
  • Per-matrix locality of Γ (E6, parts of E5): Γ is one transformer over all 24 matrices' inputs.
  • "A LoRA is easier to decompose" (E7): its read side is random by construction and stays so.
  • The paper's matrix as the LoRA site (E7): the decomposition reads 21% of its input energy.
  • The first component-training learning-rate grid (E2): steps 3 to 100 times the fine-tune's, adversarial ratio 41; redone at matched size.
  • Comparing adversarial ratios across GPUs (E7 vs E2): a 35% miss that was the card.
  • A warm-started Γ refit at the released starting rate is harmless (E15): the control took the unchanged model's adversarial ratio to 2.6 to 6.3, and it ran second, after the split arm. Rule: a refit protocol's control on the unchanged model runs first and alone, before any state arm.
  • "The adversarial failure is cancellation among the changes" (A1, E15 stage 0, the 2026-10-21 direction review): E17 found the changes add coherently and the part of them the adversary removes carries at most 7% of its excess. The selective-mask signature was real; its cause is not the original subcomponents either (E20) but the interaction between the adversary's removal of the alive and the dead subcomponents (E20's amendment).
  • "The adversary's excess is the original subcomponents the updated model leans on" (E17's remainder, written as inferred into Q6, the main-line claim and the claims table): E20 measured it and found none of the excess there on three of four failing states. Rule kept: a remainder is not a measurement, and a claim built on one is marked inferred until measured.
  • Plain SGD's learning-rate grid (E4): its rates ran from 0.3 to 30, and the smallest already overshot on the first step. Read as "no SGD rate works", it became the reason every later component-training run used Adam, with no decision ever written down, until a grid from 0.001 to 0.1 found SGD learning within the limit (E4's card). So every component-training failure since is Adam's, not yet the operation's. The second too-high grid after E2's. Rule: a grid whose lowest value overshoots is extended down before "no rate works" is written, and a failed precondition is not reused as a premise without a decision row.

Operational
  • E7's pod terminated before the final file was downloaded; three arms rebuilt from the log. Rule: download each arm as written, verify checksums, then terminate.
  • The ladder left 1.4 GB for one download; a RunPod resume moved the SSH port mid-transfer; the states were lost. Rule: save only what the analysis reads, in small dtypes.
  • Downloading a directory nested it.
  • E15's budget was set from the plan's arithmetic and a smoke benchmark at batch 4 (0.37 s per step); the full batch took 2.29 s, six times more, so one refit of six fit the cap. Rule: the ten-step benchmark that sets the budget runs at the full batch, in prep, before the first refit starts, and the cap is confirmed from it.
  • E2's component-training states never saved; E5's for emoticon only. Rule: every trained state is written at the save steps.
  • A null target mis-selected by an argmin over an all-infinite tensor (E6).
  • Write-ups the user called unreadable ("I have no idea what is going on"). Led to the write-up skill; the 2026-10-20 review then found overclaims in three reports written under the old habits.

Rule: when the user's words and a file's definition disagree, flag it and get explicit acknowledgement; never pick a side silently.
