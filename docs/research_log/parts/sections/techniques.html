How the numbers are made

Techniques

The decomposition and the masked model

W = Σc uc vcT + Δ, W(m) = Σc mc uc vcT + mΔ Δ

vc is a piece's read vector (the input direction it responds to), uc its write vector (what it adds to the output). A masked model runs the forward pass with W(m) in place of W: one mask mc ∈ [0, 1] per piece and one mΔ ∈ [0, 1] for the residual, chosen afresh at every token position. Each check is a choice of m. The CI-masked model sets mc = gc(x), the importance value Γ gives piece c on input x, and mΔ = 0. The unmasked model sets every mc = 1 and mΔ = 0. The stochastic check draws each mc uniformly from [gc(x), 1], with Δ under its own random mask. The adversarial check lets gradient ascent choose m to do the most damage. Faithful means the masked model reproduces the full model's output distribution: its KL to the full model is within the floor measured at the unchanged model.

The operations, with their equations

Drawn in the Overview. Notation shared by the cards, for one weight matrix:

SymbolMeaning
W = U Vᵀ + Δthe weights: the sum of the alive pieces plus the residual Δ, the part the pieces do not carry
U, Vthe alive pieces' write and read vectors, one column per piece; the pieces are frozen in shape, only these vectors may move
δWa finished update to W, from a fine-tune or a LoRA
dU, dVwhat a map adds to U and V; the weights then change by dU Vᵀ + U dVᵀ, to first order
K(M) = UUᵀM + MVVᵀthe weight change from moving the pieces by dU = M V, dV = Mᵀ U: the part of M the pieces can express, at a size that is not M's. M is the gradient in component training and δW in the adjoint splits. One plain-SGD step of component training changes the weights by −ηK(G), which is the adjoint split of the fine-tune's own step −ηG; the two differ only over a run. The adjoint split applies K once, open loop, to an update made along the fine-tune's path, so what K distorts stays; component training applies K to each step's gradient at its own current weights, so later steps correct it. Under Adam, component training's step is not a multiple of K(G) at all.
ΓVPD's importance network, which switches pieces on per input; frozen under every map below
Reference
Do-nothing

W′ = U Vᵀ + (Δ + δW)

What it does. The whole update goes into the residual; pieces and Γ are untouched.

Status. The yardstick every map is compared with, not a candidate: it puts the whole update in Δ, which the pass criterion rules out. On large updates it also fails VPD's adversarial check, because the masked models leave Δ out, and with it the whole update (E2, E11).

Discarded
Least-squares split

min ‖dU‖² + ‖dV‖² s.t. dU Vᵀ + U dVᵀ ≈ δW

What it does. Splits a finished update over the pieces as the least-norm first-order change. The obstruction (I − PU) δW (I − PV) cannot be expressed and goes to Δ, with a small second-order term, so W′ equals the fine-tuned weights exactly.

Status. Discarded as a map: it keeps the behaviour only by leaving a large part of the update in Δ, a quarter or more even on small fine-tunes, and on the large fine-tune it fails the adversarial check (E16, E21, E22). Still run as a comparison.

Scored once
Adjoint split

dU = δW V, dV = δWᵀ U

What it does. Puts a finished update onto the pieces with no solve: the move one gradient step on the pieces would make if δW were the gradient. The weights change by K(δW), and Δ stays fixed. That move points along what the pieces can express, but its size is not δW's, because the pieces overlap and VPD leaves their vectors' lengths free.

Status. Scored once, as E31's Amendment 3 on three emoticon fine-tunes. It keeps the whole behaviour where the scaled version loses it, but changes the model's other predictions 1.3 to 32 times as much as the fine-tune itself, and fails the adversarial check on the large update. So the fitted c of the next card trades behaviour for harmlessness.

Best split
Scaled adjoint split

dU = c δW V, dV = c δWᵀ U
c = argminc Ex ‖(c K(δW) − δW) x‖²

What it does. The adjoint split (previous card), with its move multiplied by one number c per matrix, so the weights change by c K(δW).

Why c is needed. K(δW) points along what the pieces can express, but its length is wrong: with orthonormal pieces and an update wholly inside their spans, each of the two terms returns δW, so K(δW) = 2δW, and overlap and VPD's free vector lengths change it further, differently per matrix.

How c is chosen. Least squares on one number, c = ⟨K(δW), δW⟩ / ⟨K(δW), K(δW)⟩ over the matrix's real inputs x (the fine-tune's training text); in the clean case c = ½, and the fitted c ranges from 0.03 to 2.5, mostly below 1. One number fixes only the length: the direction of K(δW) still differs from δW, so the state is a different model from the fine-tuned one.

Status. The best of the splits: the only one that stays faithful with Δ untouched at every step and size tried. But it loses the behaviour as the update grows: on the large fine-tune target probability falls from 0.93 at step 10 to 0.12 by step 1000 (E16), and on the word tasks it keeps little (E31, E34). Every "adjoint split" result in the experiment cards is this version.

Works
Component training

δWstep = −η K(G)

What it does. One gradient step on the pieces' vectors moves the weights by the part of the gradient G the pieces can express. Repeated every step, with Γ and Δ frozen. The equation is the SGD step; most runs used Adam. Variants: rank-r offsets, one matrix, the top few pieces, and only the pieces Γ keeps off (the gated-off control).

Status. Works, but is not a map of a finished update: it trains the pieces in place of the fine-tune. With Adam it keeps the behaviour and faithfulness with Δ untouched to step 1000 on emoticon, and passes most tasks once its early damage to the rest of the model fades (E16, E21). Teacher training turns it into a map.

Best
Teacher training

mindU, dV Σt wt KL(pT ‖ pW′)
T = W₀ + δW, W′ = (U + dU)(V + dV)ᵀ + Δ

What it does. Component training with a distillation loss (the user's technique, 2026-10-23). The teacher T is the fine-tuned model, frozen. The student is the decomposition: its alive pieces' vectors move by dU, dV from zero, with Γ and Δ frozen. The loss is the KL from the teacher's next-token distribution to the student's at each position t of some text. It needs only the weights and text: no labels, no target token. The text is the task's own windows, where wt makes the trigger weigh as much as all other positions together, or plain Pile rows with every wt equal.

Status. The best map so far: the first map of a finished update that keeps the behaviour, faithfulness and Δ together, on three tasks and without labels (E34). Trained on ordinary text with the triggers removed, it still learns the emoticon behaviour, by a weight change unlike the fine-tune's. It holds at two batch orders and under an adversary eight times longer, and copies a LoRA from the task's text. On a fine-tune with no trigger it keeps the behaviour but fails the adversarial check, also trained four times longer or from a passing start (E37). Not yet tested: a LoRA from ordinary text alone, the larger model.

Discarded
Rescaling split

δW ≈ Σc αc uc vcᵀ

What it does. Rescales the existing pieces by a least-squares fit; the pieces' directions never move.

Status. Discarded after E1: almost none of a real update lies in the span of the rescaled pieces, 0.06% against 0.035% for a random direction. Applied as a state it keeps none of the behaviour (E16).

Where an update can land: four readings

The Overview's three arrows make two axes. Rows: do the moved subcomponents carry the gain where Γ uses them, or are the subcomponents Γ uses as they were, so that the gain sits elsewhere? Columns: do the masks Γ gives the subcomponents stay as before, or change? Γ is frozen, but it reads the updated model's activations, so its masks can change whichever row the gain took. The four cells are four readings of what post-training does to a mechanism, and each is measured by the route-split share named in it (two shares for create). The rows are not where an operation puts the update: do-nothing leaves every vector alone and can only land in the lower row, while the splits and component training move the subcomponents, and the route split then reports which cells carry the gain.

This grid is one way of operationalising the re-weighting hypothesis, fixed on 2026-10-20 so that the readings have names; it may be adjusted as the definition firms up (the work-in-progress box in section {{n:overview}}).

Where the data have landed so far: re-weight for behaviours the model already has, common next words and copying (E5, E12); create for the random-mapping cipher (E10, E12) and in part for the rarest next words (E5's figure); strengthen about a tenth or less under every operation route-split so far (E16, second figure; E7 the highest); re-wire near zero everywhere (too thin to see in E5's figure; reports/analyses/2026-10-20_analysis_new-vs-existing-signatures.md). The Overview's E12 figure shows re-weight and create side by side.

The route split

After an update, the model puts more log-odds on the target token at the trigger. The route split asks which part of the decomposition that gain runs through. Two things can change under a map. The pieces' vectors move (the mapped pieces C1 against the original C0). And the masks change, because Γ, frozen as it is, reads the updated model's activations and so gives different importance values (g1 against g0). Two more places sit outside the CI-masked model altogether: the pieces Γ keeps switched off, and Δ.

So the target's log-odds is measured on the same held-out windows under six models: original pieces with the old masks, moved pieces with the old masks, original pieces with the new masks, moved pieces with the new masks, every piece switched fully on, and the full model (the last two before and after the update). Write L(C, g) for the CI-masked model built from pieces C with masks g. The differences between neighbours in that list, divided by the total gain, are five shares that sum exactly to 1. A large direct share means the moved pieces themselves carry the behaviour. A large re-switching share means the behaviour was already in the pieces and Γ now switches it on. The last two shares mean it lives outside what the CI-masked model keeps. Under the current criterion the split describes mechanism and does not decide a pass.

Outside share

How much of an update the pieces cannot take: the part of δW that reads outside the pieces' read span and writes outside their write span, (I − PU) δW (I − PV), which has to go to Δ. Measured by its effect δW x on real inputs, not by raw entries.

Reported as share ÷ isotropic null (the same share for a random update of the same size): 1 is no better aligned than noise, below 1 is aligned. From E14 it is split into four blocks (inside both, write side only, read side only, outside both) that sum to one (E14's figure shows only the read and write sides; the four blocks are in reports/analyses/2026-10-20_analysis_lora-split.md).

Switched-on set

Alive pieces whose mean importance at the trigger rises by more than 0.3 when Γ reads the updated activations. Compared by Jaccard overlap against a seed floor; tested by recovery, the share of the re-switching effect obtained when only that set takes the new masks.

Floors and controls

Every ratio has a floor measured at the unchanged model in the same job. Every plan names what each metric is blind to and a control that must fail: gated-off component training for the route split (must read d = 0), the least-squares split for the Pile adversary (must fail for a fine-tune), do-nothing under the original masks for trigger-local faithfulness.
