How the numbers are made

Techniques

The decomposition and the masked model

W = Σc uc vcT + Δ, W(m) = Σc mc uc vcT + mΔ Δ

Each subcomponent c reads along vc and writes along uc; Δ is the part of the matrix they do not cover. A masked model runs with W(m) in place of W, one mask per subcomponent and one for Δ, each in [0, 1], chosen afresh at every position. The CI-masked model sets each mc to the importance value Γ gives c on that input, with Δ out. The stochastic check draws each mask between its importance value and 1. The adversarial check lets a 20-step search choose the masks that do the most damage on Pile text, read as a ratio to the unchanged model. Faithful means these masked models stay as close to the full model as they do at the unchanged model.

Notation

SymbolMeaning, for one weight matrix
U, Vthe alive subcomponents' write and read vectors as columns, so W = U Vᵀ + Δ; only these vectors may move
δWa finished update, from a fine-tune or a LoRA; W₀ is the unchanged model
dU, dVwhat a map adds to U and V; the weights change by dU Vᵀ + U dVᵀ, to first order
K(M)UUᵀM + MVVᵀ: the weight change from moving the subcomponents along M, the part of M they can express, at a size that is not M's (glossary)
ΓVPD's importance network, its parameters frozen under every map except the Γ refit. The two models' gates differ (below the table)

Two gate variants. On the 67M (the paper's VPD) Γ reads only the model's activations, so a moved read vector cannot change a gate. On Qwen (the newer VPD) each gate also adds a readout of that subcomponent's own activation, which depends on its read vector, so a moved read vector changes its own gate even with Γ's parameters frozen. A split's moved V crosses 0.14% of the on positions on gold and 2.3% on the Gaussian change; the direct share moves at most 0.0022 (one row set, two states).

The operations

Reference
Do-nothing

W′ = U Vᵀ + (Δ + δW)

The whole update goes into Δ; the subcomponents and Γ are untouched. The yardstick, not a candidate. It fails the adversarial check on the large Adam fine-tune and on every broad fine-tune read on Pile rows 0-255, one an SGD fine-tune smaller than a passing emoticon one, so size alone does not decide it (E22, E39).

Discarded
Four discarded splits
  • Rescaling (δW ≈ Σ αc uc vcᵀ, directions never move): almost none of a real update lies in that span (E1); as a state it keeps none of the behaviour (E16).
  • Least squares (the least-norm first-order move; what the subcomponents cannot express goes to Δ): Δ keeps a quarter to a half of the update (E22); it fails the check on the large Adam fine-tune and the cipher (E10, E21).
  • Step split (the scaled adjoint split at every step): by step 1000 Δ holds more than the update itself, and the large path fails the check (E19, E25).
  • Adjoint, unscaled (dU = δW V, dV = δWᵀ U, Δ fixed): keeps the whole behaviour but changes other predictions 1.3 to 32 times as much as the fine-tune, and fails on the large update (E31).
No answer
Changing Γ

Changes Γ's importance values instead of the subcomponents: the refit trains Γ on a mapped state; the fixed shift applies one change to every input. The refit's control drifted off the floor, so no answer yet (E15). The fixed shift recovers part of the switching and fails the adversarial check (E30).

Best split
Adjoint split, scaled

dU = c δW V, dV = c δWᵀ U, c = argminc Ex ‖(c K(δW) − δW) x‖²

What it does. The unscaled split with its move multiplied by one number c per matrix, fitted on the task's inputs; c runs from 0.03 to 2.5, mostly below 1.

Status. Faithful with Δ untouched in every state read, but it keeps less of the behaviour as the update grows (E16, E31); on broad fine-tunes it keeps most and passes (E39). Experiment cards mean this version unless they say unscaled.

Needs labels
Component training

δWstep = −η K(G)

What it does. Trains the alive subcomponents' vectors on the fine-tune's own loss, Γ and Δ frozen, in place of the fine-tune. Variants: rank-8 offsets, the top few subcomponents, and a control that moves only those Γ keeps off.

Status. With Adam it keeps the behaviour and passes from step 100 on most tasks tried, after early damage, but never cleanly on the rare target (E21, E26); with plain SGD it fails late. It needs labels and is not a map of a finished update.

Best map
Teacher training

mindU, dV Σt wt KL(pT ‖ pW′), T = W₀ + δW, W′ = (U + dU)(V + dV)ᵀ + Δ

What it does. Component training whose loss is the fine-tuned model's predictions, on the task's text with the trigger weighted up, or on plain Pile text; no labels (the user's technique, 2026-10-23).

Status. The only map of a finished update that keeps the behaviour, passes the check and leaves Δ alone, on narrow fine-tunes (E34, E42); weakest on the earliest update (E41), and it fails on broad full-weight fine-tunes (E37, E39). Adam at 1e-5 (decided 2026-10-28).

Where the gain sits: four readings and the route split

The route split does not split the weights. It splits a change in behaviour: how much more the fine-tuned model predicts the token it was trained to predict, its target. Score any model by its log-odds of the target, at the position where the fine-tune trained it, averaged over held-out inputs. The gain is the fine-tuned model's score minus the original's. Log-odds are not additive in the weights, so a share of the gain is not a share of the update; the outside share (glossary) measures the update itself.

Let W0 and W1 be the original and fine-tuned weights, C0 and C1 their subcomponents (C1 can come from any technique), and g0 and g1 the gates Γ computes from the activations of W0 and W1. F0 and F1 score the full models W0 and W1. S0 and S1 score the subcomponents alone, C0 and C1, with Δ left out and every gate open. Mab scores the masked subcomponents, Ca under gates gb, also without Δ. The gain is F1 − F0, and it splits exactly into five shares:

  • Residual share eΔ = [(F1 − S1) − (F0 − S0)] / gain. F − S is what Δ adds to the score, so this is how much more the update relies on Δ.
  • Masked-off share egate = [(S1 − M11) − (S0 − M00)] / gain. S − M is what opening the closed gates adds, so this is gain in subcomponents that Γ keeps off.
  • The rest, (M11 − M00) / gain, is the gain the masked model shows. Either the subcomponents or the gates can cause it:
    • Direct share d = (M10 − M00) / gain: new subcomponents, old gates.
    • Re-switching share r = (M01 − M00) / gain: old subcomponents, new gates.
    • Interaction share i = (M11 − M10 − M01 + M00) / gain: what the two do only together.

The re-switching share r is the key quantity (the re-gating or re-weight share). Keep C0 and change only its gates, from g0 to g1: M01 − M00 is what that alone adds to the masked score, and r is its fraction of the real gain. It never uses C1, so no technique changes it. A high r suggests the fine-tune reuses existing subcomponents that Γ now switches on (section {{n:overview}}), subject to the cautions below. r is about 0.8 on copy tasks; on random maps egate is about a half (E12).

A share can read above 1 or below 0. The stand-in ratio on later cards is (M11 − M00) / (S1 − S0): the masked gain over the gain of the subcomponents with every gate open.

Γ's masks as beforeΓ's masks changed

The shares describe mechanism and do not decide a pass. So far, behaviours the model already has read as re-weight and the cipher as create; strengthen is about a tenth or less and re-wire near zero (E5, E12, E16). The shares are read in VPD's stand-in, which the fine-tuned model does not contain. r reads the same for a fine-tune that learned nothing (E36). And r treats the unchanged model's masked error, M00 − S0, as constant though it differs by target, so comparing r across targets is unsafe where that error is large against the gain (E53, E58).

What the comparison shows

No operation leaves Δ untouched and also reproduces the fine-tuned weights; the criterion is only that Δ may not absorb the update. Only component training and teacher training keep the behaviour and pass the adversarial check with Δ untouched; component training needs labels, so only teacher training maps a finished update. The scaled adjoint split loses behaviour as the update grows; unscaled, it damages the rest of the model.

Teacher training's known failures are broad full-weight fine-tunes and plain SGD at rate 0.03 run to step 1000 (E34 Amendment 6; the six smaller cells pass on both teachers, E47); the rare target is not yet placed.

Gaps
  • Early updates: the step-10 reading sits at the pass line; short links along one emoticon fine-tune passed (E41); other tasks and a second seed are untested.
  • Without task data: plain text, plain SGD and the rare target are read (E34); two of three LoRA copies from plain text had not finished learning.
  • Broad updates: on eight blocks of Pile rows, Adam's teacher-trained decomposition fails every one and the LoRA's passes every new one (E39, E49). The check had no power on SGD there (plain SGD's whole update left in Δ passes too), so SGD stays unresolved (E49).
  • The larger model: only do-nothing has been read there (E40); simple splits of E40's update are planned (E44).
