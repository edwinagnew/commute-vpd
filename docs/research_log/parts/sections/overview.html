Overview

Does post-training create new mechanisms, or draw from existing ones? A common assumption is that pretraining builds the mechanisms and post-training only re-weights them. The evidence for that is behavioural or activation-based. This project looks where post-training actually acts: in the weights.

A parameter decomposition (VPD) writes each weight matrix as a sum of rank-one subcomponents, each meant to be one piece of mechanism, plus a residual Δ, with an importance function Γ saying which pieces matter on each input. A post-training update is nothing but a change to the weights. So: does the update land on the existing subcomponents, or outside them?

Work in progress: what "re-weighting" means

The hypothesis is usually stated informally: post-training re-weights mechanisms pretraining built, rather than creating its own. A precise version is not written down yet. What counts as a mechanism, and what counts as re-weighting one in VPD terms (a strengthened piece, a re-switched one, a new one) is open. The grid in section {{n:techniques}} is one operationalisation, adopted for now: re-weighting as the reading where the update leaves the subcomponents as they were and Γ switches them on differently, measured by the re-switching share. Until this is fixed, "reveal" and "create" below are readings of the data, not verdicts on the hypothesis.

How it is tested

Take a released decomposition, run a small fine-tune, and try simple rules for carrying the update onto the pieces with Γ and Δ frozen. A rule passes if its output is still a faithful decomposition of the updated model by VPD's own checks, with Δ not swallowing the update.

Where we are

Result 1

Adam component training keeps the decomposition faithful while it learns, once its early damage elsewhere fades

Training the subcomponents themselves with Adam, Γ and Δ frozen, learns every task tried, including a cipher the model has never seen (E10). On emoticon it is the only operation that keeps both the behaviour and VPD's adversarial check to step 1000 with Δ untouched (E16, first figure). Read at step 10 it passes on four of nine tasks (figure below), but its failures came while it was still damaging the model's other predictions. Later in training it passes two of the failing word tasks, at steps 100 and 1,000, and the 32-pair cipher, which also passes at a lower rate (E21; E10, amendment). The rare-target " in" then " Fig" passes under no setting tried. The verdicts are not an artefact of the adversary's short search (E20, third amendment). Its caveat, that it trains in place of the update rather than mapping a finished one, is what teacher training answers (Line 2 below).

Result 2

No split of a finished update keeps the behaviour and faithfulness with Δ untouched; teacher training does

All five operations on emoticon, on one card (E16, first figure): the least-squares split keeps the behaviour but puts a large part of the update in Δ, and for the fine-tune fails the adversarial check. The scaled adjoint split stays faithful but loses most of the behaviour by step 1000. The rescaling split keeps none of it. Splitting every step of the fine-tune instead does no better (E19, first figure). On small fine-tunes the least-squares split passes the adversarial check but still leaves a quarter or more of the change in Δ (E21, E22). The most promising split is the scaled adjoint split: it always stays faithful with Δ untouched, and keeps most of a small SGD fine-tune's emoticon behaviour, but less as the update grows and little on the word tasks (E31, E34). Teacher training, which trains the pieces to match the fine-tuned model's predictions instead of splitting its weights, keeps all three on three tasks (E34).

Result 3

The moved subcomponents never carry the behaviour

Under every operation route-split so far they carry about a tenth of the gain on the target or less (E5's figure, E16's second figure). For common next words and copying, Γ switching existing subcomponents on carries most of it (E5, E12); for a new association, subcomponents Γ keeps off do (E10, E12). Emoticon and the rarest next words sit between.

Line 1 · Re-gating (Q5)

The question. On tasks the model already does, a fine-tune's gain is carried mostly by Γ switching existing subcomponents on or off, not by the subcomponents changing (E5, E12, E16). What is that switching, and can it be trusted?

Where it stands. The working hypothesis is the fixed-shift hypothesis: after a narrow fine-tune, one fixed change to Γ's importance values per cue word carries most of the switching (E18, E28, E32, E33). A form that names no cue works as well on the toy tasks: each input gets the change of the inputs the original model treats most alike (E35). On a fine-tune with no trigger, lowercased text, that form recovers three-quarters or more of the switching once enough positions are compared, against about a half for the last few tokens and a third for the current token (E36). A restatement, "positions the original model treats alike get alike changes", waits on the user.

Next. A summary of the whole preceding text as a harder null, and what the remaining tenth to quarter depends on; then a fine-tune of a real behaviour, or the larger model (Q9).

Line 2 · Commute (Q1)

The question. Is there a simple map that carries an update onto the subcomponents, so that the decomposition stays faithful to the updated model without Δ absorbing the update?

Where it stands. Training the subcomponents directly passes once its early damage elsewhere fades: Adam at steps 100 and 1,000 on emoticon and two word tasks, at two batch orders (E21, E26). Splitting a large finished update fails, but small fine-tunes pass every split tried (E22, E25), and the scaled adjoint split of an SGD fine-tune keeps most of the behaviour on emoticon, but only while the update is small, by a margin that depends on the task; a second training seed repeated this (E22, E31). Teacher training, component training aimed at a finished fine-tune's predictions, is the first map of a finished update that keeps the behaviour, faithfulness and Δ together, on three tasks and without labels (E34). The surprise: trained on ordinary text with every full trigger removed, it still learns the emoticon behaviour, by a weight change that is not the fine-tune's.

Next. Teacher training's result has held up: it passes at a second batch order and under an adversary eight times longer, copies a LoRA from the task's text, and loses only a few hundredths when the partly-on trigger positions leave its text (E34). The next step is the larger model (Q9), with teacher training as the map (decided 2026-10-26). Section {{n:next}} has both lines' plans.
