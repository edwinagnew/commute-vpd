Overview

Does post-training reuse mechanisms the model already has, or build new ones? Several accounts of how models come to behave as they do share one assumption: pretraining builds the mechanisms, and post-training mostly selects among them or re-weights them. The persona selection model (Marks, Lindsey and Olah, 2026) says post-training picks out one of the characters pretraining learned to simulate. Alignment pretraining (Tice et al., 2026) finds that what pretraining text says about AI carries through post-training. Jain et al. (2023) describe fine-tuning as a thin wrapper over capabilities already there. The evidence for the assumption is behavioural or read from activations. This project tests it inside the model, on real fine-tunes.

The work has two steps.

  1. Define reuse. Say precisely when a fine-tune reuses what the original model computed, in terms that do not depend on any interpretability method. The working definition (box at the end of this section) asks how far the variables the fine-tune needs for a behavior still have the base model's mechanisms.
  2. Measure it. Find a method that reads reuse off a real fine-tune. Our main candidate is a parameter decomposition (VPD, Goodfire's adversarial parameter decomposition). It writes each weight matrix as a sum of many rank-one pieces, each meant to be one small piece of mechanism, plus a leftover Δ; a small network Γ says which pieces are switched on at each input. If a fine-tune reuses old mechanism, its effect should come from switching on old pieces, not from new ones (the picture below). We compare this reading with cheaper baselines such as steering and neuron readings, first on a small model and then on a larger one.

Most runs use a small 4-layer language model (the 67M) with Goodfire's released decomposition; the larger Qwen3-1.7B tests whether findings carry over. Experiments are numbered E1, E2, …; each code links to its card on the experiments page. "You" in this log is the project's owner. The order things were actually done in is in the timeline (section {{n:timeline}}) and the changelog.

Our read: most promising, most uncertain

Most promising

On the nine built 67M pairs, the re-gating share is the only single reading that puts the side built to reuse higher on every pair. We made nine pairs of 67M fine-tunes: one side reuses something the model half does already (copying a token, lowercasing), the other learns something unfamiliar (a cipher, a random map). The share keeps the original pieces, changes only which of them Γ switches on, and asks what share of the log-odds gain in VPD's masked model that alone gives back. It reads clearly higher on the reuse side of all nine pairs, with no overlap between seeds. That includes the three pairs whose sides changed the weights by the same amount. But the original model's prior and steering together already point the same way on all nine, so the share adds calls on the equal-size pairs and gap sizes neither predicts, not a new direction. Alone, steering agrees on four pairs and reverses three, the neuron readings agree on three to six, and update size on none (E133; part 2 gate page of 2026-11-16).

The re-weighting share and the definition agree on direction, not on order. Part 2's re-weighting share, which uses no decomposition, puts the reuse side above the new side of every size-matched pair. The held-model reading, one cheap reading of the current definition, agrees in sign on 8 of those 9 pairs but not in order (rank correlation 0.13 at saved sizes, -0.27 after matching), and it tracks how far its map reaches, the site and the size (E205, E206). The trained pairs have not yet been read under the current definition; cell P1.31 will do that at the level that passes.

Key uncertainties

Is the share more than the prior? The original model's probability for the trained answer points the same way on all nine pairs. The share still separates the two pairs made with the prior matched, and its gap sizes do not track the prior's, but nine pairs is few. One pair where the prior and steering disagree was built (cell P2.32, section {{n:next}}), and on it the share follows steering, not the prior.

Does the share read the kind of change? On fine-tunes that imitate hand-made edits of known kind, it reads built rewiring like built re-weighting and like an edit that learned nothing, all from 1.10 to 1.23; only the one large-gain edit, a new lookup table, reads low at 0.26, and kind could not be scaled apart from size. Provisional: no independent check (E200).

"Reuse" still means "made to reuse". Every side is labelled by how it was made. The working definition does not yet give those labels on real fine-tunes, so the comparisons measure agreement with the builder's intent.

Scale. On Qwen readings sit at a ceiling or cannot be computed, so nothing is shown on a model people use.

The working definition · revised 2026-11-18, option (b) by write adopted 2026-11-21/22

In one line. A fine-tune reuses the base model, for a behavior, to the extent that what it changed in the variables the behavior runs through is the base model's own mechanism turned up or down; read as Eb, the share of the write change of K*'s changed variables that the base mechanism at its best output gain explains, with the untouched share of K* reported beside it.

The definition

The definition is stated in the vocabulary of causal abstraction (Geiger et al., 2025; "Def. n" is their journal numbering) wherever it has a term. Comparing a base model with its fine-tune follows the precedent of Prakash et al. (2024), though here a variable is given M's mechanism by a soft intervention rather than M's value by patching. Two steps are ours: one featurizer, fixed from M, applied to two networks, and the soft intervention whose new mechanisms come from the base model. The closest precedent is grafting (Panigrahi et al. 2023): a smallest set of a fine-tune's weights grafted onto the base model that keeps the task.

Networks. Let M be the base model and F its fine-tune. Each is a deterministic causal model (Def. 5) on the same signature: the same variables, input and output, with different mechanisms (weights). A graft adds a variable to the fine-tune; the base model gets that variable with a mechanism that outputs zero, so both share one signature.

Featurized model. From M alone we fix a signature Σ̃ and a map τ0 from Σ̃'s values to the network's values, the identity on the input and the output. The intermediate variables U of Σ̃ are the features, one remainder variable e per featurized site, and one remainder for the rest of the network. Each network N has a featurized model Ñ, a causal model on Σ̃ with mechanisms F̃u(i), such that N is an exact transformation of Ñ under τ0 (Def. 25), with interventions that set the inputs. The featurization is exact, so there is no fit error ε. Coverage (your decision, ask 50): every mechanism the fine-tune changed has a variable, and the remainder "the rest of the network" covers what the features do not. With an SAE at one site, that is the other layers; at the unit level, the embeddings, unembedding and layer-norm gains. There are two ways to build the pair.

  • On activations. Fix σ0 from the network's values to Σ̃'s with τ0∘σ0 = id, so τ0 is a bijective translation (Def. 28) and the features are Geiger's modular features (§3.6). Then F̃u(i)(ṽ) = Proju σ0(F(i)(τ0(ṽ))), which depends only on u's parents Pa(u) (Remark 6). The remainder is whatever the features miss, for an SAE e = h − dec0(enc0(h)). Units and rotations need none.
  • On weights (VPD). Fix the decomposition W0 = ∑c ucvcT + Δ and a split rule that writes any update as δW = ∑c δWc + δWout, where δWout is everything the rule does not assign to a component (the least-squares, adjoint or rescaling split, declared with the reading). Each component's contribution to the layer output is a variable, and τ0 sums them. For the layer's input x: F̃c(0) = ucvcTx and F̃c(1) = (ucvcT + δWc)x; F̃e(0) = Δx and F̃e(1) = (Δ + δWout)x. Only the definition read at VPD needs a declared split of δW; r does not.

The cases: units (neurons, heads, MLP layers, blocks; a variable merge, Def. 37; no remainder; every reading so far is this case), rotations (PCA, DAS; Def. 28; no remainder), SAE (features enc0(h), remainder as above) and VPD (on weights; remainder Δx, plus δWoutx in the fine-tune), and coarser groupings of any of these (variable merges, Def. 37). Not yet a case: transcoders, whose feature mechanisms on activations are the base encoder in both networks, so every change would land in the remainder; they need a construction on weights. Not a case: attribution graphs, which freeze attention patterns and error terms for each prompt, so a variable has no mechanism to compare.

One featurization for both networks (ours). The featurization is fixed from M alone and applied to both networks, giving M̃ and F̃ on one signature, so a variable is the same in both. F's features are not fitted. F(i) = {F(i)X} are the network's own mechanisms (Def. 5); F̃u(i) is the mechanism of variable u in the featurized model, a function of its parents Pa(u) (Remark 6): superscript (0) is M, (1) is F, matching the paper's N0 and N1. Reuse compares F̃u(0) with F̃u(1). In practice, on activations F's features on an input are σ0 applied to F's activations at each featurized site; on weights they are the declared split of δW. Why one featurization: anything F computes outside M's vocabulary lands in the remainder, which enters K* with a changed mechanism when the behavior runs through it. A second, separately fitted featurizer would need a fitted pairing, which is the freedom that makes reuse vacuous (Sutter et al. 2025, by analogy); it may be reported only as an optional localisation analysis. The cost is that new structure outside M's vocabulary is found but not named; the remainder's share of K* is stated beside every reading and says how large it is. Geiger relates no two trained networks and has no remainder variable (§3.6.1 treats lossy SAEs as approximate transformations, Def. 41); the mandatory remainder and the construction on weights are ours. A variable the featurization declares absent from a network, such as a unit F adds or one F zeroes, has no mechanism there.

Behavior and mean ablation. A behavior B is a partial function from inputs to outputs (§3.5.2): here F's own input-output map on a declared set D of inputs where F's output differs from M's. For each variable u, μu is its mean value on D in the fine-tune. For K ⊆ U, the hard intervention (Def. 9) iK = {u ← μu}u∉K fixes every variable outside K to its mean. This is the one place a value, not a mechanism, is fixed.

The circuit the behavior runs through. K* is a smallest K such that F̃iK preserves B within a declared α, the reference being full ablation (sub-circuit analysis, §3.5.2). Every variable the behavior runs through counts, whether or not the fine-tune changed its mechanism (your decision, ask 48). The circuit is a set of variables, not of connections. K* = U always preserves B, so a reading always exists.

Reuse (option (b) by write, adopted 2026-11-21/22). Call a variable u in K* changed if its mechanism differs between F and M, and write wF(u,x) and wM(u,x) for what u writes at input position x of D. Its write change is ddu = ∑x∈D ‖wF(u,x) − wM(u,x)‖2. The base mechanism at its best output gain gu = nu/mu, with nu = ∑x 〈wF(u,x), wM(u,x)〉 and mu = ∑x ‖wM(u,x)‖2, explains eu = (nu − mu)2/mu of that change; eu = 0 when the best gain is not positive. Reuse is Eb = ∑ eu / ∑ ddu, both sums over the changed variables of K*. It is 1 when every changed variable's mechanism is the base model's up to a gain, and near 0 when the base mechanisms explain none of the change; it reads 1 by convention when no variable of K* changed. The share of K* that F leaves untouched is reported beside Eb. Graft: a variable F has and M lacks, such as an added head, has a base mechanism that outputs zero, so no gain explains its write and it counts fully as new (eu = 0). Why by write: each changed variable is fitted on its own in closed form, so there is no search over sets of variables, no tolerance bar and no dependence on the order of visiting. Reading behaviour per part instead (E258, E259) is a stated limit: a single part barely moves the output, so the definition tests writes and is blind to whether the unexplained part of a write matters downstream.

Fit of the featurized model. The featurization is exact, so there is no fit error; the remainder's share of K* is stated instead. A review of the definition against Geiger et al. found four repairs it needs before the paper states it formally; all four are answered, and you approved the wording on 2026-11-18 (decisions.md, "Wording approved").

The definition is graded and is relative to the featurization and the behavior; Eb has no free grade to declare (at VPD the split rule is still declared with the reading).

Properties. These follow from the definition. Reuse is relative to the featurization; there is no featurization-free reuse that a reading falls short of. It is not invariant to the fine-tune re-coordinatizing an old computation: on activations a feature the fine-tune has moved is a different variable and the move shows in the remainder (a move small enough that M's mechanism still preserves B within the tolerance never enters K), while on weights a component whose read or write vector moved is still that component, with a changed mechanism. K* need not be unique; Eb is read on the K* chosen. A reading always exists (K* = U). A behavior M already has: when F changed no variable of K*, Eb reads 1 by convention; when F strengthened M's circuit, Eb reads the share that is a gain, so at VPD a neuron rescale reads about 0.76, because doubling a few neurons is only about three quarters a strength change of VPD's subcomponents. A memorised fact reads new where it is stored: its changed variables are not a gain of M's. A circuit reused on a new cue no longer reads as partial reuse (this reverses what this card said after ask 48): untouched variables do not count in Eb, so only the new cue's changed variables are read, and they read as new. The old circuit's part shows in the untouched share reported beside Eb, not in Eb.

Readings so far

Two readings were tested; their numbers stand as readings, not as the definition. ρ, the mean mechanism agreement over E208's circuit of the change K (not K*), with single neurons as variables (E195, E196, E208), saturates: it read 0.999 or more on every fine-tune in E208, and the gaps between pair members followed update size; in your words, "the neuron level is not a high-enough level abstraction to detect new". The held model's survival S (E197, E201, E205, E206) separated only items that are new by construction from old-task fine-tunes. The reuse score R itself, read at coarse levels, at neurons and at VPD subcomponents, sits near 1 and its reuse-minus-new gaps follow update size: at matched size only 5 of 9 block-level pair signs survive (E232), and with VPD subcomponents the edit built as reuse by a ten-fold gain scores 0.9755, below a built new edit at 0.9963 (E226). The cause is the squared-cosine grade, which is part of the wording, so you decide what to do with it (section {{n:decisions}}). A $0 recomputation from saved readings found that weighting parts by behaviour keeps the failure, while removing update size orders all six built edits and puts reuse higher on 7 of 9 pairs, a lead only because behaviour change orders them too (E238). Dividing each part's loss by its weight change then puts all four built reuse edits above both new ones, but on the pairs bigger updates read as more reuse (E239); you chose a matched-update-size test next (P1.44). Switching M's own VPD subcomponents on and off by re-reading F does not pass either: built reuse edits sit under VPD's reconstruction floor, and random updates read as high as they do, though the trained pairs show reuse above new (E241).
