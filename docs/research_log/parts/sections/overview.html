Overview

Does post-training reuse mechanisms the model already has, or build new ones? Several accounts of how models come to behave as they do share one assumption: pretraining builds the mechanisms, and post-training mostly selects among them or re-weights them. The persona selection model (Marks, Lindsey and Olah, 2026) says post-training picks out one of the characters pretraining learned to simulate. Alignment pretraining (Tice et al., 2026) finds that what pretraining text says about AI carries through post-training. Jain et al. (2023) describe fine-tuning as a thin wrapper over capabilities already there. The evidence for the assumption is behavioural or read from activations. This project tests it inside the model, on real fine-tunes.

The work has two steps.

  1. Define reuse. Say precisely when a fine-tune reuses what the original model computed, in terms that do not depend on any interpretability method. The working definition (box at the end of this section) asks whether the fine-tune's behaviour comes from setting a variable the original already computed, and how that variable gets set.
  2. Measure it. Find a method that reads reuse off a real fine-tune. Our main candidate is a parameter decomposition (VPD, Goodfire's adversarial parameter decomposition). It writes each weight matrix as a sum of many rank-one pieces, each meant to be one small piece of mechanism, plus a leftover Δ; a small network Γ says which pieces are switched on at each input. If a fine-tune reuses old mechanism, its effect should come from switching on old pieces, not from new ones (the picture below). We compare this reading with cheaper baselines such as steering and neuron readings, first on a small model and then on a larger one.

Most runs use a small 4-layer language model (the 67M) with Goodfire's released decomposition; the larger Qwen3-1.7B tests whether findings carry over. Experiments are numbered E1, E2, …; each code links to its card on the experiments page. "You" in this log is the project's owner. The order things were actually done in is in the timeline (section {{n:timeline}}) and the changelog.

Our read: most promising, most uncertain

Most promising

The re-gating share detects reuse where cheaper methods do not. We made nine pairs of 67M fine-tunes: one side reuses something the model half does already (copying a token, lowercasing), the other learns something unfamiliar (a cipher, a random map). The share keeps the original pieces, changes only which of them Γ switches on, and asks how much of the fine-tune's gain that alone gives back. It reads clearly higher on the reuse side of all nine pairs, with no overlap between seeds. No baseline that looks at the fine-tune does this: steering agrees on four pairs and reverses on three, neuron readings agree on at most three, update size on none (E133).

The definition agrees in direction. Part 2's re-weighting share, which uses no decomposition, also puts the reuse side above the new side of every matched pair it has read (E142). Reading both numbers on the same pairs is the next cheap check.

Key uncertainties

Is the share more than the prior? The original model's probability for the trained answer points the same way on all nine pairs. The share still separates the two pairs made with the prior matched, and its gap sizes do not track the prior's, but nine pairs is few. One pair where the prior and steering disagree was built (cell P2.32, section {{n:next}}), and on it the share follows steering, not the prior.

"Reuse" still means "made to reuse". Every side is labelled by how it was made. The working definition does not yet give those labels on real fine-tunes, so the comparisons measure agreement with the builder's intent.

Scale. On Qwen readings sit at a ceiling or cannot be computed, so nothing is shown on a model people use.

The working definition · adopted 2026-11-18

In one line. A fine-tune's change is reuse to the degree that it survives when every part of the fine-tuned model is swapped for the matching part of the original, still following the original's rule.

As an interchange. The wording is a cross-model interchange intervention, in the sense of causal abstraction (Geiger et al., 2025) and of the cross-model patching of Prakash et al., 2024. Let M be the original model and F its fine-tune, and let a unit be an MLP neuron, an attention head or a whole MLP block. A unit's rule is the value it takes once its inputs are set. A unit of F keeps M's rule to the degree that the two agree when their inputs are set to the same values. The change from M to F is reuse to the degree that it survives when every unit of F is interchanged for M's matching unit held to its rule.

Formal core. Build a held model H: M with each layer's output weights replaced by a fit of F's write at that site from M's units there. The fit uses M's own inputs at that layer and never F's outputs. Everything else in H, including the embeddings, the unembedding and the final norm, is M's, and H runs end to end. The survival is S = 1 − KL(F ‖ H) / KL(F ‖ M), at the scored position on windows the fit never saw. It reads 1 when H reproduces F and 0 when H is no closer to F than M is. The frozen specification.

What is not interchange. How far "matching unit held to its rule" may stretch is a declared parameter, the map class. One gain per unit is the strict end. The main reading is one linear map per site, from all of M's units there to F's write. Fitting that map is a stitching-style step (as in model stitching, Bansal et al., 2021), not an interchange. Under it a linear re-mix of M's units counts as reuse: that is your stipulation, not a finding.

Caveats. The reading depends mostly on the map class. With one gain per neuron and head, 0.01 to 0.06 of any trained change survives; with the per-site map, 0.98 or more of every old-task fine-tune does. So far the main reading has separated only items that are new by construction, a head grafted onto the frozen model (0.13) and a rebuilt circuit (0.56), from old-task fine-tunes. Within the matched pairs the reuse and new members differ by 0.011 at most, so nothing is claimed about them. Embeddings are held at M's values, so a change there counts as lost. A lookup on the current token built on 256 new last-layer neurons reads 0.954, close to old-task fine-tunes, while four new last-layer heads read 0.14 to 0.22 (E206); and shrinking an update to a partner's size moves a state's score by up to 0.030, as much as the pair gaps (E205). So a lookup on the current token, built on new neurons, reading as mostly kept is a stated consequence of the linear re-mix rule, which you tentatively accepted on 2026-11-18. No cut-offs and no labels are set (the calibration report).

What it replaces and what comes next. It supersedes the second wording, reuse at a variable, tentatively adopted on 2026-11-10, which sorted a behaviour into re-weighted, rewired or built. Both follow-ups have run (grafts at other sites and offsets; the main reading on the size-matched pairs). Elsewhere in this log the "reuse" and "new" sides of a pair mean how the pair was made.
