Overview

Does post-training create new mechanisms, or draw from existing ones? A common assumption, resting on behavioural or activation evidence, is that pretraining builds the mechanisms and post-training only re-weights them. This project looks where post-training actually acts: in the weights.

A parameter decomposition (VPD) writes each weight matrix as a sum of rank-one subcomponents, each meant to be one piece of mechanism, plus a residual Δ, with an importance function Γ saying which pieces matter on each input. A post-training update is a change to the weights, so: does it land on the existing subcomponents, or outside them?

Definition · existing and new, without VPD

From Jain et al. 2024, Mechanistically analyzing the effects of fine-tuning on procedurally defined tasks, section 3. The original model possesses a capability f if a linear readout of some layer, trained on pretraining data, gives f on a chosen set of inputs. A fine-tune reuses f if its behaviour is g∘f for some map g and it passes a causal test: switching off or patching f breaks the behaviour, or a little pruning or reverse fine-tuning brings the old behaviour back. Otherwise the behaviour is new. Precise statement in the glossary.

Ours, not the paper's: the causal test, "new means not reuse", and a filed threshold in place of its "for all inputs". The paper defines possession and relevance only. Decided 2026-11-05: "new" is relative to a named capability, removing its direction is the causal label, and a capability counts only if reading it beats ignoring it on held-out inputs. A label needs a located mechanism, so the old tasks' labels stay assumptions. The definition is silent on the emoticon, ladder, many-cues and lowercase tasks; on Qwen it writes only for the template fine-tune (E90). Only the copying task gets a verdict under the refined rules (E99).

Measured? Not in full on any fine-tune, none on Qwen. On the 67M the readouts failed (E59, E64) and a context patch gave no label (E66); the copying pair has a causal test but no readout (E62); every other map is labelled by how it was built. No VPD reading has been scored against this, so "reveal" and "create" below are readings, not verdicts.

How it is tested

Take a released decomposition, run a small fine-tune, and carry the update onto the pieces with Γ's parameters and Δ frozen (fixed gates on the 67M, not on Qwen). A rule passes if the result is still faithful to the updated model by VPD's own checks, without Δ swallowing the update.

Result 1

Adam component training keeps the decomposition faithful while it learns, once its early damage elsewhere fades

Training the subcomponents with Adam, Γ and Δ frozen, learns every task tried, including an unseen cipher (E10), and on emoticon alone keeps the behaviour and VPD's adversarial check to step 1000 with Δ untouched (E16). It fails early, while it still damages other predictions, then passes two word tasks and the 32-pair cipher (E10, E11, E21). The rare-target " in" then " Fig" passes under no setting tried. It trains in place of the update rather than mapping a finished one (Line 1 below).

Result 2

No split of a finished update keeps the behaviour and faithfulness without Δ absorbing it; teacher training does for trigger fine-tunes

On emoticon (E16) the least-squares split puts much of the update in Δ, the scaled adjoint split stays faithful but loses most of the behaviour, more as an update grows (E31), and the rescaling split keeps none. Teacher training (matching the fine-tuned model's predictions) keeps all three on three trigger tasks (E34), passing from every adversary start (E47). With no trigger (lowercasing) it keeps the behaviour, but the Adam full-matrix copy fails the faithfulness check on every block of new text read, the rank-8 LoRA's copy passes, and the check cannot fail an SGD update even left in Δ (E37, E49).

Baselines

Baselines: can anything without a decomposition tell eliciting from new?

We compare VPD's reading with cheap readers, on fine-tunes built either to strengthen something the model already does or to teach something new. A weight-change spectrum does not separate them; a steering vector and neuron copying do so only partly. VPD's re-gating share separates that pair at five seeds, but on the small model it follows how likely the original model already was to give the answer. No neuron reading separates all the pairs (E63; low power), and you ended the search (E66).

Line 1 · Commute (Q1)

The question. Given a finished fine-tune, can we move the pieces, with Γ's parameters and Δ frozen (fixed gates on the 67M, not on Qwen), so the decomposition describes the fine-tuned model? Three tests hold together: the fine-tune's behaviour is kept; VPD's faithfulness check passes (an adversary switching pieces off does no more damage than on the original); and Δ does not absorb the update, which would pass trivially. Then the update can be written in the existing pieces.

What works: teacher training. Training the pieces to reproduce the fine-tuned model's predictions passes all three tests on every trigger fine-tune tried: three cued-answer tasks (E34) and a word cipher the model never knew (E42). One-shot splits of the finished update lose the behaviour or leave much of it in Δ (Result 2).

So far. An update that teaches a cued answer can be written in the existing pieces; a broad one, such as lowercasing, not yet (E39, E41).

Line 2 · Re-gating (Q5)

The question. After a fine-tune, frozen Γ reading the new activations switches on different pieces: re-gating. If post-training only re-weights existing mechanisms, re-gating should explain much of a fine-tune. Does it, and what does a re-gating number measure?

What we found. On toy fine-tunes that teach a fixed answer after a cue, the pieces Γ newly switches on are mostly ones the original model already uses before that answer, and putting back their outputs takes back about a fifth of the gain (E38). Where only the gating changes, the gate gain tells a behaviour the model already had from a new one (E12).

So far. Re-gating reads whether the trained answer is one the original model already gives for that input, not whether the behaviour runs through a mechanism it already has; the ratio and the share sort two of three families of saved runs (E53, E54, E58, E60; Q5 has the detail).

Line 3 · Qwen3-1.7B (Q9)

The question. Do the small model's answers carry over to a larger model with a real post-training update? Our port reproduces Qwen3-1.7B's decomposition.

What we found. The commute pass test has no teeth here, with masks from either model (E44), so since 2026-10-30 this line describes where an update lands. The re-gating share reads 0.99 or more on every state, real or random. The direct share, what changing the subcomponents alone carries with the masks held, is higher for the gold-solutions fine-tune than for random changes: 0.038 above for a finished one, level for a part-way one (E61).

So far. No reading on this model tells an update that uses what is already there from one that adds something new; the direct share does not separate them on the maths fine-tune.
