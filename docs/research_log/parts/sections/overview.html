Overview

Does post-training reuse mechanisms the model already has, or build new ones? Several accounts of how models come to behave as they do share one assumption: pretraining builds the mechanisms, and post-training mostly selects among them or re-weights them. The persona selection model (Marks, Lindsey and Olah, 2026) says post-training picks out one of the characters pretraining learned to simulate. Alignment pretraining (Tice et al., 2026) finds that what pretraining text says about AI carries through post-training. Jain et al. (2023) describe fine-tuning as a thin wrapper over capabilities already there. The evidence for the assumption is behavioural or read from activations. This project tests it inside the model, on real fine-tunes.

The work has two steps.

  1. Define reuse. Say precisely when a fine-tune reuses what the original model computed, in terms that do not depend on any interpretability method. The working definition (box at the end of this section) asks whether the fine-tune's behaviour comes from setting a variable the original already computed, and how that variable gets set.
  2. Measure it. Find a method that reads reuse off a real fine-tune. Our main candidate is a parameter decomposition (VPD, Goodfire's adversarial parameter decomposition). It writes each weight matrix as a sum of many rank-one pieces, each meant to be one small piece of mechanism, plus a leftover Δ; a small network Γ says which pieces are switched on at each input. If a fine-tune reuses old mechanism, its effect should come from switching on old pieces, not from new ones (the picture below). We compare this reading with cheaper baselines such as steering and neuron readings, first on a small model and then on a larger one.

Most runs use a small 4-layer language model (the 67M) with Goodfire's released decomposition; the larger Qwen3-1.7B tests whether findings carry over. Experiments are numbered E1, E2, …; each code links to its card on the experiments page. "You" in this log is the project's owner. The order things were actually done in is in the timeline (section {{n:timeline}}) and the changelog.

Four questions, and where each stands

Part 1 is the question the project started from: before reading a fine-tune through the decomposition, can the decomposition be brought along with it? Parts 2 to 4 are the two steps above: define reuse, measure it on the 67M, repeat on Qwen; each ends at a decision for you (section {{n:next}}). Each box links to its experiment cards.

Our read: most promising, most uncertain

Most promising

The re-gating share detects reuse where cheaper methods do not. We made nine pairs of 67M fine-tunes: one side reuses something the model half does already (copying a token, lowercasing), the other learns something unfamiliar (a cipher, a random map). The share keeps the original pieces, changes only which of them Γ switches on, and asks how much of the fine-tune's gain that alone gives back. It reads clearly higher on the reuse side of all nine pairs, with no overlap between seeds. No baseline that looks at the fine-tune does this: steering agrees on four pairs and reverses on three, neuron readings agree on at most three, update size on none (E133).

The definition agrees in direction. Part 2's re-weighting share, which uses no decomposition, also puts the reuse side above the new side of every matched pair it has read (E142). Reading both numbers on the same pairs is the next cheap check.

Key uncertainties

Is the share more than the prior? The original model's probability for the trained answer points the same way on all nine pairs. The share still separates the two pairs made with the prior matched, and its gap sizes do not track the prior's, but nine pairs is few. Pairs where the prior and steering disagree are being built (cell P2.17, section {{n:next}}).

"Reuse" still means "made to reuse". Every side is labelled by how it was made. The working definition does not yet give those labels on real fine-tunes, so the comparisons measure agreement with the builder's intent.

Scale. On Qwen every reading so far sits at a ceiling or cannot be computed, so nothing found on the 67M has been shown on a model people use.

The working definition · adopted tentatively 2026-11-10

Let M be the original model and F its fine-tune. A variable of M is a small linear read-out of its activations, in the sense of the causal abstraction framework (Geiger et al., 2025): a quantity inside the model that can be set to a chosen value while the rest of the run carries on. It drives a behaviour if giving M the fine-tune's value there makes M act like F, and giving F the original's value makes F revert. The behaviour is re-weighted if F sets that variable by one fixed affine function of M's own value of it (on, up, or shifted); rewired if F sets it from other quantities M already computes, through a new connection; built otherwise. Precise statement and graded shares in the glossary.

It replaces a wording after Jain et al. 2024, which gave no verdict on any task (its wording). You adopted this wording tentatively on 2026-11-10, with cut-offs of 0.8 for the re-weighting share and 0.5 for the rewiring share, to be revised if it cannot detect built. Results are reported within matched pairs. Elsewhere in this log the "reuse" and "new" sides of a pair mean how the pair was made, and "built" means only the definition's third label.
