The question and its sub-questions

The question now

Does post-training reuse mechanisms the model already has, or build new ones, and can a parameter decomposition tell which?

The project began, on 2026-10-14, from a narrower question in your words: "Given a VPD parameter decomposition, can one commute small gradient updates from post-training past the parameter components? [...] If not, is that because post-training teaches new capabilities rather than strengthening existing ones, or because parameter decompositions are too brittle?" The second half of that question is now the main one; the first is the fourth group below.

Grouped by part: part 1 is the starting question, parts 2 to 4 the paper draft (section {{n:next}}). Numbers are kept from when each question was opened, so they are not in order.

Part 1 · Commute: carrying an update onto the decomposition

Q1
Is there a simple map that keeps the decomposition faithful to the updated model, with Δ not absorbing the update? The weak version of the starting question; the pass criterion.
A strong candidate: teacher training keeps the behaviour, passes the adversarial check and leaves Δ alone on every trigger fine-tune and a cipher (E34, E42, E47), and fails on a broad full-weight fine-tune (E37, E39). On Qwen3-1.7B the masked test cannot pass even the whole update, so placements there were scored on a clean-model table, which least squares passes on four fine-tunes and narrowly misses on three small RL updates (E44, E75). No more carrying runs are planned.

Q2
Which operation carries an update onto the subcomponents and keeps the decomposition faithful: the rescaling split, the least-squares or scaled adjoint split of a finished update, or component training?
On emoticon, only component training with Adam keeps the behaviour and faithfulness with Δ untouched to step 1000 (E16); the splits lose the behaviour or push the update into Δ (E22, E31). Teacher training does better (Q1).

Q3
Is a LoRA easier to commute than a full-weight fine-tune?
No. A LoRA's read side barely leaves its random start, so it lines up with the pieces no better than a random update, and its split leaves more in Δ (E9, E14, E27).

Q4
Does it matter whether the behaviour is new to the model? The "new capabilities" branch.
Newness has not been seen to break faithfulness: both trainings pass on a cipher the model never knew (E21, E42). But a new behaviour runs through pieces Γ keeps off, which no map with Γ frozen avoids (E12, E48).

Q6
Why does the adversarial check fail where the stochastic one does not? Is the decomposition brittle?
In part. Not cancellation, not the original pieces: the excess comes from removing alive and dead pieces together, which offset each other in the original model and not after a large update (E17, E20, E23). Why is open.

Q8
Should the map act on each training step rather than on a finished run?
No, for the adjoint form, on one task: splitting each step leaves more of the behaviour in Δ than splitting the finished update (E19, E25). Teacher training chained over three checkpoints matched one copy (E41).

Q11
Does the optimiser matter? SGD and Adam on the same tasks, both as component training's optimiser and as the fine-tune optimiser whose update the splits consume.
Neither optimiser decides alone (E21, E26). Small SGD updates keep more behaviour than Adam ones when split (E22, E31); whether teacher training needs Adam is unresolved (E34, E47, E49).

Part 2 · A definition of reuse

Q12
Is there a working definition of reused against new, one that needs no decomposition and gives sensible labels where the answer is known by construction? Part 2 of the paper draft; adopted tentatively 2026-11-10.
Partly. The second wording (overview) recovers the labels of four hand-made changes (E137). On real fine-tunes its labels do not follow construction: 11 of 16 old tasks read rewired, reuse and new alike (E142), and copies of a re-weighted change read rewired (E139). Its re-weighting share does order every matched pair, reuse side higher. Three built-control attempts gave no clean control (E148), and on the last the search read only 0.47 on a no-teaching control the plain model holds exactly, so "built" is not cleanly read and reuse may be missed too. Adopted tentatively 2026-11-10, cut-offs 0.8 and 0.5; what the wording does now is waiting on you (section {{n:decisions}}).

Part 3 · Re-gating against baselines, on the 67M

Q5
After a fine-tune, the frozen Γ switches on different pieces than on the original model: re-gating. What does the re-gating share measure, and does it tell reuse from new better than methods that need no decomposition? Part 3 of the paper draft; gate 2026-11-18.
On nine 67M pairs constructed so that one side reuses and one learns something new, the re-gating share reads higher on the reuse side in all nine, and no cheaper method does: steering four, neurons at most three, update size none (E133). But it follows the original model's prior for the answer on every pair where both were read (E58, E133), reads high even for a fine-tune that made its target less likely (E36), and separates only one of four real fine-tunes from its shuffled-label twin. Provisional.

Part 4 · The larger model

Q9
Do the 67M findings carry over to a larger model with real post-training updates? Part 4 of the paper draft; gate 2026-11-25.
Not yet. The carrying pass test has no teeth on Qwen3-1.7B (E44, E80). On Goodfire's newer decomposition the re-gating share reads 0.991 to 0.9992 for every update and for random changes alike (E132), and in log-odds it cannot be computed because every update lowers its trained answer (E144). You stopped Qwen re-gating there; the 11-18 gate decides what part 4 repeats.
