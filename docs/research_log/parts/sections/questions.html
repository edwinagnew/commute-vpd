What we are asking

The question and its sub-questions

The central question · in the user's words, 2026-10-14

Given a VPD parameter decomposition, can one commute small gradient updates from post-training past the parameter components?

If so, the decomposition stays interpretable continuously along training, within some limit. If not, is that because post-training teaches new capabilities rather than strengthening existing ones, or because parameter decompositions are too brittle?

Precisely: is there a simple operation that maps a post-training update, say a LoRA, to an update on the components, such that the result is a faithful decomposition of the new model?

Q1 is that question; the others break it down. The three being worked on come first: Q1 and Q5, the two parallel lines (section {{n:next}}), and Q11, whether the optimiser matters, a priority subgoal. The rest follow in number order.

Q1
Is there a simple map that keeps the decomposition faithful to the updated model, with Δ not absorbing the update? The weak version; the pass criterion. The original question, open; one of two parallel lines since 2026-10-22 (user).
Partly. Teacher training, which trains the subcomponents to copy the fine-tuned model with Γ and Δ frozen, is the best answer so far: without labels it keeps the behaviour and passes on three trigger tasks, at two batch orders and under a longer adversary, using Adam, not plain SGD (E34). It also passes on a cipher the model lacked, though the subcomponents Γ marks important at the trigger carry almost none of it (E42). At step 10, where doing nothing fails, it sits at the pass line, which the user accepted; copies every 100 steps along one fine-tune, each from the last, kept the behaviour and passed where the check can tell (E41). The rare target is not yet placed; a pending card asks whether to check its closer copy once more (E34). A fine-tune with no trigger is copied badly, except for a rank-8 LoRA, whose pass repeats and is not explained by a rank cap (E37, E39). Splits of a finished update pass only while it is small (E16, E22, E31), one fixed shift of Γ fails (E30), training with labels passes once its early damage fades (E21, E26), and the pass line itself is in question (pending decisions).

Q5
When a fine-tune changes behaviour, is it because Γ switches existing subcomponents on and off differently (re-gating)? If so, what is that switching, and can it be trusted? One of the two parallel lines. About mechanism, not the pass criterion.
Yes, on tasks the model already does: most of a fine-tune's gain comes from Γ switching existing subcomponents on, not from them changing; on tasks it lacks, subcomponents Γ normally keeps off carry it instead (E5, E12, E16). On the toy tasks the switching follows how the original model treats each input (the fixed-shift hypothesis, de-prioritised on 2026-10-27; E18, E28, E32, E33, E35, E36). Checked (E38): on the 67M model after a 10-step fine-tune, putting back the switched-on set's trigger outputs takes back about a fifth of the gain on two tasks, far above random sets, but sets ranked by pull toward the target take back at least as much at the same count. So nothing in Γ's set has been shown to carry a part of the gain that a target-aware ranking at the same count misses: Γ finds, without the target, a set rich in target-pointing subcomponents, and its share is what those members carry.

Q11
Does the optimiser matter? SGD and Adam on the same tasks, both as component training's optimiser and as the fine-tune optimiser whose update the splits consume. A priority subgoal since 2026-10-21 (user). Its runs are E21, analysed 2026-10-22, with follow-ups E22, E23, E26 and E27.
Neither the optimiser nor the size of the update decides alone (E21). In component training, Adam fails only while it is still damaging the model's other predictions, which fades by step 100; SGD fails once its change grows large, so SGD looks better early and Adam better late, at two batch orders (E21, E26). When a finished update is split, small SGD updates keep more of their behaviour than Adam ones (E22, E31). As teacher training's optimiser, plain SGD at the one rate tried (0.03) failed on all three fine-tunes of one task, while Adam passes, so teacher training uses Adam (E34; decided 2026-10-28).

Q2
Which operation carries an update onto the subcomponents and keeps the decomposition faithful: the rescaling split, the least-squares or scaled adjoint split of a finished update, or component training?
Answered on emoticon: component training with Adam is the only one that keeps the behaviour and faithfulness with Δ untouched, to step 1000 (E16). The rescaling split keeps none of the behaviour, the least-squares split pushes much of the update into Δ, and the scaled adjoint split loses the behaviour as training goes on. Of the splits, the scaled adjoint split is the most promising: the only one that always stays faithful with Δ untouched, and on a small SGD fine-tune it keeps part of the behaviour, less as the update grows (E22, E31). Teacher training, added since and not a split, does better (Q1).

Q3
Is a LoRA easier to commute than a full-weight fine-tune?
No, on the point that matters. A LoRA's read side barely moves from its random start, so it lines up with the subcomponents no better than a random update does, while a fine-tune lines up better (E9, E14). Its split passes the adversarial check where the large fine-tune's fails, but leaves more of the update in Δ (E16). This comes from the LoRA's form, not its optimiser (E27).

Q4
Does it matter whether the behaviour is new to the model? The "new capabilities" branch.
Partly. Newness has not been seen to break faithfulness as the Pile checks read it: component training learns a cipher the model has never seen and passes once training settles (E10, E21), and teacher training copies a cipher fine-tune and passes with Δ untouched (E42). But a new behaviour takes another route: its gain runs through subcomponents Γ keeps off at the trigger, so the model built from those Γ marks important keeps almost none of a random cipher, and no map read so far with Γ frozen avoids that; every masked version tried failed its own control, so none was made (E12, E42). Whether that should count against a map is a pending decision. For re-gating newness matters: one fixed change to Γ carries the switching on seven familiar tasks and on none of three new ones (E28).

Q6
Why does the adversarial check fail where the stochastic one does not? Is the decomposition brittle?
Answered in part. It is not that the changes cancel each other out (E17), nor that the original subcomponents carry the damage (E20). The adversary's extra damage comes from removing alive and dead subcomponents together: in the original model those two removals offset each other, and after a large update they no longer do (E20, E23). The verdicts do not depend on how long the adversary searches. Why the offset is lost is open.

Q7
Can the measurements see what they claim to? Noise floors, blind spots, positive controls.
Fixed twice, still open in places. Several checks turned out blind: scores on ordinary text cannot see the trained behaviour (E2), two early task-local checks had no power (E4, E5), and the sparsity loss cannot see the map while Γ is frozen (E10). The adversarial noise floor depends on the GPU (E8, E11).

Q8
Should the map act on each training step rather than on a finished run?
No, for the adjoint form, on one task. Splitting each training step as it happens, with the subcomponents carried along, keeps the weights exact but leaves more of the behaviour in Δ than splitting the finished update once (E19). On small fine-tunes it passes the adversarial check but still loses that comparison (E25). Component training, per-step by construction, is the per-step map that works. Teacher training chained over three checkpoints ended within the filed tolerances of one copy, with a larger weight change (E41).

Q9
Do the answers carry over to a larger model with a real post-training update?
No result on an update yet (started 2026-10-27). The larger model is Qwen3-1.7B, whose decomposition our port reproduces (every check against the authors' code passes); at the authors' size (128 rows) the paper's adversary reads 1.6 nats on the unchanged decomposition, three starts agree within 19%, so one start serves as the pass test (its readings are lower bounds). The first task is GSM8K, with every update scored on new hand-written problems beside GSM8K test. The first fine-tune (E40) was a large format change that lowered accuracy, and doing nothing passed the web-text test at steps 1 and 10, a test that cannot fail the trivial placement. The Qwen re-plan therefore reads simple maps of E40's saved update first, after checking that the pass test can fail bad placements (E44, planned), then two anchor updates of known kind; the teacher-training rerun (E40 Amendment 1) is held. No GSM8K update is called strengthening or new before its readings (review of 2026-10-28). Reinforcement learning is held for later.
