What we are asking

The question and its sub-questions

The central question · in the user's words, 2026-10-14

Given a VPD parameter decomposition, can one commute small gradient updates from post-training past the parameter components?

If so, the decomposition stays interpretable continuously along training, within some limit. If not, is that because post-training teaches new capabilities rather than strengthening existing ones, or because parameter decompositions are too brittle?

Precisely: is there a simple operation that maps a post-training update, say a LoRA, to an update on the components, such that the result is a faithful decomposition of the new model?

Q1 is that question; the others break it down. The three being worked on come first: Q1 and Q5, the two parallel lines (section {{n:next}}), and Q11, whether the optimiser matters, a priority subgoal. The rest follow in number order.

Q1
Is there a simple map that keeps the decomposition faithful to the updated model, with Δ not absorbing the update? The weak version; the pass criterion. The original question, open; one of two parallel lines since 2026-10-22 (user).
Partly. Teacher training (the subcomponents trained to copy the fine-tuned model, Γ and Δ frozen) is the best answer so far: without labels it keeps the behaviour and passes on three trigger tasks and a cipher the model lacked (E34, E42), and on two teachers where doing nothing fails (E47). A fine-tune with no trigger is copied badly by Adam, a rank-8 LoRA's copy passes, and plain SGD's is unresolved (E39, E47, E49). On the 67M the line closed on 2026-11-02 (your choice; claims table, E34). On Qwen3-1.7B no masked test can yet pass a placement (E79, E80), so D1's test is the clean-model table, which fails wrong changes at the update's norm (E75). Least squares misses that table narrowly on three small reinforcement-learning updates (E93, provisional); teacher training runs on every update next.

Q5
After a fine-tune, the frozen Γ names different pieces on the fine-tuned model than on the original one: re-gating. Is that difference a true description of how the model changed, and what does it say about the update? One of the two parallel lines. About mechanism, not the pass criterion.
Partly. The new values account for most of a single-trigger fine-tune's gain, but the same share appears for a fine-tune that learned nothing (E5, E12, E36), and restoring the pieces Γ switches on measures relay, not kind (E38, E50). The ratio and the share put every built-to-reuse run above every built-to-be-new one on two of three families of saved runs (E60). On the small model the share follows whether the trained answer was the original model's own guess, and one cut on that probability sorts all eight maps (E58). On Qwen neither VPD's readings nor the carrying neurons tell new from existing (E94, E109). Elicitation is mechanistic (your decision, 2026-11-02), and a new circuit of old pieces reads as elicited, so the share is a candidate to score against a causal check, not the definition.

Q11
Does the optimiser matter? SGD and Adam on the same tasks, both as component training's optimiser and as the fine-tune optimiser whose update the splits consume. A priority subgoal since 2026-10-21 (user). Its runs are E21, analysed 2026-10-22, with follow-ups E22, E23, E26 and E27.
Neither the optimiser nor the size of the update decides alone (E21). In component training SGD looks better early and Adam later (E21, E26); when a finished update is split, small SGD updates keep more behaviour than Adam ones (E22, E31). Whether teacher training needs Adam is unresolved: plain SGD passes at the six smaller cells and fails at rate 0.03 run to step 1,000 (E34, E47, E49).

Q2
Which operation carries an update onto the subcomponents and keeps the decomposition faithful: the rescaling split, the least-squares or scaled adjoint split of a finished update, or component training?
Answered on emoticon: component training with Adam is the only one that keeps the behaviour and faithfulness with Δ untouched, to step 1000 (E16). The rescaling split keeps none of the behaviour, the least-squares split pushes much of the update into Δ, and the scaled adjoint split loses the behaviour as training goes on. Of the splits, the scaled adjoint split is the most promising: the only one that always stays faithful with Δ untouched, and on a small SGD fine-tune it keeps part of the behaviour, less as the update grows (E22, E31). Teacher training, added since and not a split, does better (Q1).

Q3
Is a LoRA easier to commute than a full-weight fine-tune?
No, on the point that matters. A LoRA's read side barely moves from its random start, so it lines up with the subcomponents no better than a random update does, while a fine-tune lines up better (E9, E14). Its split passes the adversarial check where the large fine-tune's fails, but leaves more of the update in Δ (E16). This comes from the LoRA's form, not its optimiser (E27).

Q4
Does it matter whether the behaviour is new to the model? The "new capabilities" branch.
Partly. Newness has not been seen to break faithfulness as the Pile checks read it: component training learns a cipher the model has never seen and passes once training settles (E10, E21), and teacher training copies a cipher fine-tune and passes with Δ untouched (E42). But a new behaviour takes another route: its gain runs through subcomponents Γ keeps off at the trigger, so the model built from those Γ marks important keeps almost none of a random cipher, and no map with Γ frozen avoids that (E12, E42). The pass stays the ordinary-text reading, the cue reading beside it (E48).

Q6
Why does the adversarial check fail where the stochastic one does not? Is the decomposition brittle?
Answered in part. It is not that the changes cancel each other out (E17), nor that the original subcomponents carry the damage (E20). The adversary's extra damage comes from removing alive and dead subcomponents together: in the original model those two removals offset each other, and after a large update they no longer do (E20, E23). The verdicts do not depend on how long the adversary searches. Why the offset is lost is open.

Q7
Can the measurements see what they claim to? Noise floors, blind spots, positive controls.
Fixed twice, still open in places. The adversarial noise floor depends on the GPU (E8, E11).

Q8
Should the map act on each training step rather than on a finished run?
No, for the adjoint form, on one task. Splitting each training step as it happens, with the subcomponents carried along, keeps the weights exact but leaves more of the behaviour in Δ than splitting the finished update once (E19). On small fine-tunes it passes the adversarial check but still loses that comparison (E25). Component training, per-step by construction, is the per-step map that works. Teacher training chained over three checkpoints ended within the filed tolerances of one copy, with a larger weight change (E41).
