Cheaper methods beside the decomposition

Baselines

Why baselines. The re-gating share needs a trained decomposition, which is expensive. Each baseline here is a cheaper explanation of what the share picks up: if a baseline separates reuse from new as well, the decomposition adds nothing. This section holds the two comparisons run so far. The first is current; the second is the earlier record it grew from.

The baselines. Prior: how likely the original model already was to give the trained answer; it needs no fine-tune. Steering: add the fine-tune's average activation change to the original model. Neurons: the share's own swap done on MLP neurons, keeping the original weights and using the fine-tune's firing (whole width; "half", with the neurons' input weights also original; or a top set). Untrained decomposition: the share read on each matrix's plain SVD. Update size and effective rank: how big the weight change is and how many directions it spreads over.

1. On nine constructed 67M pairs, only the share and the prior point the right way every time

  • The prior is the only rival. It still fails to explain the share fully: on the two pairs made with the prior matched, the share separates the sides anyway, and across the nine pairs the share's gap sizes do not track the prior's (rank correlation 0.02).
  • Steering reverses on three pairs because its strength is searched; added at the fine-tune's own size the reversal goes away.
  • The decomposition's training matters. On a plain SVD the share's gaps fall from 0.51–0.69 to 0.07 or less.
  • Size and rank do not explain it. Where update size differs, the new side has the bigger update.

The card E133 has the pair-by-method grid and the four real-against-shuffled-twin pairs, where the share separates only one.

2. The same swap on neurons reverses where the decomposition separates (record of 2026-11-06)

On Qwen3-1.7B the picture was the other way round (E118, before the newer decomposition of E131). VPD's readings did not beat cheap numbers there:

  • Which updates pass the least-squares split's faithfulness table (E93) is ordered by update size, by gain and by the carrying-neuron share, and not by VPD's share left in Δ or by steering.
  • VPD separates the template fine-tune from the fictional-facts one well beyond seed spread, but so do gain, steering and the carrying-neuron share.
  • Nothing, VPD or baseline, separates RL from its shuffled-reward control at step 100.

So the decomposition's edge over baselines is a 67M finding so far. Since then the share has been read on Qwen's newer decomposition, where it sits at a ceiling (E132, E144).
