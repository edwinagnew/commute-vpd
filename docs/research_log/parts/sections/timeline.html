2026-10-14 to 2026-10-27, at a glance

Timeline

What each day was about, for skimming. The cards (section {{n:experiments}}) and the changelog have the detail.

  • 2026-10-14 · Set-up

    Question made precise and the testbed chosen. First two experiments: rescaling the existing subcomponents does not explain an update; training the subcomponents directly looks promising (E1, E2).

  • 2026-10-15 · The checks were blind

    Found that VPD's standard checks cannot see the behaviour we train. Built task-level measurements and the rule that every metric names its blind spots (E3, E4).

  • 2026-10-16 · Measuring where the gain goes

    The route split: a way to say which part of the decomposition carries an update's effect.

  • 2026-10-19 · Many tasks, first patterns

    Ran across a ladder of tasks, LoRAs and a cipher the model lacks (E5–E11). Settled the success criterion. Main pattern: the gain mostly comes from Γ switching existing subcomponents on, not from them changing.

  • 2026-10-20 · LoRA split and Γ refit

    Built the LoRA split the user had been asking for (E14). Old and new behaviours take different routes (E12). Started retraining Γ (E15). Fixed the project's vocabulary after a terminology review. This log started.

  • 2026-10-21 · Re-gating takes centre stage

    No split of a finished update passes; training the subcomponents does, on one task (E16). Ruled out cancellation as why the adversarial check fails (E17, E20). First evidence that one fixed change to Γ carries the switching (E18). Found that an early grid had wrongly ruled out plain SGD, so started comparing SGD and Adam (E21).

  • 2026-10-22 · Two lines, small updates

    Most failures turn out to be about large updates; small ones pass every split (E22–E27), and the scaled adjoint split of a small SGD fine-tune keeps the emoticon behaviour on two seeds, though not on two word tasks (E31). One fixed shift of Γ, applied everywhere as a map, keeps the trigger behaviour but breaks faithfulness elsewhere (E30). Adopted the fixed-shift hypothesis and tested it on new tasks: one change for all inputs failed on the copy task (E28), but one change per cue word works everywhere tested (E32, E33). Split the work into two parallel lines, re-gating and commute, and cut pod set-up from 15 to about 3 minutes.

  • 2026-10-23 · Teacher training works; per cue, tentatively

    Teacher training, the user's technique, copies a finished fine-tune onto the subcomponents from the fine-tuned model alone and stays faithful on three tasks, even from ordinary text (E34). The surprise: the copies learn the emoticon behaviour with every trigger removed from their text, by a weight change that is not the fine-tune's. The fixed-shift hypothesis is restated as one fixed change per cue, tentatively, and the scaled adjoint split keeps an SGD fine-tune's behaviour only while the update is small, which a second training seed confirmed (E31). The 32-pair cipher failure belonged to the step it was read at (E10).

  • 2026-10-26 · Teacher training holds on triggers, not yet on a broad fine-tune; re-gating without cues

    Teacher training's passes survive a second batch order and an adversary eight times longer (E34), but a teacher-trained decomposition of a fine-tune with no trigger keeps the behaviour and fails the adversarial check, provisionally (E37), and the check's verdict on a fine-tune turned out to depend on the adversary's random start (E34). On 2026-10-27 the user decided to keep the paper's one start and 20 steps for the check (readings stay lower bounds), and to answer the failure with E37 Amendment 1: train longer, masked teacher training, an adjoint-split start. On the re-gating line, the change in Γ's values follows how the original model treats each position better than a cue word does: on the toy tasks, and for three-quarters or more of the switching on a lowercase-text fine-tune (E35, E36). Each importance value alone recovers far less than positions the original model treats alike (E36); text within each value's room is untested, so the restatement goes to the user with two caveats.

  • 2026-10-27 · Training longer does not rescue teacher training on a broad fine-tune; the switched-on pieces already work before the fine-tune

    Trained four times longer, or started from the adjoint split, the teacher-trained decomposition of the lowercase fine-tune still reads about twice the original's damage under the adversarial check (E37, Amendment 1); VPD's masked losses failed their own control. On the re-gating line, removing the pieces Γ switches on from the fine-tuned weights takes a third to a half of the gain on two of three tasks, far more than random pieces, but removing them from the original model already takes a sixth to a quarter, so the test cannot say how much of the fine-tune runs through them (E38). Both next steps went back to the user.
