2026-10-14 to 2026-10-28, at a glance

Timeline

What each day was about, for skimming. The cards (section {{n:experiments}}) and the changelog have the detail.

  • 2026-10-14 · Set-up

    Question made precise and the testbed chosen. First two experiments: rescaling the existing subcomponents does not explain an update; training the subcomponents directly looks promising (E1, E2).

  • 2026-10-15 · The checks were blind

    Found that VPD's standard checks cannot see the behaviour we train. Built task-level measurements and the rule that every metric names its blind spots (E3, E4).

  • 2026-10-16 · Measuring where the gain goes

    The route split: a way to say which part of the decomposition carries an update's effect.

  • 2026-10-19 · Many tasks, first patterns

    Ran across a ladder of tasks, LoRAs and a cipher the model lacks (E5–E11). Settled the success criterion. Main pattern: the gain mostly comes from Γ switching existing subcomponents on, not from them changing.

  • 2026-10-20 · LoRA split and Γ refit

    Built the LoRA split the user had been asking for (E14). Old and new behaviours take different routes (E12). Started retraining Γ (E15). Fixed the project's vocabulary after a terminology review. This log started.

  • 2026-10-21 · Re-gating takes centre stage

    No split of a finished update passes; training the subcomponents does, on one task (E16). Ruled out cancellation as why the adversarial check fails (E17, E20). First evidence that one fixed change to Γ carries the switching (E18). Found that an early grid had wrongly ruled out plain SGD, so started comparing SGD and Adam (E21).

  • 2026-10-22 · Two lines, small updates

    Most failures turn out to be about large updates; small ones pass every split (E22–E27), and the scaled adjoint split of a small SGD fine-tune keeps the emoticon behaviour on two seeds, though not on two word tasks (E31). One fixed shift of Γ, applied everywhere as a map, keeps the trigger behaviour but breaks faithfulness elsewhere (E30). Tested the fixed-shift hypothesis on new tasks: one change for all inputs failed on the copy task (E28), one change per cue word works everywhere tested (E32, E33). The work split into two parallel lines, re-gating and commute.

  • 2026-10-23 · Teacher training works; per cue, tentatively

    Teacher training, the user's technique, copies a finished fine-tune onto the subcomponents from the fine-tuned model alone and stays faithful on three tasks, even from ordinary text (E34). The surprise: the copies learn the emoticon behaviour with every trigger removed from their text, by a weight change that is not the fine-tune's. The scaled adjoint split keeps an SGD fine-tune's behaviour only while the update is small, which a second training seed confirmed (E31).

  • 2026-10-26 · Teacher training holds on triggers, not yet on a broad fine-tune; re-gating without cues

    Teacher training's passes survive a second batch order and an adversary eight times longer (E34), but a teacher-trained decomposition of a fine-tune with no trigger keeps the behaviour and fails the adversarial check, provisionally (E37). The user kept the paper's one start and 20 steps for the check. On the re-gating line, the change in Γ's values follows how the original model treats each position better than a cue word does, for three-quarters or more of the switching on a lowercase-text fine-tune (E35, E36).

  • 2026-10-27 · Training longer does not rescue teacher training on a broad fine-tune; putting the switched-on pieces back undoes part of a narrow fine-tune

    Trained four times longer, or started from the adjoint split, the lowercase fine-tune's teacher-trained decomposition still reads about twice the original's damage under the adversarial check (E37, Amendment 1). Random moves of the same size pass and a rank-8 LoRA's trained decomposition passes, but an SGD fine-tune's fails like Adam's (E39). On the re-gating line, putting the switched-on set's trigger outputs back to their original values takes back about a fifth of the gain on two of three tasks, and no random, null or already-active set does (a target-ranked set does, the next day) (E38).

  • 2026-10-28 · Teacher training copies a new behaviour, outside the subcomponents Γ marks important, and sits at the pass line on the earliest update; Qwen3-1.7B's first update runs

    Teacher training copied fine-tunes that teach the 67M model a word cipher, passing with Δ untouched, yet at the trigger the subcomponents Γ marks important carry almost none of it; every masked version of teacher training broke its own nothing-to-copy control, so no masked copy was made (E42). On a fine-tune stopped at step 10, three emoticon copies gave one pass and two inconclusive readings, accepted as at the pass line (E41); plain SGD fails (E34). Teacher training every 100 steps kept the behaviour at all seven checkpoints (E41 Amendment 2); putting the rest of the fine-tune into Δ afterwards fails the check, so teacher training's own state stays the map (E43). Re-gating: E38 ended with a Γ-free, target-ranked set taking back as much of the gain as Γ's switched-on set, and a red team found the line's founding share reads the same for a fine-tune that learned nothing; the user adopted a reset plan (E46) whose stage 0, at $0, includes a direct-write null that can retire the reading. A red team of five commute-line claims weakened all five; cheap tests follow (E47). On Qwen, a re-plan holds E40's rerun and teacher training until E44 (planned) checks that the pass test can fail bad placements, then E45 trains four matched updates, two of known kind; "GSM8K is the strengthening case" is withdrawn.
