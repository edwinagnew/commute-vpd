Re-gating: what it is, and whether it can be trusted

What. Q5. The moved pieces carry at most 11% of the gain in every run route-split so far, all of them Adam component training or finished updates. On tasks the model already does, most of the rest comes from Γ switching existing pieces on (E5's figure); on tasks it lacks, pieces Γ keeps off carry it instead (E10, E12). Before re-gating can stand as a reading of the re-weighting hypothesis it has to survive three controls, each through the design check before anything else on this line.

The controls, first and in order. (1) Mechanism or leak: Γ reads the updated model's activations, so a test is needed that tells "the old pieces, switched on, compute the behaviour" from "Γ's input already carries it and the masks pass it through". Designed as E18 (reports/2026-10-21_plan_regating-leak.md): on do-nothing states of emoticon and two ladder tasks, how much of the re-switching effect returns when the changed importance values are applied at the trigger only, as one pattern shared by every window, or as a different-target fine-tune's pattern; and whether the switched-on set is the model's own machinery for the token. Run as E18 (section 5). (2) Faithfulness under Γ: the user's definition needs the pieces to still describe the model, so re-gating is read only on states that pass, against the same update's do-nothing. (3) What the switched-on set did at the original model, with enough Pile rows to see it.

What E18 found. One fixed pattern, the updated model's mean importance values at the trigger, gives back 0.70 to 0.86 of what each input's own values give, above its reference on the original model, on all three tasks; random sets give back nothing and a different target's pattern lowers the target. The filed pattern, the original values plus one shared change, read 0.72 and 0.73 on emoticon, but 0.40 and 0.41 on " which" then " was" and 0.62 on " we" then " just": between the mechanism bar of 0.7 and the leak bar of 0.3. No leak is seen, but the filed test failed, in a band the plan did not interpret. Report: reports/2026-10-21_analysis_regating-leak.md.

What the amendment found. The gap between the filed and mean patterns on the word tasks is 67 to 81% the original values' input-to-input variation and 19 to 33% the clip. About half the gap is what averaging the original values does with no change at all. No pattern in the amendment reads the updated model on the held-out input, yet a fixed change recovers at least half the effect on every task, 0.70 to 0.86 on averaged values. So the filed test's miss is how the change meets each input's own values, not information about the input: a mechanism reading. One caution: the switched-on set alone recovers 0.07 to 0.32, so most of the effect is spread over many small changes, which no random null has tested yet.

What the user decided, and what followed. The mechanism reading is the leading hypothesis, not backed, and is to be tested harder (reports/2026-10-21_decision_mechanism-reading.md). H: one change to the importance values, the same for every input, specific to which subcomponents receive it, and enough on its own. Amendments 2 and 3 tested each part. The first two survived: shuffled, the change recovers nothing, and another window's updated values do 0.89 to 1.00 as well as the window's own. The third failed as filed: the input-varying remainder alone recovers 0.16 to 0.31, at the leak line on " which" then " was". Also new: the subcomponents the update turns down carry about as much as the switched-on set. Amendment 4, the user's choice, took the remainder from another window: it recovers 0.82 to 0.96 as much as the window's own, close to the original model's averaged values, so the remainder is mostly an averaging baseline and 0.01 to 0.05 of the effect is input-specific. The failure of the third part stands. The user was "sort of ok" (2026-10-22) to work with the restated H, as a working hypothesis and not a finding: one fixed change carries most of the switching effect, and inputs' own variation adds a few hundredths. Still open: whether the turned-down subcomponents are the original model's for competing tokens. Report: reports/2026-10-21_analysis_regating-leak.md, Amendments 2 to 4.

What each reading means. A leak drops the line and sends the work back to Q1. A mechanism that also holds on a larger model with a real post-trained sibling (Q9) makes re-gating the project's main result and a candidate signal for watching post-training.

Is component training's failure the operation's or Adam's?

What. Q11, and through it Q1. Component training uses the run's optimiser, either SGD or Adam, and the user made comparing the two on the same tasks a priority subgoal on 2026-10-21, both as component training's optimiser and as the fine-tune optimiser whose update the splits consume. Every component-training verdict since E4 used Adam at one learning rate, tuned on emoticon, and every split was of an Adam update (section 9). E4's follow-up found plain SGD learning emoticon within the off-target limit, so the optimiser can now be varied.

What has run. Seven cluster jobs, filed as E21 and analysed (reports/2026-10-22_analysis_sgd-vs-adam.md): neither the optimiser nor the size decides alone. A small fine-tune passes under do-nothing and the least-squares split whichever optimiser made it, so there the size of the change matters. Component training at step 10 favours SGD on the word tasks; at steps 100 and 1,000 Adam at its usual rate passes " it" then " is" and " which" then " was" while SGD fails them at step 1,000. On the cipher, a lower Adam rate passes 32 pairs and SGD fails, at a size of change that does not explain it.

What each reading means. An optimiser or rate that passes on the failing tasks at steps 100 and 1,000 meets the main line's own rule for moving back, and the commute question returns to first place. Failures under both optimisers make them the operation's. Adam component training meets the rule on two failing word tasks and emoticon, one seed; the 20-step reading is not a budget artefact (E20, third amendment). Whether the main line moves is the user's decision, not yet taken. Open: a second seed, an H100 re-read of the near-line verdicts, and the size and damage rules tested on new states.

Ranked, with costs: reports/2026-10-21_review_sgd-finding-influence.md. Order approved by the user on 2026-10-21: the ladder comparison (item 3a) runs after item 1, the SGD state's checks, which is its control.

Why the adversary breaks component training where the other checks do not

What. Q6. Component training's decomposition fails VPD's adversarial check on three of five ladder tasks while the stochastic, CI-masked and unmasked checks move far less (E11's figure). The 2026-10-20 analysis found the damage needs a selective mask, and the lead explanation was cancellation: opposing changes over many subcomponents that only reconstruct the model in full.

What it found. Not cancellation (E17, section 5). On E11's saved states the changes add coherently, a random half costs about a quarter of the full revert, and removing exactly the fraction of the changes the adversary removes costs 4 to 7% of its extra damage on the failing states, the same as a random pattern. The rest was inferred to be the original subcomponents; E20 measured that and refuted it. Report: reports/2026-10-21_analysis_cancellation.md.

What followed. The complement revert ran as E20: the original subcomponents carry none of the excess on three of four failing states and a fifth on the fourth; its amendment split the rest: the excess is the interaction between removing the alive and the dead subcomponents, Δ alone negligible, and the adversary removes less of the dead block after the update, not more. Next, through the design check: the original model's saved levels applied at the updated models, and a stronger adversary at the original model (reports/2026-10-21_analysis_cancellation-complement.md, Amendment 1, What next). Still not run: component training against the fine-tune at matched off-target KL. Amendment 2 then ran both: the original model's mask costs about the same at the updated models, so each failing model's search finds a different mask; its climb to 14 nats by 100 steps came from a fixed step of 0.1. A third amendment, with the VPD paper's step rule, found every verdict tried reads the same from 20 to 160 steps, so the 20-step verdicts stand.

Then

Is the cipher failure about the update's size or the number of associations?

What. Q4. Component training learns a cipher the model lacks and stays faithful for one new pair (adversarial ratio 1.20) and for the identity control (1.19), but not for 32 pairs (2.81), where the split reads 39 (E10). Four causes are confounded in that failure: the update's size, its off-target damage (five times the identity control's), the number of associations, and the scored step. Until they are separated, "faithfulness breaks somewhere between 1 and 32 new associations" cannot be read as a statement about new capabilities.

How it is tested. The Pile harness on the states E10 saved, with no new training: the identity states at steps 100 and 300, where the update is as large as the 32-pair state's, and the 32-pair states at steps 100 and 300. A ten-minute job. If the number of associations is the cause, the next runs sweep the pair count between 1 and 32 to find where the check breaks, repeat with a second random bijection, and put a LoRA on the same tasks so the LoRA split has a new-capability case.

What each reading means. An identity state that stays near the floor at the 32-pair update's size while the 32-pair state fails says the content of the update breaks the decomposition, which is the "new capabilities" branch of the question. An identity state that fails at matched size says the size does, and the cipher result is an update-size result, not a novelty result. A 32-pair state that passes at step 100 or 300 says the scored step was the problem.

Source: reports/2026-10-19_analysis_key-value-task.md; parked by the user on 2026-10-19 as "trying to do too much at once", so it goes through the design check again before it runs.
