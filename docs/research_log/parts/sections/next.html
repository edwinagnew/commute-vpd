Re-gating: what it is, and whether it can be trusted

The working hypothesis (user, 2026-10-22; restated 2026-10-23, tentatively; not a finding). After a narrow fine-tune, the part of the gain that comes from Γ switching existing subcomponents at the trigger is carried mostly by one fixed change to Γ's importance values per cue: the same for every input with that cue, aimed at particular subcomponents, partly shared when cues share an answer. "Cue" is the trigger token, which real post-training does not have.

How it got here. One change for every input held on three tasks and two small fine-tunes, did nothing when shuffled among subcomponents, and worked as well from another input's values (E18, E24). It failed on the copy task and the ciphers (E28), and one change per cue word fixed that on every task (E32, E33). The change sits in the MLP matrices and is not one direction, one move of Γ's input, or the word's subcomponents on ordinary text (E32).

Without cues (2026-10-26). Giving each input the change of the training inputs whose original importance values are most similar does as well as one change per cue added the same way (E35). On a fine-tune with no trigger, lowercased text, the same rule predicted Γ's change at every position better than the current token or any small set of groups, but recovered 0.72 of the switching only at step 10 and about half later, under the filed 0.6 (E36). By the filed tree the change then depends on more than a position's own original values. A restatement, "positions the original Γ treats alike get alike changes", waits on the user (section {{n:decisions}}).

What would kill it. On a fine-tune that learns a real behaviour, the most-similar-positions rule recovering under half of the switching, or doing no better than the current token; or failure on the larger model's post-trained sibling (Q9). Limits. One 67M model, one seed per state, toy tasks plus one synthetic style; the planned code-text fine-tune did not learn (E36).

Next. The filed branch: a rule on Γ's input or on neighbouring positions, against the most-similar rule. Cheap checks first: more fitted positions, and the most-similar rule within one token. Then a fine-tune of a real behaviour that learns, or the larger model (Q9). The toy-task anatomy stays deferred.

Commute: is there a simple map that keeps the decomposition faithful?

What. Q1, the weak version (reading A): a simple map that takes the decomposition and an update to a decomposition of the updated model that passes VPD's checks, with Δ not absorbing the update. Plan: reports/2026-10-22_plan_commute-second-line.md.

What has run (2026-10-22). Small fine-tunes, by SGD or by Adam at a low rate, pass under do-nothing, the least-squares split and the scaled adjoint split at steps 10, 100 and 1,000. The scaled adjoint split of the SGD fine-tune keeps most of the behaviour (0.61 of the target's probability at step 1,000) where Adam's loses it (0.24), against the filed prediction (E22). The step split along the same small paths passes at every step (E25). Component training's verdicts replicate at a second batch order (E26). A LoRA trained with plain SGD sits between noise and a full fine-tune in where it writes, and every split of it passes (E27). The SGD adjoint result holds on a second emoticon seed, with every state faithful and Δ fixed, but not on two word tasks, where SGD at a three times higher rate made an update nearly as large as the large Adam one and lost the behaviour like it; the share of the update the split reproduces does not explain the difference (E31). Swapping the rates (2026-10-23) settled it: within each task the split keeps less the larger the SGD update, and at one size the tasks still differ, so the scaled adjoint split of small SGD fine-tunes does not meet the weak version in general (E31, fourth figure). A second training seed for each of those fine-tunes gave the same answer, so this is now a result on three tasks rather than a single-seed reading (E31).

A fixed shift of Γ as the map (E30). Keeping the subcomponents and Δ as they were and adding the task's fixed change to Γ's values at every position reproduces the trigger behaviour, but the decomposition stops being faithful on ordinary text: the adversary finds many times the damage, and the sparsity cost doubles or triples. So a change that is the same for every input is not a map; it has to depend on the input.

Teacher training (E34, 2026-10-23). Training the subcomponents to copy a finished fine-tune's predictions, Γ and Δ frozen, keeps the behaviour and passes the adversarial check for six fine-tunes on three tasks, trained on the task's text or on ordinary text alone; on the two word tasks the scaled adjoint split of the same update loses the behaviour. The surprise: on ordinary text it learns the emoticon behaviour even with every trigger removed, by a weight change unlike the fine-tune's. Since then (2026-10-26): taking out the rows where the trigger is only partly on lowers the ordinary-text copies a little, so they may need the trigger weakly present; and a LoRA teacher is copied well from task text but only part of the way from ordinary text. A second batch order moves no copy by more than a few hundredths, and under an adversary eight times longer every copy stays at the unchanged model's level while five of the six fine-tunes sit two to ten times above it, so the result does not rest on one seed or one adversary budget; teacher training is the map to carry to the larger model. Report: reports/2026-10-23_analysis_teacher-training.md, Amendments 1 to 4.

The scaled adjoint split's scale (E31, 2026-10-26). Without the fitted scale the adjoint split keeps the whole emoticon behaviour but changes the rest of the model several times more than the fine-tune does. The scale trades behaviour for harmlessness, so the scaled split is fitted to each update, not a fixed map.

Next. The larger model, with teacher training as the map (decided 2026-10-26: the firming-up is done). Two checks travel with it rather than wait here: the longer adversary reads the fine-tunes far above the 20-step check at the same 20 steps (the r1 SGD fine-tune 9.2 against 1.59, and both r0 fine-tunes above the 1.241 line though the 20-step check passed them), cause unknown, so the 20-step verdicts on these fine-tunes look lenient; every trigger token removed from the ordinary text, to settle whether the text-only copy needs the trigger weakly present; longer ordinary-text copies of the LoRA, and the teacher's KL on the ordinary-text rows for a LoRA against a fine-tune. On the shift map, the next step stays the change applied only where the trigger is (E30).

What each reading means. The teacher-trained copies passed at a second batch order and under a longer adversary, so teacher training is the commute line's working map for full-weight updates; the text-only map for a LoRA stays open. If the disagreement between the two adversaries lies in the rows they read, the pass line needs re-deriving; if it lies in how the damage is read out, every "inconclusive" verdict in the project needs re-reading. If a shift applied only where the trigger is passes on the Pile, the map needs the update only to find where to switch; if it fails, maps that leave the subcomponents frozen are exhausted and Γ itself must change.

Is component training's failure the operation's or Adam's?

What. Q11, and through it Q1. Component training uses the run's optimiser, either SGD or Adam, and the user made comparing the two on the same tasks a priority subgoal on 2026-10-21, both as component training's optimiser and as the fine-tune optimiser whose update the splits consume. Every component-training verdict since E4 used Adam at one learning rate, tuned on emoticon, and every split was of an Adam update (section {{n:gaps}}). E4's follow-up found plain SGD learning emoticon within the off-target limit, so the optimiser can now be varied.

What has run. Seven cluster jobs, filed as E21 and analysed (reports/2026-10-22_analysis_sgd-vs-adam.md): neither the optimiser nor the size decides alone. A small fine-tune passes under do-nothing and the least-squares split whichever optimiser made it, so there the size of the change matters. Component training at step 10 favours SGD on the word tasks; at steps 100 and 1,000 Adam at its usual rate passes " it" then " is" and " which" then " was" while SGD fails them at step 1,000. On the cipher, a lower Adam rate passes 32 pairs and SGD fails, at a size of change that does not explain it.

Where it stands. Adam component training at its usual rate passes at steps 100 and 1,000 on " it" then " is", " which" then " was" and emoticon, and SGD fails the two word tasks at step 1,000, at two batch orders (E26). SGD's failures have the same shape of excess as Adam's, and the gap holds to 160 adversary steps (E23). This met the rule the retired "main line" set for moving back; the commute question now runs as its own line (third card). On the fine-tune side, the scaled adjoint split keeps a small SGD update's emoticon behaviour, not a small Adam one's, on two seeds, and keeps less as the SGD update grows, at a task-dependent level (E31). Adam's damage rule held only weakly on its first states filed in advance (E10, amendment). Open: the size rule tested on new states.

Why the adversary breaks component training where the other checks do not

What. Q6. Component training's decomposition fails VPD's adversarial check on three of five ladder tasks while the stochastic, CI-masked and unmasked checks move far less (E11's figure). The 2026-10-20 analysis found the damage needs a selective mask, and the lead explanation was cancellation: opposing changes over many subcomponents that only reconstruct the model in full.

What it found. Not cancellation (E17, section {{n:experiments}}). On E11's saved states the changes add coherently, a random half costs about a quarter of the full revert, and removing exactly the fraction of the changes the adversary removes costs 4 to 7% of its extra damage on the failing states, the same as a random pattern. The rest was inferred to be the original subcomponents; E20 measured that and refuted it. Report: reports/2026-10-21_analysis_cancellation.md.

What followed. The complement revert ran as E20: the original subcomponents carry none of the excess on three of four failing states and a fifth on the fourth; its amendment split the rest: the excess is the interaction between removing the alive and the dead subcomponents, Δ alone negligible, and the adversary removes less of the dead block after the update, not more. Next, through the design check: the original model's saved levels applied at the updated models, and a stronger adversary at the original model (reports/2026-10-21_analysis_cancellation-complement.md, Amendment 1, What next). Still not run: component training against the fine-tune at matched off-target KL. Amendment 2 then ran both: the original model's mask costs about the same at the updated models, so each failing model's search finds a different mask; its climb to 14 nats by 100 steps came from a fixed step of 0.1. A third amendment, with the VPD paper's step rule, found every verdict tried reads the same from 20 to 160 steps, so the 20-step verdicts stand.

The step split along the fine-tune's path

What. Q8. Every split so far was applied once, to the finished update. The step split follows the emoticon fine-tune instead: after each step the pieces take the scaled adjoint split of that step, Δ takes the rest, and the pieces carry forward.

What it found. No better than the least-squares split: more of the behaviour runs through Δ at every step, and it fails the adversarial check at step 1,000. The pieces' movement made no difference; refitting the scale every step doubled Δ's share of the update by the end (E19, section {{n:experiments}}). Dropped as a candidate map for now. Report: reports/2026-10-21_analysis_step-split.md.
