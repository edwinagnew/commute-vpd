Retraining Γ: let the importance function see the updated model

What. Q1 and Q6. Take a state whose pieces already carry the update (the least-squares split, component training, the scaled adjoint state, do-nothing, and the unchanged model as control), hold U, V and Δ fixed, and retrain every parameter of Γ on the released run's own objective minus its faithfulness term: importance minimality, the stochastic-mask reconstruction KL and a persistent-adversary reconstruction KL, on Pile rows, for 3,000 steps from the released Γ. Plan: reports/plans/2026-10-20_plan_gamma-refit.md; code experiments/gamma_refit, src/commute/gamma.py.

What has run. Two of six arms, the split fine-tune on Pile only and the unchanged model as control, plus stage 0 (no training). Stage 0 found the pieces the adversary turns down singly inert at both models: the fragility is a joint effect of many inert changes, not stale importance values. Report: reports/analyses/2026-10-20_analysis_gamma-refit.md.

Re-gating: does Γ's new gating describe the fine-tuned model?

What re-gating is. Γ reads the fine-tuned model's shifted activations at the trigger and switches on other original subcomponents, which then predict the target about half-way. Adam's change is spread thin and every dependence on context sits in Γ, so a change that compounds across layers reads as re-gating: weaker evidence for reveal than the log made it sound.

For and against. For: the switched-on set is target-specific, and fine-tune, LoRA and component training switch on the same one (E6, E18). Against: it carries under half the effect (E4, E6, E18), a fine-tune that learned nothing read the same share (E36), and no Γ fitted to the new model exists (E15).

Next. Putting the switched-on set's trigger outputs back to the original model's values takes back about a fifth of the gain on two of three tasks, and random sets, a set switched on by another fine-tune and other active sets take back about nothing (E38, Amendment 3). Pending the user's choice: test the pieces whose outputs changed most, which is the one alternative left (pending card, section {{n:decisions}}).

Commute: is there a simple map that keeps the decomposition faithful?

What. Q1, the weak version (reading A): a simple map that takes the decomposition and an update to a decomposition of the updated model that passes VPD's checks, with Δ not absorbing the update. Plan: reports/plans/2026-10-22_plan_commute-second-line.md.

What has run (2026-10-22 to 2026-10-26). Small fine-tunes pass under do-nothing, the least-squares split and the scaled adjoint split at every step read (E22, E25), and so does every split of a LoRA trained with plain SGD (E27). Component training's verdicts replicate at a second batch order (E26). The scaled adjoint split of an SGD fine-tune keeps less of the behaviour the larger the update (E31). One fixed shift of Γ applied everywhere keeps the trigger behaviour but fails the adversarial check (E30).

Teacher training (E34, E37). Training the subcomponents to copy a finished fine-tune's predictions, Γ and Δ frozen, keeps the behaviour and passes the adversarial check for six trigger fine-tunes on three tasks, at two batch orders and under an adversary eight times longer (E34). Chosen on 2026-10-26 as the map for the larger model, it is on hold: a copy of a fine-tune with no trigger keeps all of its behaviour but fails the check, even trained four times longer or from a passing start (E37, Amendment 1).

No run is proposed; the question is a pending decision. Still open: two checks carried from E34 (a decomposition from ordinary text with every trigger token removed, and longer ordinary-text ones for a LoRA). The check keeps the paper's one start and 20 steps (decided 2026-10-27, readings are lower bounds). On the shift map, the next step stays the change applied only where the trigger is; if it passes, the map needs the update only to find where to switch (E30).

Is component training's failure the operation's or Adam's?

What. Q11, and through it Q1. Component training uses the run's optimiser, either SGD or Adam, and the user made comparing the two on the same tasks a priority subgoal on 2026-10-21, both as component training's optimiser and as the fine-tune optimiser whose update the splits consume. Every component-training verdict since E4 used Adam at one learning rate, tuned on emoticon, and every split was of an Adam update (section {{n:gaps}}). E4's follow-up found plain SGD learning emoticon within the off-target limit, so the optimiser can now be varied.

What has run. Seven cluster jobs, filed as E21 and analysed (reports/analyses/2026-10-22_analysis_sgd-vs-adam.md): neither the optimiser nor the size decides alone. A small fine-tune passes under do-nothing and the least-squares split whichever optimiser made it, so there the size of the change matters. Component training at step 10 favours SGD on the word tasks; at steps 100 and 1,000 Adam at its usual rate passes " it" then " is" and " which" then " was" while SGD fails them at step 1,000. On the cipher, a lower Adam rate passes 32 pairs and SGD fails, at a size of change that does not explain it.

Where it stands. Adam component training at its usual rate passes at steps 100 and 1,000 on " it" then " is", " which" then " was" and emoticon, and SGD fails the two word tasks at step 1,000, at two batch orders (E26). SGD's failures have the same shape of excess as Adam's, and the gap holds to 160 adversary steps (E23). On the fine-tune side, the scaled adjoint split keeps a small SGD update's emoticon behaviour, not a small Adam one's, on two seeds, and keeps less as the SGD update grows, at a task-dependent level (E31). Adam's damage rule held only weakly on its first states filed in advance (E10, amendment). Open: the size rule tested on new states.
