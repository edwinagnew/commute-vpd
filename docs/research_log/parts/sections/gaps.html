What a critical reader should raise

Gaps and inconsistencies

The criterion and the comparisons

The pass line has no anchor

"About as well" is read as an adversarial ratio under 1.5, a line inherited from E2's filing. On the L40 the unchanged model's own floor reached 21%, so the line sits inside the noise band and the two stated rules disagree there (1.5 against 1.63). Two of seven ladder verdicts sit on the rules (E11): " we" then " just" straddles the line across seeds, and " will" then " have" passes the line but sits just over its card's floor.

The reference fails the pass check

Do-nothing changes nothing in the decomposition and still fails the adversarial check for full fine-tunes (2.18 on emoticon, 2.29 on E14's card, three of five ladder tasks), because the adversary can mask Δ and Δ now holds the update. An SGD fine-tune's do-nothing passes at 1.12, at a 4.7 times smaller size of change, and an Adam fine-tune matched to that size at 1.08; the large one passes at step 100 (E21). Both small fine-tunes pass at steps 100 and 1,000 too (E22). The failure belongs to the large update. So the 1.5 line mixes two things: how much of the update's effect on Pile sits outside the pieces, and whether the pieces are fragile. The split's 5.4 against do-nothing's 2.18 is the fragility finding; the line is not. Every adversarial verdict should be read against the same update's do-nothing on the same card until the criterion has an anchor. Which of the two to judge against is a pending decision.

"Δ not absorbing δW" has no threshold

The split LoRA's residual share (0.22 to 0.31 of the behaviour, half the energy) is judged by feel. The split fine-tune's 0.09 was read as acceptable, 0.22 as "the open reading". GOALS fixes no number.

LoRA vs fine-tune: like for like since E14

E8 scored the LoRA at step 2 and the fine-tune at step 10, and E9 compared cards. E14 puts both on one card at steps 10, 100 and 1000: the split LoRA stays at the floor where the split fine-tune reads 5.2, 1.5 and 2.0. Closed; the earlier reports keep their wording.

Component training is not "the same fine-tune"

Adam on the pieces at another learning rate, half the weight movement, and on the ladder tasks about twice the off-target damage at the same step, which the adversarial excess orders with. On emoticon the sign flips (a third of the damage). Unexplained.

One optimiser behind every result

From E4 to E20 every component-training run used Adam at one learning rate, tuned on emoticon. So the failures on the ladder tasks and the 32-pair cipher are Adam's results, not yet the operation's, until SGD is tried on them. The same holds for the splits: every one, E19 included, was of an Adam fine-tune or an Adam-trained LoRA. Comparing the two is a priority subgoal since 2026-10-21 (Q11). Its analysis (E21) says the verdicts move with the rate and the step as well as the optimiser: at steps 100 and 1,000 Adam's usual rate passes two word tasks it failed at step 10, once its damage elsewhere has faded. A second batch order reproduces those verdicts (E26). On the split side, small SGD and Adam fine-tunes pass every placement to step 1,000 (E22), and a plain-SGD LoRA sits between noise and a full fine-tune, like the Adam one (E27).

Step or run

The least-squares split is applied one-shot to a 10-step update, a first-order map on a non-small change; only component training matches the "continuous" framing. The per-step split, E19, matches it too and does worse than the one-shot least-squares split, so the step-or-run question is answered for the adjoint form only. Along small fine-tunes the step split passes the adversary but still leaves more behaviour in Δ than least squares, except on SGD at step 1,000 (E25).

Measurement

Nothing measures faithfulness at the trigger with power

Pile checks see the old model's account; the task-local adversary saturates. What remains is the route split, dominated by re-switching, whose status as mechanism or leak is unresolved. "Faithful to W + δW" has only been checked on text where the trained behaviour does not occur.

The refit's adversary is weaker than the harness's attacker

Both recipes tried take the released Γ off its own adversarial floor on the unchanged model (2.6 to 6.3; 2.1 then 7.8) while the stochastic and CI-masked checks hold. The refit's persistent attacker plateaus at 0.42 nats on the released Γ where the harness's fresh 20-step attacker reaches 0.70, and stays at 0.43 while the harness's reaches 9.4. So the two attackers disagree about the same Γ, and which one the released run's own adversary resembled is not known from anything on disk. Every refit arm is unreadable until that is settled; a matched attacker is being worked on.

The same state reads differently across runs

The fine-tune's step-10 least-squares state read 5.24 on an RTX A5000 (E14) and 6.96 on two H100 runs (E15, E16), and 5.4 to 7.3 earlier, 5.41 again on the L40 in E19; its do-nothing state 2.29 against 2.52. That failed E16's reproduction gate and left its predictions unscored. Whether the card or the fine-tune re-run differs is not established; the test is one saved update read on both cards.

The half-revert statistic has no functional positive control

E17's control rewrote component training's change as cancelling pieces with 64 times the energy in weight space, and the half revert moved only from 0.31 to 0.45: directions a training step did not choose are nearly inert on Pile, so weight-space and functional cancellation come apart. The half-revert readings stand as readings; a control that cancels in effect, not in norm, is still to be built. The adversary's own revert does not depend on it.

Component training's inside-the-pieces control, to second order

E16's control expected component training's change to lie inside the pieces; rank-8 at step 1000 put 0.0119 outside both sides against a limit of 0.01. The expected source, the second-order term dU dVᵀ, was not computed, and the filed alternative, a bug in the block code or the training hook, was not checked.

Four checks, not five

The sparsity loss follows the weights only while Γ is frozen. Reading A was recorded as five checks; the correction is proposed and not yet in the decisions file.

Seeds

E9: one LoRA seed. E10: one bijection, one adversary seed per state, and E7's floor from another card. E5: optimiser-seed noise on emoticon only. The Δ-share rise at the plateau and the 32-pair failure rest on single runs. Component training on r0, r1 and r2 now has two batch orders, which agree (E26).

Sparse or SGD component training never tried off emoticon

E4 found one-, ten- and six-piece component training faithful, with CI-masked probability 0.98 at the trigger against 0.64 for the diffuse arm; E11's failures are all for the diffuse Adam arm. A cheap, untested question. Plain SGD has since run on the ladder and the cipher (E21); the sparse arms still have not.

The adversary's masks are not shown worst-case

Every adversarial verdict reads VPD's check after 20 sign steps. Run with the VPD paper's step rule, steps times step size kept at 2, the search barely grows: 0.66 to 0.71 nats at the original model from 20 to 160 steps, and every ratio tried within a tenth, so each verdict reads the same at 160 (E20, third amendment). The earlier climb to 14 nats came from a fixed step of 0.1 (E20, fifth figure). What stays open: the masks found are not the worst possible. Keeping the alive block whole beats the 20-step search at the original model (1.08 against 0.66 nats; reports/reviews/2026-10-22_review_vpd-adversary-convergence.md), and on a fine-tune left in Δ the reading depends on the random start: 1.6 from one, 9.2 from another, while the original model reads alike from both (E34, Amendment 5). Every verdict is a lower bound; the user decided on 2026-10-27 to keep the paper's one start and 20 steps, so readings stay lower bounds. E21's step-1,000 SGD and Adam states hold to 160 steps too (E23).

Most failures rest on two large updates

E14 to E20 measured their failures on an Adam 3e-5 fine-tune and on Adam 1e-5 component training at step 10. A fine-tune 4.7 times smaller passes under do-nothing and the least-squares split, and the same component training passes r0 t352 and r1 t534 at steps 100 and 1,000 (E21). So the least-squares split's adversarial failure, do-nothing's failure and the LoRA-against-fine-tune contrast hold only for the large update. Since tested beyond it: small fine-tunes pass every placement at steps 100 and 1,000, the outside-share rise is Adam's at both sizes and the rescaling climb the large update's only (E22); E17's and E20's account holds on five failing states made by plain SGD (E23); E18's fixed change holds on small updates (E24); E19's step split passes along small paths (E25). Still untested beyond it: E15's refit.

Two readings of re-switching

E6: the share is "not target-specific" (0.51 to 0.61 for every arm, nulls included). E10: it runs 0.12 to 0.80 across tasks. Reconciled as: set by what the existing gates can route at a given trigger and format, not by which target is learned there. Easy to trip on.

The record

Asked since 2026-10-14 and answered with component training five times, the LoRA split ran on 2026-10-20: the saved LoRAs split over the subcomponents beside a fine-tune at matched steps, the outside share in four blocks with its exact null, both splits and the scaled adjoint state. One gap remains: the per-subcomponent updates were written to the pod's disk and lost at termination, so the split stage is to be rerun on the cluster before they can be looked at.

Re-weighting is undefined

Every "reveal" and "create" reading uses the route split informally. What a recruited piece must have done before the update for "recruit" to mean more than "any perturbation switches these on" is written nowhere.
