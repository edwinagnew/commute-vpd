The benchmark line's question, in one table

Every method against every fine-tune

The question. Does VPD tell reuse from new better than cheaper methods do, judged against a truth it did not define? Not yet answerable. No fine-tune has VPD and a cheaper method both read, with their controls held, against a truth set by construction or a causal check. VPD's reading separates every pair it read that is labelled only by how it was built, where the truth is an assumption.

How to read a cell. The first line is the verdict. The second gives the reuse side, then the new side, or the count labelled right. Hover for the file and path; the full record is experiments/method_table/results/table.json. The analysis is reports/analyses/2026-11-02_analysis_method-table.md.

A star marks a counting-set cell. Those labels equal task P and task N, so any method that responds to the task scores perfectly. 21 of 21 there is not discrimination. As ported to the 67M, pruning revival and reverse fine-tuning failed their positive control, so the 21 of 21 does not carry over. VPD's counting reading is from a decomposition trained for 400 of 400,000 steps: it labelled all 21 right untrained, so it enters as failing its control and not as a pass.

Three kinds of truth. By construction: the fine-tuning task fixes the answer, and each state passed behaviour and patch checks. By causal check: switching off or patching the mechanism is the label. Only by assumption: built to elicit is how the task was built, not the causal label; these pairs have no causal check.

The methods.

  • r (VPD). Share of the fine-tune's log-odds gain that comes from changed causal importances with the original subcomponents' weights held fixed.
  • Stand-in ratio (VPD). The gain reached when the original causal importances stand in for the original weights, over the whole gain.
  • Steering. One mean residual shift per block, added to the original model; share of the gain it reaches.
  • Neuron patching. The top neurons by activation shift take the fine-tuned activations; share of the gain, and how many neurons reach half.
  • Prompting. The original model shown demonstrations of the map in context; share of the gain reached.
  • Pruning revival. Jain et al.'s Algorithm 3: remove the units that matter most to the fine-tuned behaviour; share of the old behaviour that returns.
  • Reverse fine-tuning. Fine-tune back toward the old behaviour; how fast it returns.
  • Readout, 64 values. A linear readout over the pair's values, trained on Pile text; share of the gain lost when its direction is removed.
  • Readout, whole vocabulary. The same with a linear readout over every token.
  • Neuron sparsity. Five counts of how few neurons the update or its activation shift sits on.
  • Recipe or size. A labeller that reads only the learning rate, the steps or the size of the weight change.
  • Answer probe. A probe on the original model's residual that reads only which answers the state gives.

What stays attached. Neuron sparsity is a null of a low-power rule that compares one task with one task. The 64-value readout is unreadable as filed. The copying-heads fine-tunes leave r unscored as filed, with verdict copy_not_elicitation.
