The question. The method table's rows on the 67M take their truth from how the task was built: a copy map is assumed to strengthen what the model already does, a random map to teach something new (Q5). You chose a causal label before the harder counting test, since only a label that owes nothing to the construction can score VPD against cheaper methods.

What we did. A context patch: take two contexts with the same key and target, put the original model's activations from one into the other at the answer position, and ask how much of the original model's response a fine-tune passes through (a ratio of 1 means the same, above 1 stronger, below 1 weaker). Beside it, the share of neurons whose removal takes away the gain. Both were checked on the original model against hand-built references: a table written by hand into the last block (new by construction), a scaling-up of the original model's own neurons (reuse by construction), and a plain sharpening. The job stopped at its filed control; you then chose to read the patch on 14 saved fine-tunes as exploratory, with no rules and the neuron reading off.

What we found. The references were unusable: scaling up even 4,096 neurons reached at most 4.1 nats where the fine-tunes gain 12 to 21, and the hand-written table fit badly, so nothing of known kind has read high on the patch. The neuron reading is dead here (a third of the network is needed for one nat, and the hand-written table loses 0.77 to 0.95 of its gain, more than random neurons).
