Does the working definition, read as the theory note writes it, tell reuse from new on saved fine-tunes?

2026-11-18 · cell P1.30, run 2 of 2, no independent check · experiments/rho_saved_items

The question. The working definition says a fine-tune reuses the original model M to the extent that the parts it needs keep M's rules. Read exactly as the 2026-11-18 theory note writes it, does that number separate a reuse member from its twin by more than the twin's larger weight movement does? It was filed as two rivals: the new tables live in the attention heads (the reuse order), or the gaps just follow update size.

What we did. Every neuron and head is lined up with the same unit in M. A part keeps its rule when, at each input setting, it writes in the same direction as in M (squared cosine, 1 is always the same). Retention is the plain mean over the parts the fine-tune needs, pooled over both models' settings. We read it on the 20 saved fine-tunes of E196's four counted pairs (two seeds each), E195's rebuilt circuit and E206's grafts, beside today's aggregate score, a size-aware distance, activation patching, M-only settings and the zeroed parts restored.

What we found. The controls read where the rule says: do-nothing "no change", a circuit turned up two-fold and ten-fold 1, appended grafts 0, new neurons in M's own slots 0.002. Every trained fine-tune keeps 0.999 or more, and the reuse-minus-twin gaps rank by the twin's extra update (case 5.0e-4, repeat 9e-5, key tables about 2e-5) at both seeds, so rival B (update size) won. All alternatives keep that ranking; they differ mostly on the rebuilt circuit (0.995, 0.97, 0.91). Heads alone are under 1% of the parts and read 0.976 to 0.995; read alone they show the reuse order (identity/random-map gap 0.0036 and 0.0039 against repeat 0.0007 and -0.0024), which is reported, not a filed rule.

What it means. As written, the definition does not separate reuse from new on these pairs: the plain mean is dominated by thousands of barely changed neurons. It stays tested, not adopted. Three wording questions, on how a neuron's rule is scored, whether shared silence counts and whether parts are weighted, wait on you (see the pending decisions).
