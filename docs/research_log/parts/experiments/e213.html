How low can the working definition's reuse score go on trained fine-tunes, whatever circuit is found?

2026-11-18 · cell P1.34, no independent check · experiments/r_bounds_e208

The question. E208 read the working definition's score near 1 on every trained fine-tune (0.999 or more) and its gaps followed update size. Two explanations were filed: the score saturates because every neuron changes a little whatever the distance used (a property of the neuron level), or because the squared cosine is blind to a change of size (a property of the distance). Nobody has found the smallest circuit the definition scores yet, so this reads bounds instead.

What we did. E208 saved, for every neuron and head of 20 trained fine-tunes (five pairs, seeds 0 and 1), how close the fine-tune's mechanism for that part is to the original model's, under four distances. The score is the mean over the parts of a circuit, so a circuit of k parts can read no lower than the mean of the k lowest saved scores. We read that floor at k from 1 to all parts, for each distance, and each pair's reuse side minus its twin at the same k. A CPU read of saved numbers, no model.

What we found. Under the note's squared cosine every circuit of 128 or more parts reads at or above 0.99, and pair gaps stay under 0.01 with one exception at 16 parts, so the first explanation fails. Under the size-aware distance no circuit reaches 0.99 (0.970 to 0.988 over all parts), and the case pair's reuse side reads 0.012 to 0.043 above its twin at every circuit size, so the second holds. Those gaps rank the pairs in the order the twins' weights moved, as in E208.

What it means. Near-1 scores at the neuron level come from the squared cosine, not from the level. But a size-aware distance counts a pure change of gain as a loss, and its gaps still follow update size; pairs matched in size would settle whether it reads reuse. This is a bound, not a verdict under the working definition.
