Does fine-tuning Qwen3-1.7B on TOFU's invented authors teach content it lacks, so that rephrased questions are answered?

2026-11-04 · MATS cluster, $0 · experiments/qwen_tofu · ended

The question. The Qwen comparison needs one update that teaches content the model does not have. We chose TOFU, a public set of questions about invented authors. The unchanged model turned out not to be at chance on it (0.41 against 0.17 by picking one of six answers), so you chose to keep TOFU and measure learning from where it starts.

What we did. We fine-tuned for 33 and 31 minutes at two learning rates, 1e-5 and 2e-5, and read the last step on four things: the multiple-choice share (bar 0.588), recall of the answer's words when the question is asked in other words, the same in the trained wording, and real facts as a control. A diagnosis then re-read the same options under the author's own question, another author's question and no question.

What we found. Neither rate meets the filed recall test: 0.455 and 0.517 on rephrased questions. The model repeats trained answers (0.757 and 0.956 of their new words in the trained wording, 0.23 for authors it never saw), but only 0.30 and 0.38 come back when the question is rephrased. The multiple-choice pass (0.77) says little, since 0.65 appears with no question at all. The own question's edge over no question stayed about flat (0.128 at the start, 0.120 at 1e-5, 0.110 at 2e-5), while its edge over another author's question grew (0.131, then 0.194 and 0.145). That read cannot tell a general form of right answers from familiarity with these answers' wording.

What it means. The update holds new facts in memorised form that carries over only partly to rephrasings, and at the first 200 steps it holds wording and little content. You chose a new plan, training on several rephrasings of each fact (E84), whose plan is being written. Real-fact multiple choice fell from 0.79 to 0.43, largely because answers grew from 2.5 to 13 words.
