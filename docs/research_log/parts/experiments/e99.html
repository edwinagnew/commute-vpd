Does the net share at a located pair read 1 when a fine-tune is forced through the pair, and 0 when it is forbidden?

2026-11-05 · cluster L40 debug, $0 · experiments/definition_verify · provisional, not independently checked

The question. You decided that a new-against-reuse label needs a located mechanism, and the proposed way to read a located pair is its net share: the fall in the fine-tune's gain when the pair is switched off, minus the original model's own fall, as a share of the gain. Does that number read the right answer when the answer is known by construction?

What we did. On the 67M we trained small fine-tunes whose route was fixed: forced through a located pair of attention heads (everything else reset to the original model after every step), or forbidden from it (the pair held at the original model's mean). The copying pair and the lowercase pair came from E97. A read inside a band set by 20 random head pairs counts as zero. We also checked that the lowercase pair's function depends on the input.

What we found. On copying the rule works: forced reads 1.00 on both seeds, forbidden reads -0.05 against bands of 0.14 and 0.17 with 0.94 of the native gain, and the lookup control reads under 0.02. On lowercase the forced fine-tune reaches only 0.61 and 0.64 of the native gain, so the rule has no positive control there; the forbidden arm read -0.96 and -0.85, far below its band, after a stop rule ended training at step 5 against a target measured from a different reference.

What it means. The definition can give a verdict through the copying pair only; lowercase and the other fixed-answer tasks stay "does not apply", now with a measured reason, and the batch decision of 2026-11-05 holds. A new circuit built from old parts still reads as reuse, and the forbidden copy arm partly rerouted through the other copying head (3,4). Whether a forbidden lowercase arm trained to the native gain also reads below band is not measured.
