Does the new-against-reuse definition read the right answer where the answer is known, and what does it say on every old 67M task?

2026-11-05 · cluster L40 debug, $0 · experiments/definition_verify · provisional, not independently checked

The question. You decided that a new-against-reuse label needs a located mechanism, and the proposed way to read a located pair is its net share: the fall in the fine-tune's gain when the pair is switched off, minus the original model's own fall, as a share of the gain. Does that number read the right answer when the answer is known by construction, and what do the refined rules then say about every old 67M task?

What we did. On the 67M we trained small fine-tunes whose route was fixed: forced through a located pair of attention heads (everything else reset to the original model after every step), or forbidden from it (the pair held at the original model's mean). The copying pair and the lowercase pair came from E97; a read inside a band set by 20 random head pairs counts as zero. Then we read every old task again under the refined rules: a capability is admissible at a site only if it merges inputs and a hand-built reader of the input, placed downstream, survives its removal; each state gets its own band. We rebuilt the class map (96 keys sent to four class words by the embedding's leading directions) and its shuffled twin for this.

What we found. On copying the rule works: forced reads 1.00 on both seeds, forbidden reads -0.05 against bands of 0.14 and 0.17 with 0.94 of the native gain, and the lookup control reads under 0.02. On lowercase the forced fine-tune reaches only 0.61 and 0.64 of the native gain, so the rule has no positive control there. The class map fails the admissibility test: removing the class costs the hand-built reader 0.42 against a band of 0.16, and the original model shows no teeth, so the removal deletes the input. Token identity fails the same way.

What it means. Under the refined rules only the copying task gets a verdict ("reuses the copying capability"); every other old task reads "does not apply", "no capability listed", "no admissible site" or "unreadable", each with its reason, and no old task is left on which removing value directions can give a verdict. The batch decision of 2026-11-05 holds. A new circuit built from old parts still reads as reuse, and the 67M has no case that separates the two. The rule changes go to you as a pending decision (section {{n:decisions}}).
