Does the share of an update that lands on the pieces Γ marks important predict how much of a new behaviour the decomposition keeps?

run 2026-10-19 · RunPod RTX 4090 (named in the pod's smoke log) · experiments/task_ladder

The question. When the decomposition's pieces are trained on a new behaviour, how much of it does the decomposition itself keep, and what decides that (Q4, Q5, Q7)? This round's guess was coverage: the share of the first training step's update that falls on pieces Γ already marks as important at the trigger. We also guessed that how rare the target is would not matter on its own.

What we did. We picked six next-word tasks, each a trigger word followed by a target word, such as ` will` then ` be`. They come in three rungs by how likely the original model already finds the target: common, uncommon and rare, a factor of ten apart, with one high-coverage and one low-coverage task per rung. The emoticon task (after the first character of an emoticon, predict o) was the reference. Each task was learned three ways for 10 steps. Component training: Adam moves every alive piece (one whose importance ever rises above a small threshold), while Γ and the residual Δ stay frozen. The gated-off control: the same, but only pieces Γ keeps switched off at the trigger may move, so the moved pieces should carry nothing. Do-nothing: an ordinary fine-tune whose whole weight change is left in Δ, so the pieces never move. The route split then divides the gain in the target's log-odds at the trigger into parts that sum to 1. They are the moved pieces at the old switching; the original pieces switched on differently by Γ, which reads the trained model's activations (the re-gating share); the two together; pieces Γ keeps switched off; and Δ. What the decomposition keeps is the first three together.

What we found. On the two rungs where coverage could be scored, one seed each, the higher-coverage task did not keep clearly more (first figure). The rare pair's coverage gap was too small to score. Rarity was tested on each rung's high-coverage task, which should have kept at least 0.45 of the gain: the rare ` we` then ` just` kept a third and failed. The other rare task, ` in` then ` Fig`, kept 0.66, above that line but below every common and uncommon task. The route split shows why coverage could not work: the moved pieces carry under a tenth of the gain on every task, and most of what is kept comes from Γ switching the original pieces on differently (second figure). That re-gating share came out within 0.16 for all three ways of training, the fine-tune included, and was lower for the rare targets and emoticon (third figure). Each task ran with one optimiser seed and was scored at step 10. On emoticon, other seeds moved each share by 0.013 at most, and at step 30 the tasks kept the same order.

What it means. Coverage measures where an update starts, and that route carries almost nothing, so the predictor was dropped on this evidence: two scorable task pairs, one seed each. The open question became what the re-gating share is. It could be a mechanism the decomposition explains. Or it could be the trained model's computation leaking in through Γ's input, which at the trigger already encodes the target. This run cannot tell the two apart. The recruit test (E6) found a similar share for a fine-tune aimed at another token, so a high share is not on its own evidence that the pieces carry the behaviour. The re-gating line (E18 to E38) took up that question.

Run
Seven tasks, each trained three ways for 10 steps (step 30 also reported), with the route split on held-out text, optimiser-seed noise on emoticon and Pile checks on two tasks. One RunPod pod; the session's tally read $3.69, which may include other pods.
Found
  • Not as expected: on two scorable task pairs, one seed each, higher coverage did not keep clearly more, and the high-coverage rare task kept a third of the gain against the 0.45 expected.
  • The moved pieces carried under a tenth of the gain on every task; Γ switching the original pieces on differently carried most of it for common and uncommon targets (0.75 to 0.92), and pieces Γ keeps off took more for the rare targets and emoticon.
  • The gated-off control read as it should, with the moved pieces carrying nothing, but two other checks had no power. The control that trains only pieces Γ marks important did not learn in 10 steps, and an adversary run at the trigger itself varied far more across seeds than the gains did.
