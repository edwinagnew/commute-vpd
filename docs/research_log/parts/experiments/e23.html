Do the failures left after the switch to SGD break the decomposition the same way the early Adam failures did?

2026-10-22 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/cancellation_sgd

The question. The commute line asks why some updated decompositions fail VPD's adversarial check (Q6). For the early Adam failures, component training at step 10, E20 had placed the extra damage not in the subcomponents' changes but in how removing the alive and the dead subcomponents combine. E21 then switched to plain SGD (Q11) and found new failures. SGD failed at training step 1,000 on two word tasks, where Adam at step 1,000, with a change to the weights of the same size, passes. It also failed on two key-to-value tasks and at a high learning rate (0.1). Is that the same kind of break?

What we did. VPD writes each weight matrix as rank-one subcomponents plus Δ, the part they leave over, and Γ rates each subcomponent's importance on each input. About 9,900 subcomponents are alive, rated important somewhere on the original model's text; the rest are dead. Component training trains the alive subcomponents directly on a task, with Γ and Δ fixed. A word task raises one word after a trigger, such as " is" after " it". A key-to-value task teaches 32 key words each to give a paired word: a random one (32 random pairs) or the key itself (32 copies). The adversary searches for the mask that most changes the output on 128 rows of Pile text, turning each subcomponent down only as far as Γ allows. Its damage is a KL in nats. The adversarial ratio is that damage at a state over the same at the original model: up to 1.24 passes, the original model's own spread across adversary seeds, and above 1.5 fails. The mask falls on two blocks: the alive block (the alive subcomponents) and the dead-and-Δ block (the dead subcomponents and Δ). Hooks applied the mask to one block at a time, splitting a state's extra damage (the adversary's damage at the state minus its damage at the original model) exactly into parts. The key part is the offset: the damage from masking each block alone, summed, minus the damage from masking both at once. At the original model it is 0.90 nats, so the two removals partly cancel.

What we found. On all five SGD failures the largest part of the extra damage is offset the state has lost, as on E20's four Adam failures drawn beside them (first and second figures). As a share of each state's total extra damage, the lost offset is 98 to 116% on four of the five (above 100% because the dead block alone costs a little less than at the original model); the exception is 32 copies, the smallest failure, which keeps most of its offset: the lost part is 73% of its total and the alive subcomponents 33%. Removing the changes alone accounts for at most 2% of the total on every SGD failure (report). On the four step-1,000 states rerun, a search eight times longer leaves each verdict in place: SGD stays near two and a half times the original model, Adam near 1 (third figure).

What it means. E20's account held: on every failing state measured so far, Adam early, SGD late, high-rate SGD and the key-to-value tasks, the lost offset is the largest part of the extra damage, though on 32 copies only 73% of the total, with 33% on the alive subcomponents. Component training never touches the dead subcomponents, so the update must change how removing them combines with removing the alive ones; why is not shown. This rests on one decomposition, one adversary seed per state, Pile rows only and one kind of search. Why the offset is lost for SGD and kept for Adam at the same size is open; the next test was to measure how far each state's changes point along the dead subcomponents' write directions.

Run
E21's saved states rebuilt on the cluster: the five SGD failures (step 1,000 on " it" then " is" and " which" then " was"; 32 random pairs; 32 copies; rate 0.1 at step 100 on " in" then " Fig"), Adam at step 1,000 on the two word tasks, and a passing SGD control at step 100, each under its own 20-step adversary, then longer searches on the original model and the four step-1,000 states. Four MATS cluster jobs (29344, 29496 to 29498), $0; every apparatus check passed.
Found
  • As predicted before the run (this is what "held" refers to), E20's account held on 5 of 5 SGD failures, one adversary seed each: the lost offset is at least half of each total extra damage, 98 to 116% on four and 73% on 32 copies; the control and the Adam states read within 0.02 nats of zero.
  • As predicted, the step-1,000 contrast holds from 20 to 160 steps on both word tasks; the other three failures were not rerun.
  • The changes point the same way rather than cancel: summed, their squared size is 2.5 to 3.2 times the sum of each one's (report).
