Does VPD's adversary fail doing nothing on a larger update?

2026-11-05 · one H200 NVL on Vast, 2.3 h, about $10 · experiments/qwen_tofu_adversary · provisional; the independent check found no bug

Since then. The 2026-11-09 paper plan (a definition of reuse, re-gating against baselines on the 67M, then Qwen3-1.7B) replaced the framing in deliverables and parallel lines used here.

The question. The Qwen map needs a test that can fail a bad placement. On the first fine-tune update (ten maths steps) VPD's adversary passed doing nothing, so nobody knew whether it could see an update left in Δ; it had failed only random or wrong-column changes hundreds of times a real update's size (E85). Does doing nothing fail on a real update about ten times larger?

What we did. We ran the adversary, which sets each subcomponent's strength to hurt the model most, on web text, at two seeds. The update was the TOFU invented-biographies fine-tune trained on one wording of each fact (E81), 9.6 times the first update by norm. Two states: doing nothing (the whole update left in Δ), and the rarely-on half with its remainder in Δ (the update's least-squares split over the half of subcomponents that are least often on, 23% of its squared size left in Δ). The unchanged model ran first as the control and held. The score is the adversary's KL over the unchanged model's at the same seed, with the 67M's bars carried over: under 1.40 passes, over 1.61 fails.

What we found. Both states fail at both seeds, where both sat under the pass bar on the first update. Doing nothing reads about 3 (seeds 2.15 and 4.19) against 1.24 on the first update, and the rarely-on half about 6 against 1.27 (figure below). The rarely-on half, with less in Δ, reads higher than doing nothing at both seeds, so the ratio does not follow the share left in Δ. The adversary's search had not finished, so every read is a lower bound; the lowest single step of either state is still over the bar. The independent check found no bug.

What it means. The test now fails doing nothing, at a size where that update is real, so it fails an update left in Δ at this size. Why is not settled: no saved number tells whether doing nothing fails because masks do more harm on the updated model, or because the adversary can turn down Δ and so switch part of the update off. Everything here is provisional. It read the one-wording update, not the five-wording anchor you chose as the example of new content; size and kind (maths against biographies) are confounded; and no state on this update has passed, so the test is still not shown to pass a good placement. The decision card on what to judge by (section {{n:decisions}}) carries this read.

Run
One H200 NVL pod on a non-datacenter Vast host, 128 rows of 512 web tokens, six adversary starts of about 22 minutes each; the unchanged model reproduces the earlier read to 0.06%.
Found
  • Doing nothing, about 3, and the rarely-on half with its remainder in Δ, about 6, both read over the fail bar at every step; the rarely-on half is higher, which was the less likely side.
  • The size of the fail missed its filed value by about ten times (a ratio near 30 was expected for doing nothing), and the two seeds differ by 2.04; the lean that it fails held and the reasoning behind it did not.
  • What the fail is remains unread: the update's own move on web text is 0.64 nats, and adding it to the unchanged model's read does not reach either state.
Bears on
D1's test of a map: a fail where there was none, and still no pass. Not shown: that size caused it, that the test separates good placements from bad, or anything about the five-wording anchor.
