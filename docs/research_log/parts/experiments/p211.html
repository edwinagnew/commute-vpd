Which simpler methods did we compare the share with, and how do they do on the same pairs?

2026-11-10 · provisional, no independent check · no run, saved results only, $0 · experiments/method_table

The answer. On the nine pairs of fine-tunes built to differ, the re-gating share is higher on the side built to reuse something the model already does, on every pair. No simpler method does that on every pair. Steering agrees on five pairs and reverses on three. The neuron readings agree on at most three. Update size never agrees: where it separates the sides, the new side's update is the larger. The original model's prior points the same way on all eight pairs it was read on, but it has one value a side, so it gives a direction and no call. On the four real-against-shuffled-label pairs the share separates one clearly and reads the other three within 0.1. Sides are labelled as built; this gives no verdict of reused or new.

The pairs. A built pair is two sets of fine-tunes of the 67M model on matching windows, two to eight seeds a side. One side is built to reuse something the model already does, such as copying a token from the window or a map it half knows; the other is built to be new, such as a random map or a cipher. There are nine. A twin pair is a real fine-tune against one trained on the same text with the labels shuffled, so the twin can only memorise. There are four. The labels are the builders' intentions, not findings.

The methods, and why each was chosen. Each stands for a simpler explanation of why the share could read high.

Re-gating share (the method under test). It keeps the original model's subcomponents and weights and changes only which of them the decomposition switches on, and how strongly, to what it reads on the fine-tuned model's activations. The share is the part of the gain that this alone reproduces. It is blind to whether the subcomponents it switches on are interpretable.

Share on an untrained decomposition. The same reading on each matrix's singular value decomposition, with a fixed rule: at each position, keep as many of the largest contributors as the trained decomposition switches on. It stands for "the share comes from the decomposition's training, not from the model". It is blind to whether a better switching rule would recover the gap, since this rule reproduces the original model less closely.

Neuron readings (three forms). They stand for "the decomposition adds nothing over neurons". The whole-width form runs the original MLP weights on the fine-tune's neuron firing; the half form changes only the MLP's input; the top-set form keeps each block's firing only on its largest neurons. All three miss anything the fine-tune changed in attention. The top-set masking moves the original model's answer by up to 3.4 in log-odds, so it is far from either fine-tune.

Steering. Add the fine-tune's mean activation change at the read position to the original model, pooled over all windows or as one vector per key or per word, with layer and strength chosen on training windows. It stands for "a single direction in activations would do as well". The figure shows the form with the wider gap. It is blind to whether the vector writes the answer the way the fine-tune does: on key-value pairs a per-key vector is handed the answer, since each key fixes it.

Prior. The original model's probability of the trained answer before any fine-tune, one value per task map. It stands for "the share only reflects how likely the answer already was". With one value a side it has no spread, so it gives a direction and no call.

Update size. The summed norm of the weight change over all matrices. It stands for "the share only reflects how big the update was". It is blind to the update's direction.

Effective rank of the update. How many directions the weight change spreads over, from its singular values. It stands for "the share only reflects how spread out the update is". It is blind to which directions those are.

How each compares with the share on the nine built pairs. "Same direction" means the method sets the sides apart with the reuse side higher, as the share does; "reversed" means apart with the new side higher; "no call" means the sides overlap or one value a side. Where an earlier run filed its own call for a pair, that call is used; elsewhere two sides are apart when their seed ranges do not overlap, with no extra band, so the numbers in the cells show how small a gap is.

Reading the rows. The untrained decomposition's gaps run from 0.08 below zero to 0.13 above, the share's from 0.39 to 0.69. The whole-width neuron form reverses on copy maps against random maps, by 0.26; the half form reverses only there, by 0.13; the top-set form writes at most 0.05 of any gain. Steering agrees on copy against lookup, copy maps against random maps and copy against cipher at step 10, with gaps of 0.24 to 0.47, and in an older form on both 96-key pairs. It reverses on the two pairs built at equal update size and on the half-known map at the cipher's own step, which is unexplained. The prior follows the share across 24 maps (rank correlation 0.85), yet on copy against lookup the prior differs by a factor of only 1.3 and the share's gap is 0.69. Update size and rank both read no gap on the three pairs whose sides have about the same update size, and the share's gaps there are 0.52 to 0.62.

On the four real-against-twin pairs the share separates the half-known map from its shuffle, by 0.25. On the class map it reads the twin 0.04 higher, and on the two label pairs it reads no gap. There per-word steering, update size and rank all separate the sides, the twin higher each time; the label-pair run traced the steering gaps to the per-word vector carrying the answer, and the case twin trained ten times longer. These readings bear on how the twins were trained as much as on what they learned.

What the share adds. It is the only reading that sets all nine built pairs apart in one direction, and each rival is ruled out on some pair. The prior is nearly matched on copy against lookup and on the prior-matched cipher pair, and the share still separates both. Size and rank are matched on the three equal-size pairs, and the share separates all three. Steering reverses on three pairs where the share does not. Neurons and the untrained decomposition read small or no gaps on most pairs. But no single pair rules out every rival at once. On rank-1 against cipher at step 10 only the prior, one value a side, points the same way. On both prior-matched pairs pooled steering also separates the sides, by 0.41 and 0.70, so a combination, the prior where it differs and steering where it does not, could track the share on these pairs. That combination was not tested, and nothing here shows that the share's signal is reuse.
