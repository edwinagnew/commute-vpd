Do component training's pieces stay faithful on five more next-word tasks, or only on emoticon?

2026-10-19, emoticon rerun 2026-10-20 · RunPod L40S; MATS cluster L40 · experiments/reading_a_coverage

The question. The pass criterion (Q1) asks for a map that keeps the decomposition as faithful to the updated model as it was to the original, without parking the update in the residual Δ. Component training had passed VPD's adversarial check on emoticon (in an earlier split test, E2) and on one common-word task, " will" then " be" (E5). This run asks whether that pass is general, on the five tasks of E5's task ladder, a set of next-word tasks graded by how likely the target already is, that had never been scored on it.

What we did. Each task teaches the 67M model to raise one next word after a trigger word, such as " is" after " it"; the model already gives the target some probability. Component training trains the pieces' own vectors on the task for 10 Adam steps, with Γ (which switches pieces on) and Δ frozen. Do-nothing runs the ordinary fine-tune for 10 steps and puts its whole weight change in Δ. An adversary searches for the mask over the pieces that does the most damage on ordinary Pile text. The adversarial ratio is that damage over the same damage for the unchanged decomposition, so 1 means as faithful as before. Above 1.5 counts as a fail, the line set in E2. Below about 1.14 is noise: within three times the unchanged model's own run-to-run spread in this job (its floor).

What we found. Component training fails the check on three of the five tasks and sits at the line on a fourth (first figure). The report counts do-nothing as more robust under the adversary on four of the five, but only two of those gaps, on " it" then " is" and " in" then " Fig", are larger than the noise. What component training does buy is that the pieces alone, without Δ, still reproduce the model, where with do-nothing they miss by 4 to 18 times the original error on the new tasks (second figure). The tasks with the largest adversarial ratios mostly had the largest change on words other than the target (third figure).

What it means. "Passes about as well" is not a general property of 10-step Adam component training: counting emoticon and " will" then " be", 3 of 7 tasks hold, 1 sits at the line and 3 fail, and the report reads the earlier passes as the favourable cases. The caveat: each task but one ran at one seed, and the unchanged model's own adversary spread reached 21% in one job. Ratios near 1.5 are therefore undecided. The next design question was to compare at matched off-target change instead of matched steps; a later run at more training steps (E21) found that these verdicts are step-10 readings.

Run
Component training and the fine-tune were regenerated at step 10 on the five ladder tasks (one seed each, three on " we" then " just"), checked to match the ladder's own states, and scored on VPD's four Pile checks and its sparsity loss, every number a ratio to the unchanged model with a noise floor measured in the same job. One RunPod L40S job (38 min, $0.70), and an emoticon rerun on the same harness the next day on the MATS cluster (12.5 min, $0).
Found
  • Component training fails the adversarial check on " it" then " is", " which" then " was" and " in" then " Fig", reads 1.38 to 1.55 over three seeds on " we" then " just", and passes on " will" then " have"; the emoticon rerun reproduced E2's pass.
  • The milder checks (random masks, importance-weighted masks, all pieces on) and VPD's sparsity loss mostly stay inside their floors, so they cannot see what the adversary finds; the sparsity loss moves by under 1% on Pile text.
  • Component training changes predictions away from the trigger 1.8 to 2.5 times as much as the fine-tune at the same step, and its adversarial excess mostly orders with that change: a pattern, not a cause.
