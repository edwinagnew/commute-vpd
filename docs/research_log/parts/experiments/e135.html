Does steering separate the copy fine-tune from the lookup fine-tune as widely as the re-gating share does?

2026-11-09 · provisional, no independent check · cluster debug L40, job 56927, about 3.5 minutes, $0 · experiments/steering_copy_lookup

The question. Part 2 asks whether a cheaper reading tells the two sides of a pair apart as well as the re-gating share. The share is the part of a fine-tune's gain in the answer's log-odds that comes from changing only which of the original subcomponents the decomposition switches on, with their weights held fixed. The copy and lookup fine-tunes at a new marker are a pair the share separates widely, so steering was given its fairest form there.

What we did. Steering adds the fine-tune's mean change in the residual stream at one block to the original model, and its share is the part of the gain that vector reaches. We read it in two kinds: one pooled vector, and one vector per key, with layer and strength chosen on separate windows. Three seeds a side, 100 scored windows. The rule, filed first, compares the share's gap with the wider of steering's two gaps. Sides are labelled as built; no verdict on reuse.

What we found. The share reads copy above lookup by 0.69 of the gain. Pooled steering points the same way with a gap of 0.41, copy 0.52 to 0.59 and lookup 0.14 to 0.16. One vector per key raises lookup to 0.63 to 0.69 and leaves copy at 0.61 to 0.65, so that gap all but vanishes. Under the filed rule the share keeps an edge of 0.27, against a smallest gap that matters of 0.1.

What it means. The share's edge holds over one kind of steering only: what steering says about this pair depends on the vector. A likely reason, untested, is that one vector cannot hold 32 different answers while a vector per key can. The layer and strength were chosen on the windows the per-key vectors were fitted on, which favours them.
