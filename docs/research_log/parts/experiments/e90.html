E90copying only

Can the first reuse definition, Jain et al.'s with a located mechanism, label the old 67M fine-tunes as reused or new?

2026-11-05 · provisional; independent checks on three of six runs hold with caveats · cluster debug L40s and CPU, $0 · experiments/new_vs_reuse and five more directories

The question. The old 67M experiments called each fine-tune "existing" (it reuses something the original model does) or "new", but never measured it. The first definition we adopted came from Jain et al. 2024: the original model must hold a capability in a form a straight-line readout recovers, the fine-tune must carry its answer to inputs it never trained on, and removing the capability must take away the fine-tune's gain. On 2026-11-05 you added that a label also needs a located mechanism, a named part the fine-tune runs through. If the old labels cannot be read this way, findings that lean on them are weaker than the log said.

What we did. Six runs on saved 67M fine-tunes, all $0. First we applied the definition to every old task: remove one named capability of the original model (its copying head, or the directions that carry token values at block 1) and read the share of the fine-tune's gain that goes, against random removals of the same size. A hand-built key reader placed after the removal tests whether a loss means more than "the state needs its input token". Then we tried to locate parts without being told where to look: switch off each of the 24 attention heads, or pairs of them, by replacing the output with the original model's average, and also patch the original model's outputs in, part by part. A weight-side number came next. Let each of the 28 parts (24 heads, 4 feed-forward layers) be set back to the original weights one at a time; the sum of the gain shares lost is the "sum of single reverts", low when the gain is fed through barely changed parts and high when it is written into changed ones. Last, a check where the answer is known: fine-tunes forced through, or forbidden from, a located head pair, read by the net share (the fine-tune's fall with the pair off, minus the original model's own fall, as a share of the gain) against a band from 20 random pairs.

What we found. Only copying reads as assumed. The head sweep finds copying head (2,4) on every seed, taking about half the copy gain with lookup spared, and the forced arm reads 1.00 while the forbidden arm sits inside its band. Removing token-value directions costs the copy map more than a random map, on two table pairs, but the hand-built reader loses more than any fine-tune, so the loss says only that the state needs its input token. Lowercase has no head of its own: the pair (1,1)+(1,2) is needed by both models, and its forced arm reaches only about 0.6 of the native gain, so there is no positive control. Every patching read credits block 3's feed-forward layer on copy and lookup alike, which shows where the gain enters the output, not where it is made. The sum of single reverts sorts copy maps (0.22 to 0.40) from key-table maps (0.62 to 0.98), on unseen seeds too, but it tracks that split, not reuse against new.

What it means. On the old tasks the first definition labels copying and nothing else; every other row reads "does not apply", "no capability listed", "no admissible site" or "unreadable", each with its reason, so the old labels stay assumptions. You decided on 2026-11-05 to keep the wording and the assumptions, and on 2026-11-06 to drop the extra rules tested here and reset the definition to Jain's alone. The old tasks lack Jain's shape of a marker plus content, so the next step built pairs with that shape (E112), which also carried the sum of single reverts over. Since 2026-11-09 the definition is under revision and a second wording, reuse at a variable, is tested from E137 on; the readings here hold under the first wording only.

Run
Six experiments on the old 67M fine-tunes, on the cluster's debug L40s and CPU, each a few minutes of GPU time, all $0; planted parts with known answers were read first wherever a locator ran. They were first logged under their own codes, now folded into this card:
  • the definition on every old task, stages 0 to 2 and two amendments, plus the Qwen example updates on paper (E90, experiments/new_vs_reuse);
  • the hand-built downstream key reader and two fresh 32-key tables, job 52507 (was E95, experiments/token_identity);
  • the head and head-pair sweep on copy, lookup, lowercase and cipher, with three cheap nominators, jobs 52551 and 52567 (was E96, experiments/locate_mechanism);
  • four locators, removal, carry patching, edge patching and EAP-IG, jobs 52618, 52619, 52646 and 52785 (was E97, experiments/locator_table);
  • forced and forbidden fine-tunes, the refined admissibility rules and the class-map rebuild, jobs 52787, 52807 and 52895 (was E99, experiments/definition_verify);
  • the sum of single reverts on every saved state, with the emoticon trigger and a site-dependent bar, jobs 52839, 52873, 52883, 52919, 52989 and 53015 (was E100, experiments/fed_or_written).
Found
  • Copying through heads (2,3) and (2,4) is the one label the first definition gives on the old tasks, and it repeats the measurement the label came from.
  • Removing token-value or class directions deletes the input itself, since a hand-built reader that needs only the token loses most; no old task has an admissible site for direction removal.
  • The sum of single reverts separates copy maps from key-table maps on unseen seeds, but a task built to reuse (rank-1) reads written and the emoticon trigger reads fed without copying, so it is evidence beside a label, not one.
