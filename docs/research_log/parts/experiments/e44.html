On Qwen3-1.7B, can the pass test fail a deliberately bad placement of a fine-tuning update?

2026-10-29 to 2026-10-30 · RunPod A100 SXM, then MATS cluster L40 · experiments/qwen_maps

The question. The commute line asks whether a fine-tuning update can be moved into a decomposition's subcomponents so that the decomposition stays faithful and still does what the fine-tuned model does (the weak version), and whether that carries over to a larger model. On Qwen3-1.7B, E40 made the update: ten steps of fine-tuning on maths answers. Before scoring any real way of placing it, the plan checked that the pass test can fail placements that are bad on purpose. A test that passes everything says nothing when it passes a split.

What we did. The decomposition writes each weight matrix as a sum of subcomponents plus a residual, Δ. Its importance function, Γ, reads activations and sets a mask per token: how much each subcomponent is switched on. The masked model runs on the masked subcomponents with Δ off. The pass test reads the share of the update kept: the original decomposition's distance from the fine-tuned model, minus this state's, divided by the original decomposition's distance minus the floor (the original model against its own masked model). 0 means none of the update is kept and 1 means as close as the floor; a state holding the fine-tuned weights needs about 0.97. It also reads two faithfulness ratios (distance from its own model and damage from a saved adversary, each over the unchanged decomposition's; above 1.5 fails). Four bad placements were read, at step 10 (and step 1 for the first run): do-nothing leaves the whole update in Δ; two halves put it on the half of each matrix's subcomponents Γ rarely, or often, switches on for maths text; a random change is of the update's effect size. First run: each state's Γ read that state's own activations. Second run (the user's choice): every state's Γ read the original model's activations.

What we found. First run: do-nothing kept 97% of the update where about none was expected, because Γ reading the fine-tuned model's activations rebuilds the update in its masks (first and second figures; layer cuts, third figure). Second run, masks from the original model: the controls held, and do-nothing now keeps exactly none and fails the own-model ratio (22.8 against a line of 1.5). The often-on half keeps 0.062 of the update and the rarely-on half 0.001; per unit of the update each carries, 0.129 against 0.004, higher on all 128 rows but under the 0.2 gap the test needed. A random change of matched effect keeps 0.005 (fourth figure).

What it means. Neither reading gives a pass test that can tell a good placement from a bad one: with the first, every state holding the fine-tuned weights passes; with the second, even the best placement keeps a sixteenth of what is needed, so the test looks near-unpassable for this update, not toothless (an untested extrapolation). The filed outcome is no teeth, so no split was scored on Qwen and nothing here bears for or against the weak version. The user confirmed on 2026-10-30 that the Qwen line moves to describing where the update lands and which subcomponents Γ switches on (the descriptive rows of the Qwen re-plan).

Run
First run: one A100 SXM pod, $3.66 ($6.14 with a first pod that stopped on a precision rule), stopped by its filed rule before any split was scored. Second run: cluster job 46054 on one L40, 66 minutes, $0, stopped the same way. One seed, 128 rows per text type.
Found
  • With Γ reading each state's own activations, do-nothing and both halves keep about 97% of the update, so the pass test cannot fail a state holding the fine-tuned weights; Γ's masks follow whatever activations they are given.
  • With masks from the original model, do-nothing fails as it should, and the often-on half keeps more per unit carried than the rarely-on half on every row (0.129 against 0.004), but by less than the filed 0.2, so the test has no teeth at step 10.
  • No split was scored, so nothing here bears for or against the weak version on Qwen.
