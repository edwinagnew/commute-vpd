SGD vs Adam (Q11): does the optimiser decide the adversarial verdicts?

2026-10-21 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/sgd_faith, sgd_matched, sgd_finetune, sgd_ladder, sgd_ladder_long, sgd_kv

Neither alone. Adam's component training failed only while it was still damaging the model's other predictions; once that damage faded, the same runs passed at steps 100 and 1,000 (first figure). SGD's verdict followed the size of its change: small changes passed and large ones failed, including where Adam passed at the same size (second figure). Small fine-tunes passed whichever optimiser made them (third figure).

Run
Seven cluster jobs, one seed each. The saved SGD emoticon state through the Pile checks and the route split. SGD and Adam component training matched for size of change on emoticon, with and without momentum. An SGD fine-tune (rate 0.01) and an Adam fine-tune at 3e-6, matched to it in size, through do-nothing, the least-squares and the adjoint split at step 10. SGD and Adam component training at three rates on the ladder tasks at step 10, then at steps 100 and 1,000, and on the cipher. Task names: r0, r1 and r2 are " it" then " is" (r0 t352), " which" then " was" (r1 t534) and the rare-target " in" then " Fig" (r2 t275). Verdicts here use each job's own floor: a ratio up to 1.24 passes, 1.81 or more fails, between is inconclusive. Jobs 28182 to 28285, $0.
Found
  • The SGD emoticon state is faithful on Pile: adversarial ratio 1.07, beside Adam's 1.06. Its moved subcomponents carry 0.07 of the gain, re-switching 0.54.
  • Matched for size on emoticon, Adam's change is about seven times more diffuse than SGD's, with 3.8 times the damage elsewhere. Momentum keeps SGD's concentration but brings 5.2 times the damage.
  • Fine-tune side: an SGD fine-tune leaves do-nothing at 1.12 and its least-squares split at 1.16, both passing. Adam at 3e-6, matched in size, reads 1.08 and 1.12, so the 2.18 and 5.41 of the usual Adam fine-tune came with its 4.7 times larger change, not with Adam.
  • Ladder at step 10: SGD passes " it" then " is" (0.95) and " which" then " was" (1.10) where Adam 1e-5 fails. SGD also passes " we" then " just" (0.97); on " in" then " Fig" it is inconclusive (1.50).
  • Surprise, steps 100 and 1,000: Adam 1e-5, which failed both word tasks at step 10, passes them at both later steps (1.00 to 1.07). SGD passes at step 100 but fails at 1,000 (2.82, 3.00), at about the same size of change as Adam. Adam 3e-6 learns by step 100 and passes both.
  • Emoticon passes at steps 100 and 1,000 under SGD and both Adam rates. Do-nothing at step 1,000 reads 1.39, inconclusive.
  • On " in" then " Fig" nothing passes cleanly. SGD fails at both later steps (5.2, 38). Adam's probability of the target falls below 0.9 by step 1,000, and its one scored reading is inconclusive.
  • Surprise, cipher: Adam at 3.3e-6 and 1e-6 learns the 32 pairs and passes (1.09, 1.05), so E10's failure was the rate. SGD fails 32 pairs (4.16) and the identity control (2.37).
  • Size of change does not explain the cipher across optimisers: on the identity control SGD fails at a largest per-matrix change of 0.0049, where Adam 1e-5 passes at 0.0118.
  • Within each optimiser one cut nearly separates the verdicts, on different quantities (second figure): size for SGD's component training (one error in 17), damage elsewhere for Adam's (none in 25). Across all 55 decisive states neither cut does (12 and 8 errors).
  • For Adam the verdict tracks the damage at the moment it is read, not the damage it went through: Adam 1e-5 on r0 and r1 passes at step 100 after 0.2 nats of damage at step 10.
  • Prediction reversed: the SGD fine-tune's least-squares split was filed to fail like Adam's and passed (1.16). The matched Adam fine-tune passed too, so the reversal belongs to size.
