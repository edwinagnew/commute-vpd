SGD vs Adam (Q11): does the optimiser decide the adversarial verdicts?

2026-10-21 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/sgd_faith, sgd_matched, sgd_finetune, sgd_ladder, sgd_ladder_long, sgd_kv

The question. By this point every map that failed VPD's faithfulness check had been run on Adam updates (E14 to E20). One explanation was that Adam itself breaks the decomposition, and SGD would fix it (Q11, a priority the user set). If the optimiser decides the verdict, the project should switch optimiser. If not, the failures have another cause. The check is the adversarial ratio: the damage an adversary searching over masks of the pieces can do at a state, over what it does at the unchanged decomposition. 1 means no change; in this run up to 1.24 passes and 1.81 or more fails.

What we did. We compared SGD and Adam in two roles. The first is as the optimiser of component training, which trains the pieces directly on a task. The second is as the optimiser of a fine-tune that is then mapped onto the pieces, either left whole in Δ (do-nothing) or split across the pieces. The tasks were emoticon, four next-word tasks and a cipher the model lacks, read at steps 10, 100 and 1,000.

What we found. Neither optimiser is safe on its own. In component training, Adam failed only early, while its change was still damaging the model's other predictions, and passed once that faded by step 100. SGD passed early and failed at step 1,000, once its change had grown large (first figure). For fine-tunes, size decided. An SGD and an Adam fine-tune of the same size both passed, and the usual Adam fine-tune (rate 3e-5), 4.7 times larger, failed (third figure).

What it means. The earlier failures came from large updates, not from Adam, so switching optimiser would not have fixed them. Adam component training met the bar the commute line had set for moving on: a map that passes with the behaviour at steps 100 and 1,000. On 2026-10-22 the user then made re-gating and commute two parallel lines.

Run
Seven cluster jobs, one seed each, $0. Component training used SGD against Adam at 1e-5, 3e-6 and 1e-6 on emoticon, four word tasks (` it` then ` is`, ` which` then ` was`, ` we` then ` just`, and the rare ` in` then ` Fig`) and the cipher. An SGD emoticon fine-tune and a size-matched Adam one went through do-nothing and the splits.
Found
  • Adam component training fails only early, while it still damages other predictions, and passes at steps 100 and 1,000. SGD passes at 10 and 100 and fails at 1,000 (2.82, 3.00).
  • Matched for size, SGD and Adam fine-tunes both pass; the usual Adam fine-tune fails because it is larger. On the cipher, E10's failure was Adam's rate.
  • No single quantity explains every verdict, and the rare target passes under nothing.
Bears on

Moved on
The bar for moving on was met by Adam component training on three tasks, and again at a second batch order (E26). The user retired it on 2026-10-22, running re-gating and commute as two parallel lines. The step-1,000 contrast held under the VPD paper's longer adversary (E20, E23), so the verdicts are not artefacts of a short search.
