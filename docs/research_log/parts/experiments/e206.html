Does new machinery grafted at other sites also read low under the held-model definition?

2026-11-18 · cell P1.28, its one run, no independent check · experiments/graft_sites

The question. The held-model wording's main reading, one linear map per site from the original model's units, put one attention head grafted onto the frozen original model at 0.13 (E197). Is that low reading general to new machinery, or particular to a head at the last layer?

What we did. We grafted new weights onto the frozen 67M model at several sites, each trained alone on a task the model cannot do, so each is new by construction: four heads on the last layer, each outputting the token a different number of positions back (16, 12, 8 and 4); 256 MLP neurons on the last layer, sending each of the 256 most frequent tokens to a different one of them (a random lookup drawn after the model was trained); and a head on layer 1. Each was read under the per-site map and under one gain per unit, with survival as in E197.

What we found. The four head grafts keep 0.136 to 0.221 of their change, more where the original model's own heads already look at that offset. The 256 neurons keep 0.954, just under the lowest old-task fine-tune (0.984) and far above every head graft; the map rebuilds all but 0.105 of what they write, against about half for the heads. One gain per unit keeps 0 of every graft. The layer-1 head learned the task without attending at its offset, so it was not read. All filed rules held, but the near-tie with old-task fine-tunes was not expected by them.

What it means. The per-site map does not read new machinery low at every site: a lookup on the current token, built on new neurons, reads as mostly kept. Our hypothesis, not measured here, is that the original model's neurons carry the current token linearly, so any per-token table is a linear re-mix of them. You answered tentatively that a linear re-mix is more reuse than new, so this reading is kept as a stated consequence of the definition. One build per graft, one MLP task, one layer.
