Does the second wording read a skill that was built, when a copy of the model already half had it?

2026-11-11 · provisional, no independent check · cluster debug L40, jobs 57970 and 58002, $0 · experiments/built_control

The question. You adopted the second wording of reuse tentatively, to be revised if it cannot detect a built skill (section {{n:next}}). Every case it has read so far was either hand-made or an old task. Can it tell a skill that was built from one the original already had, when both are taught by ordinary fine-tuning?

What we did. One new skill on the 67M: the exclusive-or of two keyed bits, shown as a fixed push at the last position whenever the two bits differ, read only on key pairs held out in training. The plain arm fine-tunes the 67M toward it, 300 steps, two seeds. The taught arm first trains a taught original, a copy moved a third of the way toward the target, then fine-tunes it toward the same target with the same recipe. The taught arm should read reused, the plain arm built. The rewiring share is how much of the fine-tune's change an affine map of everything the original computed at one site explains; under 0.5 reads no map found. The re-weighting share is the same for one four-direction variable, read at 0.8 or more as re-weighted.

What we found. No map on either arm. The rewiring share was 0.02 on both taught seeds and 0.08 and 0.09 on the plain ones, all far under 0.5; the re-weighting share was about zero on the taught arm and 0.10 on the plain one. Both arms learned the target. Ordinary fine-tunes copying a hand-made table change, read the same way, reach 0.72. The taught original held the skill only weakly: a linear read of the bit from its activations scored 0.588, where chance is 0.50, and it ended further from the target (0.30) than the plain model (0.26).

What it means. By your condition the wording needs revising, since it cannot tell this built skill from one half there. But the control was weak, and every case where the search has worked had a variable the original held exactly. Whether it fails on ordinary fine-tunes in general, or only on weakly held variables, is open. You answered: keep the wording and find a more natural built control, so a strongly taught original is not run.

Run
Two cluster debug jobs on the L40, $0: the stops first (zero-step gap, capacity read of the plain and taught originals, learned check), which all passed, then the two arms, two seeds each, with the re-weighting share on seed 0.
Found
  • Both arms learned the target, yet the rewiring share stayed under 0.1 on both (taught 0.02, plain 0.08 and 0.09), against a filed expectation of 0.55 to 0.75 for the taught arm: the filed outcome missed.
  • The taught original read the bit linearly at 0.588 (bar 0.55, expected above 0.9), so the control was weak; the two arms differ by about two seed standard deviations of 0.028, not read as a difference.
  • On past hand-made and table-copy cases the variable was an exact linear read of the original; nothing between that and 0.588 has been tested.
