Does retraining Γ on the updated model make a split decomposition faithful again?

2026-10-20 to 21 · RunPod H100 · experiments/gamma_refit

The question. The commute line (Q1) asks for a simple map that carries a fine-tune onto the decomposition's pieces and keeps the decomposition faithful. VPD's decomposition has three parts: the subcomponents (the pieces), the residual Δ (whatever the pieces do not cover), and Γ, a small trained network that rates how important each piece is on each input. Every map tried until then kept Γ frozen at the original model. The least-squares split of a 10-step emoticon fine-tune (the change divided among the pieces by least squares, the rest left in Δ) failed VPD's adversarial check sevenfold. So we asked whether Γ had simply gone stale.

What we did. We held the pieces and Δ fixed and retrained Γ for 3,000 steps on VPD's own training objective, then re-ran the check at four checkpoints. The check is the adversarial ratio: the damage an adversary can do by turning pieces down within the limits Γ allows, divided by what it does to the original, released decomposition. A ratio of 1 means "as faithful as the original". It passes inside a band from 1 to 1.22 and fails above it. The band is 1 plus three times the spread of three adversary seeds at the unchanged model. As the control, the same retraining was run on the unchanged model, which should stay at 1. Separately, with no retraining, we compared what the adversary's pieces do one at a time and together.

What we found. The measurement went wrong: the retraining took the unchanged model out of the band, to up to 6.3 (first figure). A second recipe also left the band, faster: 7.8 by step 300. The split did reach the band at step 3,000, but only with VPD's sparsity loss 60% above the original's, Γ rating more pieces important (second and third figures). Before any retraining, a weaker adversary's pieces each do the same median damage at the updated model as at the original, yet together they do up to twice as much (fourth figure).

What it means. A retraining that moves the unchanged model on its own cannot be read as a repair of the split, so this does not yet answer Q1. A likely cause, not shown here, is that the retraining's own adversary is weaker than the check's: 0.42 against 0.70 nats on the released Γ (training log, not plotted). The project is now building a retraining whose adversary matches the check's, tested on the unchanged model first. The fourth figure needs no retraining but is narrow: one fine-tune, a weaker adversary than the check's, medians of 48 pieces. The report reads it, provisionally, as a joint effect of many pieces; but do-nothing already reads 1.36, no noise floor was measured, and medians cannot exclude a few pieces mattering more. It does not test whether Γ is stale; that is the open question the retraining was for.

Run
On the emoticon task (after the first character of an emoticon, predict o), one 10-step Adam fine-tune of all 24 matrices, one seed; Γ was retrained on Pile text for the split state and for the unchanged model, and the second recipe on the unchanged model only. Three RunPod H100 jobs, about $22.50 in all.
Found
  • Retraining Γ on the unchanged model, which should leave it at 1, took its adversarial ratio out of the band, to as much as 6.3; a second recipe left it faster, while the checks without an adversary held.
  • The split state reached the band (1.06) only at step 3,000, with VPD's sparsity loss 60% above the original's; with the control failing, that is not a repair.
  • Without retraining, with a weaker adversary, one fine-tune and medians of 48 pieces per group, single pieces do the same damage as at the original model while the whole mask does up to twice as much. The report reads this, provisionally, as a joint effect; do-nothing already reads 1.36 with no piece changed, and no noise floor was measured.
