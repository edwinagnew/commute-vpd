Can reinforcement learning on Qwen3-1.7B grow its update to a readable size while the model still improves, and is it apart from a null?

2026-11-12 · runs 1 and 2 of 3, provisional, no independent check · RunPod H100 pods and cluster L40 reads, about $226 so far · experiments/qwen_rl_readable (run 2 is E167)

The question. Part 4 reads a reinforcement-learning update beside supervised fine-tunes on Qwen3-1.7B (section {{n:next}}, cell P3.4). The saved reinforcement-learning updates from E69 change the model's output about as much as the decomposition's own error does, so any reading of them sits in the noise. Can the update be grown to a size the decomposition can see while the model still answers held-out maths well, and at that size is its pass rate above that of a null trained the same way with the rewards made meaningless?

What we did. The output change is the KL divergence from the unchanged model to the trained one, per token, on 128 maths rows; the decomposition's own error on those rows is 0.0932 nats per token, and the multiple is the ratio. Run 1 tried three peak learning rates on one seed. Run 2 trained the one you chose, 3e-6, to step 400 on two seeds, three ways: reinforcement learning on school maths (GSM8K), the same loop with the rewards shuffled among each problem's answers (the shuffled-reward null), and plain training on the model's own correct answers. States were saved where reinforcement learning crossed 2, 5 and 10 times the error, and the null and the fine-tune were saved at the same steps. The held-out pass rate is greedy accuracy on 1319 GSM8K test problems; the unchanged model reads 0.782.

What we found. Reinforcement learning reached twice the error at step 75 on both seeds, and five times on seed 70 only, with the pass rate staying near 0.88. By the filed rule it is apart from the null at twice the error, but the null collapsed on both seeds: its answers shrank from about 190 tokens to under 10 and its pass rate fell to 0.039 and 0.000, so the comparison carries no claim. The cause was not checked. Every update of every kind sits outside the decomposition's subcomponents as much as a random change of the same size does (0.979 to 0.993 of a Gaussian change's).

What it means. Part 4 now has reinforcement-learning updates at two and five times the noise floor, read on the decomposition, but no behaviour comparison against a working null. At a third of this rate the null held to step 100 in earlier runs, so a working null may exist; whether to train one is your question (section {{n:decisions}}). Until then the cell reads: apart at twice the error by the filed rule, against a null that broke.
