E30mixed

Can one fixed change to Γ's values, applied everywhere, serve as the map?

2026-10-22 to 2026-10-23 · RunPod 4090, MATS cluster L40 · experiments/gamma_shift_map

The question. The commute line (Q1) looks for a simple map that turns the original decomposition into a faithful decomposition of the fine-tuned model, without parking the update in the residual Δ. In the decomposition each weight matrix is a sum of pieces, called subcomponents, plus Δ. Γ gives each piece an importance value between 0 and 1 at every position. After a small fine-tune, one fixed change to those values at the trigger gives back about three quarters of the effect of Γ switching pieces on (E18, E24). That is the fixed-shift hypothesis. The simplest map would keep every piece and Δ as they are and add that fixed change to Γ's values everywhere. This run asks whether that map works.

What we did. The fixed change is, for each piece, its mean importance at the trigger under the fine-tuned model minus its mean under the original model. We added it to Γ's values at every position and scored the result against the fine-tuned model. At the trigger we read how much of the switching effect it recovers: 0 for no change, 1 for the fine-tuned model's own values. On 256 rows of ordinary Pile text we read the adversarial ratio. It is the damage an adversary searching over masks of the pieces can do, over the damage it does with no change; it passes at or below 1.45 and fails above 1.55. We also read VPD's sparsity cost, where more than 1.05 times the unchanged decomposition's reads as cheating. Two controls sat beside it. One is the change from a null fine-tune toward the unrelated token ` http`. The other is the task's change shuffled among each matrix's alive pieces: the same values, so the same total size, in the wrong places.

What we found. At the trigger the change applied everywhere does as well as at the trigger alone: 0.75 on both emoticon fine-tunes and 0.44 on ` which` then ` was` (first figure). On Pile text it fails: the adversary finds 5 to 26 times the no-change damage (second figure). The null's change fails as badly, while the shuffled change stays inside the pass line at the same sparsity cost (third figure). Since the shuffled change has the same values on other pieces, the damage appears to depend on where a fine-tune's change lands, not only on its size; this rests on one shuffle draw, one training seed and one adversary seed per bar, and scaling the change was not tried. A follow-up on emoticon by plain SGD applied the raised and the lowered halves of the change separately (fourth figure). The lowered half fails at 6.36 times the no-change damage; the raised half reads 1.49, between the lines, but pays 3.13 times the sparsity cost.

What it means. The result is mixed: the fixed-shift hypothesis holds where it was measured, at the trigger, in that spreading the change matches the trigger-only figure (0.40 on ` which` then ` was`, already lower there), but the change is not a map, because applied to all text it breaks the decomposition. This rules out this fixed shift as the map. Only one fixed change was tried and it fails; the report reads that as the change having to depend on the input. The caveat is scope. Only one fixed change was tried, the mean at the trigger, on one training seed, three updates on two tasks, at step 10, with one adversary seed per arm. The halves follow-up, on emoticon by plain SGD only, also made the report set aside, without running it, a map that raises values everywhere and lowers them only at the trigger: on ordinary text that map would act as the raised half, which pays the whole sparsity cost. The next candidate is the change applied only where the fine-tuned model's own values move.

Run
Three ten-step fine-tunes of all 24 matrices of the 67M model, on one training seed, each with a null fine-tune toward ` http` at the same settings: emoticon (predict o after an emoticon's first character) by plain SGD at 0.01 and by Adam at 3e-5, and ` which` then ` was` by plain SGD at 0.03. One RunPod 4090 job, about $0.5, then one MATS cluster L40 job ($0) for the halves; the new scoring path and the regenerated fine-tunes matched the ordinary harness and the earlier run exactly.
Found
  • At the trigger the change applied everywhere recovers 0.75 on both emoticon fine-tunes and 0.44 on ` which` then ` was`, as at the trigger alone, as predicted before the run.
  • On Pile text the adversary finds 5 to 26 times the no-change damage on all three fine-tunes, and the sparsity cost is 1.8 to 3.3 times the unchanged decomposition's.
  • The null's change fails the adversary as badly, while the shuffled change, at the same sparsity cost as the task's, stays inside the pass line; this was not expected and is read on one adversary seed.
  • Applied separately on emoticon by plain SGD, the lowered half of the change fails at 6.36 times the no-change damage, and the raised half is inconclusive at 1.49 while costing 3.13 times the sparsity.
