Can anything in the decomposition tell a behaviour the model already has from one it lacks?

2026-10-20 · RunPod A40 · experiments/new_vs_existing

The question. VPD writes each weight matrix as many rank-one pieces (subcomponents) plus a leftover Δ, and an importance function Γ decides, at each position, which pieces are switched on. Q4 asks whether it matters that a fine-tune teaches the model something new rather than strengthening something it already does. Every earlier run asked whether a map of the update onto the pieces passes VPD's checks; this one steps back and asks whether any number the decomposition gives can tell the two kinds of update apart at all. The key-to-value cipher run (E10) had seen one hint: its copy and random-pair tasks took different routes.

What we did. Each task puts a rare marker token before a common word, the key, in ordinary text, and fine-tunes the model to predict a fixed word next; the trigger is the key's position. In the two copy tasks that word is the key itself, which the model already has machinery for. In the two random-map tasks it is an arbitrary other word, the same 32 pairs every time, which nothing in the model does. Each fine-tune ran at two seeds, eight states in all. The least-squares split then writes each weight change as the smallest change of the pieces that reproduces it, with the rest put in Δ. We read six numbers per state, some off the update itself and some off the route split. The route split is read in a stand-in model built from the pieces, each scaled by Γ's importance value at that position. It divides the fine-tune's gain on the target at the trigger exactly into shares that sum to 1: carried by the moved pieces themselves, by pieces Γ newly switches on (the re-switching share), by pieces Γ keeps off (the masked-off share), and left in Δ. A number separates the classes when their ranges do not overlap and the gap between them is wider than the spread within either class.

What we found. Of the six candidate numbers, only the two route shares separate the classes (first figure). For copying, pieces Γ switches on carry about 0.8 of the gain; for the random maps, pieces Γ keeps off carry about half (second figure). Nothing read off the update itself separates them (third figure). It lies outside the pieces equally often in both classes. It lands on pieces that already mattered at the trigger in both; copying reads higher there, but the spread within each class is wider than the gap. Γ also switches on several times more pieces for copying (fourth figure). That count was reported without a rule set beforehand; it would clear the separation bar, and like the route shares it is read from Γ, not from the update.

What it means. For Q4, on these two tasks per class, the difference between a new and an existing behaviour showed up only in how the frozen Γ reads the fine-tuned model inside the stand-in, not in the weight change written in the pieces; a map that keeps Γ frozen did not see it here. The caveat is scope: two tasks and two seeds per class in one format, so newness is equated with one kind of task. The random maps also learned less cleanly: one only just reached the bar, and the other needed three times the steps and twice the update size, though its numbers match the first's. Later runs reused these tasks as updates of known kind. E46 reproduced the separation; E50 found that resetting pieces in the real fine-tuned model does not separate the classes, so the separation lives in the stand-in only.

Run
Two copy tasks and two random-map tasks of 32 pairs each, full fine-tunes at two seeds, each state scored at its first checkpoint that predicts the target with probability at least one half on held-out text. One RunPod A40 job of about 15 minutes, $0.24.
Found
  • The route shares separate the classes cleanly, as predicted: the re-switching share is about 0.8 for copying against under 0.3 for the random maps.
  • Against the prediction, nothing read off the update separates them: the outside share is about 0.6 in both, two seeds move the same pieces about equally often in both, and the moved pieces matter at the trigger in both, more for copying but with a gap smaller than the spread within each class.
  • Γ switches on more than 100 pieces for copying and at most 51 for the maps, and these are not the pieces the update moved most.
