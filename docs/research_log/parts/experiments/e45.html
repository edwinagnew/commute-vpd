Can fine-tunes of known kind, compared at equal movement, show which readings tell a strengthened ability from a new one on Qwen?

2026-10-28 to 2026-10-29 · MATS cluster, $0 so far; the readings are E57's, on the cluster at $0 · experiments/qwen_updates

The question. The project asks whether a post-training update strengthens something the model can already do or teaches it something new, and whether the decomposition can tell the two apart. On the larger model, Qwen3-1.7B (Q9), that needs updates whose kind is known by design, so each reading of the decomposition can be checked against a right answer first. E40 gave a fine-tune recipe with a sensible first step, and E44 is building the table of readings these updates will go through. E45 makes the updates; nothing has been read through the decomposition yet.

What we did. Five fine-tunes of all 196 decomposed weight matrices, one recipe. Two are on GSM8K grade-school maths: the model trained on its own correct answers, and on the human-written solutions (gold). Two are anchors of known kind. Anchor 1 is "already there": the model's answers under a prompt, trained without it. Anchor 2 is "new": question-answer pairs about invented people. The self-distillation null trains on the model's own answers unfiltered, to show what training on itself does alone. Only the anchors carry predictions. Size is KL to start, the average per-token KL divergence between the original and fine-tuned model's predictions, on two scales: the fine-tune's own training text, or 128 GSM8K solutions shared by all (the maths rows). Each run was saved where KL first crossed 0.01, 0.03, 0.1 and 0.3, the equal-movement points, to be compared at 0.1 on own text. That grid was the original plan; Amendment 2 replaces it with matching on the maths rows alone, the one scale all runs share (own-text KL is measured on different text per run, so it does not compare across runs). A run is read only where it has learned; for anchor 2, at least a quarter of held-out invented facts recalled. Two first-minute checks: a control repeating E40's gold fine-tune must reproduce its KL, and KL on the maths rows at step 2, the first step with a nonzero rate, must stay small, which catches a missing warm-up (E40's failure).

What we found. The control reproduced E40 to within 0.1% (T_gold_s0.json, checks against E40). Anchor 2 passed every equal-movement point by step 12 but learned its facts only by step 100, on both seeds (first figure). Its maths accuracy without a prompt then fell to 0.13-0.20, and a 4-shot prompt brought it mostly back. Own answers and the null stopped at the step-2 check, whose limit had been set from the gold run alone (second figure). None of three prompts raised accuracy, so anchor 1 had no prompt to teach as designed (third figure).

What it means. Neither anchor can be read where the plan said, so no reading can be checked yet. On 2026-10-29 the user took three changes, being written as Amendment 2. Anchor 2 is read at step 100, where its KL on the maths rows averages 0.62 over the two seeds. Each other run is read at its saved state nearest that value on the maths rows, and counts as matched within a factor of 1.5 either way (the green band in the first figure). Anchor 1 becomes "answer in a fixed template", and the step-2 limit becomes 0.0107 for every arm. The caveat: the advisory review reads anchor 2's maths-row movement as mostly the shorter answer format, not its facts (reports/reviews/2026-10-29_review_fable-anchor2-grid.md). The remaining fine-tunes run next on the cluster for $0.
