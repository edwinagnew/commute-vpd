Can fine-tunes of known kind, compared at equal movement, show which readings tell a strengthened ability from a new one on Qwen?

2026-10-28 to 2026-10-30 · MATS cluster, $0 · experiments/qwen_updates

The question. The project asks whether a post-training update strengthens something the model can already do or teaches it something new, and whether the decomposition can tell the two apart. On the larger model, Qwen3-1.7B (Q9), that needs updates whose kind is known by design, so each reading of the decomposition can be checked against a right answer first. E40 gave a fine-tune recipe with a sensible first step, and E44 built the table of readings these updates go through.

What we did. Fine-tunes of all 196 decomposed weight matrices, one recipe, 100 steps. Two are anchors of known kind. Anchor 1 is "already there": the model's answers in a fixed template (a "Given:" line, a "Steps:" list, then "The answer is") that a system prompt asks for, trained without the prompt. Anchor 2 is "new": question-answer pairs about invented people. Three more carry no label: the model trained on its own correct maths answers (at the filed rate and at twice it), the same on its answers unfiltered (the null), and the human-written solutions (gold). Size is KL to start, the average per-token KL divergence between the original and the fine-tuned model's predictions, read on 128 maths answers shared by every run. Each run is read at its saved state nearest anchor 2's step-100 value of 0.62, and counts as matched within a factor of 1.5 either way (0.41 to 0.93), only where it has learned.

What we found. The apparatus held: step-2 sizes stayed under the limit, the stopped runs reproduced exactly, and gold matched at 0.70. Anchor 1 learned its template on both seeds (answers follow it without a prompt from step 19; 94% of the gap to the prompted model closed), but its movement on the maths answers levels off at 0.33, under the 0.41 floor, so it is unmatched on both seeds. Anchor 2 matched at step 100 (0.63 and 0.61), but most of its movement there is a shift to short answers, not its facts. The own-answer fine-tunes and the null never lowered their held-out loss, though one raised maths accuracy by five points. Anchor 1 is a format fine-tune: it fixes the novelty of a surface format the model already follows on request, not of a better way to solve problems. It shares its domain and data generation with the maths fine-tunes; anchor 2 does not.

What it means. No two anchors can be read at one size where both have learned, so no reading of the decomposition has been checked against a right answer, and E57 reads these states as description only, labelled unmatched. Anchor 2 has no saved state between steps 12 and 100, so a smaller shared size is not ruled out; the options are in the pending card. Own-answer fine-tunes are not read on the decisive rows, and changing their loss rule after the data is your call.

Run
One-L40 jobs on the MATS cluster, $0: the control, gold, anchor 2 on two seeds, anchor 1 on two seeds, the own-answer, double-rate and null fine-tunes (jobs 46194 to 46478). Every fine-tune uses E40's warm-up recipe (rate 1e-5 after 10 warm-up steps, then cosine decay). Due to run: E57 reads the saved states for $0.
