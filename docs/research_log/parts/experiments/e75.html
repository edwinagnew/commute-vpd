On Qwen3-1.7B, does a table read on the clean model fail bad placements of an update, and does the least-squares split pass it?

2026-11-03 to 11-06 · provisional, not independently checked · MATS cluster L40s, $0 · experiments/qwen_rl_reads, qwen_d1_table

The question. The commute line asks whether a finished fine-tuning update can be carried onto a decomposition's subcomponents so that the decomposition stays faithful and the changed model still does what the fine-tuned one does. On Qwen3-1.7B no test under masks could pass even the whole update (E44), so on 2026-11-03 the user made a table read on the clean model, with no masks, the test for the first deliverable: a map on Qwen that keeps the decomposition faithful. Two things needed settling. Can that table fail a bad placement of the update's own size? And does the simplest map, the least-squares split, pass it on every saved fine-tune?

What we did. Nothing was trained. The decomposition writes each weight matrix as a sum of subcomponents plus a leftover, Δ. The least-squares split moves each subcomponent by the smallest change that reproduces the update to first order. Its moved part is what the subcomponents take, and the rest of the update stays in Δ. The adjoint split, the other closed-form map, drops what it leaves out instead. The table adds a part of the update to the original weights and reads how far the result sits from the fine-tuned model. The yardstick is the original model's own distance from it. First we read three wrong changes at the update's size in each matrix: a random one, another update's, and the update with a random sign per matrix. We read them on three supervised updates and two reinforcement-learning ones. Then we ran both splits on eleven saved states, among them the template and TOFU facts fine-tunes at two seeds, two supervised maths fine-tunes and three small RL arms. The table has four checks. The moved part must sit within 0.02 of the yardstick. Less of the update must stay in Δ than of a random update. The change's squared size is read beside the update's. And the moved part must keep most of the behaviour. Do-nothing (all of the update in Δ) and random updates of the same size are the references.

What we found. The table fails all three wrong changes on all five updates (first figure). But it barely grades parts of the update against each other: the moved part beats a random slice of its size by under 1% of the yardstick (second figure). Least squares passes every check, at every seed, on the template, TOFU facts and both supervised maths fine-tunes (third figure). There it keeps 0.99 to 1.00 of the behaviour, where a tenth of the update keeps about 0.3. It misses on the three small RL arms, by 0.0004 nats or less, so by the filed rule the table fails. The share left in Δ sits at about 97% of a random update's, a weak check (fourth figure). The adjoint split fails all eleven (fifth figure). RL's update, its shuffled-reward run and fine-tuning on the model's own samples read alike on every label.

What it means. A test with no masks can reject a wrong direction at the update's size, and least squares nearly passes it. Its misses sit on updates that barely move the model, so they may say more about tiny updates than about the map. A pass still would not show that the update sits on the right subcomponents, and no adversary ran. Least squares stays the closed-form candidate and the adjoint split is a weak baseline beside teacher training. On 2026-11-09 the paper plan put further tests of the carrying map out of scope.

Found
  • The clean-model table fails random, borrowed and sign-flipped changes of the update's size on every update, but separates parts of the update by little more than their size.
  • Least squares passes every check on the template, TOFU facts and two supervised maths fine-tunes and misses by 0.0004 nats or less on the three small RL arms, so the table fails; we had leaned to a pass everywhere.
  • The adjoint split fails all eleven, and leaves out more of a real update than of a random one, against our lean.
