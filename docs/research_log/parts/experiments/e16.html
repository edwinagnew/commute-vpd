The five operations side by side

2026-10-21 · RunPod H100 · experiments/split_comparison

On one card at steps 10, 100 and 1000, component training is the only operation that keeps both the adversarial floor and the behaviour with Δ untouched; no split of a finished update does. That is this large Adam update's result; on a small emoticon fine-tune the splits pass the adversary, and the adjoint split of an SGD one keeps most of the behaviour. The reproduction gate failed, so the filed predictions are unscored and these are readings.

Run
Emoticon, one card. A fine-tune and a rank-8 LoRA, each at steps 10, 100 and 1000, carried onto the subcomponents by each operation; component training trained in their place (free beside the fine-tune, rank 8 beside the LoRA). Every state scored on VPD's adversarial check, the held-out behaviour, and how much of the update ends up in Δ.
Found
  • Component training: as faithful as the original model and keeps the behaviour at every step, with Δ untouched. The only operation that does all three.
  • Least-squares split: keeps the behaviour but fails the adversarial check for the fine-tune (7.0 at step 10) and leaves 36 to 54% of the update in Δ.
  • Adjoint split: faithful, but the fine-tune's held-out probability of the target falls from 0.93 at step 10 to 0.10 at step 1000 (first figure, middle panel). Rescaling split: faithful, and keeps none of it.
  • At step 1000 the moved subcomponents carry a few percent of the gain at most, under every operation (second figure).
  • Caveat: the run did not reproduce E14's step-10 fine-tune numbers (7.0 against 5.2), so the filed predictions are unscored and these are readings.
