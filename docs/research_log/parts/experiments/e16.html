The five operations side by side

2026-10-21 · RunPod H100 · experiments/split_comparison

The question. To read a fine-tune in the decomposition, its weight change has to be carried onto the pieces. A good way of doing that keeps three things at once. It keeps the fine-tune's behaviour. It keeps VPD's faithfulness: the adversarial ratio, the damage an adversary searching over masks of the pieces can do, over what it does to the unchanged decomposition, stays near 1. And it leaves little of the update in the residual Δ, since an update parked in Δ is not explained by the pieces.

What we did. We put the five candidate operations side by side on one task (emoticon), for a full fine-tune and a rank-8 LoRA, both Adam, at steps 10, 100 and 1,000. Do-nothing leaves the whole change in Δ. The least-squares, adjoint and rescaling splits divide the finished change among the pieces in three different ways. Component training skips the fine-tune and trains the pieces on the task directly.

What we found. Only component training was good on all three counts, at every step (first figure). Each split failed at least one. Least squares left 36 to 54% of the update in Δ and failed the check on the fine-tune. The adjoint split lost the behaviour as training went on, from 0.93 to 0.10. Rescaling never had it.

What it means. No operation yet mapped a finished update: component training replaces the update rather than mapping it. Teacher training (E34) later filled that gap by training the pieces to copy the finished fine-tune. The splits' failures here belong to this large Adam update. On small fine-tunes the splits pass the check (E21, E22), and the adjoint split of a small SGD fine-tune keeps about 0.6 of the behaviour (E31).

Run
Emoticon, one seed. A fine-tune and a rank-8 LoRA were each carried onto the pieces by every operation at steps 10, 100 and 1,000, with component training trained in their place. Every state was scored on the adversarial check, held-out behaviour, and Δ's share of the update.
Found
  • Component training keeps the behaviour, passes the check and leaves almost nothing in Δ at every step, beside both updates.
  • On this large fine-tune each split fails at least one count: least squares fails the check (7.0 at step 10) and leaves 36 to 54% in Δ, the adjoint split loses the behaviour, and rescaling keeps none of it.
  • Under every operation the moved pieces carry a few percent of the gain at most; most of what is explained comes from Γ switching pieces on differently. The run did not reproduce an earlier number (E14's step-10 check, 7.0 against 5.2), so the predictions written beforehand are unscored and these are readings.
