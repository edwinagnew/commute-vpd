Can VPD's adversary fail a bad state on Qwen3-1.7B's first update?

2026-11-04 · Vast H200 NVL, about $10.6 · experiments/qwen_d1_teeth · provisional, independent check running

The question. D1 needs a test that can fail a bad placement of an update. VPD's adversary passed doing nothing on the first fine-tune's update (E80), so we did not know it can fail anything on this model.

What we did. We read the adversary on two states built to be bad, on E80's rows, settings and code, with the unchanged model read first on the same host. One is the weights plus a random change at the update's size, kept in Δ (the weights outside the subcomponents). The other puts the update on the half of the subcomponents least often on, with the remainder in Δ. Six starts in all, two per state. The pass bar is a ratio of 1.40 to the unchanged model and the fail bar 1.61.

What we found. At the filed read of 20 steps the adversary passed both bad states: the random change reads 1.01 and the rarely-on half 1.27 (figure). The search had not finished at 20 steps: the damage it finds was still climbing about 8% to 10% per step over the last five (about 5% for the rarely-on placement's first start), so each filed read is a lower bound. The rarely-on placement's mean ratio reached 1.62 at step 10, over the 1.61 fail bar, before falling to 1.27 at the filed read; the rise and fall has no measured cause.

What it means. The filed outcome, "the adversary cannot fail a bad state here", holds for this search length only; with a longer or differently stopped search the rarely-on placement may fail. An independent check of this claim is running, and until it reports the card stays provisional. The question of what D1 should be judged by goes to you in card decision-after-e76.
