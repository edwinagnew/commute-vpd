LoRA on one matrix: split and rank-r component training (round 4)

2026-10-19 · RunPod RTX 4090 · experiments/lora_commute

Takeaway: where a LoRA sits matters, its rank does not. Confined to the paper's one matrix, which the decomposition reads poorly, the LoRA's split reads above this card's noise floor at every rank from 1 to 32 and over the 1.5 line at rank 8 (right panel); spread over all 24 matrices it stays under the 1.5 line (E8; E16's first figure, left panel). So this run's "a LoRA is harder" is about the matrix, not about LoRAs.

Run
LoRAs of rank 1, 4, 8 and 32 on the paper's emoticon matrix, and the fine-tune, each placed do-nothing and least-squares split. Component training confined to that matrix (rank-r and free) and on all 24. Route split, outside share, Pile ratios with a three-seed floor.
Found
  • Outside share at the isotropic null at every rank (0.38 to 0.43 against 0.42; fine-tune 0.27 against 0.53): the read factor stays near its random start.
  • The LoRA's split leaves a third to two fifths of the behaviour in Δ; the fine-tune's split a twelfth.
  • Pile adversary: LoRA splits 1.35 to 2.0, one-matrix component training 2.1 to 4.1, all-matrix component training 1.10. Fine-tune split 7.3 against E2's 5.4, a cross-card miss.
  • Rank is not the axis: rank 1 behaves like rank 32. Direct share 0.05 to 0.11, re-switching 0.5 to 0.6 for every arm.
  • Eight filed predictions: four null, four neither.
