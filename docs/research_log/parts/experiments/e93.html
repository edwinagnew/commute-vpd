Does the simplest split of a finished Qwen fine-tune over the subcomponents keep the decomposition faithful, on every fine-tune we have saved?

2026-11-06 · cluster L40, eight jobs, $0 · experiments/qwen_d1_table · provisional, not independently checked

The question. The map for the Qwen3-1.7B decomposition (the first deliverable, {{n:next}}) is judged by a table of checks on the clean model, with VPD's adversary left out as a stated limit. Does the least-squares split, the simplest way to move the subcomponents onto a finished update, pass that table on every fine-tune we have saved? If it does, nothing more elaborate is needed; if it does not, the plan's next step is training the subcomponents directly against the fine-tuned model (teacher training).

What we did. For eleven saved states (the template fine-tune and the TOFU facts fine-tune at two seeds each, the three small maths reinforcement-learning arms, and the two supervised maths fine-tunes) we split the full update by least squares: the moved part is what the subcomponents take, and the rest stays in the leftover Δ. We read four checks. First, how far the moved part alone sits from the fine-tuned model, as a share of the original model's distance from it (line: under 0.02). Second, the share of the update left in Δ against a random update's, which must be lower by more than the seed-to-seed spread. Third, the moved part's squared size against the update's. Fourth, how much of the behaviour the moved part keeps. The clean-model checks were also read under the state's own masks. Doing nothing (all of the update in Δ) and random updates of the same size are the references. At your request (Amendment 1) we then ran the scaled adjoint split, the other closed-form map, through the same eleven states and the same rules; it drops what it leaves out instead of putting it in Δ.

What we found. It passes every check, at every seed, on the template and TOFU facts fine-tunes and on both supervised maths fine-tunes; the moved part keeps 0.99 to 1.00 of the behaviour on the first two, against about 0.88 for half the update, and a tenth of the update keeps only 0.3, so that check could have failed. It misses narrowly on the three small reinforcement-learning arms: one seed of the reward-trained arm on the clean model (0.024 against the 0.02 line) and under masks (0.0000286 nats worse than doing nothing against a line of 0.0000251); the shuffled-reward arm, both seeds, on the share left in Δ (gaps of 0.0025 and 0.0026 against a seed spread of 0.0029); and the own-samples arm on the share and under masks. By the filed rule the table fails, and our lean that it would pass everywhere reversed. The share check barely separates real from random: the share sits at about 97% of a random update's on most arms. The adjoint split fails every one of the eleven updates, as we leaned: on the clean model its moved part leaves 0.11 to 0.35 of the original model's distance, against least squares' 0.006 to 0.024, and it keeps 0.85 and 0.74 of the behaviour on the two example updates against a line of 0.88. Two weak leans reversed on all eleven: it leaves out more of a real update (0.63 to 0.73) than of a random change (0.59 to 0.61), and under its own masks it sits further from the fine-tune than doing nothing, by 1.6 to 11 times the line.

What it means. One simple map already keeps the decomposition faithful, in the weak sense and without the adversary, on both updates the Qwen goal was framed on, but a filed miss stands and the table fails. The misses are 0.0004 nats or less and sit on updates that move the model by 0.001 to 0.135 nats, so they may be a limit of these tiny updates more than of the map. A pass would not show that the update sits on the right subcomponents, nor that the adversary would pass it. Of the two closed-form maps only least squares is a candidate for the first deliverable (a map on Qwen3-1.7B that keeps the decomposition faithful); the adjoint split is a weak baseline column that teacher training has to beat. The reversals do not name a cause, and a miss in the plain weight norm says nothing about which inputs the dropped part acts on.
