Where does each saved Qwen update land in the decomposition, and how much of its effect is Γ switching existing subcomponents?

2026-10-30 · MATS cluster L40, $0 · experiments/qwen_reading

The question. The Qwen line (Q3) could not give the pass test teeth, so it now describes what each saved full-weight update does to the decomposition, on free cluster cards. E57 replaces E45's dropped $15.50 H100 run.

What we did. Stages A to C are done and written up: the original model read as its own update, two think-mode reference sets, two random changes sized to the real update, and the real updates (E40's, the gold one and anchor 2) as description only. For each change we split it over the subcomponents and measured two shares of its effect: the re-gating share (recovered by letting Γ recompute its switching) and the direct share (carried by the changed subcomponents alone, with the original switching). The last stage (D), on anchor 1 and the other E45 states, is coded and merged and waits on the pending card.

What we found. The re-gating share reads 0.99 to 1.00 on every state, real or random, above the 0.8 no-teeth line. The direct share reads 0.02 to 0.03 for random changes, 0.06 for E40, 0.09 for gold and 0.01 for anchor 2; gold and random do not overlap in a row bootstrap. But among the real updates the direct share rises with the size of the change, and gold moves the outputs 1.7 to 2.3 times as much as the random changes, so the gap is confounded with size. Overlap with the reference sets is 3 to 4 times chance for every change, random included.

What it means. At this scale the re-gating share cannot tell re-gating from switching that follows the activations, and the direct share separates real updates from random only until size is matched. Stage D would hit the same confound (anchor 1 is smaller than anchor 2), which favours rerunning anchor 2's intermediate states (option 6); the choice is yours (pending decision).
