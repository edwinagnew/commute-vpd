Do simple statistics of the weight change alone tell a fine-tune that brings out copying from one that teaches a random word map?

2026-10-29 · MATS cluster CPU job, $0 · experiments/baseline_weights

The question. A reviewer's first objection to reading the kind of an update (bringing out something the model already does, or teaching something new) from the decomposition is that a simpler method would say the same. The cheapest such method looks only at the weights: how much of the change lies along the original weights' main directions, and how spread out the change is. This is baseline B1 in the baselines plan, run first because it costs nothing. Earlier, on these same fine-tunes, only the route split separated the two kinds (E12).

What we did. We took E12's eight fine-tunes of the 67M model: four that teach copying a key (meant as "existing") and four that teach a random word map (meant as "new"), two tables and two seeds each. For the change in each of the 24 decomposed matrices we measured three standard numbers. The top-10 share is the part of the change's energy along the original matrix's ten largest directions. The intruder count is how many of the changed matrix's top ten directions have no close match in the original (Shuttleworth et al. 2024). The effective rank is how many directions the change spreads over. A ninth update, the code fine-tune that learned nothing, is reported beside them, and every number sits beside a random change of the same size. The filed rule: a number separates the kinds if the gap between the kind means exceeds the larger spread within a kind. Before any reading, a pure re-weighting and a single injected direction had to read their exact expected values, and they did.

What we found. The top-10 share (about 0.03, three times a random change) and the intruder count (zero for every update) do not separate the kinds (figure 1). The effective rank passes the rule by a hair, a gap of 24.5 against a spread of 23.9, but it falls as the update grows and every random-map update is larger than every copy update (figure 2). The learned-nothing update reads like the others on all three numbers. The route split's re-gating share separates the same eight by 0.76 to 0.80 against 0.27 to 0.29.

What it means. The weight-only objection is answered on this set: a spectrum of the weight change does not show what the route split shows. It is "no answer" rather than "no" because the effective rank cannot be pulled apart from update size here, and the intruder count could barely move at this update size. The route split also reads the learned-nothing update as "existing" (0.82), so this set does not show it beating the weight statistics where it matters.
