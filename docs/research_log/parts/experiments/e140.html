E140mixed

Can steering or neurons tell a real fine-tune from its shuffled-label twin, where the re-gating share cannot?

2026-11-10 · provisional, no independent check · cluster debug L40, jobs 57740 and 57752, $0 · experiments/real_vs_twin

The question. Part 2 asks whether a cheaper reading tells apart fine-tunes the re-gating share reads alike. The share is the part of a fine-tune's gain that comes from changing only which of the original subcomponents the decomposition switches on, with their weights held fixed. It reads a real fine-tune and its shuffled-label twin alike on the label pairs. Can steering or a neuron reading do better there?

What we did. Two pairs of fine-tunes, three seeds each. After a marker the model says one of two label words: the real fine-tune learns a rule about the word before the marker (capitalised or not; seen earlier in the window or not), while its twin sees labels shuffled per word and can only memorise. We read the share, steering with one vector per word or one pooled vector, and a neuron version that keeps a top set of neurons. A method separates the arms when the gap is at least 0.1 and twice its standard error. Labels as built; no verdict on reuse.

What we found. Per-word steering separates real from twin on both pairs, twin above: 0.776 against 1.282 of the gain on the case pair, and 0.023 against 1.109 on the repeat pair. Pooled steering separates the case pair by 0.101, right at the bar of 0.1. The share does not separate either pair, with gaps of 0.018 and 0.090, and the neuron version writes almost nothing, no share above 0.021 in size.

What it means. The steering gaps have plain causes that this run does not rule out. A twin's label is a fixed function of the word, so a per-word vector fitted on that word's windows can carry the answer itself. The twins also wrote larger changes: on the case pair the third twin's update is about 13 in summed norm against the real's 4.6. The premise check came back doubtful: a coherent update against a memorising one may be easy for any reading.
