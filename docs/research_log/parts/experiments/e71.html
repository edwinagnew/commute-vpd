Does the least-squares split of a broad fine-tune pass the adversarial check, and does the part the subcomponents can express pass alone?

2026-11-03 · MATS cluster L40, debug QoS, $0 (job 50287) · experiments/scorecard_ls

The question. The commute line asks whether a finished update can be written on the existing subcomponents (Q1). The Q1 slide scores each way of doing that on a broad task, a fine-tune that makes the model write lowercase text, and its least-squares row was empty. You asked for that row, and, as an extra reading, the part of the update the subcomponents can express, alone.

What we did. The least-squares split moves each subcomponent by the smallest change that reproduces the update to first order, and leaves the rest of the update in Δ. The expressible part, a name used here, is the update without that rest, with the same moved subcomponents; Δ stays put. Two exact versions differ by a second-order term, and both were read. Each state went through the adversarial ratio: the damage an attacker searching over masks of the subcomponents does at a state, over what it does at the unchanged model. 1.40 or less passes and 1.61 or more fails. One attacker start on 256 Pile rows, for a full-matrix Adam fine-tune and a rank-8 LoRA. Do-nothing, which leaves the whole update in Δ and must fail, and the scaled adjoint split, which must pass, ran first.

What we found. Least squares fails on Adam (12.3) and passes on the LoRA (1.23), and it leaves 36% and 38% of the update in Δ (first figure). We predicted fails on both. The expressible part fails on Adam and passes on the LoRA at 1.14, keeping 0.93 of the gain with Δ unmoved (second figure). Why the two fine-tunes differ is not established: the LoRA is 1.6 times smaller and has rank 8, and the share outside the subcomponents is near its random null on both.

What it means. Least squares does not meet the weak version on either fine-tune, because Δ absorbs over a third of the update. The LoRA's pass is real but thin: one start, under 1% below the line measured for this card. The LoRA's expressible part is the one new lead, if it survives other starts. Whether to re-read it is a question for you (section {{n:decisions}}).
