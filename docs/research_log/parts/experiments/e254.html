Do the cheap readings still split a copy task from a cipher when the base model finds both answers equally likely?

2026-11-21 · cell P2.57 (filled), runs 1 and 2, no independent check · experiments/prior_matched

The question. The second claim, as softened on 2026-11-21, is that we have ways of following the mechanism that are better than just the prior. A pair of tasks that differ in mechanism but not in the base model's prior of the answer tests it: a reading that splits such a pair cannot be reading the prior.

What we did. Key copy, which the base model's copy heads can do, was fine-tuned against a cipher whose answers the base model finds about as likely (prior gap 0.20 decades; 1.6 for the ordinary cipher), at ten seeds each (E254). A second matched cipher, drawn with the first one's answers banned, was fine-tuned at three seeds and read against the same ten copy seeds (E255, gap 0.16). Each update was read eight ways: the copy-head switch-offs, the re-gating share, effective rank, pooled and per-key steering, and two neuron shares.

What we found. All eight readings put every copy seed apart from every cipher seed on both pairs (p 1.1e-5 on ten against ten, 0.007 on ten against three), with the filed signs. The share's gap is 0.41, about a hundred times its seed spread; pooled steering is twenty to thirty, the top three heads and effective rank about ten; the neuron share at matched size clears its spread only narrowly.

What it means. At matched prior the readings follow the copy-head label, not the prior, which backs the softened second claim. But the share, steering, effective rank and the head switch-offs all split every pair, so no one reading stands out. The pairs are one task family, key copy against ciphers.

Run
Run 1 (E254, job 63108) ten seeds each of copy and the matched cipher; run 2 (E255, job 63110) three seeds of a second matched cipher; MATS cluster L40, about 22 and 10 minutes, no cost.
Found
  • Every reading splits every pair with no seed overlap; per-key steering splits it the other way, as filed.
  • The copy-head labels hold in both runs (copy 0.25 lost with (2,4) off, ciphers 0.001 and 0.002), and random updates read nothing.
  • The neuron share at matched size clears its band only narrowly, and also on the unmatched reference pair, so it barely tracks the mechanism at any prior.
Bears on
Part 3, the second claim: the cheap readings follow the mechanism at matched prior; none is singled out.
