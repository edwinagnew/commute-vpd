E133mixed

Does any simpler method tell the two sides of a pair apart the way the re-gating share does?

2026-11-09 to 11-16 · provisional, no independent check · cluster and CPU jobs, $0 · experiments/method_table

The answer. We built nine pairs of fine-tunes of the 67M model. One side of each reuses something the model already half does; the other learns something new. The re-gating share reads clearly higher on the reuse side on all nine. No single simpler method does: the prior agrees on all nine but mostly with one value a side, effective rank on seven by small margins, steering on four, the neuron readings on at most six, update size on none. Pairs are labelled as built; this is not a verdict on reuse.

The pairs. Each built pair is two sets of fine-tunes with two to eight seeds a side. The reuse side does something the model half does already, such as copying a token; the new side learns something unfamiliar, such as a cipher. Below the line in the figure are four twin pairs: a real fine-tune against one trained on shuffled labels, which can only memorise. Each baseline tests one cheap explanation of what the share picks up; if one separated the sides as well, the decomposition would add nothing.

Every baseline on this card, by family: prior · steering · rank and size of the update · untrained decomposition · neurons: neuron share, top-set swap, top neurons before and after, idle neurons, neurons for half the gain, neurons on the twins, carrying neurons' old jobs, Goodfire's two readings · the Qwen versions · what the share adds.

The prior: was the answer already likely?

Asks
Does the share only track how familiar the trained answer already was?
How
log10 of the original model's probability of the trained answer, one value per map or task, before any fine-tune.
Found
The closest rival: it points the share's way on all nine pairs; on lowercase against ciphers the original model rates lowercase's answer 1.63 decades higher. On the two pairs built with the prior matched (gaps of 0.12 and 0.20 decades), the share still separates the sides.
Caveat
Eight of nine pairs have one value a side, so it calls direction only.
Where
Jobs E133 and E145; reports/plans/2026-11-09_plan_e133-prior-vs-share.md, reports/analyses/2026-11-10_analysis_e145-prior-lowercase.md.

Steering: is one direction in activations enough?

Asks
Does adding one average activation change to the original model reproduce the fine-tune?
How
Add the fine-tune's mean change at a block input, pooled over training windows or per key; layer and strength chosen by the largest gain, strength searched up to 8 times the change's own size. Eight random vectors of the same norm are the floor.
Found
Agrees on four pairs and reverses on three. The reversals come from the strength search: the ciphers keep gaining at four to eight times, the reuse sides stop at two. Added at the fine-tune's own size, the reversal is gone on both scored pairs.
Caveat
On every key-value pair the per-key vector is handed the answer, so it reads 0.87 to 1.26 on both sides. Fixing the layer or subtracting the random floor did not remove the reversal.
Where
E135 (copy against lookup), E143 (six more pairs, cell P2.10), E146 (why it reverses, cell P2.14); 2026-11-10_analysis_e143-holes-fill.md, 2026-11-10_analysis_e146-steering-reversal.md.

Rank and size of the update

Asks
Does the share only track how big the weight change is, or how many directions it spreads over?
How
Effective rank from the singular values of each saved weight change (Roy and Vetterli), and update size as its norm; each read as log10 of the ratio of the two sides' means. Then the larger side of every pair was shrunk by one number to its partner's size and every reading taken again (E187).
Found
Effective rank is higher on the reuse side on seven pairs, but by at most 17% more directions; on the four refit 96-key pairs all four are apart, reuse side higher (E171). Update size never agrees: where it differs, the new side's update is bigger. Matched in size, the share still separates all nine pairs, and steering the same seven pairs it called before.
Caveat
Effective rank cannot change under one scalar, so size matching does not test it.
Where
E141 (cell P2.9), E171 (cell P2.25), E187 (cell P2.28, its own card, with the 67M and Qwen panels); 2026-11-10_analysis_e141-update-svd.md, 2026-11-12_analysis_p2-25-refit-rank.md, 2026-11-16_analysis_p2-28-size-matched.md.

The share on an untrained decomposition

Asks
Does the signal come from training the decomposition at all?
How
The same reading on each matrix's plain SVD: at each position keep the components with the largest contribution, as many as the trained decomposition switches on (1 to 30 per matrix).
Found
It agrees on two pairs, reverses on two and makes no call on five; the share's gaps of 0.51 to 0.69 fall to 0.07 or less. On the four refit 96-key pairs it never follows the share.
Caveat
The rule reproduces the model poorly: the original model's answer log-odds sit 3.1 below the original on average, against 0.8 above for the trained decomposition, so a low share may come from the rule.
Where
E136, E147; 2026-11-09_analysis_e136-share-untrained.md, 2026-11-10_analysis_e147-refit-96key.md.

The neuron readings. These follow Goodfire's essay "Neural networks are already clean" and its follow-up "Neurons as an interpretable basis": if MLP neurons are already a clean basis, a neuron reading could tell reuse from new with no decomposition. The user's version in plain words: "the same top neurons firing a bit harder" would read as reuse, "new top neurons" as new. Seven readings on the 67M's 4 blocks of 3072 neurons; none shows that split.

Neuron share: the share's own swap, done on neurons

Asks
Is the part of the gain carried by the original neurons firing differently, weights unchanged, higher on the reuse side?
How
Whole width: the original output weights applied to the fine-tune's own neuron firing. Half: the original MLP, both matrices, run on the input the fine-tune hands it.
Found
Whole width agrees on three pairs and reverses on four; half agrees on three and reverses on two. On copy against random maps, the whole-width reading puts copy below random maps (gap -0.38).
Caveat
A control fine-tune that changes only the MLP output weights already reads 0.43 on the half form, so readings near 0.43 do not show reuse.
Where
E98 (its own card), E143, E147; 2026-11-05_analysis_e98-neuron-r.md.

Top-set swap: do the neurons entering the top set carry the learning?

Asks
The essay's umbrella-to-bee swap: a fine-tune that relabels an umbrella as a bee switches on existing bee neurons, and swapping those into the original model makes it say "bee". Do our fine-tunes work that way?
How
For each window, take each block's top-firing neurons before and after the fine-tune (64 per block, or as many as the decomposition switches on) and run the original model keeping only one set or the other.
Found
The swap moves under 0.05 of the gain on every built pair; the largest is 0.047, a lowercase fine-tune. Under the one colour rule its seeds sit apart on all nine pairs, four the share's way and five the other, by 0.03 or less.
Caveat
The calls rest on tight seeds, not large gaps; masking alone moves the original model's answer log-odds by up to 3.4.
Where
E143 (cell P2.10); experiments/holes_fill/run.py, src/commute/masked_reads.py.

Top neurons before and after a fine-tune: same or new?

Asks
After a fine-tune, are the most strongly firing neurons the same ones as before, or new ones?
How
In each block, the 240 neurons that fire most strongly (7.8% of the width, the follow-up article's layer top-K; ranked by absolute firing, where the article ranks by firing), before and after; overlap is the share of the set in common (Jaccard), averaged over rows and blocks. A random update of the same size sets the floor.
Found
Mostly new neurons on both sides: a real fine-tune keeps at most 0.69 of the top set, while a random update keeps at least 0.84. The reuse side overlaps more on two of seven pairs, and neither side does on five.
Caveat
The positive control failed: "the same neurons firing a little harder" read 0.962, below a random update's 0.965, so a high overlap was never shown to mean reuse. One of the two calls may come from update size.
Where
E155 (cell P2.18); 2026-11-11_analysis_e155-neuron-overlap.md, figure below.

Idle neurons: did the fine-tune wake up quiet neurons?

Asks
Does the new side lean on neurons that were silent in the original model?
How
Of the 16 neurons the fine-tune changed most, the share that were idle before it, on copy against random maps.
Found
0.41 on the copy side against 0.13 on random maps: the opposite of the hoped-for direction.
Caveat
The copy side's seeds spread over 0.5, so no call. These 16 neurons carry at most 0.03 of the gain.
Where
E52 (its own card); 2026-10-29_analysis_e52-baseline-neurons.md.

Neurons for half the gain: does reuse sit on a few neurons?

Asks
Does the reuse side need fewer of the original neurons to carry its learning?
How
Rank the original neurons by contribution to the gain; patch in the fine-tuned firing on the top k only; the count is the smallest k (on a power-of-two grid) that recovers half of what patching all 12288 recovers.
Found
The reuse side needs clearly fewer on six pairs, three to eight times fewer; no difference on three pairs or either twin.
Caveat
Five of the six pairs also have a clearly larger new-side update, and matched in size one of them loses its call (E187). The ciphers had not passed E98's learned bar and are read as a description.
Where
E98 (four pairs), E172 (the rest, cell P2.24); 2026-11-12_analysis_p2-24-neurons-for-half-gain.md.

Neurons on the twin pairs

Asks
Do the neuron shares tell a real fine-tune from its shuffled-label twin?
How
Whole-width and half neuron shares on the case- and repeat-label twins, three seeds a side.
Found
Case: no call, the two sides' seeds overlap in both forms. Repeat: the shuffled twin reads higher in both forms, the reverse of part 1's order.
Caveat
The re-gating share makes no call on either twin under its own rule, so there is nothing to agree with.
Where
E158 (cell P2.21); 2026-11-11_analysis_p2-20-part1-vs-methods.md.

The carrying neurons' old jobs, read the essay's way

Asks
Did the neurons that carry a fine-tune already have a job related to the task before training?
How
The essay's method cut to size: each carrying neuron's top windows in the original model on Pile text, a one-line job written blind, a judge asked whether the job relates to the task, and random neurons as the floor.
Found
On the 67M (E101) almost no carrying neuron's job was tied to its task, on either side. On Qwen3-1.7B (E102) the carrying neurons overlap a system prompt's neurons more than random, but nearly whatever the fine-tune teaches.
Caveat
No power: on the 67M the positive control read 0 of 10, then 0 of 30 under a sharper judge; on Qwen the judge tied biography neurons to biography only half the time, against about a third for random ones. The route was closed by its filed rule.
Where
E101, E102 (baseline-lead); 2026-11-05_analysis_e101-carrying-jobs.md, 2026-11-05_analysis_e102-qwen-carrying.md.

Goodfire's two readings: do moved neurons keep their job, and does the gain survive keeping the top neurons?

Asks
Do the neurons a fine-tune moves most still fire hardest on the same Pile text afterwards, and does the fine-tune's gain survive when each block keeps only its strongest neurons per token?
How
For each fine-tune on the nine built pairs, the 32 neurons per block whose firing on the task changed most; the share of each one's 20 strongest Pile windows (of 2,000) that stay its strongest afterwards, against a random update of matched size per matrix. The check came first: neurons made to fire harder kept 0.67 of their windows, neurons given each other's jobs 0.10. Then the gain kept with 240, 768 or 1,536 of 3,072 neurons per block.
Found
Real fine-tunes keep 0.60 to 0.80 of their windows, against 0.88 to 0.97 for random updates, on all 18 sides. The reuse side keeps more on 7 of 9 pairs, by 0.04 to 0.11; lowercase against cipher and the copy pair give no call. Gain kept under the mask is unreadable: with no update at all, keeping 240, 768 or 1,536 neurons adds 5.76, 3.79 and 0.89 nats of Pile loss.
Caveat
The lean filed for no call on at least 6 pairs did not hold (2 of 9). Update size as a covariate was not checked here. Goodfire's top-K works on a much wider model; this dense 67M is not sparse enough per token. Descriptive, no verdict on reuse.
Where
E190 (cell P2.30, job 60144, $0; the first stage, P2.29, stopped on its sparsity check); 2026-11-16_analysis_e190-neuron-jobs.md.

What the share adds

  • Prior plus steering at the fine-tune's own size together call all nine pairs the share's way; each alone misses at least one. Where the prior is matched, steering carries the pair; where steering gives no call, the prior does. Direction only.
  • The share's gap sizes follow neither. Rank correlation with the prior's gaps is 0.02, with steering's -0.10. The share's largest and smallest gaps both fall on the two prior-matched pairs. Nine pairs: a description, not a test (cells P2.16, P2.19).
  • Against part 1's order on five shared pairs (cell P2.20): the share agrees on the three built pairs; steering ranks the five as part 1 does but reverses both twins; no method agrees in sign on all five.
  • The twin pairs. The share separates half-known from its shuffle, real side higher, reads the shuffled twin higher on two others, by 0.03 and 0.09, and does not separate the case pair; steering, size and rank read the twin higher there, which may reflect a twin trained ten times longer.
