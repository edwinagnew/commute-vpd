E133mixed

Does any simpler method tell the two sides of a pair apart the way the re-gating share does?

2026-11-09 to 11-12 · provisional, no independent check · seven cluster and CPU jobs, $0 · experiments/method_table

The answer. We built nine pairs of fine-tunes. One side of each pair reuses something the model already does; the other side is new. The re-gating share reads clearly higher on the reuse side on all nine. No single simpler method does that. Steering agrees on four pairs and points the other way on three. Effective rank agrees on seven, but by small margins. Neurons agree on at most four, and the top-neuron form points the other way on five. Update size agrees on none. The original model's prior and steering at its natural size together call the share's direction on all nine, though they say nothing about how far apart the sides are. Pairs are labelled as built; this is not a verdict on reuse.

The pairs. Each built pair is two sets of fine-tunes of the 67M model, with two to eight seeds a side. The reuse side does something the model half does already, such as copying a token. The new side learns something unfamiliar, such as a cipher. Below the line are four twin pairs: a real fine-tune against one trained on shuffled labels, which can only memorise.

The methods. Each simpler method tests one cheap explanation of what the share picks up. Each cell of the figure is the reuse side minus the new side. Its colour is a call, not a score: red or blue only when the two sides' seeds do not overlap, the same rule in every column, however small the gap.

re-gating shareKeeps the original weights and only changes which subcomponents are switched on.The original model is run with the subcomponents the fine-tune switches on; the share is the part of the fine-tune's gain in the answer's log-odds that this recovers (0 none, 1 all).The method under test.
share on untrained decompositionThe same reading, on each matrix's plain SVD instead of the trained decomposition.With no trained gates, a fixed rule decides what is switched on: at each position keep the SVD components with the largest contribution, as many as the trained decomposition switches on (1 to 30 per matrix). This reproduces the model poorly: with those gates the original model's answer log-odds sit 3.1 below the original on average, against 0.8 above for the trained decomposition. So a low share here may come from the rule, not the pieces.The signal comes from training the decomposition.
neuron readings (three forms)The same idea on MLP neurons: keep the original weights, use the fine-tune's neuron firing.Whole width: the original output weights applied to the fine-tune's own neuron firing. Half: the original MLP, both matrices, run on the input the fine-tune hands it, so only re-firing caused upstream counts. Top neurons: keep each block's 64 most active neurons (or as many as the decomposition switches on) and read the difference. The half form has a floor: a control fine-tune that changes only the MLP output weights, giving no neuron a new input, already reads 0.43, so readings near 0.43 do not show reuse.Neurons would do as well.

What stands out.

  • The prior is the closest rival. It points the same way as the share on all nine pairs, including lowercase against ciphers, where the original model rates lowercase's answer 1.63 decades higher. But on two pairs the prior is nearly equal on both sides (gaps of 0.12 and 0.20 decades), and the share still separates them.
  • Size does not explain the share, and rank only weakly follows it. On three pairs both sides have about the same update size, and the share still separates all three. Where size does differ, the new side has the bigger update. Effective rank is higher on the reuse side on seven pairs, but the reuse side's update spreads over at most 17% more directions.
  • The top-neuron form now separates every built pair, by tiny gaps. Under the one colour rule its seeds sit apart on all nine, four the share's way and five the other, but no gap exceeds 0.03 of the gain: the seeds are tight, not the gaps large. Neurons for half the gain point the share's way on six built pairs: the reuse side needs clearly fewer neurons, with gaps of 0.52 to 0.90 in log10 (3 to 8 times fewer). On three pairs there is no difference: copy at a new marker against lookup, copy against cipher at step 10, and half-known against cipher at equal size. Five of the six pairs where the count separates also have a clearly larger new-side update (log10 0.08 to 0.28); the sixth, rank-1 against cipher at step 10, separates at equal size, but its cipher had not passed E98's learned bar. Nine pairs cannot show that the count adds anything beyond update size. Neither twin pair is apart. E98 had skipped the cipher sides because their held-out P(target) was under its 0.5 learned bar (0.23 to 0.45); E172 reads them anyway, as a description. Copy against random maps reads 0.70 as the log of the ratio of means and 0.60 as the mean of the logs, against the 0.48 relayed from the user; that difference is unexplained.
  • Steering points the wrong way on three pairs because of how its strength is chosen. Added at the fine-tune's own size (strength 1), the reversal is gone on both scored pairs. The reuse sides stop gaining once the vector is doubled, while the ciphers keep gaining up to four or eight times, and the search picks those larger multiples. Fixing the layer, subtracting a random baseline or changing the denominator did not remove it.
  • Neurons and the untrained decomposition read small or mixed gaps. Without its training, the decomposition loses most of the share's gap. On the four 96-key pairs, refit from E58's recipe and seeds, the untrained decomposition never follows the share; the neuron readings and steering follow it on some of them, by gaps of 0.07 or less, except steering's 0.59 on copy against the prior-matched cipher.
  • Prior plus steering at strength 1 together call the share's direction on all nine built pairs; each alone misses at least one. Where the prior is matched, steering carries the pair (gaps of 0.31 and 0.44 of the gain); where steering gives no call, the prior carries it (3.2 to 3.7 decades). With steering's searched strength, three pairs have the two pointing opposite ways. This reads direction only, not gap size, on nine pairs.
  • The share's gap sizes follow neither the prior's nor steering's. Across the nine built pairs the rank correlation of the share's gap with the prior's is 0.02, and with strength-1 steering's it is -0.10. The share's largest and smallest gaps (0.69 and 0.39) fall on the two pairs built with the prior matched. Nine pairs from a few task families: a description, not a test.
  • What the share adds, from the nine built pairs (cell P2.19, no run, no verdict on reuse): it is the only single reading that puts the reuse-built side higher on all nine, with gaps of 0.39 to 0.69 (seed spread at most 0.06) that follow neither the prior's nor steering's; but it gives no different call on direction, since the prior and strength-1 steering together reproduce all nine, and the neuron readings separate few pairs (under the one colour rule of P2.23 the top-neuron form separates all nine, but by 0.03 or less and five the other way). Whether the share or a cheap reading is right about reuse waits on scoring against part 2's labels. Source: reports/analyses/2026-11-11_analysis_p2-19-what-share-adds.md.
  • Against part 1's order on the five shared pairs (cell P2.20, no run, no verdict on reuse): all five part 1 pairs overlap the method table on the same saved fine-tunes, and part 1 puts the reuse-built side higher on all five (gaps 0.19 to 0.43). The share agrees on the three built pairs and made no call on the two label twins (under the one colour rule it now reads the repeat twin higher on every seed, by 0.09); steering ranks the five as part 1 does (rank correlation 1.0) but reverses both twins; update size reverses four; no method agrees in sign on all five. The neuron readings (filled on the twins by E158) make no call on the case twin and read the repeat twin higher on every seed, the reverse of part 1. Part 1 repeats the build labels on direction, so only the gap sizes are new. Source: reports/analyses/2026-11-11_analysis_p2-20-part1-vs-methods.md, figure experiments/part1_vs_methods/figures/part1_vs_methods.png.
  • The neuron baselines, in plain words: "the same top neurons firing a bit harder" would read as reuse, "new top neurons" as new, and the runs do not show that split. (1) The top-set swap: for each window we take each block's top-firing neurons before and after the fine-tune and run the original model keeping only the after-set or only the before-set; the swap moves under 5% of the gain on every built pair, so neurons entering the top set do not carry the learning. (2) Idle neurons: of the 16 neurons the fine-tune changed most, the share silent before it is 0.41 on the copy side against 0.13 on random maps (seed spread 0.5, so no call), the opposite of the hoped-for direction, with more already-quiet neurons on the reuse side. (3) The plain overlap of the top sets before and after is a separate run (reports/analyses/2026-11-11_analysis_e155-neuron-overlap.md, cell P2.18, provisional): a real fine-tune keeps 0.34 to 0.69 of the 240 top neurons per block against 0.84 to 0.95 for a random update of the same size, and the two sides differ by more than their seed spread on only two of seven pairs, so mostly new neurons arrive on both sides; its positive control, "the same neurons firing 0.5% harder", failed (overlap 0.962 against 0.965 for a random update), so a high overlap was never shown to mean reuse. Sources: experiments/holes_fill/run.py, src/commute/masked_reads.py, experiments/baseline_neurons/results/baseline_neurons.json (idle share, score P2), experiments/method_table/results/final.json.

The twin pairs are less clear. The share separates half-known from its shuffle, real side higher. It reads the class map's twin and the repeat label's twin higher, by 0.03 and 0.09, with the seeds apart, and does not separate the case label pair. There, steering, size and rank read the twin higher, which may reflect how the twins were trained (one trained ten times longer) more than what they learned.
