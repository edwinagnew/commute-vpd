Does the original model's prior for the answer order the saved fine-tunes the way the re-gating share does?

2026-11-09 · provisional, no independent check · cluster debug L40, job 56889, about 1 minute, $0 · experiments/prior_vs_share

The question. Part 2 asks what the re-gating share measures and whether a cheaper reading does as well. The share is the part of a fine-tune's gain in the answer's log-odds that comes from changing only which of the original subcomponents the decomposition switches on, with their weights held fixed. Earlier small checks found it tracks how likely the original model already found the answer. This run asks how closely, over every saved fine-tune.

What we did. We took 24 saved task maps, each with its share already read, and computed the original model's prior: its geometric-mean probability of the trained answer before any fine-tune. We then compared the two orderings by rank correlation, with a bootstrap interval and a shuffled-share null, under a rule filed first. Two controls ran first and alone: the prior reproduced its saved value exactly on a copy map and on an elicit map. Sides are labelled as each map was built; no verdict on reuse.

What we found. The prior orders the maps much as the share does: rank correlation 0.85, interval 0.67 to 0.93, against a null of 0.35. The interval is too narrow, because one trained table enters at several key counts. Three maps sit off the line: the copy map at the new marker reads 1.09 and one older copy map 0.80, both high, and the lookup at the new marker 0.40, low.

What it means. Most of the share's ordering over these maps is already in the original model's prior. The share differs from the prior mainly on copy maps. Copy maps at a prior of 4e-5 to 6e-5 read 0.77 to 0.80; a cipher matched to their prior reads 0.38. The premise check came back doubtful: part of the link may be built in, since the share's denominator starts from the original model's log-odds of the same answer.
