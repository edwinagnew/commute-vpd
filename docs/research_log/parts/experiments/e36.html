Re-gating on a fine-tune with no trigger

2026-10-26 · RunPod RTX 4090 · experiments/regating_spread

Yes, once enough positions are compared, and it needs the original model's view of the context. On a fine-tune of lowercased text, with no trigger, the change at each position follows the positions the original model treats alike: fitted on all 262,144 positions, their mean change recovers 0.76 to 0.89 of the switching (first figure). Γ's own input does as well; a summary of the last few tokens recovers about half, the current token about a third. The planned code-text fine-tune did not learn.

Run
A fine-tune of all 24 matrices on the next-token loss at every position, Adam 3e-5, batches of eight 512-token rows used once, as a do-nothing state with Γ frozen. Scored at every position of 64 held-out rows: the gain in mean log-probability, the share of it the CI-masked model gets from Γ reading the fine-tuned model, and how much of that each rule recovers. Rules fitted at 16,384 positions of training rows: one change everywhere, one per current token, the 8 nearest positions by original values, k-means groups up to 512, a line per subcomponent. First task: the 5% of Pile rows richest in code punctuation, mostly LaTeX and maths. Second task, after the first did not learn: Pile text lowercased, read at steps 10, 100 and 300. Each run under 2 min.
Found
  • Lowercase: gain 0.07 to 0.13 nats per token; switching carried 1.05, 0.90 and 0.79 of it. The code-like update, which learned nothing, gave about 0.8 too, so the share alone does not show learning.
  • The 8 nearest positions recover 0.72, 0.54 and 0.51, missing the filed 0.6 at steps 100 and 300; one change per current token, covering only 90 tokens, 0.24 to 0.32; one change everywhere 0.03 to 0.07. A third of the nearest positions hold the same token, far above chance. By the filed tree, step 300 reads "the change depends on more than a position's own original values".
  • Groups of the original values climb with their number: 32 groups already beat the 90 token groups, and 512 reach 0.44 to 0.64, below the nearest rule. The line per subcomponent falls from 0.39 to −0.01, against its filed lean.
  • Amendments 4 and 5: on 262,144 positions the original model's attention inputs, which Γ reads, predict the change as well as Γ's values (0.87, 0.78, 0.74), while hashed features of the current and preceding tokens, in effect the last four, reach 0.53, 0.54 and 0.50, still above the token rule; a summary of the whole preceding text is the fairer null, not yet run. The token rule refitted on the full sample covers 71% of positions and recovers 0.33 to 0.42; position in the row 0.04 to 0.07; no near-duplicate text.
  • Amendments 2 and 3: with four and sixteen times as many fitted positions the nearest rule recovers 0.80, 0.67, 0.58 and then 0.89, 0.78, 0.76, so the scored run's miss was sampling; we predicted it would stay under 0.7. Drawing the neighbours only from the same token adds nothing. A tenth to a quarter of the switching stays unexplained. Whether Γ's original values beat any rich summary of the context, such as Γ's input, is not yet tested.
  • Code-like text: the fine-tune made held-out text worse, 0.05 to 0.07 nats per token, with a flat training loss, so nothing was scored. Switching still carried about 0.8 of that change.
