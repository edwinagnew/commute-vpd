Do eliciting updates map more cleanly onto neurons than new ones?

2026-11-02 · MATS cluster L40 debug QoS, $0 (job 48527, 2 min 12 s) · experiments/neuron_sparsity

The question. The user asked whether fine-tunes built to bring out something the model already does sit on fewer MLP neurons than fine-tunes that teach something new. If they do, a reader with no decomposition gets part of what the re-gating share is claimed to give (Q5). Earlier neuron patching (E56) ranked neurons by their mean shift over all windows, so a map using different neurons per key looked dense by construction, and it never read the update's own sparsity.

What we did. Nothing was trained. On 26 saved fine-tunes of three families (the half-known map and cipher of E54 and E56 at five seeds, the identity and random tables of E46, the lowercase and cipher broad fine-tunes of E53) we read five neuron numbers: how few neurons carry the weight change, the same on the first gradient, whether the change lies along the neurons' own weights, how few neurons the activation shift sits on, and how few top-shifted neurons are enough for half of what patching recovers. "Built to elicit" means how the task was made, not the causal label of decisions row 112: these pairs have no causal check. A number counted only if elicit was on the sparser side and separated from new at equal update size, at equal learning and on both other families, and beat its random-rotation, Gaussian or shuffled-target reference.

What we found. No reading shows elicit on fewer neurons on all the pairs, so none answers yes. The finished update is flat, 0.98 of a fully spread update on the main pair, and does not lie along neurons' own weights; the activation shift is narrower for elicit on families A and C and wider on B, and the count of top neurons separates only on C. On the main pair the count's gap of 0.97 (in log2) falls just under its floor of 1 and shrinks to 0.17 at the cipher's own, larger-update step, where elicit was not read. On saved numbers, not a filed test, the re-gating share r follows the build on all three families (0.76 to 0.80 for elicit against 0.12 to 0.29 for new).

What it means. Counting neurons does not follow how the tasks were built, as the decomposition's share does on the same fine-tunes, so a cheap neuron rival to the share is not found at this size. Two limits sit on that: every test compares one task with one task, so a real kind effect as large as one task's spread would pass only about a third of the time, and the pairs are built to elicit with no causal label. This is a null of a low-power rule, not a measured absence, and it is not evidence that r is right. Whether the activation shift and the count follow a causal label on the truth-set pair (E55) needs your say.
