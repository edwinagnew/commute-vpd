Can we name the part a 67M fine-tune runs through, across the old tasks, for the new-against-reuse definition?

2026-11-05 · cluster L40 debug, $0 · experiments/locator_table · provisional, not independently checked

The question. You decided that a fine-tune gets a label (new or strengthened) only through a located mechanism ({{n:decisions}}). E96 found the copying head with a sweep but could not read lowercase. Can one locator, checked first on planted parts, name the part on each of the old tasks?

What we did. We planted two known changes in the original model to test the locator, then tried four ways of finding the part on the copy, lookup, lowercase and cipher fine-tunes. Removal switches a part off in the fine-tune and reads how much of the gain goes (the state's own terms), beside the original model's own fall. The others patch the original model's outputs into the fine-tune, part by part (carry) or connection by connection (Amendment 1, the edges you suggested).

What we found. The planted parts were found exactly. Only removal at head grain passed the known answer: head (2,4) takes 0.56 to 0.65 of the copy gain and the pair (2,3)+(2,4) 0.78 to 0.88, with lookup spared. Every patching read, whole parts or single connections, put block 3's feed-forward layer first on copy and lookup alike, so those show where the gain enters the output. On lowercase the pair (1,1)+(1,2) takes 0.80 and 0.85 of the gain, but the original model falls by more with it off, so against capitalised text the pair takes only 0.10 to 0.13.

What it means. For the reuse table only head-grain removal can name a part, and only copying is located. The lowercase pair is something both models need; whether that counts as located is your call. The edge run stopped on a mis-specified tolerance (single edges do not add up), not a bug. One amendment is left, and a literature review on published locators comes first.
