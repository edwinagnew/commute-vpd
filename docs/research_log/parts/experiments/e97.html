The question. You decided that a fine-tune gets a label (new or strengthened) only through a located mechanism ({{n:decisions}}). E96 found the copying head with a sweep but could not read lowercase. Can one locator, checked first on planted parts, name the part on each of the old tasks?

What we did. We planted two known changes in the original model to test the locator, then ran it on the copy, lookup, lowercase and cipher fine-tunes: it switches a part off in the fine-tune and reads how much of the fine-tune's gain goes (the state's own terms), beside the original model's own fall under the same switch-off, and ranks parts by how much of the gain passes through them.

What we found. The planted parts were found exactly. Switching off head (2,4) on copying again removes 0.56 to 0.65 of the gain and spares lookup (at most 0.01). The pass-through ranking put a feed-forward layer ahead of (2,4), so that route was not run further. On lowercase the pair of heads (1,1) and (1,2) removes 0.80 and 0.85 of the fine-tune's gain, but the original model falls by more (1.10 nats) with the same pair off, so against capitalised text the pair takes only 0.10 to 0.13 of the gain. A hand-written copy mechanism missed its filed bar, and the bar was degenerate (both numbers were zero to the noise).

What it means. The lowercase pair is something both models need; it is not a removal of what the fine-tune added. Whether that counts as located is your call in the morning, not ours.
