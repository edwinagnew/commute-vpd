Qwen3-1.7B: does VPD's adversary fail doing nothing, and does it pass a good placement?

2026-11-04 · RunPod H100 PCIe, 1.94 h, $6.21 billed; Amendments 1 and 2 on two Vast H200 NVL pods · experiments/qwen_d1_adversary · first run independently checked; Amendments 1 and 2 not independently checked

The question. D1 is a map on Qwen3-1.7B that keeps the decomposition faithful for a real update, judged by a test that can fail a bad placement (Q1). E79 showed the state's own-mask read cannot be that test, since doing nothing reads as well as the best trained change. Does VPD's own adversary, which searches for the masks that hurt a state most, do better? The user then asked for the Qwen column of the slide's scorecard: the same four methods and checks on the saved first update.

What we did. We read four ways of placing one fine-tune update into Qwen's decomposition with VPD's adversary on web text (20 sign steps of 0.1 up the KL, two seeds): doing nothing (the whole update left in Δ), least squares, adjoint, and E76's clean-loss teacher-training state. We also read how much of the update's gain each state's weights keep, on 256 held-out GSM8K problems. The first run had read the adversary on maths rows for doing nothing, in-span and the clean-term change, with the bars filed in E71.

What we found. We can't yet tell on Qwen. Every method passes the adversary, doing nothing included, and a tenth of the update alone keeps 0.93 of the gain, a quarter 1.00, so neither check separates the methods on this update. Not independently checked.

What it means. Telling the methods apart needs a test that can fail a bad placement and an update whose gain builds gradually. The first run's finding stands beside this: the adversary reads doing nothing above the original model, and the filed bars do not cohere, so that run ranks no placement.

Faithful is the state's final damage under the adversary over the unchanged decomposition's, seed by seed, averaged. The bars are the slide's: pass at 1.40 or under, fail at 1.61 or over. The unchanged decomposition's two seeds read 1.703 and 1.438, a spread of 18.4%; every pair of rows sits closer than that, so no ranking is claimed.
Behaviour kept is (loss at W0 − loss of the state) / (loss at W0 − loss of the fine-tune), no masks, response tokens only. W0 reads 2.495 nats per token and the fine-tune 0.684.
Left in Δ is the plain share of the update's squared size outside the subcomponents, as on the slide. Qwen's earlier 36% was an input-weighted norm ratio on another measure.
Why the kept column cannot separate. The same read on a fraction of the update: a tenth keeps 0.93, a quarter keeps 1.00, the whole update 1.00 (the control); by the filed rule this is "saturated". E40's first step alone has 87% of step 10's gain. The check tests only the update's own direction, so it does not say how the adjoint or the teacher reached the gain.

Differences from the slide's numbers. The adversary matches E71's settings but uses two seeds rather than one, web rows rather than Pile, one block of 128 rows rather than two, fp32 components, batch 1, and the card. The bars come from the 67M's spread; Qwen's own spread gives a pass bar near 1.26, which least squares would miss. The update's gain is 1.81 of 2.50 nats, against 0.13 on the 67M, so a kept share here is not comparable with one there. E76's state moves 200 columns per matrix at Adam 1e-6 on maths and web rows, where the 67M's recipe moves all alive columns at 1e-5. The adjoint's scale is taken on the update's own first training rows, as on the 67M; E68's scale on maths rows is within 5-11% at every matrix.
