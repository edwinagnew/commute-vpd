Does a crosscoder, a learned dictionary over the original and fine-tuned models together, put a fine-tune's change on latents the original also has?

2026-11-17 · cell P2.31, runs 1 and 2 (E203) done ·experiments/crosscoder

The question. Part 2 compares the re-gating share and the neuron reading with simple baselines. A crosscoder baseline (Minder et al. 2025) asks how much of a fine-tune's activation change sits on latents shared with the original model against latents only the fine-tune has. It is descriptive: no side is called reused or new.

What we did. Run 1 (one H100, about 15 min, $0.92) trained a small crosscoder of 24,576 latents on the original model's and a fine-tune's activations at the input of block 2. A relative norm of 1 means a latent's decoder sits wholly on the fine-tune side, 0.5 shared. The original model against itself passed (every alive latent at 0.5). On a planted new direction one latent carried 0.987 of the change, but its relative norm was about 0.86, under the literature's 0.9 bar, so it was not labelled fine-tune-only.

What we found. The crosscoder finds a planted change but, at this short training (16.7M tokens against Minder's 200M), does not label it new under the literature's bar. No real pair was read.
