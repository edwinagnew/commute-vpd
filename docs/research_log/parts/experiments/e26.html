Do E21's SGD and Adam verdicts come back when the training batches arrive in a different order?

2026-10-22 · RunPod H100 ×2 · experiments/ladder_second_seed

The question. E21 asked whether the optimiser decides if VPD stays faithful (Q11). It used component training, which trains the decomposition's pieces directly on a task while Γ and Δ stay frozen. Adam passed the faithfulness check at steps 100 and 1,000; plain SGD passed at step 100 and failed at step 1,000. Adam's result also met the condition set for moving the commute line (Q1) back to first place: a map that stays faithful and keeps the behaviour at both steps. All of this rested on one order of training batches, the only randomness in this training, so we tried a second order.

What we did. We reran E21's recipe with a new batch order on three next-word tasks. On two the target is a common word: after " it" predict " is", and after " which" predict " was". On the third it is rare: after " in" predict " Fig". Each task had an SGD arm and an Adam arm, read at steps 100 and 1,000. The check is the adversarial ratio: the damage an adversary searching over masks of the pieces does at the trained state, over what it does at the original model; 1 means nothing changed. The line is 1.5, moved by the original model's own spread over adversary seeds, 7.5% here. So a state passes at 1.40 or below, fails at 1.61 or above, and in between is too close to call. A pass also needs the target's held-out probability to stay at 0.9 or more.

What we found. No verdict changed on the two common-target tasks (first figure). Adam passed at both steps; SGD passed at step 100 and failed at step 1,000, at about three times the original model's damage. At step 1,000 both optimisers had made about the same largest change to any weight matrix, and only the adversary told them apart (third figure). On the rare-target task, as in E21, no state was both faithful and kept the target (second figure).

What it means. E21's findings are not an accident of one batch order. The evidence is two orders, one adversary seed per state and a short adversary. Nothing was read between steps 100 and 1,000, so when SGD crosses the line is unknown. The move-back condition was met, but the user dropped the idea of a main line the same day, so it no longer decided anything. Why SGD fails where Adam passes at the same size of change is still open.

Run
E21's recipe at a second batch order (SGD at rate 0.03 on the common-target tasks and 0.1 on the rare one, Adam at 1e-5, 1,000 steps), each state checked at steps 100 and 1,000, after the original model had read its known values and the first order's rerun had matched E21 on the common-target tasks. Two RunPod H100 pods, the first stopped partway by a usage-limit halt, about $6 in all.
Found
  • On both common-target tasks the second order gives E21's verdicts: Adam passes at both steps (0.95 to 1.14) and SGD passes at step 100 and fails at step 1,000 (about 3), as expected.
  • At step 1,000 the two optimisers change the weights by about the same amount (0.017 to 0.019) and random masks cannot tell them apart; only the adversary separates them.
  • On the rare-target task nothing passes, as expected: SGD fails the check (52 at step 1,000), and Adam stays under the line while the target slips below 0.9, just under at step 100 (0.895) and well under by step 1,000 (0.80).
