Does the fixed change of Γ's importance values follow the cue word, or the answer?

2026-10-22 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/many_cues

The question. Γ, VPD's importance function, is a small frozen network that gives each subcomponent of the model's 24 decomposed matrices an importance value between 0 and 1 at every position. After a fine-tune Γ reads different activations, so its values change. On the re-gating line, sub-question Q5 asks whether that change describes a fine-tune, and the fixed-shift hypothesis says that at the trigger (the position just before the word the fine-tune teaches) it is mostly one fixed change, the same for every input. E32 found that on the copy task one fixed change fails but one per key word works. There each key has its own answer, so E32 could not tell whether the change belongs to the cue (the word at the trigger) or to the answer. This run separates the two.

What we did. In Pile text we looked for the target with the most cue words after which the original model already favours it: " of", with 137 such words, of which we kept the 32 most frequent, such as " number", " part" and " because". We built three do-nothing states of E28's ten-step fine-tune, on one seed. In a do-nothing state the fine-tune's whole weight change is set aside in the residual Δ, the part of the weights no subcomponent explains, and the subcomponents stay unchanged. Only Γ sees the fine-tuned model, so the fine-tune acts only through the importance values Γ hands out. Different answers: the 32 cues each fine-tuned toward its own word, chosen with a similar starting probability, such as " number" then " one". One answer: the same cues all fine-tuned toward " of". Control: " will" then " have", one cue, E28's state. On held-out inputs we gave the model substitute importance values at the trigger. As in E18 and E32, the share is the rise in the target's log-odds that a substitute gives over the original model's values, divided by the rise each input's own updated values give: 0 is the original model's values, 1 the input's own, and above 1 means the substitute raised the target slightly more than the input's own values did. The fixed change is the training inputs' mean updated values, scored net of the original model's mean, since averaging alone gives something back. We compared one fixed change for every input, one per cue, and random groups of training inputs the same sizes as the cues'. Before the run we leaned that the change follows the answer, so that with one shared answer the shared change would come close to the per-cue one.

What we found. One change per cue gives back most of the effect on both 32-cue states, while one shared change gives back 0.04 with different answers and 0.55 with one answer (figure 1). Random groups match the shared change, so the per-cue gain comes from grouping by cue, not from averaging fewer inputs. So the change follows the cue, against our lean. With one answer a different cue's values also give back more, 0.44 against 0.07 (figure 2), and different cues' changes are more alike (figure 3). That contrast is not a clean test of the shared answer: the different-answers fine-tune learned less in its ten steps (figure 4, a caveat the report gives).

What it means. On one seed, at the trigger only and with one shared target, " of", the fixed change follows the cue: with one shared answer, one shared change gives back only about half of what one change per cue does, so cues that share an answer share part of their change and need their own for the rest. The larger sharing seen with one answer may come partly from that fine-tune being stronger. The report reads this, on this one target and seed, as evidence against "tasks the model already does" as the hypothesis's scope, since the task is old and still needs a change per cue (report). With E32, the user restated the hypothesis on 2026-10-23, tentatively, as one fixed change per cue, partly shared when cues share an answer.

Run
Three do-nothing states of a ten-step Adam fine-tune (seed 0), read at the trigger on held-out inputs: 99 for the one-cue control and 192, 6 per cue, for each 32-cue state, with the control first; it reproduced E28's number. One MATS cluster job (29625) on an L40, 197 s, $0.
Found
  • One change per cue gives back most of the effect on both 32-cue states, and one shared change only 0.55 even when every cue has the same answer; so, on one seed and one shared target, the change follows the cue, against our lean that it follows the answer.
  • With one shared answer a different cue's values give back 0.44, against 0.07 when the answers differ, but the two fine-tunes also differ in strength.
  • The different-answers fine-tune was weaker, reaching a held-out target probability of 0.56 against 0.81, so the two states differ in more than the shared answer.
