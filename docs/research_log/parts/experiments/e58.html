Does a fine-tune of the half-known key map on some of its keys carry to the keys it never saw, and do the route split's re-gating share and the stand-in ratio agree with that behavioural label?

2026-10-30 · MATS cluster L40 debug QoS, $0 (jobs 46266, 46332, 46383, 46398, 46426, 46450, 46489, 46673) · experiments/open_pair

The question. The re-gating line's "elicited" and "new" labels have all been set by how a task was built. No 67M fine-tune carried a label earned from behaviour alone, so the programme's second step was blocked. This experiment builds one at $0, following Fable's advice and your standing instruction to build the open pair ourselves.

What we did. E54's half-known map and its size-matched cipher (the same keys, values the model cannot know) were fine-tuned on 24 of the 32 keys, three seeds each. The label is behavioural: a state is "reuses" if the 8 unseen keys gain at least half as much as the trained ones, counting each answer's rise over the other values of the map, and "new" if that share is within three bootstrap errors of zero. The re-gating share r (the share of the gain recovered when no subcomponent changes and only Γ's switching does), the stand-in ratio (the masked gain over the subcomponent gain, no ceiling at 1) and steering were read on the same states and scored against the label. Follow-ups gave the label two positive controls, a copy map (each key maps to itself) and a rank-1 map (each key maps to the model's own top word), and then trained the maps on 96 of 128 keys, four times as many, with the cipher as the no-rule control and three seeds per map. A last pair of jobs trained a shuffled map: the half-known map's words, but each trained key takes another key's value, so no word is the model's guess for its key. The last job trained a new four-word rule: each key's input-embedding row is projected onto the two leading directions of the 96 trained keys' rows, the sign pair gives four classes, and each class gets one word the model never says after these keys, with a twin whose class labels are shuffled over the keys (same words, no rule).

What we found. At 24 keys the unseen keys gained nothing beyond a small boost: the label reads "new" on all three seeds (share about −0.03 against a "reuses" bar of 0.5), while r read 0.76 to 0.79 and the ratio 0.93 to 0.95, so the filed prediction that they agree failed; the label also failed its own positive control (copy map "reuses" on one seed of three). At 96 keys every map carried further and the copy map reads "reuses" on all three seeds (carry 0.83), the label's working control. Over three seeds r differs between the three maps whose answers are the model's own guess (r 0.79, 0.90 and 0.87 at step 10) by more than seed noise, and not in carry order: the rank-1 map has the highest r and carries a fifth, the copy map carries over four fifths. All three sit far above the cipher (0.32 to 0.33). The shuffled map reads 0.477 over three seeds, between the cipher and the half-known map but only 0.015 to 0.03 under the 0.5 cut. A new cipher whose values the model rates almost as likely as the copy map's (prior 3.7e-5 against 5.9e-5, a factor of about 1.6) reads 0.37 to 0.38 on all three seeds, against copy's 0.77. A first-letter rule built to carry did not (0.04 to 0.08 beyond letter frequency), so it was not read. The new four-word rule did carry: 0.55 to 0.64 beyond its null at unseen keys, 0.40 to 0.49 on plain carry (about half of copy's 0.8), while its twin carried 0.08 to 0.09. Yet its r read 0.23 to 0.25, within 0.04 of the twin's 0.27 to 0.28 and with the lookups. At the first step over the gate (step 11) the behavioural label read the rule "reuses" on all three seeds while r read it new.

What it means. On the small model r agrees with whether the model already guessed the trained answer for that key, high when it did, low when not. That guess is how "own guess" is defined, so it separates these maps by construction; but r does not follow the model's prior of the answer along a smooth trend, since at a near-equal prior copy and the new cipher read 0.77 and 0.38 (one map pair; the independent check limits this to excluding a smooth trend). The one comparison with a reading that ignores the decomposition scored steering better. The new rule settles that r does not read carry: a rule that generalises reads as new, like a lookup over the same words. One cut on the model's own probability of the trained answer (between 3.7e-5 and 5.9e-5) sorts all eight maps into r's two bands, so on the small model VPD has not yet shown anything the model's logits do not. The granularity limit stands beside this: a new circuit built from existing subcomponents would read as old, and whether this rule's update ran through old subcomponents was not measured, so the limit is neither confirmed nor ruled out. One rule, one model, one decomposition. What "elicitation" means, and so whether r is the instrument at all, is a pending decision.
