Does a fine-tune of the half-known key map on 24 of its 32 keys carry to the 8 keys it never saw, and do the route split's re-gating share and the stand-in ratio agree with that behavioural label?

2026-10-30 · MATS cluster L40 debug QoS, $0 · coded and filed, not run · experiments/open_pair

The question. The re-gating line's "elicited" and "new" labels have all been set by how a task was built. No 67M fine-tune carries a label earned from behaviour alone, so the programme's second step is blocked. This experiment builds one at $0, following Fable's advice and your standing instruction to build the open pair ourselves.

What we did. Nothing has run yet. E54's half-known map and its size-matched cipher are fine-tuned on 24 of the 32 keys, three seeds each. A state is labelled "reuses" if it gains at least half as much on the 8 unseen keys as on the trained ones, and "new" if the unseen-key gain is within three row-bootstrap errors of zero (the reuse line's rule, read on behaviour only). Then the re-gating share r, the stand-in ratio and steering are read on the same states and scored against that label. The first stage alone checks that a zero update reads exactly zero and that E54's first state reproduces; the cipher is labelled right after each fine-tune and the job stops if it reads "reuses".

What we expect. The half-known map is about even odds to be labelled "reuses"; every cipher should read "new". If r and the ratio agree with the label where steering does not, it is the first earned case of the decomposition reading what cheap baselines cannot (E56); a "new" label beside a high r would be the first concrete case of the granularity limit, or a decomposition error. Rules are filed in experiments/open_pair/results/predictions.json.
