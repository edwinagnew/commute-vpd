Does splitting the fine-tune step by step, instead of once at the end, keep its behaviour in the pieces?

2026-10-21 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/step_split

The question. To read a fine-tune in the decomposition, its weight change has to be carried onto the pieces (the subcomponents), with whatever they cannot take left in the residual Δ. The splits so far act once, on the finished update, and on this large Adam fine-tune each failed something (E16). Q8 asks whether acting on every training step instead does better, letting the pieces move along with the fine-tune; Q1 asks whether any simple map keeps the decomposition faithful with Δ not absorbing the update.

What we did. We followed E16's emoticon fine-tune (Adam, 1,000 steps) one step at a time. After each step, per matrix, the pieces took the scaled adjoint split of that step's change. Each piece moves by how much the step acts along its own read and write directions. That move is multiplied by one number per matrix, the scale, refit every step to best match the step on the task's inputs. Δ took the rest, so the weights equal the fine-tuned model's at every step: this is the step split. We compared it with do-nothing (the whole update in Δ), the least-squares split of the finished update, and the one-shot adjoint split (the same split applied once to the finished update). A twin with the pieces held at the original model's, read on the energy measure only, showed what moving them does. Faithfulness is the adversarial ratio: the damage an adversary searching over masks of the pieces does on Pile text, over what it does to the unchanged decomposition. It passes under 1.63, which is 1 plus three times that ratio's spread over attacker seeds on this card (0.21).

What we found. The step split leaves more of the trained behaviour in Δ than the least-squares split at every step checked, 0.31 rising to 0.61 of it (first figure). It fails the adversarial check clearly at step 1,000; its passes at steps 10 and 100 are weak evidence, one attacker seed inside a wide band (second figure). Moving the pieces left Δ's share of the update unchanged (third figure; the held twin was not run through the other checks). The surprise, against a prediction that it would track the one-shot split: by step 1,000 Δ's change is larger than the whole update, 1.38 of its energy against the one-shot split's 0.69.

What it means. On this large fine-tune, acting on every step leaves more of the behaviour in Δ than least squares, which is what Q1 asks a map not to do, so the answer to Q8 is no, for the adjoint form on one task and one seed. A candidate explanation for Δ's excess, untested: with the pieces held and one fixed scale the per-step split equals the one-shot split exactly, so the scale refit every step is the remaining difference. Whether it causes the excess was not measured, because each matrix's scale per step was not saved. Along two small fine-tunes (E25) the step split passed the check at every step, which suggests the step-1,000 fail belongs to this large update.

Run
One emoticon fine-tune (Adam, rate 3e-5, seed 0) split every step, read at steps 10, 100 and 1,000 against do-nothing and the least-squares split on the same run, on one MATS cluster L40 in about 31 minutes ($0). A first try stopped at its control, the unchanged decomposition's spread (0.209) being above a line set on an H100; the line was raised to 0.25 before the rerun.
Found
  • The step split keeps the weights exact but leaves more of the behaviour in Δ than the least-squares split at every step, about a third early and over a half at step 1,000.
  • It fails the adversarial check at step 1,000 (2.67), the highest of the three states there; it sits under the line at steps 10 and 100, at one attacker seed.
  • Unexpected: moving the pieces left Δ's share unchanged, yet by step 1,000 Δ's change holds 1.38 of the update's energy, twice the one-shot split's; no single step hands Δ more than half of itself, so the steps' pieces must partly cancel as they add up; the per-step scale refit is a candidate cause, untested.
Bears on
Q8 (should the map act on each training step rather than on a finished run?), Q1 (is there a simple map that keeps the decomposition faithful with Δ not absorbing the update?)
Moved on
The report dropped the per-step form as a candidate map for now, and proposed a short rerun saving each matrix's scale per step to find which matrices carry the cancellation; it has not run. E25 then ran the step split along two small fine-tunes, where it passes the check at every step and still leaves more behaviour in Δ than least squares except on SGD at step 1,000.
