Does splitting a fine-tune one step at a time still fail the adversarial check when the fine-tune is small?

2026-10-22 · RunPod 4090 · experiments/step_split_small

The question. To read a fine-tune in the decomposition, its weight change has to be carried onto the pieces (the subcomponents), with whatever they cannot take left in the residual Δ. Q1 asks whether some simple way of doing that keeps the decomposition faithful without Δ soaking up the update. E19 tried the step split along a large Adam fine-tune and found it failed the adversarial check at step 1,000. This run asks whether the same failure appears on smaller updates, by repeating it on the two small fine-tunes of E21's SGD-against-Adam study (Q11, whether the optimiser matters).

What we did. Both fine-tunes teach the 67M model the emoticon task (after the first character of an emoticon, predict o), one with plain SGD at rate 0.01 and one with Adam at 3e-6, for 1,000 steps on one seed. The step split takes each optimizer step's weight change and moves every piece by how much that change acts along the piece's own read and write directions, times one scale per matrix refit every step to match the change on the task's inputs; Δ takes the rest, so the weights always equal the fine-tuned model's. We compared it at steps 10, 100 and 1,000 with two splits of the finished update: least squares (the smallest change of the pieces that matches the update as closely as they can, the rest in Δ) and do-nothing (the whole update in Δ). The adversarial ratio is the damage a 20-step search over masks of the pieces does on Pile text, over the damage it does to the original decomposition: 1 means no worse. On this card a ratio below 1.45 passes and one above 1.55 fails. The lines sit either side of 1.5, apart by the original's run-to-run spread; between them a reading is undecided. The spread is wider on E19's L40, so E19's lines sit at 1.24 and 1.81.

What we found. On both small fine-tunes every placement passed at every step, the step split reading 0.98 (SGD) and 1.02 (Adam) at step 1,000 where E19's large path read 2.67 (first figure). With Δ removed, the step split's pieces still carried less of the behaviour than least squares' in five of six readings; the exception is SGD at step 1,000 (second figure). The surprise: we had guessed Δ would hold less of the SGD update than of the Adam one, since an SGD step is a gradient the pieces should take closely (E21). In plain size it held more at every step. Counted by how much the task's inputs use each direction, the two agree (third figure).

What it means. On these two small fine-tunes, one seed each, the step split's adversarial failure does not appear. That fits E19's failure coming from the update's size, an untested explanation: the large fine-tune also had ten times the Adam rate (and differs from the SGD one in optimiser) and ran on another card. Do-nothing passes here too, so on these small updates the check does not separate the placements. The step split is no better than least squares at keeping the behaviour in the pieces, apart from the one SGD reading, which sits 0.07 above least squares against a 0.05 cut on one seed. Two next steps were proposed, each needing /design. One is a second SGD seed for that reversal. The other applies the same split once to saved small finished updates, to see whether the SGD excess in Δ comes from the split's choice of scale rather than from splitting step by step.

Run
The three placements were read at steps 10, 100 and 1,000 along E21's small SGD and Adam emoticon fine-tunes (seed 0), on the adversarial check over 256 Pile rows and on the target probability with Δ removed; the run first reproduced E21's step-10 update sizes and do-nothing ratios. One RunPod 4090 job of 2,491 s, about $0.82.
Found
  • All eighteen small-fine-tune readings pass the adversarial check (0.98 to 1.18), on one seed per fine-tune; E19's step-split fail at step 1,000 (2.67) does not appear on either, though size is confounded with learning rate and card.
  • With Δ removed, the step split's pieces keep less of the behaviour than least squares' on the small Adam fine-tune at every step, and on the small SGD one at steps 10 and 100; at step 1,000 on SGD they keep more, 0.99 against 0.92. The SGD gaps at steps 100 and 1,000 sit just outside the 0.05 band, so they are marginal and from one seed.
  • Surprise: Δ was guessed to hold less of the SGD update than of the Adam one and in plain size held more at every step, by 0.34 to 0.43 of the update, while the input-weighted shares agree within 0.07; the cause is not established.
Bears on
Q1 (is there a simple map that keeps the decomposition faithful with Δ not absorbing the update) and Q11 (does the optimiser matter)
Moved on
E19's adversarial fail and its rising Δ share were scoped to the large update, with size not separated from rate and card; "less behaviour in the pieces than least squares" holds on the small Adam fine-tune and reverses on SGD at step 1,000. The report also finds that moving the pieces' directions changes nothing here (a twin with them held agrees within 0.004) and that refitting the scale each step pushes little extra into Δ on small fine-tunes; the earlier three-panel figures with E19 beside each small path were redrawn as the three above and stay in the report. Not run: a second seed, a longer adversary, another task, the large fine-tune on this card. Report: reports/analyses/2026-10-22_analysis_step-split-small.md (no notebook).
