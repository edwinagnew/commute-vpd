On trained fine-tunes, how much of each changed circuit part does the base model's own part explain?

2026-11-21 · cell P1.50 (open), definition-lead, provisional, no independent check · experiments/rb_states

The question. You chose to count reuse only over the circuit parts a fine-tune changed, each weighted by how much it changed (option b). On the hand-built edits that reading put the two pure strength edits at 0.76 and the new and random edits near 0.01. Does it still put reuse above new on real fine-tunes?

What we did. For each of 94 trained fine-tunes of the 67M model, we took every changed part of the circuit and asked what share of its change the base model's own part explains when only turned up or down. A random update, which changes things at random, reads 0.011; the do-nothing reads 1. The 94 fine-tunes are nine task pairs, copy against lookup at their own sizes, and five key tasks. The side with the larger update in each pair was scaled to the other's size.

What we found. Every trained fine-tune reads between 0.015 and 0.062, close to the random update and far below the built reuse edits at 0.63 to 0.76. In six of the nine pairs the side built to be new reads higher; in the other three the reuse side leads by at most 0.005. The saved reinforcement-learning repeat updates read 0.10 to 0.22, against 0.015 to 0.023 for supervised updates on the same task and 0.03 to 0.05 for a shuffled-reward control.

What it means. This reading does not reproduce on trained fine-tunes the order it gave on the built edits, so it cannot yet say which fine-tunes reuse the base model. It does read reinforcement-learning updates 5 to 10 times higher than supervised ones on the same task, which is the one place it moves. The card is preliminary: the reinforcement-learning figures here come from the decision card, and the E257 report still lists that stage as pending. No reuse or new verdict is given.
