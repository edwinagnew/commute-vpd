Does the re-gating share follow how a fine-tune works, or how likely its answer already was?

2026-11-21 · cell P2.54 (filled), runs 1 and 2, no independent check · experiments/prior_mech_2x2

The caveat first. The low-prior copy is a row selection of the copy task: the same task, trained on the rows where the original model found the answer least likely. So every reading of the update lands near copy, and this 2x2 cannot test the re-gating share against steering or effective rank. It separates readings of the update from the one reading of the rows, the prior.

The question. Part 2 asks whether the re-gating share separates reused from new fine-tunes even when the original model's prior of the answer is held fixed, and whether it still does when the prior moves. An earlier grid (E237) left the prior and the mechanism confounded.

What we did. Four corners, three seeds each, every update scaled to one size. Copy is high prior and reused; a prior-matched cipher is high prior and new; a cipher is low prior and new. Run 2 built the missing corner, a low-prior copy, by training the copy task only on its lowest-prior rows. Run 1 switched off copy head (2,4) to label each corner by the share of its gain that is lost (0.2 or more counts as reused). Each reading gets a mechanism effect (mean of the two reused corners minus the two new) and a prior effect (high-prior minus low-prior corners).

What we found. Every filed rule held: the low-prior copy lost 0.22 of its gain with the head off, just over the 0.2 bar. The re-gating share reads 0.77 on copy and 0.73 on the low-prior copy against 0.37 and 0.27 on the two ciphers, a mechanism effect of 0.43 against a prior effect of 0.07. Pooled steering and effective rank split the same way and as cleanly, and so does the top-64 neuron share (all under 0.03); only the prior follows the prior.

What it means. The share follows mechanism, not prior, on this 2x2, which backs the paper's second claim. But the alternatives follow mechanism too, so "the share follows the mechanism, the alternatives follow the prior" does not hold here. Copy does not run through one head: head (1,1) carries as much as (2,4) on the low-prior copy (0.18 to 0.27 lost), so a label from (2,4) alone undercounts the copy.

Run
Run 1 (E249) switched off heads on saved key-task fine-tunes; run 2 (E250, job 62976) trained the low-prior copy (1214 training and 286 read rows, log10 prior -5.73 against the cipher's -5.86) and read all four corners; MATS cluster L40, about ten minutes, no cost.
Found
  • The low-prior copy runs through the copy head (0.22 lost, bar 0.2) and copy, both ciphers keep their labels; copy itself loses only 0.25, "between", not fully through.
  • Share 0.43 against 0.07, pooled steering 0.56 against -0.06, rank 66 against 4: all follow mechanism; the prior follows prior (1.47 against 0.16).
  • The top-64 neuron share called mechanism where no call was expected, on values near zero; per-key steering also calls mechanism, near 1 everywhere.
Bears on
Part 2, the second claim: the re-gating share tracks how a fine-tune works, not the prior; it does not yet beat steering or rank.
