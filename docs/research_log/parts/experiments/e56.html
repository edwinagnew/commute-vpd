Does patching a few MLP neurons separate the half-known key map from the cipher as the route split does, and does the route split's lead over steering hold at five seeds?

2026-10-30 · MATS cluster L40 debug QoS, $0 (jobs 46170, 46171) · experiments/keydependent_patch

The question. E54 found that the route split's re-gating share separates a key-to-value map the original 67M model half-knows from a cipher on the same keys, more clearly than one steering vector does. One mean vector is the same for every key, so a key-dependent map may beat it by construction; the claim the re-gating line wants is that no cheap activation baseline that depends on the input reads this either. E52's neuron patch was not run on this pair, and E54's margin over steering rested on two seeds. The user approved E56 (2026-10-30).

What we did. Patching neurons means giving the original model the fine-tuned model's activations for the 16 (and up to all 12,288) MLP neurons whose activations changed most, then reading the share of the fine-tune's gain that this recovers (0 for the original model, 1 for the fine-tuned one). We did this on E54's six saved fine-tunes, and then fine-tuned seeds 2 to 4 of both tasks and read the route split's share r (the share of the gain recovered when no subcomponent changes and only Γ's switching does) and steering's share, so each task has five seeds. The half-known map is read at step 10, the cipher at step 9 where its update has the same size, and also at its own step 30. Before any patch was scored, the apparatus checks passed: patching every neuron with the original model's own activations changed nothing, and E54's numbers reproduced exactly.

What we found. Sixteen neurons carry nothing for either task (shares between -0.04 and 0.03). With all neurons, the half-known map recovers 0.94 of its gain and the size-matched cipher 0.69, so at matched size the neuron share separates the pair (figure 1). But the matched cipher has learned much less of its table (held-out probability 0.26 to 0.31 against 0.95 to 0.97); trained to its own step it recovers 0.96 with all neurons and meets the half-known map's curve, while its r stays at 0.24 to 0.27. The route split separates the pair on all five seeds (0.76 to 0.77 against 0.21 to 0.27, a gap of 0.52 against a seed range of 0.055); one steering vector does not (gap 0.034 under its seed range 0.065), and the lead is 5.2 times on the filed cautious form (figure 2). The cipher's r moves by at most 0.02 between its two steps.

What it means. Neuron patching is not a reader of kind: it separates the two tasks only while the cipher has learned much less of its table, and agrees with the half-known map once progress is matched, whereas r separates at both steps. The filed prediction that neuron patching does not separate the pair passed only under a rule merged before the run (neurons for half the gain counted in one bin above 1,000); on the rule as first filed it failed, since the half-known map needs 4,096 neurons and the cipher 12,288 on every seed. The progress reading rests on one cipher trajectory at two steps, and the claim still rests on one pair, one 67M model and a label taken from the original model's behaviour. The claim "no cheap activation baseline reads the kind" stands with one phrase narrowed. Next: ask the user for Jain et al.'s readout on the elicit keys.
