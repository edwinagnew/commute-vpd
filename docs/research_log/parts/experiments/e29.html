Are the subcomponents a fine-tune turns down the original model's machinery for the tokens competing with the target?

2026-10-22 · RunPod RTX 4090 · experiments/regating_down

The question. After a narrow fine-tune, Γ, the decomposition's importance function (it rates, input by input, how much each subcomponent is needed), kept frozen, reads the fine-tuned model and changes how important it finds each subcomponent at the trigger, the input position where the fine-tune now predicts its target. That change is re-gating, the subject of Q5. Earlier runs (E18) found that one fixed change, the same for every input, carries most of it: some subcomponents are switched on, and others are turned down. The switched-on half was already known to be the original model's machinery for the target. This card asks what the turned-down half is: the machinery for the tokens the target must beat, so that the fine-tune works by switching off the competitors?

What we did. We read ten saved fine-tunes at the original weights: seven tasks on the 67M model, three of them with two seeds. A task teaches a token to follow a trigger: emoticon, or a word pair such as ` will` then ` be`. The pool is the subcomponents on at the trigger in the original model, and the turned-down set is the pool members the fixed change lowers by 0.1 or more. The competitors are the five tokens the original model finds most likely at the trigger, the target excluded. A token set's machinery is the subcomponents most important in Pile text just before one of its tokens, keeping as many as the set it is compared with. We asked two things, each against random sets drawn from the pool: does the turned-down set overlap the competitors' machinery, and does lowering it take probability from the competitors?

What we found. The turned-down set overlaps the competitors' machinery no more than a random pool set of its size, on all seven tasks (first figure); four of them are scored, one seed each. Yet lowering it takes most of the competitors' probability on emoticon and on ` will` then ` be`, more than the same amounts placed elsewhere in the pool, and adds two thirds on ` which` then ` was` (second figure). The two tests ask different things: the first whether the set is the subcomponents that precede a competitor in ordinary Pile text, the second what lowering the set does at the trigger. A set can cut the competitors at the trigger without being that Pile-defined machinery; the report notes the definition may miss structure, but why the two come apart on emoticon and ` will` then ` be` was not established. Where the lost probability goes differs by task (third figure).

What it means. On this run's definitions, "switch off the competitors" is not a general description of the turned-down half, so the fixed-shift hypothesis, re-gating's working hypothesis, gains no mechanism sentence. The result rests on those two definitions: machinery as the Pile next-token contrast, which E6 found weak, and competitors as the original model's own top tokens rather than the tokens the fine-tune actually pushed down. The plan's tree read this as "mixed: report, no action", and the line moved on.

Run
One RunPod job read E18's saved fixed changes (emoticon, ` which` then ` was`, ` we` then ` just`, two seeds each) and E28's four word tasks (` it` then ` is`, ` will` then ` be`, ` will` then ` have`, ` in` then ` Fig`, one seed each), with a control run first that reproduced E18's numbers exactly. It took 99 s, about $0.04.
Found
  • Not expected: the turned-down set overlaps the competitors' machinery no more than chance on all seven tasks (four scored, one seed each), while the same test finds the target's machinery in the switched-on set on six of the seven.
  • Partly expected: lowering it removes the competitors' probability beyond chance by more than 0.2 on four tasks (two of them scored, one seed each), falls short on two, and on ` which` then ` was` adds 67% on both seeds, for reasons the run does not establish.
  • Only E28's four tasks count toward the verdict: E18's six states (emoticon, ` which` then ` was`, ` we` then ` just`) have saved fixed changes that differ from the importance recomputed in the job by up to 0.0008, against a tolerance of 0.0001, though their controls reproduce E18, so they are shown hatched but unscored (report).
