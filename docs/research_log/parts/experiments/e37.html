Can training the decomposition's pieces to imitate a fine-tune that changes text everywhere keep it faithful?

2026-10-26 to 2026-10-27 · RunPod H100 · experiments/teacher_spread

The question. Q1 asks for a simple way to carry a fine-tune into the decomposition so that its pieces explain the new behaviour and it stays faithful. Teacher training trains the pieces themselves to imitate the fine-tuned model; in E34 it worked on fine-tunes that act at one trigger token. Post-training changes text everywhere, so we tried a fine-tune that does too: E36's, which teaches the model to write in lowercase.

What we did. The fine-tune changes all 24 decomposed matrices of the 67M model. Teacher training trained the pieces' read and write vectors to match its predictions, with the importance function Γ and the residual Δ frozen. It ran on the lowercase texts at two batch orders, and on the same texts with capitals kept. Each result is scored on the share of the fine-tune's held-out gain it keeps, and on the adversarial ratio. An adversary searches over masks of the pieces for the largest damage to predictions on Pile text; the ratio is that damage over what it finds at the unchanged decomposition, about 0.7 nats per token. A high ratio means the pieces no longer stand in faithfully for the model. It passes at or below 1.40 and fails at or above 1.61: 1.5 divided and multiplied by one plus the unchanged decomposition's spread across adversary seeds. Two references: the adjoint split, a closed-form division of the finished weight change among the pieces, and do-nothing, which leaves the whole change in Δ.

What we found. Teacher training keeps all of the lowercasing but fails the check, at ratios of 1.74 to 2.20 across its three runs, while the adjoint split keeps 0.80 of it and passes at 1.05 (first figure). Two batch orders land on nearly the same weight change (second figure). Training four times longer leaves every read above the fail line, between 1.89 and 2.23, while the match to the fine-tuned model keeps improving; started from the adjoint split, training crosses the fail line within 1,000 steps (third figure). VPD's own masked losses could not be tried as a fix, since with nothing to copy they alone pushed the ratio to 3.84 (fourth figure).

What it means. On a broad update, teacher training gives a decomposition that copies the behaviour but fails the check at every read, at 1.7 to 2.2 times the original's damage, so it is not yet a faithful answer to Q1 for post-training. The surprise is that starting from the adjoint split, which passes, does not avoid it. Do-nothing, not plotted, reads 4.87, high by construction: the adversary may also mask Δ, and switching Δ off undoes the whole fine-tune; nothing here is judged against it. Why the trained pieces are fragile is untested, and cheap tests of the cause ran next as E39.

Run
Teacher training as in E34 (Adam, 1,000 steps) at two batch orders and on capitals-kept text, beside the adjoint split and do-nothing, then a run to 4,000 steps, a run from the adjoint split, and the masked-loss control, each after its controls read as expected. Scored on held-out rows and a 20-step mask adversary on 256 Pile rows; $1.40 and then $2.27 on an H100.
Found
  • Teacher training copies the lowercase fine-tune, as expected: 0.998 of its gain, a change on plain Pile text matching the fine-tune's by the report's reading (0.234 against 0.235 nats, not plotted), and nearly one weight change across batch orders (cosine 0.93).
  • Against expectation it fails the adversarial check, at 1.74 to 2.20, and stays above the fail line at 4,000 steps and when started from the adjoint split, which itself keeps 0.80 of the gain and passes.
  • VPD's masked losses could not be tried, because with nothing to copy they alone raised the ratio to 3.84; whether the check tracks how much a state changes ordinary text is untested, since that control's retry at a tenth of the weight failed with a thirtieth of teacher training's change.
