Does the re-gating share on Qwen3-1.7B come off its ceiling when it is measured in log-odds?

2026-11-10 · provisional, no independent check · cluster debug L40, job 57859, about 25 minutes, $0 · experiments/qwen_regating_new

The answer. No: the share could not be measured at all. Every update, and every random change, made the model less sure of the answer it was trained toward, so there was no gain to split. This run is a post-hoc deviation from E132's filed method, chosen after seeing E132's result. The user has since decided to stop Qwen re-gating here.

Why we ran it. A decomposition rewrites the model's weights as many small subcomponents, each switched on or off per input. The re-gating share is the part of an update's effect that needs no new subcomponents. The original ones are kept fixed, and only which of them are switched on changes. E132 measured this share through the KL divergence and read about 0.99 for every update. Random changes of matched size, not trained to teach anything, read the same, so the share could not tell them apart. The user then asked to read the share in a different unit. That unit is the log-odds of the trained answer: how strongly the model prefers that token over all others.

What we did. We took four saved updates of Qwen3-1.7B on the new decomposition. Each was read on its own rows, the text it was trained on. For two updates these are maths problems with reference answers; for two seeds of a third update they are short facts. Two random changes were read on the same rows. The controls ran first and passed: the unchanged model read zero change, and an edit that leaves every switch alone read a share of zero.

What we found. Every update lowered its trained answer's log-odds on its own rows, by 1.1 to 8.0 per token. A rise of about 2 was expected. In all, 253 of 256 rows fell. The random changes lowered it too, by 5.0 to 7.1. With no gain to divide by, the share reads unreadable. Only the fact-trained updates stood apart from the random changes, losing much less.

What it may mean. A likely explanation, which this run did not test, is that the unchanged model is already near-certain of these tokens. Its mean log-odds is 9.3 on the maths rows and about 12.5 on the fact rows. A near-certain token can lose a lot of log-odds at almost no cost to the model's loss. The advisory premise check judged the question doubtful from the start. The user chose not to re-read. Whether part 3 keeps E132's KL share or drops re-gating from Qwen is decided at the gate on 18 November.
