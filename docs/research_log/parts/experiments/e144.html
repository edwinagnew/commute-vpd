E132 Amendment 1: does the log-odds re-gating share vary on Qwen3-1.7B, where the KL share sat at its ceiling?

2026-11-10 · provisional, no independent check · cluster debug L40, job 57859, about 25 minutes, $0 · experiments/qwen_regating_new

The question. This run is a post-hoc deviation from E132's filed re-gating method, and it is also called E132 Amendment 1. E132 filed a share built on the KL divergence, and every state read near its ceiling. After seeing that, the user chose to read the share in log-odds instead, the unit that GOALS.md defines as r. The question is whether the share can vary at all on the new decomposition once it is measured that way.

What we did. The log-odds re-gating share is the part of an update's gain in its trained answer's log-odds that comes from changing only which original subcomponents are switched on. We read it for the four saved Qwen3-1.7B updates on the new decomposition. The target is the trained answer on each update's own rows. Those are gold rows for the step-10 fine-tune and the gold update, and fact rows for each seed of the second anchor. The two random changes, one Gaussian and one confined to the live columns, were read on the same rows. The controls ran first and all passed. The original model read as an update summed to zero, and two KL readings that need no decomposition matched their earlier values exactly. Doubling the final norm's weight left the switches bitwise equal and read share 0.

What we found. Every update lowers its trained answer's log-odds on its own rows, by 1.1 to 8.0 per token on average. In all, 253 of the 256 rows fall. The filed expectation was a rise of 1.8 to 2.9. The random changes lower it too, by 5.0 to 7.1, so no share could be scored and the filed rule reads unreadable. The second-anchor updates lose only 1.1 and 1.4 on their fact rows. The random changes lose about 6.4 to 6.8 there. On the gold rows the updates do not stand apart from them.

What it means. The filed expectation reversed in sign, and this reading is provisional, with labels as built and no verdict. The likely cause, untested, is that the original model already gives these tokens a mean log-odds of 9.3 on the gold rows and about 12.5 on the fact rows. Near-certain tokens can lose a lot of log-odds at almost no cost in loss. The final-norm control only sharpens the output, and it gained about 9.85 per token. No log-probability read was saved to test this. The advisory premise check judged it doubtful, since nothing in the run shows the updates raised these targets. The cell's one run is spent, and any follow-up needs a new cell from the user.
