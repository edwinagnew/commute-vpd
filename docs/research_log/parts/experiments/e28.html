Does the fixed-shift hypothesis hold on tasks it was never fitted to?

2026-10-22 · RunPod RTX 4090 · experiments/regating_tasks

On tasks the model already does, yes; on tasks it lacks, no. On four new word tasks one fixed change of Γ's importance values at the trigger gives back three quarters or more of what each input's own values give (first figure), stops working when moved to the wrong subcomponents (second figure), and another input's values do as well as the input's own (third figure). On the three key-value tasks the fixed change gives back under half. Surprise: we leaned "survives" on the copy task, and it failed two of the three tests.

Run
Ten-step Adam 3e-5 fine-tunes, seed 0, on four more word tasks from the ladder and three key-value tasks the model lacks: the copy task (32 keys, each word followed by itself), a 32-pair cipher and a one-pair cipher. Each in the do-nothing state, read at the trigger on about 100 held-out windows. Every arm is a share of what each input's own updated values give back of the switching effect. The three kill tests are the decision report's: the fixed change (the updated model's mean values net of the original model's) under 0.5; the change shuffled among each matrix's alive subcomponents (20 draws) over 0.2; another input's updated values (5 derangements) under 0.5, scored where that input's original values give back 0.2 or less. Emoticon ran first as the control and reproduced E18 and its amendments within 0.002. Plan reports/2026-10-22_plan_regating-tasks.md, predictions filed before the run. 142 s, about $0.16.
Found
  • All four word tasks survive all three tests: fixed 0.74 to 0.99, shuffled 0.004 or less, another input's values 0.96 to 1.00. With E18's three, the fixed-shift hypothesis holds on seven tasks the model already does.
  • All three key-value tasks are killed on the fixed change: 0.07 on the copy task, 0.15 on the 32-pair cipher, 0.45 on the one-pair cipher.
  • Surprise, copy task: switching carries 0.80 of its gain, as on the word tasks, so we leaned "survives" at low confidence. The fixed change gave back 0.07 and another input's values 0.26 (reference 0.17). The key's identity is the natural reading of what is input-specific there, but no arm measured it.
  • On the two ciphers the kill lines up with E12: switching carries only 0.27 and 0.12 of their gain.
  • On the key-value tasks the original model's mean values already give back 0.26 to 0.45 of the own values' recovery, against 0.09 to 0.28 on the word tasks. Not predicted.
  • E18's filed pattern, the change added to each input's own original values, again read far below the mean pattern on two word tasks (0.40, 0.45 against 0.74, 0.88).
  • Controls separate patterns on all eight states: a different target's change gives back −0.24 to +0.06, a random set about 0.
