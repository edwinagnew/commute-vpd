When an updated model fails the adversarial check, is the extra damage on the original subcomponents?

2026-10-21 to 2026-10-22 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/cancellation_complement

The question. The commute line asks whether a small update, written onto the decomposition, keeps it faithful (Q6). VPD's adversarial check searches for the mask over the subcomponents that does the most damage. The adversarial ratio is that damage at the updated model over the same at the original model; above 1.5 counts as a fail, the line set in E2. E17 inferred that the failing models' extra damage came from the mask on the original subcomponents, which would mean Γ's importance around them had gone stale. This run measured that directly.

What we did. Each weight matrix is a sum of rank-one subcomponents plus Δ, the part they leave over. About 9,900 subcomponents are alive: Γ rates them important somewhere on the original model's text. The rest are dead; the same split is used at every updated model. We took E17's eight updated models. Six come from component training, 10 Adam steps on the subcomponents' own vectors, each on a task that raises one word after a trigger, such as " is" after " it". Two write the emoticon fine-tune onto the decomposition, by its least-squares split and by its adjoint split. Four pass the check and four fail. Each model gets its own adversary, searched at that model. Forward hooks then removed what that one mask removes, one block at a time: the alive original subcomponents, their changes, the dead subcomponents, and Δ. Damage is the KL from the model to the masked copy on 128 rows of Pile text.

What we found. On three of the four failing models, removing the adversary's share of the original subcomponents costs no more than at the original model (first figure). On " in" then " Fig" it carries about a fifth of the extra damage. Nearly all of it appears only when the dead block is removed together with the alive one. At the original model those two removals offset each other; on the failing models that offset is mostly or wholly lost (second figure). Under the VPD paper's step rule a longer search barely adds damage, and every ratio reads the same from 20 to 160 steps (third and fourth figures).

What it means. E17's inference is refuted. That rests on four failing models, one adversary seed each and one kind of search, and on one of them, " in" then " Fig", about a fifth of the extra damage is on the originals. With it goes the case that stale importance around the original subcomponents calls for a Γ that sees the updated model. The failure is a joint effect of the mask, not a property of one block, and its cause is not explained. The caveat is the search: the verdicts do not depend on the budget within the paper's recipe, but no other kind of search was tried, and each updated model ran one seed. Later runs showed three of the four failing states pass at a smaller or later update (E21).

Run
E17's eight saved states and the original model, each under its own 20-step adversary on the first 128 Pile validation rows, with the hooks checked against the adversary's masked model to 4e-9 nats; three follow-ups split the rest by block, tried the original model's mask at the updated models, and ran longer searches. Cluster jobs 28206, 28251, 28306, 28307, 29200 and 29218 to 29220, all $0.
Found
  • Against the prediction, the original subcomponents carry almost none of the extra damage on three of four failing models, and shuffling which of them the adversary removes changes the damage no more than at the original model (shown in the report).
  • As predicted, the extra damage is the lost offset between removing the alive and the dead subcomponents; applied unchanged at the updated models, the original model's mask costs about what it costs there (shown in the report), so each failing model's search found a different, costlier mask, for reasons not explained.
  • Under the paper's step rule every ratio at 160 steps is within about a tenth of its 20-step value, so every verdict stands against search length; a second seed at the original model moves the ratios by about a sixth, leaving " it" then " is" just above the line.
