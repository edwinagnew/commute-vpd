Is the adversary's extra damage in the original subcomponents?

2026-10-21 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/cancellation_complement

No. E17 inferred that the adversary's excess on updated models came from its mask on the original subcomponents. Measured directly, removing the adversary's share of them costs no more than at the original model on three of four failing states, and a fifth of the excess on the fourth. Most of the excess sits in the rest of its mask (first figure). An amendment split that rest: neither Δ nor the dead subcomponents costs more alone, and Δ alone costs almost nothing. The excess appears only when the dead block is removed together with the alive one, whose removals offset each other at the original model and much less after the update (fourth figure). A second amendment, run with a fixed step of 0.1, saw the adversary's damage climb steeply with steps (fifth figure). The third reran it with the VPD paper's step rule, steps times step size kept at 2: the damage barely grows, and every ratio read at 20 steps reads within a tenth of it at 160, so every verdict stands.

Run
E17's eight saved states and the original model, each under its own 20-step adversary on the first 128 Pile validation rows. Forward hooks remove the adversary's fraction of four blocks per matrix: the alive original subcomponents, their changes, the dead subcomponents and Δ. The excess splits exactly into the originals' part, the changes' part, their interaction, and the rest, the mask on the dead subcomponents and Δ with its interplay. Also the originals' revert with the adversary's levels shuffled, and per importance quarter and layer. The gate reproduced the adversary's masked model to 4e-9 nats; the control reproduced E17 exactly. Job 28206, 476 s, $0.

E17's inference is refuted, and with it the stale-importance-around-the-originals argument for a Γ that sees the updated model, though not the refit itself. Amendment 1 (same report, dated section) split the rest: the excess is the interaction between removing the alive and the dead subcomponents, Δ alone negligible. Its cause is not verified. Candidate readings with the run that separates each: where a short search lands (a stronger adversary at the original model); the same mask costing more (the original model's saved levels applied at the updated model, one debug pass); the alive and dead subcomponents' outputs no longer overlapping. Amendment 2 ruled out the same mask costing more. Its reading that the 20-step search is far from converged is withdrawn by Amendment 3: under the paper's step rule every verdict tried reads the same from 20 to 160 steps, so the claims Amendment 2 flagged stand as first read. Amendment 3 shows the verdicts do not depend on the budget within the paper's recipe, not that its masks are the worst possible; another kind of search (per-row masks, restarts) was not run. Not run: budgets past 160, a second seed on the updated models. Current verdict: three of the four failing states pass at a smaller or later update (E21). The account also covers five failing states made by plain SGD: SGD at step 1,000 on r0 and r1, high-rate SGD on r2, and both 32-pair key-to-value tasks, 5 of 5 with Δ alone at most 0.003 nats (E23).
