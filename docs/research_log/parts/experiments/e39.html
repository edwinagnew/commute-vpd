Why does teacher training on a broad fine-tune fail the adversarial check?

2026-10-27 · RunPod H100 · experiments/broad_fragility

Not because any move of the subcomponents that size breaks the check: random moves of matched weight size pass. The failure sits in the MLP changes acting together, and a rank-8 LoRA fine-tune of the same task gives a trained decomposition that passes. Neither reading is settled, because the random moves changed the model's outputs far less, and the LoRA also changed ordinary text half as much.

Run
One H100 job on E37's trained decomposition of the lowercase fine-tune (stage 0 controls first, all passed): a random move of matched size per matrix, reverts by layer, by attention or MLP, and by Γ's importance halves, and teacher training on a plain-SGD and on a rank-8 LoRA fine-tune of the same task. Three filed claims were tested: generic brittleness, the rarely important subcomponents, and update shape.
Found
  • A random move of the same weight size passes (1.03 and 1.07, against 1.00 to 1.075 for the unchanged model), against the filed prediction that it would fail; it does about a twenty-fifth of the trained decomposition's damage on plain text, so this rules out size in the weights, not size in the outputs.
  • Reverting all MLP matrices removes the whole excess (1.00), reverting attention raises it (2.70), and the rarely important half removes less than a random half (0.63 against 0.97), the opposite of what was filed; plain-text damage fits this as well as the MLP reading does.
  • A rank-8 LoRA's trained decomposition passes at 1.27 where the Adam one reads 2.20, against the filed prediction; its ordinary-text damage and its trained move are also about half as large, so rank is not separated from size of change. SGD gave no answer: every rate in its grid was too large.
