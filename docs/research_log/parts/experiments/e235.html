Can the authors' crosscoder, run properly, see a planted change and tell a fine-tune from noise?

2026-11-21 · cell P2.49 runs 1 and 2 of 2 (run 2 is E243), no independent check · experiments/crosscoder_proper

The question. Part 2 sets baselines beside the re-gating share, and the earlier crosscoder attempt on the 67M model failed its control with several shortfalls: a half token budget, full-precision maths, a planted direction instead of a head. This run removes them and asks whether the method can then separate the built pairs at all.

What we did. We trained Minder et al.'s crosscoder with their pinned trainer, settings and 100M tokens, between the original model and one changed model at two places in the network, three seeds each. A crosscoder is one dictionary of sparse latents that rebuilds both models' activations at once; a latent is fine-tune-specific when it points at the changed model and the original model's activations do not already hold it. The "specific part" is the share of the activation change those latents carry. Controls ran first: the model against itself, an extra attention head grafted on (bar 0.5 on every seed), and a Gaussian update matched to the elicit fine-tune's activation change. Then the elicit fine-tune (built to reuse) against the cipher fine-tune (built to be new) at three seeds.

What we found. The model against itself passed at both places. At block 2 the grafted head missed: specific part 0.30, 0.21 and 0.37 against the 0.5 bar, so that site stopped. At block 3 the head passed (0.56, 0.63, 0.52) but it raises the loss on ordinary text from 2.71 to 11.30 nats and changes activations 14 times as much as the elicit fine-tune does. There the random update read 0.0035 to 0.0070, elicit 0.0056 to 0.0092 and cipher 0.0014 to 0.0106, so all three overlap and each is under one hundredth of the change.

What it means. The crosscoder is not applicable at this update size on the 67M, at either place, so it cannot be set beside the share on the built pairs. Removing the earlier attempt's shortfalls did not change the outcome, so they were not the cause. This is a limit of reach, not a finding that the method is wrong. Run 2 (E243) scaled the block-2 head over five sizes, from 1.04 to 103.7 times a fine-tune's amplitude: its fine-tune-specific share read 0.0030 near fine-tune size and levelled off at 0.45 to 0.46 at the top, under the 0.5 bar on every rung, where the head has already wrecked the loss (9.86 and 13.32 nats against 2.71). So block 2 is not applicable up to about 104 times a fine-tune's amplitude, and the five tasks and random changes were not run, by the filed stop rule.

Run
Fifteen to twenty-three crosscoders on RunPod H100 pods in two stages, about 30 to 38 minutes each, $99.85 in all (stage 0 $38.49, stage 1 $61.35).
Found
  • At block 2 the grafted head's specific part sat under 0.4 on every seed, against the bar of 0.5; changing the number of latents kept per token did not fix it (0.39 at 32, 0.19 at 200).
  • At block 3 the head passed only at a size that wrecks the model; a random update read like both fine-tunes. At block 2 the share never reached 0.5 over a 104-fold range of amplitude (E243).
  • Counts of fine-tune-specific latents were 7 to 50 on every trained side and the random update, so a nonzero count does not mark a learned change.
