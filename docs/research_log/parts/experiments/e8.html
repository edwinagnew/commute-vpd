Is a LoRA on all 24 matrices, split onto the subcomponents, as fragile as the fine-tune's split?

2026-10-19 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/lora_matched

The question. The commute line asks whether a finished update can be carried onto the decomposition's subcomponents without breaking it (Q1), and whether a LoRA is easier to carry than a full fine-tune (Q3). E7 put its LoRAs on one matrix only, unlike the fine-tune, so it could not tell the LoRA from the matrix. Here a rank-8 LoRA sits on all 24, so both updates change the same matrices and go through the same split. We expected the split LoRA to be at least as fragile. Its read side is the set of input directions the change responds to; for a LoRA that is its first factor, which starts random and barely moves in training, so the subcomponents, fitted to real inputs, should read it poorly.

What we did. On the emoticon task, where the 67M model learns to answer a trigger with the letter o, we trained the LoRA and a full fine-tune, both with Adam. Each was scored at the first step where it gave o with probability at least 0.9: step 2 for the LoRA, step 10 for the fine-tune. Each update was placed two ways. Do-nothing leaves the whole change in Δ, the residual part of each matrix the subcomponents don't cover. The least-squares split writes the change as the smallest change of the subcomponents that reproduces it and leaves only what they cannot express in Δ. Every state got the adversarial ratio: an adversary searches over masks of the subcomponents for the most damage on Pile text, divided by its damage to the unchanged decomposition. A ratio of 1 means no worse than before. The unchanged decomposition's own result varied by 21% across adversary seeds, so a state passes below 1.63, one plus three times that. We also measured how much of the target's gain at the trigger the split leaves in Δ, unexplained by the subcomponents.

What we found. The prediction reversed in direction. At step 2 the split LoRA reads 1.13 on the adversarial ratio, as faithful as doing nothing, while the fine-tune's split at step 10 reads 5.41 in the same job (first figure). The LoRA's read side was random as expected, and its split left about a fifth of the gain in Δ against a tenth for the fine-tune (second figure). The fine-tune's numbers also match E2's on the same kind of GPU to three digits; the report found the GPU the only difference from E7's higher 7.31 on an RTX 4090 and attributes that reading to it, on this one rerun.

What it means. The split LoRA was not more fragile than the split fine-tune here. The report reads this as consistent with E7's difficulty coming from its one-matrix placement rather than from LoRAs as such, on this one run; E7's own numbers are on its card, not shown here. The caveat is the step. The LoRA left whole in Δ also passes, though the report measures its change as larger than the fine-tune's. And E2 had found the fine-tune's own split passing at step 1. So this run compared two updates at different points of training. E14 then measured both at the same steps on one GPU and one seed: the split LoRA stayed near 1, while the split fine-tune sat well above the pass line at steps 10 and 1,000 and just above it at step 100 (figure 3). The LoRA's split pays for its faithfulness by leaving more of the update in Δ; E16's card reports the same trade.

Run
A rank-8 LoRA on all 24 matrices (two seeds) beside a full fine-tune on emoticon, each left in Δ and split by least squares through one shared script; the fine-tune's split was rerun on the L40 to check E7's GPU. One MATS cluster L40 job, 18 minutes, $0.
Found
  • Unexpectedly, the split LoRA passes the adversarial check at step 2, on one seed, where the fine-tune's split fails fivefold at step 10. The second LoRA seed had no adversarial check.
  • As expected, the LoRA's read side stayed at its random start, yet its split left only about a fifth of the gain in Δ on both seeds (figure 2). Where on the matrices the change lands explains that on one seed but not the other (report).
  • On one rerun, the L40 reproduces E2's fine-tune readings to three digits; the report attributes E7's higher reading to the RTX 4090's adversary, the only difference it found.
