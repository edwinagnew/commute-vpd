Can the subcomponents copy a finished fine-tune from the fine-tuned model alone?

2026-10-23 · RunPod H100 · experiments/teacher_training

The question. The commute line wants a map that writes a finished fine-tune in the decomposition's pieces, without parking the update in the residual Δ (Q1). The earlier maps split the fine-tune's weight change directly across the pieces. They either lost the fine-tune's behaviour or failed VPD's faithfulness check (E16).

What we did. Teacher training trains the pieces instead, with Γ and Δ frozen, so that the decomposition's predictions match the fine-tuned model's. It needs no labels, only the fine-tuned model, and runs on the task's text or on ordinary Pile text. We copied six fine-tunes this way: SGD and Adam, each on three trigger tasks. The check is the adversarial ratio: the damage an adversary (a search over masks of the pieces) can do at the new state, over what it does at the unchanged decomposition. 1 means no change; 1.40 or less passes and 1.61 or more fails.

What we found. Every copy keeps the behaviour, at 0.90 or more target probability, where the adjoint split keeps 0.05 to 0.26 on two tasks (first figure). Every copy also passes the check (second figure). This held at a second batch order and under an adversary eight times longer. A surprise: copies trained on Pile text alone learnt the emoticon behaviour even with every trigger removed from the text.

What it means. This made teacher training the commute line's map. Two limits come with it. First, the copy reaches the behaviour by a different weight change from the fine-tune's (third figure), so it shows the update can be written in the pieces, not that the fine-tune itself used them. Second, the check's verdict can depend on the adversary's random start (last figure), so every pass is a lower bound.

Run
The six teachers are E31's fine-tunes at step 1,000. Each was copied for 1,000 steps on the task's text or on Pile text, with Adam as the copy's optimiser; the amendments added trigger-free Pile text, LoRA teachers, a second batch order, a longer adversary, plain SGD as the copy's optimiser and a rare target. H100, about $31 over six runs.
Found
  • Copies keep the behaviour and pass on all six fine-tunes. A rank-8 LoRA is copied as well from task text; from Pile text alone it gets to about the 0.9 bar only after 5,000 steps.
  • Surprise: the emoticon behaviour is learnt from Pile text with no trigger rows (0.94, 0.89). Two copies at different batch orders reach the same behaviour by weight changes far apart (cosine 0.43).
  • Limits: plain SGD as the copy's optimiser failed at one rate run to step 1,000 (about 3), and passes at the smaller rates (E47). A rare target (` in` then ` Fig`) is not yet copied, a narrow miss.
