Can the subcomponents copy a finished fine-tune from the fine-tuned model alone?

2026-10-23 · RunPod H100 · experiments/teacher_training

Yes, on three tasks. Teacher training, component training whose loss is the fine-tuned model's predictions instead of the task's labels, kept all six fine-tunes' behaviour and passed the adversarial check, including where the adjoint split loses the behaviour (first figure). Surprise: copies trained on ordinary Pile text alone learnt the emoticon behaviour, which we filed they would not; they still learn it with every trigger removed from the text, by a weight change that is not the fine-tune's (third figure).

Run
Six teachers, E31's fine-tunes at step 1000: plain SGD and Adam 3e-5 on r1, r0 and emoticon. The alive subcomponents' read and write vectors trained for 1000 steps to minimise the KL from teacher to copy, Γ and Δ frozen, in three arms: the task's windows with Adam, Pile rows only with Adam, the task's windows with plain SGD. Scored at steps 100 and 1000 beside each teacher's do-nothing and adjoint split. Controls first: the unchanged model, a teacher equal to it (nothing moved), and a reachable teacher (gap closed to 0.5%). 67 min, $6.13. Amendment 1: the Pile arm on emoticon again with the trigger masked from the loss or its rows replaced, plus the cosine with δW; 20 min, $3.26. Amendment 4: all twelve copies of the six teachers again with the training batches in a second order, then every copy and every teacher's do-nothing state under E20's budget adversary at 20 and 160 sign steps on the first 128 Pile rows; about $9 over three H100 pods, two of them lost to session disconnects.
Found
  • Task windows: all six copies pass at both steps, target probability 0.92 to 1.00.
  • Pile only: all six pass at step 1000, 0.90 to 0.98. Filed: emoticon below 0.5; read 0.96 and 0.90, reversed.
  • Plain SGD keeps the behaviour but fails late on r0 and r1 (ratio 3.4 and 3.1), as in component training.
  • Amendment 1: about 90 trigger positions in the Pile rows are not the cause; the trigger-free copies reach 0.94 and 0.89. Their weight change's cosine with δW, 0.08 and 0.22, is below the adjoint split's.
  • Amendment 1 against its filed predictions: learnt for the SGD teacher, just under the 0.9 line for the Adam one; masking and dropping disagree for the Adam teacher; the cosine reached the filed bar for the Adam teacher only.
  • Amendment 2: dropping also the 104 rows where the trigger subcomponent is partly on (importance above 0.1) lowers both Pile-text copies, SGD 0.94 to 0.87 and Adam 0.89 to 0.84, with the cosine with δW unchanged. The filed lean "not carried" missed; both read "between". Against Amendment 4's batch-order spread of 0.004 for the SGD teacher's copy and 0.032 for the Adam teacher's, both falls are above the floor: the SGD fall is twice the larger spread, the Adam fall 0.017 above it from one seed pair, so the Adam one is the less firm.
  • Amendment 3, a rank-8 LoRA teacher: the task-text copy keeps 0.93 to 1.00 on all three tasks and every copy is faithful, where the LoRA's adjoint split keeps 0.04 to 0.23. The Pile-text copy reaches only 0.57, 0.88 and 0.52; the filed lean "learnt" missed on every task.
  • Amendment 4, a second batch order and a longer adversary: all twelve copies pass again, target probability 0.93 to 1.00, moved by at most 0.005 on task text and 0.032 on Pile text (the emoticon Adam fine-tune's Pile-text copy); their 20-step harness ratios moved by up to 25%. Under 160 sign steps every copy reads 0.95 to 1.15 times the unchanged model, inside its own second-seed reading of 1.23, while five of the six teachers read 2.4 to 10.4; the two r0 teachers sit above the 1.241 line though the 20-step harness had passed them. All four filed predictions held.
