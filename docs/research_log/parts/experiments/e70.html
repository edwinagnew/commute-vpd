On a counting pair whose answer is known, does removing the old count hurt the task that reuses it more than the task that needs a new one?

2026-11-03 · MATS cluster L40, debug QoS, $0 (jobs 50203 to 50363, 50499, 50643) · experiments/pcfg_anchors · provisional, no independent check · ended at its two amendments

The question. The benchmark line defines "reuses an existing capability" as: a linear readout trained on the pretrained model recovers it, and taking the readout's direction away breaks the fine-tuned behaviour. The removal clause had never been read on a pair whose answer is known by construction. Three removals were tried in turn, because each left a loophole.

What we did. A small transformer is pretrained to count the letter a. Two fine-tunes of it: task P answers the count of a plus one (it reuses the count), task N answers the count of b (it needs a new one). We removed one direction from the residual at the answer position, block by block: the regression probe's, then the direction along which the count moves the residual most, then the largest direction with no covariance with the count. The share of gain lost is the state's extra drop in the log-odds of its answer, beyond the pretrained model's own drop, over the log-odds it gained in fine-tuning. The last removal is the control for size: the covariance direction holds 0.30 to 0.78 of the variance, and this one holds 0.09 to 0.27.

What we found. The last filed branch is "count-specific at this size", not size-matched, and Amendment 1's "one direction cannot serve as ground truth" stands (the new task loses more at block 6). At blocks 3 to 5 the covariance direction costs the reusing task 0.43, 0.78 and 0.88 of its gain and the new task 0.16, 0.34 and 0.49, and costs the pretrained model up to 12 log-odds. The control direction costs the pretrained model nothing above the random band there and the reusing task about zero (first figure); the new task's loss stays unexplained.

What it means. For the benchmark line this supports a causal role for the count's direction at blocks 3 to 5, on one pair and a size that cannot be matched, since a direction unrelated to the count cannot be as large. It is provisional with no independent check. Block 1 is not read as the count, block 6 carries the classes, and the -0.50 correlation of the two counts stays untested. Asks 15 and 17, whether to run Amendments 1 and 2, were taken by default after two hours; the user did not choose them. E70 ends here, and no further amendment is proposed.

Run
First reading: retrained the pretrained model and 20 fine-tuned states, six cluster jobs, about two hours. Amendment 1: job 50499, 15 minutes. Amendment 2: job 50643 (exit 0), 15 minutes. All on an L40, $0. No independent check.
Found
  • "One direction cannot serve as ground truth" stands: the new task loses more than the reusing task at block 6, 0.17 against 0.07, though the reusing task loses far more at blocks 3 to 5.
  • The filed last branch is "count-specific at this size": the largest direction with no covariance with the count costs nothing at blocks 1, 3, 4, 5, but it is smaller, so size is not matched.
  • The new task is not spared at blocks 3 to 5 and its loss is unexplained; the first regression direction did nothing because a refit still reads the count at 1.0.
