On a counting pair whose answer is known, does removing the old count hurt the task that reuses it more than the task that needs a new one?

2026-11-03 · MATS cluster L40, debug QoS, $0 (jobs 50203 to 50363) · experiments/pcfg_anchors · provisional, no independent check

The question. The benchmark line defines "reuses an existing capability" as: a linear readout trained on the pretrained model recovers it, and taking the readout's direction away breaks the fine-tuned behaviour. The removal clause had never been read on a pair whose answer is known by construction.

What we did. A small transformer is pretrained to count the letter a. Two fine-tunes of it: task P answers the count of a plus one (it reuses the count), task N answers the count of b (it needs a new one). We trained probes for the count of a on the pretrained model and removed what they read from the residual at the answer position, block by block. The share of gain lost is the state's extra drop in the log-odds of its answer, beyond the pretrained model's own drop, over the log-odds it gained in fine-tuning. Two probes: a regression probe with one direction, which is the test as asked, and a softmax probe with six directions.

What we found. Removing the one regression direction did nothing, even on the pretrained model, so the filed headline (P loses more than N) misses and says nothing about the definition (first and second figures). Removing the six softmax directions hurts the pretrained model and costs P more than N at blocks 2 to 5, 0.72 against 0.34 at block 5, but N loses far more than predicted (third figure).

What it means. The one-direction test of new against strengthened capabilities is unanswered, not failed. The six-direction reading is a provisional sign that a removal can separate the two by degree: at blocks 2 and 3 it rests on subtracting the pretrained model's drop, which is negative there. Log-odds also overstate lost answers, since these states are near-certain; the share in nats is 0.16 and 0.31 for P at blocks 4 and 5 against 0.002 and 0.013 for N. Whether to try a different single direction is a question for you (section {{n:decisions}}).

Run
Retrained the pretrained model and 20 fine-tuned states (two learning rates, five seeds, per task) from the same code, then six cluster jobs on an L40, about two hours, $0. No independent check.
Found
  • The one regression direction reads the count perfectly from block 2 yet removing it costs the pretrained model nothing; the filed miss is not evidence against the definition. Why it does nothing is unexplained.
  • Six softmax directions: P loses more than N at blocks 2 to 5 (0.72 against 0.34 at block 5), N far above the near-zero we predicted; the correlation of the two counts, -0.50, is a candidate, not tested.
  • Log-odds overstate lost answers; accuracy after removal was not recorded.
