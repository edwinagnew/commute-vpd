Does one fixed change of importance values carry a small update's behaviour too?

2026-10-22 · RunPod 4090 · experiments/regating_small

Yes, as much as for the large update. E18's test, repeated on the two small emoticon fine-tunes that pass the adversarial checks, gives the same answers as on the large one that fails. About 60% of each fine-tune's gain comes from Γ switching existing subcomponents on (first figure), and one fixed change of Γ's values gives back 0.74 of that effect for all three (second figure). On a word task a smaller SGD update reads 0.40, beside the large one's 0.41. The fixed-pattern reading is not a large-update artefact.

Run
E18's trigger-only patterns on the do-nothing states of three ten-step emoticon fine-tunes: SGD 0.01 and Adam 3e-6 (E21's small pair, sizes 0.0024 and 0.0022) beside the large Adam 3e-5 (0.0113). On " which" then " was", SGD at 0.03 (E4's rate rule; size 0.0055, half the large one's) beside its large Adam twin. Each with a same-optimiser wrong-target (" http") control and twenty random-set draws. The large states reproduced E18 within 0.002; the small ones reproduced E21. Pod run 143 s, about $0.20.
Found
  • Switching share: 0.62 (SGD) and 0.59 (Adam 3e-6) against 0.60 for the large update, inside the filed 0.1.
  • Fixed change over own values: 0.74 for all three emoticon states; 0.40 for the small SGD word-task state against 0.41 for its large twin.
  • Controls: random sets give back 0; a " http" fine-tune's change gives back −0.17 to −0.30, so a wrong target's change lowers this one. Each input's own values at the trigger give back 0.85 to 0.98.
  • The small updates switch on fewer subcomponents, 154 and 170 against 210, mostly the same ones (overlap 0.70 and 0.77 with the large set).
