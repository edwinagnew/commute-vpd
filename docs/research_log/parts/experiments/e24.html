Does a small fine-tune read the same as the large one in E18's fixed-change test?

2026-10-22 · RunPod 4090 · experiments/regating_small

The question. On the re-gating line (Q5: is the change in which subcomponents Γ switches on after a fine-tune a true description of the update?), a fine-tune's weight change is left whole in the residual Δ: the do-nothing placement. The frozen importance function Γ gives each subcomponent, at each position, an importance value from 0 (off) to 1 (fully on); reading the updated model, it switches on subcomponents it kept off before. The switching effect is the rise in the target's log-odds at the trigger, the position just before the target, when Γ reads the updated model instead of the original, with the subcomponents unchanged. E18 found that on emoticon one fixed change to Γ's values, the same for every input, carries most of that effect. But E18 read only the large Adam fine-tune, and E21 then showed that this update fails VPD's faithfulness check while two smaller fine-tunes pass it. The check is the adversarial ratio: the damage an adversary searching over masks of the subcomponents does at a state, over what it does at the unchanged decomposition; in E21 up to 1.24 passes and 1.81 or more fails. Was E18's reading an artefact of an unfaithful update?

What we did. We repeated E18's test, with E18's definitions, on E21's two small emoticon fine-tunes (after :) predict o), one by SGD at rate 0.01 and one by Adam at 3e-6, beside the large Adam fine-tune at 3e-5 that E18 read; each ran ten steps. On a second task, after which predict was in varied Pile contexts, an SGD fine-tune ran beside its large Adam twin. Its rate, 0.03, is the lowest of five tried (0.001 to 0.1) whose fine-tune gives the target probability 0.9 or more on held-out inputs while changing other predictions little. An update's size is the largest change of any one weight matrix relative to that matrix's norm, so it compares across optimisers. The small emoticon updates are about a fifth of the large one's size; the word-task SGD update, 0.0055 against 0.0132, is about 0.42 of its twin's, so only moderately small. The switching share is the switching effect over the fine-tune's whole gain on the target. For the test, Γ's values at the trigger only are replaced by a pattern. Its share is what it gives back of the switching effect, divided by what each input's own updated values give back there: 0 is the original model's values, 1 is each input's own updated values. The fixed change is the average, over training inputs, of updated minus original values; the test adds it to each input's original values and clips the sum to 0 to 1, setting any value below 0 to 0 and above 1 to 1. Lines set before E18 ran read a share of 0.7 or more as a fixed pattern, and 0.3 or less as needing each input's own values.

What we found. The chip "held" means the predictions written beforehand came out as expected. On emoticon the small updates read about the same as the large one, on one seed each with no noise floor measured. Their switching shares are 0.59 and 0.62 against 0.60 (first figure), and the fixed change gives back a share of 0.74 for all three, above the 0.7 line (second figure). On which then was the smaller update also reads like its large twin: a share of 0.40 against 0.41, between the two lines, as E18 found. So there the answer is "as undecided as for the large update", not "yes". Its switching share is higher, 0.85 against 0.75; that was not predicted and rests on one seed.

What it means. On these two tasks, one training seed each, E18's reading came out the same at the two sizes tested, a fifth of the large update on emoticon and about 0.42 of it on the word task: a fixed pattern on emoticon, undecided on the word task. On emoticon this includes two updates that pass the faithfulness check in E21; the word-task updates' check was not read here. The test is blind to anything off the trigger and to E18's later follow-ups, which were not repeated here. The fixed-shift hypothesis, adopted from E18 that day as a working hypothesis, went on to more tasks rather than more seeds of these.

Run
Do-nothing placements of five ten-step fine-tunes, seed 0, read at the trigger on 73 held-out emoticon inputs and 100 word-task ones; the large states reproduced E18's switching share within 0.001 and what each pattern gives back of the switching effect within 0.002, and the small ones reproduced E21. One RunPod 4090 job of 143 s, about $0.20.
Found
  • On emoticon, two small fine-tunes that pass the faithfulness check in E21 read about the same as the large one that fails it: a switching share near 0.6, and a fixed-change share of 0.74, above the 0.7 line, on one seed each.
  • On which then was an SGD update about 0.42 of its large twin's size reads like it, a share of 0.40, between the lines, so the word task stays undecided; its higher switching share, 0.85 against 0.75, was not predicted and rests on one seed.
  • The small emoticon updates switch on fewer subcomponents (raised by more than 0.3 in the fixed change), 154 and 170 against 210, mostly the same ones: subcomponents in both sets over those in either, 0.70 and 0.77 with the large set (report, no figure).
