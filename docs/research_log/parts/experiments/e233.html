Does the share put British spelling above a scrambled twin?

2026-11-21 · cell P2.48 (filled), no independent check · experiments/british_style

The question. Part 2 asks whether the re-gating share, the part of a fine-tune's gain carried by switching on the original model's subcomponents differently, still tells a task the model already prefers from one it must learn, on running text and not on hand-built pairs. The earlier real-text pairs differed in data and format as well as in what the model knew, so this run builds one pair matched on everything else.

What we did. We fine-tuned the 67M model on Pile rows in which every American spelling from a public list of 1,759 pairs is replaced by its British form ("color" becomes "colour"), and on the same rows with each such word replaced by a fixed letter scramble of the British form. The original model prefers the British forms by 6.41 log-odds on average, so the scrambles are forms it starts far from. Each task ran three times (three batch orders, 4,800 rows, Adam at 3e-5), saved at relative update size 0.0175, and was read at the same 500 held-out word occurrences. Three checks ran first and passed: a saved lowercase fine-tune reproduced its share (0.7538 against 0.7519), a zero update read exactly 0, and the British preference was above the bar of 2.

What we found. The filed prediction, every British seed's share above every twin seed's, failed. Both sides read low: British 0.077 to 0.136, twin 0.124 to 0.170, so the ranges overlap and the British mean is 0.043 below the twin's. The British fine-tune also gained less at equal size (1.43 to 1.45 log-odds against 2.11 to 2.28), so its share is a ratio over little more than the floor of 1. The update's effective rank put British above the twin by 0.0037 in log10, under one per cent. Neurons for half the gain cannot be read on British: patching every neuron carried at most 0.03 of its gain.

What it means. On the one real-text task with a twin built to its own premise, the share does not separate familiar from unfamiliar forms, so its agreement with the built pairs does not carry over here, and the card's stop rule closes further real-text twins for the share. Two caveats: the twin also has rarer tokens and, for about two in five words, a different token count, and this is one pair on one model. No reused or new verdict is given for either fine-tune; that needs the featurized model's reading.
