What changes when only the upper tail of each neuron counts as a feature?

2026-11-21 · cell P2.45 (filled), descriptive, no independent check · experiments/topk_upper

The question. The earlier top-activating-neuron run (E217) counted a neuron as a feature on a token when it fired above its 97th percentile or below its 3rd, and found low overlap between base and fine-tune. The small model's GELU flattens at about -0.17 and every neuron's 3rd percentile sat on that floor. How much of E217's reading came from the floor tail?

What we did. We repeated E217 on the same 20 fine-tunes of ten tasks at two seeds, with the same saved percentiles, windows and search, changing one thing: a neuron is a feature only above its 97th percentile, and the bottom tail joins the pooled remainder. The 99th and 90th percentiles sit beside it. The definition's score R is the average squared cosine, over the smallest set of variables that keeps the fine-tune's behaviour, between the fine-tune's and the base model's write; the overlap is the Jaccard index of the base's and the fine-tune's strong-neuron sets. The base read against itself and the full-width reading came first.

What we found. The control was exact. At the main threshold the overlap is 0.451 to 0.674 against E217's 0.231 to 0.361, and R is 0.988 to 0.998 against 0.960 to 0.990, still below the full-width 0.9991 to 0.9999 on all 20. The remainder's lowest cosine is 0.847 to 0.958, about as before. The gap in R between each built pair's two sides shrinks several-fold: the case pair, E217's largest, from 0.011 to 0.002, and at the 99th percentile two pairs turn slightly negative. No gap reaches 0.01.

What it means. Much of E217's low overlap and lowered R came with the floor tail. Some change remains, but the upper-tail featurized model barely tells the built pairs apart in R. This is a description; there is no verdict on reuse or new.
