Does the fine-tuned model itself use the subcomponents Γ switches on?

2026-10-27 to 2026-10-28 · RunPod RTX 4090, MATS cluster L40 · experiments/recruitment_test

The question. After a fine-tune, the frozen Γ switches on some subcomponents at the trigger that it kept off before; call that set S. The re-gating line asks whether this change describes the update (Q5). If it does, S should be machinery the fine-tuned model really uses for its new behaviour, not just pieces Γ happens to light up.

What we did. Ten-step fine-tunes taught the 67M model to follow a trigger with a target token, on three tasks with two seeds each. We asked two things. First, what do S's pieces do in the original model? Second, how much of the fine-tune's gain on the target is undone when only S's outputs at the trigger are reset to their original values?

What we found. Γ picks out the model's own machinery for the target, without being told the target. In the original model, 60 to 96% of S's pieces usually fire just before that target, against 2 to 8% of random sets (first figure). Resetting S undoes about a fifth of the gain on two of the three tasks, where random sets and the pieces whose outputs changed most undo none (second figure).

What it means. Part of the fine-tune runs through pieces the model already had, and re-gating points at them. Re-gating therefore shows what an update draws on, which is the project's question. The caveat sets the next test: ranking pieces by their pull on the target, which needs no Γ but does need the target, finds an equal-sized set that undoes as much or more. So Γ has not been shown to see anything that ranking misses. Its value here is finding the machinery without the target. How much of an update it accounts for is open.

Run
The tasks are emoticon, ` which` then ` was`, and ` we` then ` just` (E18's), with a fine-tune to ` http` at the same triggers as the null. S is the alive subcomponents whose importance at the trigger rises by more than 0.3 (21 to 213 of them). A piece's pull on the target is the first-order effect of its change on the target's log-odds. One RunPod job (about $0.23) and four cluster jobs ($0); every restore step was checked by an independent agent.
Found
  • S is the target's existing machinery, found without the target: 0.60 to 0.96 of it, against 0.02 to 0.08 for random sets.
  • Resetting S undoes about a fifth of the gain on emoticon and ` which` and under 0.05 on ` we`; random sets and the largest-change pieces undo none, so the size of a change does not explain S's share.
  • A ranking by pull on the target matches or beats S at the same count, and adding S to it adds about S's own share. Removing S from the weights was uninformative, since that hurts the original model too.
