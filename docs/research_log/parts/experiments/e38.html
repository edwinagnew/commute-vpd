Does the fine-tuned model itself use the subcomponents Γ switches on?

2026-10-27 to 2026-10-28 · RunPod RTX 4090, MATS cluster L40 · experiments/recruitment_test

Partly: putting the switched-on set's trigger outputs back to the original model's values takes back about a fifth of the fine-tune's gain on two of three tasks, while sets whose outputs moved as much or more take back none. Whether a ranking by the direction of each subcomponent's change, with no Γ, would find an equally strong set is untested.

Run
A 10-step fine-tune teaches the 67M model to follow a trigger with a target token (E18's tasks: emoticon, ` which`, ` we`; two seeds each; a fine-tune to ` http` at the same triggers is the null). With Γ frozen, a set of the original model's subcomponents is switched on at the trigger: the switched-on set S, the alive subcomponents whose importance there rises by more than 0.3 (21 to 213 of them). Every number is a share of the gain: the fine-tune raises the target's log-odds at the trigger by 10 to 14 nats, and a share is how much of that rise an intervention undoes, 0 none and 1 all, on held-out windows. Four steps. (1) Remove S from the weights, everywhere (RunPod, about $0.23), then at the trigger only, from both models. (2) Census what S does in Pile text. (3) Put back only S's trigger outputs, to their original-model values, beside random, null and already-active sets. (4) Do the same for the subcomponents outside S whose trigger outputs changed most, matched to S per layer by count and by summed change; for every alive subcomponent at once; and on emoticon for layer 3's active pool, whole and one at a time. Steps 2 to 4 ran on the cluster, $0 (step 4: job 40041). Controls ran first each time; steps 3 and 4 were checked by an independent agent.
Found
  • S is mostly the original model's own machinery for the target (60 to 96% of it is followed by the target in Pile text, against 2 to 8% of random sets), and removing it already costs the original model 0.14 to 0.27, so removal cannot say how much of the fine-tune runs through it.
  • Putting back S's trigger outputs takes back 0.18 to 0.24 of the gain on emoticon and ` which` and under 0.05 on ` we`, while random sets, the ` http` fine-tune's set and sets already active at the trigger take back about none on the word tasks.
  • Checked claim: on the 67M model after a 10-step fine-tune, putting back the original trigger outputs of the subcomponents Γ switched on takes back about a fifth of the gain on emoticon and ` which`. The same number of the subcomponents whose trigger outputs changed most carry about four times the change, yet take back about none on ` which` and less than none on emoticon. So how much a subcomponent's output moved does not explain the switched-on set's share. (Against the filed expectation, S is only 5 to 11% of the top movers; every alive subcomponent at once takes back under half, 0.38 to 0.49.)
