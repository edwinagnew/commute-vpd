Does the fine-tuned model itself use the subcomponents Γ switches on?

2026-10-27 to 2026-10-28 · RunPod RTX 4090, MATS cluster L40 · experiments/recruitment_test

Partly: putting the switched-on set's trigger outputs back to the original model's values takes back about a fifth of the fine-tune's gain on two of three tasks, far above random sets; but subcomponents ranked by their pull toward the target, at the same count, take back at least as much, so the set's share is what its target-pointing members carry. Γ finds, without being told the target, a set rich in them.

Run
A 10-step fine-tune teaches the 67M model to follow a trigger with a target token (E18's tasks: emoticon, ` which`, ` we`; two seeds each; a fine-tune to ` http` at the same triggers is the null). With Γ frozen, a set of the original model's subcomponents is switched on at the trigger: the switched-on set S, the alive subcomponents whose importance there rises by more than 0.3 (21 to 213 of them). Every number is a share of the gain: the fine-tune raises the target's log-odds at the trigger by 10 to 14 nats, and a share is how much of that rise an intervention undoes, 0 none and 1 all, on held-out windows. (1) Remove S from the weights (RunPod, about $0.23). (2) Census what S does in Pile text. (3) Put back only S's trigger outputs to their original-model values, beside random, null and already-active sets and the subcomponents whose outputs changed most. (4) Do the same for the subcomponents outside S whose changes pull hardest on the target to first order (this ranking reads the target and no Γ), as many as S has in each matrix. (5) Restore S and that set together, and S split by the direction of its members' changes. (6) Restore the next-ranked set beside the first, and the top set by pull on the target at twice S's count, free to take S's members. Steps 2 to 6 ran on the cluster, $0 (jobs 40041, 40121, 40196, 40240). Controls ran first each time; every restore step was checked by an independent agent.
Found
  • S is mostly the original model's own machinery for the target (60 to 96% of it is followed by the target in Pile text, against 2 to 8% of random sets), and removing it already costs the original model, so removal cannot say how much of the fine-tune runs through it.
  • Putting back S's trigger outputs takes back about a fifth of the gain on emoticon and ` which` (under 0.05 on ` we`); random sets, the null's set, already-active sets and the subcomponents whose outputs moved most take back about none, so the size of a change does not explain S's share.
  • Checked claim: a set ranked by first-order pull toward the target, outside S and at its count in each matrix, takes back as much on ` which` and more on emoticon. Restoring the two together takes back close to the sum of the two apart, as two adjacent target-ranked sets also do, and the top target-ranked set of twice S's count, free to take S's members, takes back at least as much as both together: clearly more on emoticon, the same within noise on ` which`. So on this model nothing in Γ's set has been shown to carry a part of the gain that a target-aware ranking at the same count misses; Γ finds, without the target, a set rich in target-pointing subcomponents, and its share is what those members carry.
