Does the reuse score separate built reuse from built new with VPD subcomponents as the variables?

2026-11-20 · cell P1.37 (filled), no independent check · experiments/kstar_vpd

The question. Part 2 asks whether the working definition's reuse score R, the mean over the variables a fine-tune needs of how closely each keeps the base model's mechanism, reads reuse above new. At coarse levels and at neurons it did not, and R sat near 1. VPD subcomponents were the one featurization finer than heads and coarser than neurons that nobody had read.

What we did. The variables were the 38,912 VPD subcomponents of 24 decomposed matrices, one remainder per matrix, one variable per neuron the fine-tune adds, and the embedding and unembedding. We read R on six edits built to be reuse (the copy neurons' outputs doubled, multiplied by ten, moved onto eight new neurons, or zeroed and rebuilt by retraining later layers) or new (a lookup written into eight quiet neurons, or spread thinly over a whole layer), after a do-nothing control that must read exactly 1; then on E187's nine trained pairs, 68 saved states. Each variable's grade is the squared cosine between the fine-tune's write and the base model's write, averaged over inputs.

What we found. The filed prediction, every reuse edit above both new edits, reversed: the ten-fold edit scored 0.9755 and the thinly spread new edit 0.9963, a gap of 0.021 against a noise band of 0.0010. The other three reuse edits scored 0.9954 to 0.9992, above both new edits by under a hundredth. An uncapped circuit search moved R by under 0.002. On the nine pairs R lay between 0.9970 and 0.9997, each gap at most 0.00056, ranking with minus the update-size gap (Spearman 0.87) and not with the held-model reading (-0.02).

What it means. The grade, not the circuit search, causes the reversal: a large gain on a few directions reads as a changed mechanism and a small change spread widely reads as kept. The score saturates near 1 here as at neurons, and the grade is part of the adopted wording, so the user decides what to do with it; the question is a pending card in section {{n:decisions}}. No reused or new verdict is given.
