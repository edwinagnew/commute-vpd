Does Wang et al.'s circuit-overlap reading tell reused from new on our updates?

2026-11-24 · cell P2.69, definition-lead · experiments/eapig_overlap · cluster debug job 64845

The question. Wang et al. 2025 ask whether a fine-tune reuses a model's circuit or builds a new one, by counting the connections (edges) the two circuits share. Is that published baseline a fair rival to our reading on the 67M?

What we did. We ran their pinned code on 44 updates: for each, the original model's circuit and the updated model's circuit (the top 5% of edges by attribution score), and the edge overlap (IoU, 1 when no edge changed). Every value sits beside the original model's own resampling range and a matched random update.

What we found. Both built controls passed: doubling eight neurons keeps all 37 edges, and the edits built to add a lookup read 0.50, under the original model's resampling range, where a random update at matched output change reads 0.82. On trained fine-tunes it mostly gives no reading, since the circuits fall under Wang's 70% faithfulness bar. The key-grid copy fine-tune reads 0.29 to 0.30 ("new"), but the original model's response to the key is about eight times weaker than the fine-tune's.

What it means. The baseline works on built edits and gives no reuse-or-new reading on most trained updates. Its one headline, "new" for the copy fine-tune on short inputs, compares against a circuit for much less behaviour, so it does not test whether the fine-tune reuses heads the original model has.
