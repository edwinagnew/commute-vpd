Does a teacher-trained copy land in the same place when only its batch order changes?

2026-11-06 · two cluster debug L40 jobs (54763, 54772), $0 · experiments/copy_reproducibility · provisional, not independently checked

The question. The regeneration run (E119) stopped because one teacher-trained copy, the emoticon task's Pile-text copy of the Adam fine-tune, read a target probability of 0.74 against E34's 0.90. Was that copy an outlier at its first batch order, or does batch order alone move it? That decides whether E119's other eleven readings can stand.

What we did. We rebuilt that copy and E119's three re-switching copies on the same L40 card with two new batch orders each, changing nothing else, and read them with E119's route split and frozen labels. A copy is unreadable if it reaches under half of its teacher's gain.

What we found. The emoticon copy is unstable: 0.744, 0.812 and 0.678 at orders 0, 1 and 2, a spread of 0.135 against 0.032 on the H100, and unreadable at every order (0.34 to 0.38 of the teacher's gain). The three re-switching copies stayed in the corner at both new orders, with re-switching shares 0.72 to 0.76 and masked-off shares 0.17 to 0.25.

What it means. E119's stop was one unstable copy that carries no route reading, not a fault in the other eleven, and its re-switching placements do not depend on batch order. E34's emoticon Pile-text number now carries a spread of 0.135; task-text copies stay the ones to cite. Card and order are not fully separated for this cell, since the H100 was not run at orders 1 and 2.
