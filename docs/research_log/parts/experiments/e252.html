Do the copies run through several copy heads, so a label from three heads is firmer than one from a single head?

2026-11-21 · cell P2.55, run 1 of 2, no independent check · experiments/prior_mech_2x2

The question. Part 3's test of the second claim labels each fine-tune as reused or new by switching off one copy head, (2,4). That head took only a quarter of key copy's gain, and head (1,1) took as much, so the label rested on one head. Does a label from several copy heads separate copies from ciphers more firmly?

What we did. On the 27 saved fine-tunes of E249 and the copy-map study, we switched off the base model's top k heads by their score on its own copying (k = 1, 2, 3, 5; the top three are (2,4), (2,3) and (2,2)), and read the share of each fine-tune's gain that is lost. Head (1,1) was kept out of the ranking, and a separate arm switched off (2,4) and (1,1) together. The reference is k random heads, 20 draws per k.

What we found. With the top three heads off, key copy loses 0.52 of its gain and the old low-prior copy 0.49, while the ciphers lose 0.02 to 0.05. Three random heads cost each fine-tune 0.08 to 0.16, more than the 0.1 we filed, so the label stands about five times over its reference rather than against a 0.1 floor. The pair of (2,4) and (1,1) costs the ciphers 0.12 to 0.13, so (1,1) is a head every fine-tune leans on, not a copy head.

What it means. The copy label is firmer: three layer-2 heads carry about half of each copy's gain and almost none of the ciphers'. The label is specific to copying only with (1,1) left out. Four of the filed rules (K2, K5 to K7) held; K1 (the old low copy at 0.49, just under 0.5), K3 (ciphers 0.053 to 0.058 at k = 5) and K4 (the random-head floor) missed.

Run
Mean-ablation of the top k heads on the 27 saved states, 20 random-head draws per k, and a (2,4) plus (1,1) arm; MATS cluster L40, job 63063, 5 minutes, no cost.
Found
  • Top three heads off: key copy 0.52, old low copy 0.49, copy map 0.80; every cipher, rank-1 and elicit fine-tune 0.02 to 0.05.
  • Random heads cost 0.06, 0.11, 0.16 and 0.30 (median, largest fine-tune) at k = 1, 2, 3, 5, above the filed 0.1.
  • Head (1,1) costs every fine-tune alike (ciphers 0.12 to 0.13 with (2,4)), so it is a general head.
