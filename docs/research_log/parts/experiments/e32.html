Why did one fixed change of Γ's importance values fail on the copy task, and what is that change made of?

2026-10-22 · RunPod RTX 4090 · experiments/shift_anatomy

The question. Γ, VPD's importance function, is a small frozen network that reads the inputs to each of the model's 24 decomposed matrices, at every position, and gives each subcomponent an importance value: a raw score clipped to between 0 and 1. After a fine-tune Γ reads different activations, so its values change. The re-gating line, sub-question Q5 (does the change in Γ's values describe a fine-tune?), works with the fixed-shift hypothesis: at the trigger (the position just before the target word the fine-tune teaches), that change is mostly one fixed change of the values, the same for every input. E28 found this held on word tasks and failed on the copy task, where one fixed change gave back 0.07. This run asks why, and what the change is made of.

What we did. We rebuilt E28's eight do-nothing states on one seed. In a do-nothing state a ten-step fine-tune's whole weight change goes into the residual Δ, the part of the weights no subcomponent explains, and the subcomponents stay as VPD released them; the model is run without Δ, each subcomponent scaled by its importance value, so the fine-tune acts only through Γ reading the fine-tuned activations. Five are word tasks: emoticon (after an emoticon's first character, predict o) and four trigger-then-target pairs such as ` it` then ` is`. Three are key-value tasks, where after a marker each of 32 key words is followed by itself (copy) or by a fixed random word (32-pair cipher), or one key by one random word. On about 100 held-out inputs we gave the model substitute importance values at the trigger. The share is the rise in the target's log-odds that a substitute gives over the original model's values, divided by the rise each input's own updated values give: 0 is the original model's values, 1 the input's own, and above 1 means the substitute raised the target slightly more. The fixed change is the training inputs' mean updated values, scored net of the original model's mean, since averaging alone gives something back. We tried four substitutes: a fixed change per key, one direction with a per-input strength, the fixed change in one block of matrices only, and a fixed move of the activations Γ reads.

What we found. On both 32-key tasks one fixed change per key gives back most of the effect, 0.84 on the copy task, where random groups of the same sizes give back 0.07 (figure 1). There a fixed move of what Γ reads gives back almost nothing (figure 2); one direction with a per-input strength is low too, but that arm missed its own check on two word tasks, so it is weak evidence. With the MLP at each input's own values, attention's change can be dropped on every task. On word tasks the MLP's fixed change carries the effect, but on the copy task it gives back under half, so there the input-specific part sits in the MLP (figure 3). Attention was only tested with the MLP at its own values: on the copy task the MLP-only fixed change gives 0.46 but both blocks fixed give 0.07, so attention may matter jointly there. Each copy key's change does not resemble that word's own values on ordinary text (figure 4).

What it means. E28's failure came from averaging over 32 keys, so the hypothesis survives as one fixed change per key, on this one seed and at the trigger only. What a key's change is made of stays open: three simple guesses failed on the copy task. With E33 the user restated the hypothesis as one fixed change per cue, tentatively, on 2026-10-23.

Run
E28's eight do-nothing states, regenerated exactly (ten-step Adam fine-tunes, seed 0, read at the trigger), each scored by the four arms above, with the Pile match on the copy task only. Emoticon ran first and reproduced the earlier run's numbers exactly; one RunPod RTX 4090 job, 118 s, $0.21.
Found
  • One fixed change per key gives back 0.84 on the copy task, against 0.07 for random groups, and the 32-pair cipher reads the same way; on one seed, and as we had predicted for the copy task (at least 0.7).
  • With the MLP at each input's own values, attention's change barely matters on any task; on word tasks the MLP's fixed change carries the effect, and on the copy task the input-specific part sits in the MLP. We had predicted a fixed "spot the cue" part in attention, and it missed.
  • On the copy task a fixed move of what Γ reads gives back 0.03, as we had predicted, and each key's change does not match that word's own values on ordinary text, though we had predicted it would; the per-input strength arm gave back almost nothing too, but it failed its own check on the word tasks.
