What do the decomposition's readings say about the case-label pair?

2026-11-06 · provisional, no independent check · one cluster debug L40 job (54711), $0 · experiments/case_label_both

The question. E112 built four 67M fine-tunes: two learn a label from capitalisation, two twins memorise shuffled labels. The definition reads all of them "not through C". Does the decomposition separate the real arm from its twin?

What we did. On the same four states we ran the decomposition's readings with the filed lines of E12, E119 and E38 Amendment 2: the route split (the share of the gain from switching released subcomponents on, r, against the share carried by subcomponents Γ keeps off), the same on teacher-trained copies, and the removal of the switched-on set against 20 random sets. The twin trained 300 steps against the real arm's 30, so its update is about three times larger; that gap is a confound.

What we found. The route split and the teacher-trained copies read all four fine-tunes masked-off (r 0.10 to 0.17), real and twin alike. Removing the switched-on set reads the twin "through", while the real arm splits by seed, which the filed joint calls "reversed".

What it means. The decomposition agrees with the definition only that the real arm is not through existing structure, and neither separates it from its memorising twin. No 67M task has yet been read "reuses C" by both sides; E125 builds one.
