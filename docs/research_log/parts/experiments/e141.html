Does the spectrum of a fine-tune's weight change alone tell a pair's two sides apart, as the re-gating share does?

2026-11-10 · provisional, no independent check · one CPU run on saved weight changes, $0 · experiments/update_svd

The question. Part 2 asks how easily a cheaper reading beats the re-gating share. The cheapest one needs no model and no forward pass: the singular values of the weight change itself. Does their effective rank, which counts how many directions the change spreads over, separate the side built to reuse from the side built to be new, on the same fine-tunes the share was read on?

What we did. For every saved fine-tune of nine task pairs (two to eight seeds a side) we took the effective rank of its weight change, averaged over matrices by squared size, and applied the earlier weight-statistics run's separation rule: the gap between the sides over the larger spread inside a side. The controls passed first: ten equal directions read 10, one reads 1, and two earlier fine-tunes read their old values exactly. Sides are labelled as each pair was built; no verdict on reuse.

What we found. The rank separates five of the nine pairs and the share seven, and they agree on only three. The rank misses both pairs whose sides have about the same update size (gaps 0.07 and 1.6 against spreads of 6.7 and 11.4). It separates both real-against-shuffled-label pairs, which the share does not, but there the memorising twin trained three to ten times longer, and on those two the reuse side reads lower. The fine-tune that learned nothing reads 562, above every pair.

What it means. The update's spectrum is no substitute for the share on these pairs: it looks more like a record of training length than of which side was built to reuse, a cause this run does not test. Four pairs are holes because a side kept no weight changes (rank-1 against cipher at 96 keys, copy against the prior-matched cipher, half-known against its shuffle, the class map against its twin).
