Does the adversary break updated decompositions by unbalancing changes that cancel?

2026-10-21 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/cancellation

No. The idea was that a map writes the update as large per-subcomponent changes that oppose each other, so masking a few leaves a big uncancelled leftover. Component training's changes instead add up, in the weights and in the output, and the least-squares split's cancel only mildly (first figure). Removing exactly the part of the changes the adversary removes explains under a tenth of its extra damage, no more than a shuffled pattern (second figure). The rest was inferred to come from the adversary's mask on the original subcomponents; E20 measured it and found it does not, on three of four failing states.

Run
Nine states rebuilt from E11's saved files, each read on the first 128 Pile validation rows: component training at step 10 on emoticon and five ladder tasks (three pass the adversary, three fail), the emoticon fine-tune's least-squares split (fails) and adjoint split (passes), and a control with component training's changes mixed to cancel in the weights. Per state: the coherence of the changes in the weights with a sign-flip null; a forward hook that removes a fraction of each change while the original subcomponents stay whole, for a random half and for the adversary's own fractions, shuffled as a null; and the adversary's extra damage. Both gates passed: the hook reproduces the weight edit, the null reads 1.
Found
  • Coherence in the weights: about 3 for component training on every task, 2.4 for the adjoint split, 0.44 for the least-squares split; the null reads 1.
  • Half revert: about 0.3 for component training and the adjoint split, on the curve their weights predict; 0.65 for the least-squares split. Nothing follows the pass or fail ordering.
  • Removing the adversary's fraction of the changes: 4 to 7% of its extra damage on failing component-training states, 1% on the least-squares split; the shuffled pattern costs the same.
  • The control failed: its weight cancellation hardly reaches the output. That leaves the half revert unscored but does not rescue cancellation, since the adversary's revert reads the mask directly.
  • That the rest comes from the original subcomponents was inferred, not measured; E20 measured it and refuted it on three of four failing states.
