Why does VPD's adversarial check fail updated decompositions that its random-mask check passes?

2026-10-20 to 10-22 · MATS cluster L40, and one note from saved numbers · experiments/cancellation, experiments/cancellation_complement, experiments/cancellation_sgd

The question. VPD writes each weight matrix as rank-one subcomponents plus a residual Δ, and Γ rates each subcomponent's importance on each input, from 0 to 1. A map carries a fine-tune onto this by changing the subcomponents; a subcomponent's change is its new piece minus its old one. Early maps, such as component training (10 Adam steps on the subcomponents themselves, on tasks that raise one word after a trigger, such as " was" after " which"), failed VPD's adversarial check on some tasks while its random-mask check barely moved. Q6 asks why the two checks disagree.

What we did. A mask turns a subcomponent down, at most as far as Γ allows, and damage is the KL from the model to the masked copy on 128 rows of Pile text. The random-mask check draws random masks over about half the matrices at a time. The adversary masks all matrices and searches for 20 steps for the most damaging mask. The adversarial ratio is its damage on the updated decomposition over its damage on the original; above 1.5 fails, the line set in E2. A state's extra damage is that difference in nats. About 9,900 subcomponents are alive (Γ rates them important somewhere on the original model's text); the rest are dead. We first read saved scores against the code. Then, with forward hooks that apply one adversary mask to one block at a time, we split each failing state's extra damage into parts: the original alive subcomponents, their changes, the dead subcomponents with Δ, and the offset, which is the cost of masking the alive block alone plus the dead-and-Δ block alone, minus the cost of masking both together. We did this on four Adam and five plain-SGD failures, with passing states and controls, and reran longer searches.

What we found. The adversary's mask is selective: it does 7 to 300 times the extra damage of turning every subcomponent down together, while random masks add almost nothing (figure 1). The failure is not cancellation: the changes add up rather than oppose, and removing the adversary's share of them carries under a tenth of the extra damage, no more than a shuffled pattern (figure 2). Nor is it on the original subcomponents, except for about a fifth on " in" then " Fig". On every failing state measured the largest part is the lost offset: at the original model masking alive and dead together costs 0.90 nats less than the two alone, and failing states lose most or all of that (figures 3 and 4). Verdicts hold from 20 to 160 search steps under the VPD paper's step rule (figure 5).

What it means. The adversary finds a joint pattern across the alive and the dead subcomponents that the random-mask check, masking only part of the model at a time, would rarely hit; that reading of the random check is from the code, not tested. With the original subcomponents carrying little, the case that a stale Γ around them calls for a Γ that sees the updated model was dropped. Component training never touches the dead subcomponents, so the update must change how removing them interacts with removing the alive ones; why is not shown, nor why SGD loses the offset where Adam at the same size keeps it. All of this rests on one decomposition, one adversary seed per state and one kind of search. Three of the four Adam failures pass at a smaller or later update (E21); the proposed next test, how far each state's changes point along the dead subcomponents' write directions, has not run.

Run
A reading of saved component-training scores against the measurement code (was A1, no directory, $0), then three sets of MATS cluster L40 jobs, all $0, on saved states rebuilt with forward hooks over the first 128 Pile validation rows, one adversary seed each:
  • the cancellation tests on six component-training states, the least-squares and adjoint splits and a built cancelling control (E17, experiments/cancellation);
  • the block-by-block split of the extra damage on those eight states and the longer searches, jobs 28206, 28251, 28306, 28307, 29200 and 29218 to 29220 (was E20, experiments/cancellation_complement);
  • the same split on the five SGD failures, Adam at step 1,000 and an SGD control, with longer searches, jobs 29344 and 29496 to 29498 (was E23, experiments/cancellation_sgd).
Found
  • The adversary's failing mask is selective and joint: random masks and the all-down mask add almost nothing, and the update's changes add up rather than cancel.
  • On all nine failing states measured, Adam and SGD alike, the largest part of the extra damage is the lost offset between removing the alive and the dead subcomponents; the original subcomponents carry a fifth on " in" then " Fig" and a third on 32 copies, little elsewhere.
  • The verdicts stand from 20 to 160 search steps under the paper's rule; why the offset is lost, and why SGD loses it where Adam keeps it, is unexplained.
