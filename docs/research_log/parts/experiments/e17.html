Does the adversary break updated decompositions because the update is written as large changes that cancel?

2026-10-21 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/cancellation

The question. VPD writes each weight matrix as rank-one subcomponents plus a residual Δ, and Γ rates each subcomponent's importance on each input. A map carries a fine-tune onto this by changing each subcomponent; its change is its new piece minus its old one. Maps that kept the behaviour in the subcomponents passed VPD's gentle checks, yet on some tasks and updates failed the adversarial one. Q6 asks why those states fail. One candidate was cancellation: large changes that oppose each other sum to something small, so masking a few leaves a large leftover.

What we did. The adversary searches for the mask over subcomponents that most changes the output on 128 rows of Pile text. It may turn a subcomponent down only as far as Γ's importance allows. The adversarial ratio is its damage on an updated decomposition over its damage on the original; a state fails above 1.5, the line set in E2. We took nine saved states. Six are component training, the subcomponents trained directly for 10 Adam steps, on six next-word tasks: three pass, three fail. Two divide the emoticon fine-tune's weight change among the subcomponents: the least-squares split (fails) and the adjoint split (passes). The ninth is a positive control: component training's emoticon changes recombined among the subcomponents so that each grows but their sum, the weight change, stays exactly the same. We asked whether the changes cancel, and whether removing only the adversary's share of them accounts for its extra damage.

What we found. The adversary's damage is not the leftover of cancelling changes. Removing the adversary's share of the changes carries under a tenth of its extra damage on every failing state, the same as a shuffled pattern (third figure). The half-revert test reads below the cancelling line on every state (second figure). But its positive control failed: it cancels in the weights yet also read low, so that test was never shown able to catch cancellation, and its readings say nothing either way.

What it means. The claim that the failure is cancellation is refuted, on the adversary's-share test alone. That test reads the adversary's own mask and rests on a hook that reproduced the weight edit exactly, but it has no working positive control either: the control built to cancel did not fail the adversary (ratio 1.11), and removing its share carried 3% of the extra damage. This run guessed that the rest of the damage lay in the mask on the original subcomponents, without measuring it. E20 measured it and found the guess wrong on three of four failing states; on the fourth, ' in' then ' Fig', the original subcomponents carry a fifth of the excess. On all four most of the damage sits in how removing the dead subcomponents (importance never above a small threshold) and Δ combines with removing the alive ones (all the others).

Run
Nine states rebuilt from E11's saved files, one seed each, read with forward hooks on the first 128 Pile validation rows; the six tasks are emoticon, ' will' then ' have', ' we' then ' just', ' it' then ' is', ' which' then ' was' and ' in' then ' Fig'. One MATS cluster job of under ten minutes, $0; the hook reproduced the weight edit exactly.
Found
  • Component training's changes add up in the weights on every task (coherence about 3); the half revert reads near 0.3, passing and failing states alike, though that test is not validated.
  • Removing the adversary's share of the changes carries 1 to 7% of its extra damage on the failing states, no more than a shuffled pattern.
  • The positive control failed, so the half revert has no shown case of catching cancellation and its readings are unscored; the adversary's-share result does not rest on it.

The claim that the failure is cancellation is refuted, and the complement revert ran as E20: the rest is not in the original subcomponents but in the mask on Δ and the dead subcomponents. Current verdict: the states here are the large updates, Adam 1e-5 component training at step 10 and the large fine-tune's splits; r0 t352 and r1 t534 pass at steps 100 and 1,000 (E21). On the five failing states made by plain SGD (step 1,000 on r0 and r1, both 32-pair key-value tasks, r2 at step 100) the changes add up too, coherence 2.5 to 3.2, and 4 of 5 meet this card's half-revert rule (E23), so the finding covers SGD's late failures as well.
