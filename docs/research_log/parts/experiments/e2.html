Can a fine-tune be carried onto the decomposition's pieces without breaking it?

2026-10-14 · MATS cluster L40 · experiments/lift_test

The question. The project asks whether a small update from post-training can be expressed in VPD's decomposition of the model, so that the decomposition keeps explaining the model as it trains (Q1). Each weight matrix is written as its alive subcomponents (the pieces) plus a residual Δ that no piece holds. This early round compared two ways of moving an update onto the pieces (Q2), and asked how much of a fine-tune the pieces cannot hold at all (Q1, Q3).

What we did. We taught the released 67M model the emoticon task (after the first character of an emoticon, predict o) for up to 30 steps. Route one fine-tunes normally, then applies the least-squares split: the smallest change to the pieces that reproduces the finished weight change, with whatever the pieces cannot express left in Δ. Route two, component training, skips the fine-tune and trains the pieces on the task directly, with Δ and the importance function Γ frozen. The reference, do-nothing, leaves the whole update in Δ. We scored each with the adversarial ratio: how much an adversary that chooses which pieces to switch off can damage the model's output on Pile text, divided by the same on the original model. 1 means no worse than before; 1.5 was set beforehand as the pass line. Pile text holds the trigger only about three times in 131,000 tokens, so this ratio tests damage to the rest of the model, not the new behaviour.

What we found. Component training stayed near 1 through 30 steps; the least-squares split climbed past the pass line by step 2 and to 5.4 by step 10, worse than do-nothing's 2.2 (first figure). Component training also learned the behaviour, with less damage at the other positions of the window (second figure). Weighted by Pile text, about 30% of a fine-tune's update lies outside the pieces on one run each, and at step 10 the behaviour survives without that part (third and fourth figures).

What it means. On this one easy task and one run per arm, training inside the decomposition left its account of the rest of the model intact on Pile text, while translating a finished update did not; why the split is fragile was left unexplained. A review the next day (2026-10-15) drew the caveat: no ratio here says whether the pieces carry the new behaviour, since Pile text barely contains the trigger. The project then built a check local to the trigger and moved to the identity test (E3).

Run
On the emoticon task, one run per arm: the least-squares split of a finished Adam fine-tune at steps 1 to 10, component training first at too large a learning rate (adversarial ratio 41, not counted), then at a matched update size through 30 steps, and the outside share of Adam, SGD and LoRA updates. Five MATS cluster jobs on an L40, at no cost.
Found
  • Component training at matched size learns the behaviour by step 3 with about a third of the fine-tune's damage elsewhere, and its adversarial ratio stays near 1 (at most 1.07) through 30 steps, on this task only.
  • The least-squares split carries the update (run without masks, it closes about nine tenths of the gap do-nothing leaves; report, not plotted) but its adversarial ratio climbs from 1.14 at step 1 to 5.41 at step 10, against do-nothing's 2.18; the cause is unknown.
  • Weighted by Pile text, on one run each, about 30% of a fine-tune lies outside the pieces (the original weights: 13%), with Adam or SGD alike; dropping it keeps the behaviour at step 10, though that part alone also partly carries it.
