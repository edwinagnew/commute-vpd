Do sparse autoencoders trained on the base model alone read the fine-tuned states of the 67M?

2026-11-20 · cell P1.40 run 1 of 2, sae-lead · experiments/sae_train

The question. A sparse autoencoder (SAE) rewrites a layer's activations as a few active latents. For a baseline reading of reuse, SAEs are trained on the base model only and then applied to fine-tuned states: if a fine-tune stays inside the base model's features, the SAEs should still reconstruct it.

What we did. We trained one 16,384-wide top-k SAE (k 80, 500M Pile tokens, the SAEBench recipe) at each of the 13 residual, attention-output and MLP-output sites of the 67M, plus a second seed at one site. We then read the fraction of variance explained (FVE), the dead-latent share and the loss when the SAE is spliced in, on the base and on 74 changed models (do-nothing, six known-answer items, 68 trained states).

What we found. On the base, FVE is 0.875 to 0.995 and spliced-in loss recovery 0.926 to 0.999 (filed at least 0.95: two MLP sites miss). Dead latents reach 0.53 to 0.62 at the four attention sites (filed at most 0.15), a miss, since attention outputs need far fewer than 16k latents. On Pile rows 46 of 75 states lose more than 0.02 FVE somewhere; the ten-fold copy edit at one residual site loses 16, because 1% of positions hold 99.6% of the error.

What it means. The base-only SAEs are good enough to read most states, and the do-nothing reads a drop of exactly 0. The large outlier is a real reading of the ten-fold neurons' huge writes, not a bug (fp32 reads the same). The second run, E245, reads the built items at these SAEs.
