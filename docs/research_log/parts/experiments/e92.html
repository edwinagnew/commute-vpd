What do three cheap readings say beside each Qwen row of the new-against-existing comparison?

2026-11-05 · cluster L40 planned, $0 · experiments/qwen_comparators · coded, not run

The question. The Qwen comparison (D3) asks whether a decomposition can tell an update that reuses what the model has from one that adds something new. Without a cheap reading beside each row, a separation could be one that size alone or a steering vector already gives.

What we did. Nothing is run yet. On any saved Qwen state we will read three things: how large the update is, how highly the unchanged model already ranks the answer the update trains, and how much of the gain one steering vector recovers (a random vector of the same norm is the control). Anchor 1 is read on the held-out problems of the earlier rows, anchor 2 on the TOFU rows of E86 (trained rows), and the maths arms on gold solutions, not the model's own greedy answers, because those rank first for the unchanged model by construction.

What we found. Nothing yet. The filed expectations: the control state raises its own tokens' rank to 1, one steering vector recovers 0.05 to 0.4 of its gain and a random one under 0.05, and the zero state reads the unchanged model.

What it means. A D3 row whose separation a cheap reading matches at the matched step says less than its number suggests, and a row none matches is what the write-up leans on. Anchor 2's steering share at a size-matched state may be undefined because that state has learned nothing (E91).

Three cluster L40 jobs in debug, $0, 4, 15 and 26 minutes, planned; the control (anchor 1's zero state and full update) runs first and alone.
