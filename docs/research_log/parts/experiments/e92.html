What do three cheap readings say beside each Qwen row of the new-against-existing comparison?

2026-11-05 · cluster L40 debug, $0 · experiments/qwen_comparators · provisional, no independent check; Amendment 1 ran, its write-up to come

The question. The Qwen comparison (D3) asks whether a decomposition can tell an update that reuses what the model has from one that adds something new. Without a cheap reading beside each row, a separation could be one that size alone or a steering vector already gives.

What we did. On saved Qwen states we read three things: how large the update is, how highly the unchanged model already ranks the answer the update trains, and how much of the gain one steering vector recovers (a random vector of the same norm is the control). Anchor 1, the answer-format template update, is read on held-out problems; anchor 2, the facts update, on its trained rows, at matched size and in full; the maths arms on gold solutions, not the model's own greedy answers.

What we found. One steering vector recovers 0.83 of the facts update's gain at the template's size and 0.065 at its own full size, on both seeds, against the template's 0.185 at a norm of 0.487, so the comparison changes direction with the step. At matched size the facts update gains about 2 nats on tokens the unchanged model rated very low, without ranking more right tokens first. Eight of the filed leans missed, among them the matched share and the matched gain, and the first-token anomaly is real.

What it means. The steering share is a comparator with its step and size beside it, not the definition of new against reuse, and a gain at matched size is not evidence of learning. Every D3 cell of the comparator table is a gap until qwen-decomp-lead reads the decomposition's rows. Amendment 1 asks whether the matched share reads a flatter output distribution or a shift toward a style; it ran, and its write-up is to come.
