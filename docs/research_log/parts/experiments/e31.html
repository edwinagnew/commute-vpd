Does the adjoint split keep a plain-SGD fine-tune's behaviour on other seeds and tasks?

2026-10-22 · MATS cluster L40; amendments 2026-10-23 and 2026-10-26, RunPod L40S and H100, MATS cluster L40 · experiments/adjoint_sgd

The question. The weak version (Q1) asks for a simple map that carries a fine-tune's weight change onto the decomposition's pieces, keeps the fine-tune's behaviour, and leaves the decomposition as faithful as before. In E22 one candidate, the adjoint split, kept much more of a plain-SGD fine-tune's behaviour than of an Adam one's, on one task and one seed. That made the optimiser a live question (Q11). This run asked whether the SGD result repeats on a second seed and on other tasks.

What we did. We fine-tuned the 67M model on three tasks: emoticon (after the first character of an emoticon, predict o), and two word tasks, ` which` then ` was` and ` it` then ` is`. Each used plain SGD at rate 0.01 or 0.03 and Adam at a small (3e-6) or large (3e-5) rate. At steps 10, 100 and 1,000 we moved each update onto the pieces with the adjoint split, leaving the residual Δ fixed. It moves each piece's read and write vectors along the update, then multiplies each matrix's move by the one number that best matches the update on real inputs, the fitted scale. Keep is the resulting model's held-out probability of the target over the fine-tune's own: 1 is all of the behaviour, 0 none. Do-nothing, the reference, leaves the whole update in Δ and keeps 1. Faithfulness is the adversarial ratio: the damage an attack on the pieces' masks finds on Pile text, over the same at the original model. Its lines come from the original model's own seed-to-seed spread on each GPU. On the L40 a ratio below 1.24 passes and one above 1.81 fails; between is inconclusive. The H100 pass line is 1.40.

What we found. On emoticon the split kept about 0.6 of the slower SGD fine-tune on both seeds, against about 0.2 for small Adam; on the word tasks it kept much less (first figure). Within each task keep falls as the update grows, and at one update size the tasks still differ, 0.60 against 0.13 (second figure). Every adjoint state passed the adversarial check (third figure). On emoticon's first seed, the only one tried, the split without the fitted scale keeps the whole behaviour but changes the model's other predictions more than the fine-tune itself does (fourth figure).

What it means. The adjoint split of an SGD fine-tune does not meet the weak version in general: it keeps behaviour only while the update is small, by a margin that depends on the task. On that one task and seed, the falling keep is in large part an effect of the fitted scale, which shrinks the move to stay harmless: the scale trades behaviour for harmlessness, and neither version both keeps the behaviour and leaves the rest of the model alone. The open test is a scale between the fitted one and 1; the project moved on to teacher training (E34) as its map.

Run
Fine-tunes of all 24 decomposed matrices for 1,000 steps on three tasks, two seeds for each fine-tune except large Adam on the word tasks, each scored at steps 10, 100 and 1,000 under do-nothing and the adjoint split; controls (the original model at its known values, repeats of earlier rows) passed in every job. Cluster L40 debug jobs ($0), three RunPod L40S jobs ($1.01) and one RunPod H100 job for the unscaled split ($1.97). The unscaled split was scored on emoticon's first seed only.
Found
  • On emoticon the split keeps about 0.6 of the slower SGD fine-tune's behaviour on both seeds, against about 0.2 for small Adam, as expected; on the word tasks the faster SGD fine-tune kept about what large Adam does, where at least half had been predicted.
  • Within a task keep falls as the SGD update grows, on both seeds; at one size the tasks differ, so size is not the whole story.
  • Every adjoint state passes the adversarial check; on emoticon's first seed, dropping the fitted scale keeps the whole behaviour but changes the rest of the model 1.3 to 32 times as much as the fine-tune does.

Moved on
The adjoint split of an SGD fine-tune meets the weak version on emoticon only, so the two-lines decision did not move, and the scaled split is now described as fitted to each update; a size-matched test followed in E47 (its T3). A scale between the fitted one and 1 was left as the next test if taken up. Report: reports/analyses/2026-10-22_analysis_adjoint-sgd.md (with Amendments 1 to 3); no notebook. The cut figures stay there: kept behaviour against the reproduced share (the share of the update's energy the split reproduces, which tracks training length and does not explain the SGD advantage), the longer-attack sweep (the pass holds at 160 attack steps), and the four-panel steps and adversarial plots. Not run: a scale between the fitted one and 1, KL elsewhere on Pile text, a third seed, sizes beyond 0.03, other task families, SGD with momentum, other models.
