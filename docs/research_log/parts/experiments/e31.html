Does the adjoint split of a plain-SGD fine-tune keep the behaviour beyond one task?

2026-10-22 · MATS cluster L40; amendment 2026-10-23, RunPod L40S · experiments/adjoint_sgd

On emoticon yes, on two other tasks no. The adjoint split of an SGD fine-tune keeps most of the emoticon behaviour on a second seed, where the small Adam fine-tune's loses it, but on two word tasks it loses the behaviour as fast as the large Adam fine-tune's (first figure). Every adjoint state stays as faithful as the original model with Δ fixed (third figure). The share of the update the split reproduces does not explain the emoticon difference (second figure). An amendment swapped the rates between the tasks (fourth figure): the split keeps less of an SGD fine-tune's behaviour the larger the update grows, and at one update size the tasks still differ widely.

Run
Emoticon fine-tunes of all 24 decomposed matrices for 1,000 steps on two seeds: SGD at rate 0.01, Adam at 3e-6 (small) and at 3e-5 (large). On r1_t534_v369 (" which" then " was") and r0_t352_v310 (" it" then " is"): SGD at the rate a step-10 rule picked, 0.03 on both because 0.01 learned too little by step 10, and Adam 3e-5. Each update at steps 10, 100 and 1,000 placed by do-nothing and the scaled adjoint split, read on held-out probability of the target, the adversarial ratio (pass up to 1.24 on this card), Δ's share of the update and the reproduced share. A budget sweep to 160 adversary steps on the step-1,000 emoticon states. Controls passed: the original model at its known values, the seed-0 states matched the earlier emoticon run within 0.05, the large update's reproduced share matched the LoRA split study's (0.58, 0.42, 0.31). Six debug jobs, one a rerun of a crashed shard, $0. Its controls passed: the original model at its known values, and each 10-step fine-tune within 0.01 of the first run.
