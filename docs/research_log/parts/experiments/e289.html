Does the crosscoder pass its planted controls once latents with a negative base coefficient are excluded?

2026-11-23 · cell P2.70, compare-lead · experiments/crosscoder_proper (refit), experiments/crosscoder (rescore) · one H100 pod, $5.7

The question. Minder et al. 2025 count a latent as fine-tune-specific only if its base-model coefficient is not negative. Our port left that step out. Do the planted controls still pass with it, and was our coefficient fit too coarse?

What we did. We rescored all 96 saved crosscoder readings with the exclusion (the old reading reproduces every saved part to 3e-8), then refitted the coefficients of the ten planted controls on 50M tokens, the paper's count, instead of 100,000.

What we found. No planted control passes. The block-3 head reads 0.44, 0.43 and 0.42 (was 0.56, 0.63, 0.52) against a bar of 0.5, the real-text plant 0 (was 0.77), the ladder's top sizes at most 0.010. All ten planted latents' negative coefficients are real, a few thousandths of the fine-tune's. Fine-tune and random readings move by at most 0.022.

What it means. The crosscoder stays unusable at fine-tune size, and the draft's block-3 and real-text passes are reversed: the earlier passes were a porting error. On the ladder and real text, the authors' rule as published removes the planted change's own latents; at block 3 it removes latents with clearly negative base coefficients. Redteam: OK.
