Does training a LoRA much longer bring its update inside the pieces?

2026-10-19 · RunPod L40S · experiments/lora_plateau

The question. A LoRA is a low-rank update: a read factor A, which starts random, times a write factor B, which starts at zero. In E8 the LoRA learned its task while A was still near its random start, so its update fitted the pieces no better than a random one. If A only needed more steps to take the data's shape, a LoRA trained to its plateau might become something the pieces can carry. That bears on Q3, whether a LoRA is easier to map than a full fine-tune, and on Q1, whether a simple map stays faithful without parking the update in the residual Δ.

What we did. We trained E8's LoRA (rank 8 on all 24 weight matrices, Adam, one seed) on the emoticon task, predicting o after the first character of an emoticon, ten times longer than in E8, to 1,000 steps. At nine steps we measured the outside share: the part of the update's energy on real inputs that no small change of the pieces can produce. We divide it by a random update's share, so 1 means no better fitted than random. At steps 10, 100 and 1,000 we mapped the update with the least-squares split: the pieces take the smallest change that reproduces what they can, and the rest goes to Δ. Do-nothing puts the whole update in Δ. Each state got two scores. The adversarial ratio is the damage an adversary masking pieces does on Pile text, over its damage to the unchanged decomposition. 1 is no worse, 2 or less passes, 5 or more fails. The behaviour left in Δ is the share of the task's gain that comes through Δ instead of the pieces; 0.2 or less passes. The split passes only if both pass.

What we found. More training did not help: at every step the share of the update outside the pieces stayed within a fifth of a random update's (first figure); the report adds that A's cosine with its random start only fell to 0.90. The report finds its energy moved onto two layer-3 attention matrices the pieces read worst, and the share of the whole update outside the pieces rose to 62% (second figure). The split passed the adversarial check at all three steps, far under its line of 2 (third figure). But at step 1,000 it left 0.31 of the behaviour in Δ, over the 0.2 line, so it fails there on the Δ half (fourth figure).

What it means. Training longer did not make the LoRA's update fit the pieces, so the report closed the idea that more steps would make A take the data's shape, on one seed, one rank (8) and one task (emoticon). The split passes the adversarial half of the check at every step but fails the Δ half at step 1,000; the share of weights left in Δ grew at every step, while the behaviour left there went 0.19, 0.11, 0.31, and with one seed that last rise has no spread. Next the LoRA and a full fine-tune were split side by side on one card (E14, E16); a fine-tune's update fits the pieces better, as E8's 0.50 in the first figure already suggests.

Run
E8's rank-8 LoRA retrained on emoticon to 1,000 steps, one seed, with the outside share at nine checkpoints and the do-nothing and split states scored on Pile at steps 10, 100 and 1,000. One RunPod L40S job of 31 minutes ($0.70) after a $0.05 smoke run.
Found
  • The share of the update outside the pieces stayed within a fifth of a random update's for the whole run, as predicted, and A's cosine with its start fell only to 0.90.
  • The split passed the adversarial check at every step, far under the line of 2, but sat slightly above do-nothing at every step, on one seed.
  • Unexpectedly, the behaviour the split leaves in Δ rose to 0.31 at the plateau, so the prediction that the split would pass both halves at step 1,000 missed on the Δ half.

Moved on
"More steps make the read side data-shaped" is closed, the fine-tune control on one card ran in E14, and the seed spread of the behaviour in Δ at the plateau is still open. Trained with plain SGD (E27), A also stays at its start and the outside share sits between noise and a full fine-tune (0.33 to 0.87 of the null, against 0.82 to 1.02 here), so the near-random fit is the LoRA's form and its level depends on the optimiser.
