Do the neurons that carry a fine-tune have an original job on the Pile that names the task?

2026-11-05 · cluster L40 debug, $0 · experiments/carrying_jobs · provisional, not independently checked

The question. If a fine-tune built to reuse an ability is carried by neurons whose old job already relates to the task, and one built to teach something new is not, then a neuron's job is a reuse reader that needs no decomposition. E98 found which neurons of the original model carry each of 43 saved fine-tunes of the 67M; this asks what those neurons did before training, read the way the "already clean" essay reads neurons.

What we did. For each carrying neuron we wrote a one-line job from the Pile windows where it fires hardest, and asked a judge whether that job relates to the task. The same was done for a random partner neuron matched by layer (the floor), and for the ten neurons of the original model that fire hardest at each task's answer on three tasks (the positive control, which the method should tie to the task if it can tie anything). Amendment 1 re-judged every neuron with a judge shown four of each task's own windows, and replaced the control by the ten neurons whose firing at the answer most exceeds their firing on other tasks.

What we found. Almost no carrying neuron's job was tied to its task, for tasks built to reuse and tasks built to be new alike (lowercase 0 of 16, against a floor of 1 of 179). The control failed twice: 0 of 10 on each of three tasks under the first judge, and 0 of 30 under the amended judge, against random floors of 1 to 3%. The task-specific neurons turned out to be formatting neurons, whose Pile jobs are hyphen runs, line-start hashes and word fragments, never the answer.

What it means. On the 67M this reading cannot label a fine-tune as reuse or new, so GOALS.md gets no answer from it either way, and the route is closed there. Either the judge cannot see a task role in a one-line job, or these synthetic tasks have none that a Pile job names. The next test is on Qwen3-1.7B (E102), where tasks are natural language.

Found
  • The judge tied almost none of the carrying neurons' Pile jobs to their task, on built-to-reuse and built-to-be-new tasks alike.
  • The positive control failed under both judges (0 of 10 per task, then 0 of 30), though the amended judge loosened (21 of 180 random neurons tied to some task, up from 3).
  • So the essay-style job reading has no power on this model, and the reuse-against-new rows were not read.
