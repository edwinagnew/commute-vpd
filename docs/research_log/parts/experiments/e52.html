Can neuron patching, steering or prompting, which act on the running model and read no decomposition, tell a fine-tune that brings out copying from one that teaches a random word map?

2026-10-29 · MATS cluster L40, debug QoS, job 44095, $0 · experiments/baseline_neurons

The question. A reviewer would ask whether a cheap method that acts on the model's activations could say the same as the decomposition's route split, which read copying as bringing out something the model already does and a random word map as teaching something new (E12). These are baselines B2 (neuron patching), B3 (steering) and B4 (prompting) in the baselines plan, chosen by the user after E51's weight statistics failed to separate the two kinds. Prompting also doubles as a check on the labels: if copying really is something the original model already does, showing it examples should draw it out more than it draws out the map.

What we did. We took E51's eight fine-tunes of the 67M model, four that teach copying a key and four that teach a random map from key to word (two tables, two seeds each), and the code fine-tune that learned nothing. Each fine-tune's gain is how much more likely it makes the right answer, in nats of log probability on 97 held-out windows. A method's share is the part of that gain it recovers on 48 windows it did not fit: 0 is the original model, 1 the fine-tuned one. Neuron patching copies the fine-tuned model's activations into the original at the k most-shifted of the 12,288 hidden neurons. Steering adds one fixed vector, the mean shift of the residual stream at the answer position, to the original model. Prompting gives the original model 0, 4 or 16 worked examples first. A kind separates if the gap between the kind means beats the larger spread within a kind, filed before the run.

What we found. Prompting came out reversed and near zero: 16 examples recover 0.11 of the copy gain and 0.14 of the map gain (first figure), so by the plan's tree it stopped for the user. One added steering vector separates the kinds by a wide margin, 0.77 of the copy gain against 0.14 of the map's (second figure). No small set of neurons carries either: half the gain is reached only with all 12,288 neurons patched, and at 256 neurons every share is at most 0.24 (third figure). The update that learned nothing reads -0.051 against a 0.05 floor, a hair past the line.

What it means. The label check failed in the direction opposite to the labels, but both numbers are near zero, the original model gives the copy target about 1e-4 even after 16 examples, and the prompt format is weak, so it shows the labels were never verified by behaviour, not that they are wrong. Steering's separation may hold by construction: one mean vector cannot depend on the input, copying is one mode switch, and the map's answer depends on the key. So steering may separate any key-independent change from any key-dependent one, and so may the route split. The user is asked how to proceed (section {{n:decisions}}); the reports recommend a fairer prompting check, then a key-dependent pair the original model already half-does.
