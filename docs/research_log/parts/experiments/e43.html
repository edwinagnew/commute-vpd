After teacher training, can the rest of the fine-tune go into Δ, so the weights are exactly the fine-tuned model's?

2026-10-28 · RunPod H100 · experiments/teacher_training

The question. Teacher training is the commute line's map (Q1): it trains the decomposition's pieces, with Γ and the residual Δ frozen, until the decomposition predicts what the fine-tuned model predicts (E34). Its weights then end close to the fine-tuned model's but not equal to them. Call the part it did not reproduce the remainder. We asked whether adding the remainder to Δ, which makes the weights exactly the fine-tuned model's, keeps the decomposition as faithful as teacher training alone.

What we did. We took E41's emoticon fine-tune (it teaches the model to follow an emoticon with the letter o), stopped at steps 10 and 1,000, and its teacher-trained state. We compared four ways of writing that fine-tune in the decomposition. Doing nothing puts the whole weight change in Δ. The least-squares split divides the change among the pieces and puts what they cannot carry in Δ. Teacher training leaves Δ untouched. Teacher training plus the remainder adds the fine-tuned weights minus teacher training's to Δ. Faithfulness is read by the adversarial ratio. An adversary searches for the masks over the pieces and Δ that do the most damage on ordinary Pile text; the ratio divides that damage by what it does to the unchanged decomposition. 1 means no change. One adversary start finds a little more or less damage each time it runs: at the unchanged decomposition its reading moves by about 7.5% across starts. A ratio a little under 1 is therefore noise, and it counts as a pass. The pass and fail lines are set from that spread, measured afresh on each GPU: here 1.40 or less passes, 1.61 or more fails, and in between is inconclusive.

What we found. Adding the remainder fails the check at both steps, at one adversary start (first figure). It reads 2.5 and 1.9 times teacher training's own reading; at step 1,000 that reading is 0.96, a pass within noise of 1. In this run it also reads above doing nothing at both steps. Two states with the same weights can read differently because the adversary can switch Δ off as well as pieces, so where the update sits matters. On task text the remainder carries almost none of the fine-tune's behaviour; on Pile text it carries a quarter to two-fifths, the gap teacher training already leaves there (second figure). So the remainder puts real Pile-text behaviour into Δ, where the adversary can mask it. The report does not test whether that is what raises the adversarial ratio in the first figure, since which part of the remainder the adversary uses was not measured. At step 10 the remainder is five times the fine-tune's squared size and mostly undoes teacher training's own change (third figure).

What it means. On this one fine-tune and seed, exact weights reached this way cost faithfulness. Teacher training with Δ untouched therefore stays the default map, and the remainder in this form is closed. A later re-read at three adversary starts (E47) repeated the step-1,000 fail. At step 10 it did not show the state worse than doing nothing, so "above doing nothing" holds only at step 1,000. The criterion forbids Δ absorbing the update, not every change to Δ (decisions.md 2026-10-29), so a different, smaller change to Δ is not ruled out.

Run
E41's emoticon fine-tune and its teacher-trained state (Adam, 1,000 student steps) were rebuilt, and the four states were read on the same Pile rows and task windows at steps 10 and 1,000, controls first, all passing. One RunPod H100 job of about 12 minutes, about $2.5.
