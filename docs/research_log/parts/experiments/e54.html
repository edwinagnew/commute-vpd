Does the route split's re-gating share read a key-to-value map the original 67M model already half-knows as "elicited", and a cipher on the same keys as not, where steering by one mean vector should fail on both?

2026-10-30 · MATS cluster L40, $0 (jobs 45963, 45973, 45974) · experiments/keydependent_elicit

The question. The re-gating line needs a reading that tells a fine-tune that elicits something the original model already does from one that teaches something new. E52 found that one added steering vector separates a copy task from a map, perhaps only because copying does not depend on the key. The user chose (2026-10-30) a pair where both tasks depend on the key.

What we did. Each of 32 keys (common word-like tokens) gets as value the word the original model itself ranks 2nd to 20th as its next token after the key, so the map is one it already follows weakly; the cipher uses the same keys with values the model gives almost no probability. Both were fine-tuned on E12's recipe (two seeds), the elicit map at its step 10 and the cipher at the step whose update has the same size (step 9; also at its own step 30). Each was read two ways: the re-gating share r (the share of the gain that is recovered when no subcomponent changes and only Γ's switching does, E46) and the steering share S (the share recovered by adding one mean vector to the original model, E52). The first job's early check stopped on the elicit rate (1.7e-3 against a floor of 5e-3, an averaging gap); the user lowered the floor to 1e-3 after seeing the value, and the same keys ran.

What we found. The re-gating share separates the pair: 0.77 and 0.76 for the elicit map, 0.24 and 0.21 for the matched cipher, a gap of 0.54 (figure 1), as predicted. One steering vector recovered 0.38 and 0.39 of the elicit map against 0.23 and 0.18 for the cipher; the elicit map sits above the filed cap of 0.3, so the steering prediction fails as filed (figure 2). The decomposition still separates the pair more clearly than steering in every comparison, by a margin that depends on the units and on which cipher checkpoint is read, from about 1.7 times to over 10 times. The vector moves both tasks by about 2.5 nats. The cipher's r moves by only 0.02 to 0.03 between its matched step and its own (figure 3), as predicted.

What it means. On a pair built so that one vector should fail on both tasks, r still separates the half-known map from the cipher, at the same gap as E12's copy and cipher, so r on E12 was not reading only whether the answer depends on the key. Steering is not blind here, so "the decomposition reads a kind steering cannot" is too strong. The supported claim is a direction: the decomposition separates this pair more clearly than one mean vector does, with no single size for the margin. Both halves of the steering miss depend on choices: the cap miss is mostly the nats unit, and "steering does not separate" rests on the part-learned matched cipher (held-out probability 0.23; at its own step the steering gap would clear the bar). Caveats: the label is the original model's behaviour, not a check of its internal representation, a high r still means "built from pieces the original model has", and this is one small model. Provisionally (the user's reading) the next tests are the same pair on Qwen and the Jain et al. readout check on the keys.
