Does the share in its KL form read a fine-tune differently from a random change of the same output size?

2026-11-21 · cell P2.44 (filled), no independent check · experiments/random_kl_share

The question. On Qwen the share in its KL form read 0.99 or more on fine-tunes and on random changes scaled to the same KL, so the paper's Qwen results do not use it. The 67M results use the log-odds form. Does the KL form fail the same control on the 67M, and can the log-odds form be given a random control?

What we did. On 88 saved fine-tunes (every seed of the nine built pairs and the four shuffled-label twins) we read the share in both forms. The share is the part of a change's effect carried by VPD's subcomponents being switched on differently by the gate function. Beside each fine-tune we read three random weight changes, each scaled so its KL from the original model equals the fine-tune's within 10%, and one control that changes only the last matrix, inside the span of its alive subcomponents, so the gates cannot move and the share must read exactly 0. A second stage re-read the hand-built known-answer edits on whole windows.

What we found. Random changes read 0.97 to 0.99 in the KL form; the fine-tunes read 0.84 to 1.00, and a random change reached the fine-tune's own share on 67 of 88. Only 21 read above all three random changes, all on sides built to use what the original model does. The log-odds form could not be controlled: random changes moved the trained answer by -1.35 to 0.77 log-odds, under the floor of 1 on all 264, so it is undefined for them. The structured control read exactly 0 on all 88, and the saved shares were reproduced. Both forms kept all nine built pairs apart; neither put any reuse edit above any new edit on the known-answer edits.

What it means. A high KL-form share does not tell a fine-tune from a random change, on the 67M as on Qwen, so it is a property of the form, not of the model. The log-odds form the 67M results use still has no random control. This is not a verdict on any fine-tune.

Run
Three RunPod H100 pods, about 23 minutes of reading, about $5; the known-answer stage on one cluster debug job, about 2 minutes, $0.
Found
  • KL form: random changes read 0.97 to 0.99 and reach the fine-tune on 67 of 88; the fine-tune is above all three on 21.
  • Log-odds form: no random change gains the 1 log-odds it needs, so it still has no random control.
  • On the known-answer edits neither form orders reuse above new, in 0 of 6 combinations; the earlier edit readings on altered windows stay invalid, with the neuron reading of the two new edits unchanged.
