On trained pairs, does behaviour read part by part separate the reuse side from the new side?

2026-11-21 · cell P1.51 (filled), definition-lead, descriptive, no independent check · experiments/rb_member_pairs

The question. The write share puts every trained fine-tune near a random update (card E257). If behaviour, read one changed part at a time as in E258, sees reuse that the write share misses, it should put the reuse side of a pair clearly above the new side.

What we did. We ran E258's test on three of the nine task pairs: copy against lookup, copy against a prior-matched cipher, and lowercase against two ciphers, every saved seed, 18 fine-tunes. Each had 2,000 changed parts sampled, with a size-matched random update beside it. A part passes if the swap leaves at most a tenth of its effect.

What we found. Behaviour reads 0.004 to 0.096 on the 18 fine-tunes, at most 0.055 from the write share, and no part passed on any of them, the random update included. With seeds averaged, the reuse side minus the new side reads -0.023 on copy and lookup, 0.037 on copy and the prior-matched cipher and 0.028 on lowercase and the ciphers. The random update reads 0.0075 with a standard error of 0.0020, against 0.011 when every part was read.

What it means. Behaviour and write agree on trained pairs and neither separates the two sides, so the write share's near-random readings are not hiding a behavioural signal. One caveat: half to two thirds of the sampled parts on the copy, lookup and prior-cipher items sit under the noise floor, an estimated 0.10 to 0.25 of the change, and are not counted.
