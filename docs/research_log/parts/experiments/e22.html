E22surprise

Do small fine-tunes stay faithful when they are mapped onto the pieces later in training, not just at step 10?

2026-10-22 · RunPod H100 · experiments/small_finetune_splits

The question. The commute line, sub-question Q1, asks for a simple map that carries a fine-tune onto the decomposition's pieces, its rank-one subcomponents. Γ, the decomposition's gate that decides which pieces are switched on for each input, stays frozen, and the decomposition should stay faithful. Most failures in the earlier split experiments, E14 to E20, came from one large Adam fine-tune read at step 10. The optimiser comparison E21 then found that two small fine-tunes, one SGD and one Adam, pass at step 10. This run asks whether they still pass as training goes on, and whether the optimiser matters, sub-question Q11.

What we did. Three fine-tunes taught the 67M model the emoticon task, one seed, for 1,000 steps: a small SGD one, a small Adam one and the large Adam one. At steps 10, 100 and 1,000 each update was mapped three ways. Do-nothing leaves the whole change in the residual Δ, the part of the weights the pieces do not explain. The least-squares split moves the pieces by the smallest change that reproduces the update and leaves what they cannot express in Δ. The adjoint split moves the pieces along the update, scaled once per matrix, and leaves Δ untouched, so the weights change only by what the pieces carry. Faithfulness is read by the adversarial ratio: the damage a 20-step attack on the pieces' masks finds on Pile text, divided by the same on the unchanged model. It reads 1 for no change. A ratio of 1.40 or below passes and 1.61 or above fails. Between is inconclusive, a band set by the unchanged model's own spread over two attack seeds.

What we found. Both small fine-tunes read like the unchanged model under every map at every step, between 0.98 and 1.10 (first figure). The large Adam fine-tune fails at step 10 under do-nothing and least squares, and its least-squares state fails again at step 1,000. By step 1,000 the SGD update is larger than the large one at step 10 and still passes, so size alone does not decide (second figure). Each map still pays for its pass, as the third figure shows. Least squares leaves a quarter to two-fifths of the change in Δ, and the adjoint split loses behaviour. The surprise is how little SGD loses: its adjoint split held the target's probability at 0.61 by step 1,000, where small Adam fell to 0.24.

What it means. On this task and seed, every fine-tune failure of the frozen-Γ maps comes from the one large Adam update. That update differs from the passing ones in both rate and size, so this run cannot say which matters, only that size alone does not decide. Passing is not the same as working: the adjoint split stays faithful even when the target's probability has fallen to 0.10. Every verdict is a single 20-step attack on one task and one seed. The SGD adjoint result was therefore tested next on more seeds and tasks, in the follow-up E31.

Run
Full fine-tunes of all 24 decomposed matrices on emoticon, seed 0: SGD at rate 0.01 and Adam at 3e-6, the small pair matched in size in E21, and Adam at 3e-5, the large one; each mapped three ways at steps 10, 100 and 1,000 and read on 256 Pile rows, with the update's own shares read at nine checkpoints. One RunPod H100 job, 38 minutes of run, $4.55; the unchanged model and both earlier runs' step-10 and series values reproduced first.
Found
  • Both small fine-tunes pass under every map at every step, as expected, including the SGD one at step 1,000 when its update was larger than the failing large one's at step 10.
  • No map keeps everything: least squares leaves 0.25 to 0.39 of the change in Δ, and the adjoint split loses behaviour along training for both Adam fine-tunes, as filed.
  • Surprise: the SGD adjoint split did not lose its behaviour step by step as filed; it rose to 0.75, then ended at 0.61, against small Adam's 0.24. The cause was not worked out.
