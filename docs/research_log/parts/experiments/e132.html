Does the re-gating share read the same on Goodfire's second Qwen3-1.7B decomposition as on the first?

2026-11-09 · provisional, no independent check · cluster debug L40, job 56867, about 85 minutes, $0 · experiments/qwen_regating_new

Since then. E144 read the share in log-odds and could not compute it, since every update lowered the trained answer's log-odds; the user then stopped Qwen re-gating.

The question. Part 3 repeats the key findings on Qwen3-1.7B, and the reference decomposition has moved from the old one, p-e9fcffd5, to the new one, p-9707d6ff. The re-gating share is the part of an update's gain in the answer's log-odds that comes from changing only which original subcomponents the decomposition switches on. Their weights stay fixed. This run asks whether the share reads the same on the new decomposition as on the old.

What we did. We read the share on six saved Qwen3-1.7B states, on both decompositions, over 128 maths rows. Four are updates: the step-10 fine-tune, the gold update, and two seeds of the second anchor. Two are random changes, one Gaussian and one confined to the live columns, matched in size to the step-10 fine-tune. The random changes and the do-nothing reference ran first, and every control held. Sides are labelled as built; no verdict on reuse.

What we found. All six states read 0.991 to 0.9992 on the new decomposition, against about 0.993 to 0.9995 on the old. The two anchor seeds differ by 0.0020. The step-10 fine-tune and gold separate from both anchor seeds, as on the old decomposition, but not from each other. All four updates separate from both random changes on the new decomposition; on the old only the fine-tune and gold did.

What it means. The reading carries over, but it is squeezed into a band under 0.01 wide. The two random changes, which were not trained to teach anything, also read near the ceiling, at 0.991 and 0.992. Whether a share this close to 1 can say anything about what an update taught is open. The advisory premise check judged it doubtful, and the separations rest on the spread of a single seed pair.

Run
One debug job on the L40 read the share for six saved states on both decompositions, random changes first, in about 85 minutes at $0.
Found
  • All six states read 0.991 to 0.9992 on the new decomposition and about 0.993 to 0.9995 on the old, as expected.
  • Updates separate from random changes on the new decomposition, by gaps under 0.009; the fine-tune and gold are indistinguishable from each other.
  • Random changes read near the ceiling too, so the scale is compressed and what the separation means is open.
