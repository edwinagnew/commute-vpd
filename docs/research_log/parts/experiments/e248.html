Does the reuse score under wording A put the reuse side above the new side on real fine-tunes?

2026-11-21 · cell P1.48, definition-lead, descriptive, no independent check · experiments/kstar_vpd_wording_a

The question. Under wording A, the reuse score R is the share of the circuit's variables that the base model's mechanisms can stand in for, each scaled by one fitted output gain, while keeping the fine-tune's output within a tenth of its distance from the base model. On six hand-built edits it failed its test (E242): a thin new edit read 0.98, above the reuse edits, because 0.89 of its circuit was untouched and counted as reused (E247). Do real trained fine-tunes show the same leniency? You asked for the readings as descriptions, with no verdict at a bar.

What we did. On the 67M model we read R at the VPD level on 94 trained fine-tunes: nine task pairs built to reuse or to be new (the larger update scaled down to the other's size), copy against lookup, and five key tasks at one common update size, two to eight seeds each. For every item we also read the share of the circuit the update never touched, and R among changed variables only.

What we found. In all nine pairs the side built to reuse reads higher, by 0.11 to 0.32; the old squared-cosine R orders only four of the nine. Copy reads 0.98 over lookup 0.90, and the key tasks fall copy 0.98, elicit 0.89, rank-1 0.88, prior cipher 0.75, cipher 0.65. No trained fine-tune leaves more than 0.0001 of its circuit untouched, so setting untouched parts aside moves nothing, while the thin built edit drops from 0.98 to 0.82.

What it means. These readings are descriptive, and wording A failed its built-edit test (E242), so they do not show that it tells reuse from new. An independent premise check doubts that the 9-of-9 order shows reuse against new: the score may track how much was changed, and a measure that could not call the built new edits new cannot be shown to label trained pairs by this order (check: ~/commute-data/premise/P1.48.md). The untouched-parts route does not reach trained fine-tunes; whether parts changed only a little count as reused for free is untested, and every new-side search hit its cap of 2,000 evaluations, so those scores are lower bounds.

Run
Ten RunPod H100 pods across three runners, about $120, running E242's code unchanged on 94 items plus the side readings from the saved files.
Found
  • Reuse side above new side in 9 of 9 pairs (gaps 0.11 to 0.32, old R 4 of 9); the lowest reuse seed sits above the highest new seed by at least 0.08.
  • Untouched share at most 0.0001 on every trained item, so the order is the same among changed parts only; small-change leniency is untested.
  • Every new-side search was capped (and the reuse side in four pairs), so scores are lower bounds; a premise check doubts the order shows reuse against new.
