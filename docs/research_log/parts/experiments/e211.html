Does the re-gating share, or any cheaper reading, still separate the sides of real-text pairs at matched update size?

2026-11-18 · cell P2.38, run 2 of 3, no independent check · experiments/realistic_tasks

The question. The share separated all nine built pairs on the 67M, but those tasks are synthetic. Run 1 (E209) kept three real-text pairs; does the share, or any cheaper reading, tell the side built to need what the original model holds from the side built to need something it lacks?

What we did. We fine-tuned both sides of each pair, three seeds a side, and saved each at the first step it reached two update sizes (the change's norm over the original weights' norm), matched within each pair: topic label against a coin label on the same news texts, French against Yoruba Wikipedia, and lowercase against reversed word order. Every scorecard method was read on each save. The share is the fraction of the gain still present when the change is passed through the released decomposition's subcomponents; steering, the top-64 neuron reading, effective rank and the original model's prior are the cheaper readings. A cell reads "apart" when one side's three seeds all sit above or all below the other's.

What we found. The share read apart only on lowercase against reversed order, higher on lowercase at both sizes (gaps 0.29 and 0.21). On topic against coin it was level at the larger size (gap 0.00), and at the smaller size it could not be read, because the coin side gained under 1 log-odds. French against Yoruba was unreadable for every share-like method, since French gained 0.42 to 0.89 log-odds against a floor of 1. No cheaper reading agreed with the share: steering split two pairs in opposite directions, and effective rank was higher on the topic side on the label pair, against its filed direction. Controls passed (the apparatus matched saved readings to 0.002, a zero update read exactly 0).

What it means. The share's separation of the built pairs carried to one of three real-text pairs and says nothing on another; no cheaper reading does better. Nothing here is a verdict on which side reuses and which is new; the sides are as built. Run 3 (E212, 2026-11-18, below) tried the crosscoder on the same updates and stopped at its first control: a random weight change read as more shared than any trained update, so the crosscoder is not read on real text.

Run 3 (E212): can a crosscoder tell a random update from a trained one? A crosscoder (Minder et al. 2025) is one dictionary of sparse latents that rebuilds the original and the updated model's activations together; its reading is the share of the activation change carried by latents both models share, minus the share carried by latents only the update has. Before reading any real fine-tune we trained it on two controls. A random weight change the size of the French fine-tune read 0.730 on the French rows, over the filed stop bar of 0.45 (0.59 to 1.00 on the other sides' rows), where the trained sides of the built pairs had read 0.39 to 0.46. The planted control passed (fine-tune-only part 0.766 against 0.5), so the method finds a change built to be found but not which latents carry a fine-tune. The six real-text sides were not run; the crosscoder's scorecard column reads "failed its do-nothing control", and there is no further crosscoder run.
