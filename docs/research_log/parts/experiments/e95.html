Does "reuses token identity" say more than "the state needs the input token"?

2026-11-05 · cluster L40 debug, $0 · experiments/token_identity · provisional, no independent check

The question. In E90 the 32-key copy map lost more of its gain than a random map when the original model's token-reading directions were removed at the first block. An independent check asked whether that only shows the state needs the input token, since the hand-built comparison read its key upstream of the removal and was spared by placement.

What we did. We built a key reader by hand, placed one block downstream of the removal, so it must read the token through the edited signal, and measured the share of its gain lost. We also read two further tables of 32 keys that share none with the first pair, with the same rule and the same random removals matched in variance, and ran a paired test of copy against random.

What we found. The hand-built reader loses 0.74 to 0.77 of its gain, more than the copy map's 0.50 to 0.53 or the random map's 0.36 to 0.37. On the new keys the copy map loses 0.51, over the filed band of 0.43, and the random map 0.27 to 0.28, under it, with copy above random on every seed pair against random removals. The filed outcome on the hand-built reader is "no call", and its filed sentence reads backwards against the signs: every state loses less than the write.

What it means. A loss under this removal is not shown to mean more than needing the input token, so "reuses token identity" drops to "needs the input token after the first block" unless a located mechanism supports more. Copy over random now holds on two pairs of tables, on a narrow control and with its cause unmeasured; a circuit built from old parts reads as reuse. The label's wording is the pending question in section {{n:decisions}}, and nothing further is run on the old tasks until you decide.
