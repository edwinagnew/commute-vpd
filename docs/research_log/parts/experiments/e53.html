Does the stand-in ratio tell a behaviour the original 67M model already performs (lowercase) from one it cannot (a word cipher), fine-tuned on the same rows?

2026-10-29 to 2026-10-30 · MATS cluster L40, $0 (first check on a RunPod 4090, $0.08) · experiments/performed_anchor

The question. The re-gating line's one number that might tell an existing behaviour's fine-tune from a new one's is the stand-in ratio, and it had been read only on toys the original model does not perform at all (E46, E50). The user chose (2026-10-29) to read it on a pair the original model really sorts that way: continuing lowercase Pile text in lowercase (performed), against a fixed swap of 50 common words (not performed).

What we did. First the original model alone: it prefers the lowercase form in lowercased text by 1.18 nats and the real word over the swapped one by 5.53, so the pair passed its go rule. Then we fine-tuned each task on the same 2,400 rows (Adam, 3e-5, 300 steps; two batch orders), split each finished update over the subcomponents by least squares, and read the stand-in ratio. That is the gain in the model rebuilt from the subcomponents under Γ's new masks, over the gain from the moved subcomponents alone. Near 1 means Γ's new switching explains the gain; the toys' copies read 0.7 to 0.8 and random maps 0.4. Controls were a KL-matched random update and a hand-made write that builds no mechanism. Because lowercase is also the broader update, a second cipher swapped 500 words (twice the first one's breadth) was fine-tuned and read the same way (Amendment 1b); breadth is the gain per nat of change at other positions, high for a targeted update.

What we found. The ratio separates the pair: lowercase reads 0.92 and 0.93 (the two batch orders), the 50-word cipher 0.26 on both, a gap of 0.65 against the 0.3 bar. The cipher stays low at the saved states that bracket lowercase's gain (0.28 to 0.38 against 0.92). The 500-word cipher was still narrower than lowercase (7.8 against 4.5 to 4.9 gain per nat of change elsewhere; the first cipher read 15), so the breadth test is unscored; provisionally it read 0.27 to 0.29 at step 300, at most about 0.07 above the first cipher at matched gain, and 0.47 at lowercase's gain. The relay number does not separate the pair (0.66 against 0.41 to 0.42), and the hand-write check failed to reach its gain.

What it means. This is the first pair where the ratio separates a performed behaviour from a new one, but it is only one pair, and lowercase is also the broader update. Doubling the cipher's breadth moved its ratio by a tenth of the gap, which tilts against "the ratio reads breadth" over that range, but no update has yet reached lowercase's breadth, and both low readings are word swaps, so breadth, task family and kind are still not separated. One caveat on lowercase's breadth: positions the lowercasing changed but that are not scored count as off-target and inflate its off-target change, so lowercase is probably narrower than 4.5 to 4.9 and the bar was harder to pass than meant (by how much is not measured). A high reading still means only "uses pieces the original model has". Next: re-read lowercase with those positions left out ($0, minutes), or a still broader cipher; Qwen stage 1 waits.
