Does the stand-in ratio tell a behaviour the original 67M model already performs (lowercase) from one it cannot (a word cipher), fine-tuned on the same rows?

2026-10-29 to 2026-10-30 · MATS cluster L40, $0 (first check on a RunPod 4090, $0.08) · experiments/performed_anchor

The question. The re-gating line's one number that might tell an existing behaviour's fine-tune from a new one's is the stand-in ratio, and it had been read only on toys the original model does not perform at all (E46, E50). The user chose (2026-10-29) to read it on a pair the original model really sorts that way: continuing lowercase Pile text in lowercase (performed), against a fixed swap of 50 common words (not performed). The open worry was that lowercase is also the broader update, so the ratio might read breadth, not kind.

What we did. First the original model alone: it prefers the lowercase form in lowercased text by 1.18 nats and the real word over the swapped one by 5.53, so the pair passed its go rule. Then we fine-tuned each task on the same 2,400 rows (Adam, 3e-5, 300 steps; two batch orders), split each finished update over the subcomponents by least squares, and read the stand-in ratio. That is the gain in the model rebuilt from the subcomponents under Γ's new masks, over the gain from the moved subcomponents alone. Near 1 means Γ's new switching explains the gain; the toys' copies read 0.7 to 0.8 and random maps 0.4. Controls were a KL-matched random update and a hand-made write that builds no mechanism. To test breadth we read each update's breadth, the gain per nat of change at positions the task did not change (high is targeted, low is broad), on a second cipher of 500 words and, with no new fine-tune, on lowercase corrected for positions it changes but does not score, and on a second pair of tasks from E54: a key-to-word table the original model half-knows (performed) against a cipher on the same keys.

What we found. The ratio separates the pair: lowercase reads 0.92 and 0.93 (the two batch orders), the 50-word cipher 0.26 on both, a gap of 0.65 against the 0.3 bar. Breadth does not explain it. On E54's pair the half-known table is the more targeted update, 49 to 55 nats of gain per nat of off-target change against the cipher's 19.5 to 22 (about 2.2 times at the closest seeds), at matched update size and gain, yet it reads 0.95 against 0.29 to 0.34. On E53's own pair lowercase stays the broader member: corrected it reads 6.6 and 5.9 against the 500-word cipher's 7.8 and 7.7. The relay number does not separate the E53 pair (0.66 against 0.41 to 0.42), and the hand-write check failed to reach its gain.

What it means. The high-ratio member is the performed one in both pairs, whichever side of its partner it sits on for breadth, so on these two task families breadth does not carry the ratio's separation, and the ratio goes on as a candidate reading of kind. Two caveats stand. E53's own pair still confounds kind with breadth; only E54's pair breaks it. A high reading still means only "uses pieces the original model has", since a new circuit built from old pieces would also read as elicited, and E54's "performed" member is only weakly performed. A cheaper method sees part of the difference: steering does not separate E54's pair by its filed bar, but at about a sixth to a tenth of the route split's gap in that unit.
