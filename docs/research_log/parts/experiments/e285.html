Why do the needed-set readings read every trained fine-tune the same small amount?

2026-11-23 · cell P1.59, definition-lead · $0, saved sums only

The question. The write share over the needed set (the share of each needed variable's write change that lies along its original write) reads every trained fine-tune low, toy and realistic alike. The paper's Discussion must say why, and rule out a bad task choice and a poor implementation.

What we did. We re-read the saved per-variable sums behind about 300 items (fine-tunes, built edits, random updates) at VPD and at neurons, variable by variable. The filed reading took the kept share of the 1% of changed needed variables with the most flips, beside the doubling and tenfold edits.

What we found. The filed reading failed its control at both featurizations: a pure doubling edit reads near zero on it, since the variables ranked highest by flips are often not the ones the edit changed. The per-variable share of trained updates is low and near random in every class (toy, procedural, RL, realistic). At VPD, low cannot be told from spread: the median changed subcomponent of a pure doubling keeps under a thousandth of its change.

What it means. Ranking by need cannot find where an update acted, and the task choice does not explain the low reading. The test that separates a re-mix of existing parts from a spread-out change is the kept share of a whole matrix or layer. The arithmetic reproduced every saved value; the needed-set scans were not redone. Redteam-lead: OK after fixes.
