Can the pass test on Qwen tell a good placement of a fine-tuning update from a bad one, and can it pass anything?

2026-11-03 · MATS cluster L40, normal and debug QoS, $0 (jobs 50047, 50356, 50364, 50371) · experiments/qwen_pass_test

The question. D1 needs one way of placing a finished update on Qwen3-1.7B's subcomponents that keeps the decomposition faithful, judged by a test that can fail a bad placement (Q1). Before trusting any placement we asked whether the test itself can tell good from bad, and whether it can pass anything. Earlier Qwen jobs (E44) had found a pass test with no teeth.

What we did. Nothing was trained. We took four saved fine-tuning updates (the first maths fine-tune at steps 10 and 100, the gold-solutions fine-tune, and a template fine-tune) and placed each on the subcomponents in several ways: the half of each matrix's subcomponents that Γ often switches on, the half it rarely switches on, a random change of matched size, the least-squares split and the adjoint split. Each was scored against the fine-tuned model by the KL under the masks Γ gives on the original model, and by the share of the next-token gain kept. As a yardstick we also added the whole update unmasked, to see whether any placement could be expected to pass, and we opened the masks step by step on two updates.

What we found. The test fails bad placements on all four updates: the often-on half closes at least 20 times as much of the KL gap per unit of update carried as the rarely-on half, and a random change closes nothing (first figure). No split is faithful, and the whole update added unmasked closes only 0.03 to 0.14 of the KL gap where we filed 0.85 or more; it keeps 0.26, 0.44 and 0.48 of the next-token gain on the three maths updates and 0.02 on the template. With every subcomponent fully on and the update added, the KL falls to 0.013 on the two updates opened, so Δ's absence alone does not explain the gap (second figure).

What it means. At this end the test can reject a placement; at the other it cannot yet pass one, so the weak version gets no yes or no on Qwen from it, and least squares closing a tenth or less is not evidence against least squares. What is not read: Δ switched on under the masks, whether the masks would drop any change of this size (a random change of the same size is the job after this, E72), and whether KL overstates the loss. D1 is blocked on what its test should become (section {{n:decisions}}).

Run
Scoring runs on saved states, three to four hours on an L40 in all, $0; one job stopped at its wall clock and resumed; no training.
Found
  • The test fails bad placements on all four updates, by a gap many times the row noise, so it has teeth; this was expected.
  • No split is faithful, and the whole update added unmasked closes 0.03 to 0.14 of the KL gap against a filed 0.85 or more; this reversed the filed lean, and its controls were clean.
  • Fully open masks with the update added reach KL 0.013, so Δ's absence alone does not explain the gap on two updates; the half-open masks lower next-token loss with no update added, which we cannot explain.
