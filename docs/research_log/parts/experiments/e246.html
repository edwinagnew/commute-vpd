Does any cheap proxy put the side built as reuse first on the four real-text pairs?

2026-11-21 · cell P2.53 (filled), compare-lead · experiments/realtext_proxies

The question. Part 2 claims cheap proxies track reuse, so far on built items. Four real-text pairs each have one side built to need what the base model holds and one built to need what it lacks; a proxy that tracks reuse should put the first side higher on every pair it can read.

What we did. We read three proxies on the saved fine-tunes, three seeds a side: the log-odds share r (E228), the VPD-masked score S (E241), and the base model's prior on the trained token. The pairs are a news-topic against a seeded-coin label, lowercase against reversed word order, French against Yoruba, and British spelling against a scrambled twin. Do-nothing and random changes were read beside them. Nothing was trained.

What we found. No proxy orders the pairs. The prior is right on 3 of 4 pairs (the fourth, language, runs the other way, and the British hit is circular because that label was set from the prior); the share is right on 1 of the 3 pairs it can read; S is right on 1 of 4, with French and both British sides under its own floor. Do-nothing reads exactly 0 and random changes gain at most 0.065.

What it means. The paper's claim that cheap proxies track reuse stays on built items; its one real-text agreement, the prior on the British pair, is circular. S is unvalidated (E241), so its readings here carry no weight. No reuse or new verdict is given on any pair, and no further real-text proxy run is made.
