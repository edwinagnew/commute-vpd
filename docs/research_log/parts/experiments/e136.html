Does the re-gating share still separate built pairs when the trained decomposition is swapped for an untrained one?

2026-11-09 · provisional, no independent check · cluster debug L40, jobs 56942 and 56944, about 5 minutes, $0 · experiments/share_untrained

The question. Part 2 asks whether the trained decomposition earns its cost. The re-gating share is the part of a fine-tune's gain in the answer's log-odds that comes from changing only which of the original subcomponents the decomposition switches on, with their weights held fixed. If an untrained stand-in gave the same separation between the two sides of a pair, the training would add nothing to it.

What we did. We replaced the trained decomposition with each matrix's singular value decomposition, and with the same directions randomly rotated, and read the share on the saved fine-tunes. With no trained gating, a fixed rule chose the components: at each position, keep those with the largest contribution, as many as the trained decomposition switches on. Five pairs were scored under a rule filed first. Sides are labelled as built; no verdict on reuse.

What we found. On all five scored pairs the separation disappears. The trained gap is 0.51 to 0.69 of the gain; on the singular value decomposition it runs from 0.05 below zero to 0.07 above. The share itself drops too, from an average of 0.50 on the trained decomposition to 0.07, so the untrained reading is low everywhere, not only flat between the sides.

What it means. The share's separation depends on something the untrained versions lack, but this run cannot say whether that is the trained pieces or a poor gating rule. With the rule's gates the untrained versions reproduce the original model worse: on the singular value decomposition the masked answer log-odds sit 3.1 below the original on average, against 0.8 above for the trained decomposition. Only one rule was tried.

Run
Two debug jobs on the L40 on saved fine-tunes, $0. The first stopped at its reproduction check on a code bug, the original model's reference computed at the fine-tuned weights; once fixed, the trained share reproduced its saved value on all ten pairs.
Found
  • On five of five scored pairs the gap shrinks by 0.44 to 0.68 on the singular value decomposition, as filed.
  • The randomly rotated version also reads little, from 0.12 below zero to 0.16 above.
  • Real fine-tunes and their shuffled-label twins show no gap on any reading, at most 0.04 from zero; these were described, not scored.
