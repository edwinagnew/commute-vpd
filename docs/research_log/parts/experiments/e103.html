With only the label changed, do the neuron readings tell a fine-tune on facts the model knows from one on facts it lacks?

2026-11-06 · provisional · two cluster debug jobs, $0 · experiments/known_answer_pair

The question. Every Qwen reading so far was scored against labels that came with four other differences (task, rows, recipe, size). If Qwen3-1.7B is fine-tuned on 217 real-person and world facts, which the unchanged model already picks at 0.79 in multiple choice, with the recipe of the fictional-author fine-tune, only the label should differ. Do the neuron readings of E102 (prompt overlap, engaged share) separate that pair beyond what the seeds and a Gaussian random change give?

What we did. We fine-tuned on the real facts and read two states beside the fictional-author fine-tune: the step whose total weight change matches the template fine-tune's (step 33 of 335) and the last step. The control came first: the gain on the trained answers at the matched step had to reach half the template's.

What we found. At matched size every neuron reading sat level between the known-facts and fictional-facts updates, as filed; prompt overlap rose for the known-facts update only at the end, where the pair also differs two-fold in size, so it is reported, not scored. The control passed at 11.35 nats a token against a bar of 0.33, but mostly on a format habit: the training rows answer "one sentence" with a bare phrase, and the model learned to stop after it within ten small steps.

What it means. The matched pair differs in answer format as well as in label, so the level readings say little yet about whether the instruments can tell reuse from new. Amendment 1 retrains the known-facts arm with sentence answers, coded next; the same caveat goes to qwen-decomp-lead's reads of these states.
