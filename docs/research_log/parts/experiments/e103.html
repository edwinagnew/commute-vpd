E103surprise

With only the label changed, do the neuron readings tell a fine-tune on facts the model knows from one on facts it lacks?

2026-11-06 · provisional · four cluster debug jobs, $0 · experiments/known_answer_pair

The question. Every Qwen reading so far was scored against labels that came with four other differences (task, rows, recipe, size). If Qwen3-1.7B is fine-tuned on 217 real-person and world facts, which the unchanged model already picks at 0.79 in multiple choice, with the recipe of the fictional-author fine-tune, only the label should differ. Do the neuron readings of E102 (prompt overlap, engaged share) separate that pair beyond what the seeds and a Gaussian random change give?

What we did. The first run read level, but its training rows answered "one sentence" with a bare phrase, and the model mostly learned to stop after it. We retrained with every answer written as a sentence, as the fictional run's are, and read the state whose total weight change matches the template fine-tune's (step 32 of 335), with the gain counted on answer words only.

What we found. With the format matched, all four neuron readings are still level at the template's size. But each matched update gains nearly as much on the other's rows as on its own (1.35 against 1.50 on the real-fact sentences, 2.20 against 2.47 on the fictional answers), so the pair barely expresses its label at that size. The carrying neurons differ far more across the pair than between two fictional seeds (0.47 against 0.90 shared on the real rows), but the known-facts arm has one seed, so that is an observation without a null.

What it means. A level reading here is weak evidence about the instruments, since neither update has learned much specific to its facts. One more known-facts seed (the last amendment) is coded next, to give the carrying-set difference a null; qwen-decomp-lead's reads of these states inherit the caveat.
