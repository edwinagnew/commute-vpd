Do the reuse readings sort realistic fine-tunes the way blind raters expect, and agree with each other?

2026-11-23 · cell P2.66, regating-lead, no independent check · experiments/convergence_panel

The question. You asked for two things we could hope for: some relationship between intuitive expectation of reuse and measured reuse, and agreement between the readings that worked on toy tasks when they are applied to realistic tasks.

What we did. We fine-tuned the 67M model on ordinary tasks (new languages and registers, label tasks, prompt tasks), three seeds each, and read every task at matched output change (KL from the base model). Three readings were read: the re-gating share, the steering share and the effective rank of the update, beside four baselines (KL, the activation-difference score, update norm, the base model's prior on the targets). Two blind raters (Opus and GPT) sorted every task into reuse, middle or new before any reading. The panel was widened from nine to 18 tasks before scoring; the fourth reading, E_b (the write share over the interchange-needed subcomponents), was then read on the twelve scored tasks on four H100 pods.

What we found. Hope 2 reversed: on the eight tasks every share reading covers, the readings agree with each other less than the baselines do (Kendall W 0.25 against 0.29, both under the random-ranking null), where we filed "above". The nine-task set is no call (0.11 against 0.09). Hope 1: redteam-lead's verdict is that the headline holds, since the readings do not track the tiers beyond the base prior. The effective rank passed the filed test only at the margin: its raw tau (0.36) is under the 0.423 bar, it passed through the prior-partialled route (partial tau 0.42 to 0.46, p 0.03 to 0.049), it is one of three readings with no correction for that, and it is scored on 12 tasks against the shares' 8. Its direction was filed from a small earlier gap and runs against the low-rank intuition. The base model's loss on the training text alone tracks the tiers (consensus tau 0.46), and on all 12 scored tasks the effective rank does not beat it (partial tau 0.20, p 0.20, filed unit). The earlier within-text figure (partial 0.87 on the eight text tasks) is withdrawn as evidence: the effective rank's 0.906 there is the highest tau the tied tiers allow, and the loss falls short only on the summary task (without it both reach 0.873, partial 0.40). A confirmatory panel (E276) found it not separable from base loss. The re-gating share is no call; steering runs opposite to the tiers and tracks the prior (tau -0.93). E_b over the needed set runs opposite to the raters' tiers (consensus tau -0.69, Opus -0.83, GPT -0.52; the filed direction was positive, more reuse reading higher). It is not explained by output change (tau with KL -0.03, though KL was matched to within 10%, so little spread is left to follow), and across the twelve tasks base loss does not account for it (partial -0.60 to -0.68). Within the eight continued-text tasks, base loss over all positions does remove it (partial -0.17 per byte, +0.14 per token). The reading depends on the state: the needed set runs from 200 to 37,888 variables between seeds of one task, and the consensus tau is -0.59 without the four states whose needed set is under 1,000. A random update of the same size leans the same way weakly (tau -0.29), and run 4 below puts it at about half. It matches E273's real-text reversal. No fix is proposed. Two post-hoc checks ($0, descriptive) do not explain it: seeds of one task agree only a little less on reuse-tier tasks than on new-tier ones, and holding that agreement fixed moves the write share's tau only from -0.69 to -0.67. How many steps a task takes to learn is too coarse to read (five of twelve tasks tie at step 1).

Run 4, ten tasks (E307, redteamed). We retrained the four tasks under the gain floor with the same recipe. Sentiment and Spanish reached it; arXiv and German did not in 1200 steps, so the panel is ten tasks (you: "n=10 is fine"). No reading orders the fine-tunes like the raters. The effective rank's earlier pass rested on arXiv and German: it is gone at ten tasks. The write share still runs opposite to the raters (tau -0.61, against -0.69 on twelve), and random updates of the same size reproduce about half of that reversal (-0.32), not "mostly" as a gap on twelve tasks suggested. The readings agree with each other less than the baselines do (W 0.16 against 0.35). Page: analysis.

One more reading, same states (E279, $0, user via whip-lead). We read the untrained-SVD share (how much of each fine-tune's gain survives when the weight matrices are swapped for their untrained singular directions) on the wide panel's saved states. Nine of the twelve tasks have a share (arXiv, German and Spanish drop for small gains), and no order by the raters' tiers was seen (tau 0.03, p 0.50; 0.10 given base loss, 0.05 given KL; 0.17 with the write share over the needed set). All four tests are no call, and with nine tasks a moderate order could be missed. Question answering reads 1.27 and the summary task -1.24, outside zero to one, so this is not yet an interpretable share on realistic text. The paper does not cite it as a reuse reading. Page: analysis.

What it means. The panel does not support the claim that the reuse readings converge on realistic tasks. Clear reuse tasks are nearly absent at matched output change: Pile text, code and British spelling cannot be learned on held-out data to the matched KL under three recipes (probe job 63813).
