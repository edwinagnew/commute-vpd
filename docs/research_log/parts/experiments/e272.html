Do the reuse readings sort realistic fine-tunes the way blind raters expect, and agree with each other?

2026-11-23 · cell P2.66, regating-lead, no independent check · experiments/convergence_panel

The question. You asked for two things we could hope for: some relationship between intuitive expectation of reuse and measured reuse, and agreement between the readings that worked on toy tasks when they are applied to realistic tasks.

What we did. We fine-tuned the 67M model on ordinary tasks (new languages and registers, label tasks, prompt tasks), three seeds each, and read every task at matched output change (KL from the base model). Three readings were read: the re-gating share, the steering share and the effective rank of the update, beside four baselines (KL, the activation-difference score, update norm, the base model's prior on the targets). Two blind raters (Opus and GPT) sorted every task into reuse, middle or new before any reading. The panel was widened from nine to 18 tasks before scoring; the fourth reading, E_b, was not run (it is a gated GPU stage).

What we found. Hope 2 reversed: on the eight tasks every share reading covers, the readings agree with each other less than the baselines do (Kendall W 0.25 against 0.29, both under the random-ranking null), where we filed "above". The nine-task set is no call (0.11 against 0.09). Hope 1: redteam-lead's verdict is that the headline holds, since the readings do not track the tiers beyond the base prior. The effective rank passed the filed test only at the margin: its raw tau (0.36) is under the 0.423 bar, it passed through the prior-partialled route (partial tau 0.42 to 0.46, p 0.03 to 0.049), it is one of three readings with no correction for that, and it is scored on 12 tasks against the shares' 8. Its direction was filed from a small earlier gap and runs against the low-rank intuition. A check chosen after seeing the results (descriptive) found it follows the tiers more tightly within the eight continued-text tasks (tau 0.91 on the consensus) than pooled, so the label-versus-text format reading is not supported, though on some tier sets it cannot be separated from the prior. A second descriptive check (filed before computing, chosen after the first results; job 64181) took how unfamiliar each task's text is to the base model (its loss per byte on the trained tokens): it tracks the tiers moderately (tau 0.51 on the eight text tasks, 0.46 on all 12), and within the eight text tasks the effective rank still follows the tiers after removing it (partial tau 0.87, p 0.0008), but across all 12 it does not (partial 0.20, p 0.20); a confirmatory panel (E276) then found it not separable from base loss. The re-gating share is no call; steering runs opposite to the tiers and tracks the prior (tau -0.93).

What it means. The panel does not support the claim that the reuse readings converge on realistic tasks. Clear reuse tasks are nearly absent at matched output change: Pile text, code and British spelling cannot be learned on held-out data to the matched KL under three recipes (probe job 63813).
