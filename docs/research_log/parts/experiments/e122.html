Does the broad Adam fine-tune's copy fail the adversarial check because of how much it damages plain text, or because of its recipe?

2026-11-06 · three cluster debug L40 jobs (54703, 54733, 54766), $0 · experiments/damage_crossed · provisional, not independently checked

The question. Teacher training copies a rank-8 LoRA fine-tune of the lowercase task faithfully but not the broad full-matrix Adam one (E39). The two differ in two ways at once: the recipe, and how much each damages plain Pile text (about 0.24 nats for the Adam fine-tune, 0.12 for the LoRA). Which one makes the Adam copy fail matters for whether a broad update can be copied at all (Q1).

What we did. We crossed the two. One cell is the Adam fine-tune stopped early, at the LoRA's damage. Another is the LoRA trained on to the Adam's damage. A third copies the Adam fine-tune starting from its least-squares split instead of from the original subcomponents. Each copy was read with VPD's adversary on two sets of 256 Pile rows, as a ratio to the unchanged decomposition's (1.40 or less passes, 1.61 or more fails, as in E39), and its excess was mapped by position.

What we found. No call: neither crossed cell landed on one side of the lines on both sets. The early-stopped Adam copy failed on the first set (2.84) and sat between the lines on the second (1.44); the damaged LoRA copy sat between on both (1.53, 1.60) and missed its damage band by 0.0009. Lower damage gave a lower ratio on three of four row blocks for Adam, and higher damage raised the LoRA's. The copy started from the least-squares split failed far worse than any other (9.4 and 9.8), though it fits the fine-tuned model's outputs as well as the standard copy.

What it means. The pair does not separate damage from recipe; as a lean only, both copies move with damage. A start close to the fine-tune's change does not make the copy more faithful, and that start's 8.33 before any training, above doing nothing's 4.79, is unexplained, so nothing is built on split starts until it is run down. The LoRA copies keep their excess at positions predicting a capital; the failing Adam copies spread it over ordinary text. No reuse verdict is given.
