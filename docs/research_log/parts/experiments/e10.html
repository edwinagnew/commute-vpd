E10mixed

Does component training stay faithful when it teaches the model a word cipher it has never seen?

2026-10-19 · MATS cluster L40; follow-up 2026-10-23 · experiments/kv_task

The question. Earlier runs mapped updates that strengthen something the 67M model already does. The "new capabilities" branch (Q4) asks whether a map still keeps the decomposition faithful when the update teaches something the model lacks. If newness alone broke faithfulness, a parameter decomposition could not follow post-training that adds capabilities (Q1).

What we did. After a marker token that never occurs in ordinary text, the model must follow a common word with a fixed, random other word: a cipher with one pair or with 32 pairs. A copy control in the same format follows each of 32 words with itself. Component training trains the decomposition's pieces directly on each task (Adam), with the importance function Γ and the residual Δ frozen. Beside it, a weight fine-tune of each task was mapped two ways: do-nothing puts the whole change in Δ, and the least-squares split divides it among the pieces, with what will not fit left in Δ. The adversarial ratio asks an adversary to pick the masks over the pieces that most damage predictions on ordinary Pile text, and divides that damage by the same on the unchanged decomposition; 1 means as faithful as before. Across three adversary seeds the unchanged decomposition itself spans 1.00 to 1.21. That spread sets the pass line at 1.24 and the fail line at 1.81; between them is inconclusive.

What we found. Component training learned all three tasks within 30 steps (first figure). At the first step each was learned it passed on one pair and on copying, but failed on 32 pairs at 2.81 (second figure). A follow-up read the same 32-pair run at later saved steps: it passes at steps 100 and 300, although its weights have moved further, once its damage elsewhere has faded (fourth figure). Damage elsewhere is the held-out KL, in nats, between the trained and the original model at every text position except the key. Separately, copying runs through pieces Γ switches on, while the cipher runs through pieces Γ keeps off (third figure).

What it means. Teaching a new behaviour did not, by itself, break faithfulness on ordinary text. The report reads the 32-pair failure as belonging to the step it was read at and its damage elsewhere, not to the number of pairs or the size of the change; that reading rests on one failing state. Each state had one adversary seed. An earlier SGD-versus-Adam study (E21) had set a cut: Adam component training failed only while damage elsewhere was at least 0.2 nats, a cut chosen after seeing three failures. Here both step-10 states sit just above it and read inconclusive rather than fail, so it held only weakly. The pair-count sweep was dropped, the user parked the task, and E21 later traced the failure to Adam's learning rate.

Run
Three tasks on the 67M model, each trained by component training for up to 300 steps beside a weight fine-tune mapped by do-nothing and the least-squares split, all scored on the Pile adversarial and stochastic-mask checks and the sparsity loss (one cluster L40 job, 26 min, $0). The follow-up re-read seven saved states at steps 10 to 300 with three adversary seeds on the unchanged decomposition (two cluster debug jobs, $0).
Found
  • Component training learns a cipher the model has never seen, and once training settles the decomposition stays faithful on ordinary text for 32 pairs as for one; the failure at step 30 (2.81) is read as belonging to that step and its peak damage elsewhere, not to the number of pairs or the size of the change, a reading that rests on one failing state.
  • Copying runs through pieces Γ switches on and the cipher through pieces Γ keeps off, about equally under every map; the report reads this as a first example, on three tasks, of revealing an existing behaviour versus creating a new one.
  • The sparsity loss reads the same for do-nothing and the split on every task: with Γ frozen it sees only the weights, so it cannot tell maps apart.
Bears on
Q1, Q4, Q5, Q7
Moved on
The user parked the task ("trying to do too much at once"); the follow-up withdrew the earlier reading that faithfulness is lost somewhere between 1 and 32 pairs, so no pair-count sweep follows. E21 then traced the failure to Adam's learning rate: at 3.3e-6 and 1e-6 Adam learns the 32 pairs and passes (1.09, 1.05), while plain SGD fails both 32-pair tasks at a smaller change than Adam passes with. Not run: three adversary seeds on the step-10 states; the copy control past the failing state's size.
