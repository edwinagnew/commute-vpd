How much of a fine-tune's change survives in a model where every unit is held to the original model's rule?

2026-11-18 · cell P1.26, runs 1 and 2 of 2 (run 2 is E201, carded here), no independent check · experiments/held_model_retention, experiments/graft_negative

The question. Part 2 is testing your held-model wording of "reused against new" (tested, not adopted; section {{n:next}}, cell P1.26): a change is reuse to the degree that it survives in a held model, built from the original model's own units, each held to its old rule. Three readings fit three different targets, so none is averaged with another. Do they tell fine-tunes apart, and where does a head that is new by construction read?

What we did. For each fine-tune we built the held model by replacing the original model's output weights, one layer at a time, with a least-squares fit to the fine-tune's own write. Reading 1 gives every neuron and head one gain. Reading 2 gives every MLP block and head one gain. Reading 3 fits a linear map per site from all of the original model's units. Survival is 1 minus the divergence from the fine-tune left in the held model, over the divergence of the original model; 1 means the held model reproduces the fine-tune and 0 means it is no closer than the original. We read code checks, the rebuilt circuit of E195, the five pairs of E196 at four seeds, and (run 2, E201) one attention head grafted onto the frozen original model, which is new by construction.

What we found. The gain readings keep almost nothing of any fine-tune: 0.01 to 0.06 under reading 1, about zero under reading 2. Reading 3 keeps 0.98 to 0.999 on the eight old-task fine-tunes and 0.56 to 0.999 on every trained or rebuilt item, but only 0.13 of the grafted head (one build, one site, so n = 1). Under reading 3 the reuse member keeps a little more than its twin on three of four counted pairs (0.005 to 0.011), the repeat pair ties, and the gaps are not ordered by update size. The memorised fact's low reading is mostly its embedding change.

What it means. The three readings are three different targets, reported with no verdict. Only reading 3 tells items apart, and it puts the head built to be new lowest. The graft is one build at one site, so whether reading 3 stays low on other new machinery is untested, and so is whether its pair gaps survive size matching. Nothing is labelled reuse or new, and no bar was set.

Run
Two cluster debug jobs' worth of runs (run 1: five pair jobs plus one for the code checks and the rebuilt item, about 31 s per item; run 2: job 60945), one L40 each, $0. Every code check met its arithmetic value and the graft's build checks held.
