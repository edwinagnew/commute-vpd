Does a LoRA look like noise to the subcomponents because it is a LoRA, or because it was trained with Adam?

2026-10-22 · RunPod H100 · experiments/lora_sgd

The question. The commute line asks whether a fine-tune can be carried onto the decomposition's pieces, its rank-one subcomponents (Q1). Earlier runs (E9, E14) found that a rank-8 LoRA, a fine-tune written as a product BA of a small write factor B and a random-start read factor A, lies outside the pieces about as much as a random update does, where a full fine-tune lies well inside (Q3). Every one of those LoRAs was trained with Adam, and E22 had just found a plain-SGD full fine-tune far more inside than an Adam one. So the LoRA's reading might be Adam's doing, not the LoRA's (Q11).

What we did. We retrained the same rank-8 LoRA with plain SGD on the emoticon task, where the 67M model learns to answer a trigger with the letter o, on one seed for 1,000 steps. The main number is the outside ratio: the share of the update's effect on real inputs that no small move of the pieces can produce, divided by the same share for a random update of equal size, so noise reads 1 and an update the pieces could make entirely reads 0. We also tracked how far A moves from its random start. Then we placed the update three ways. Do-nothing leaves it all in the residual Δ, the weights the pieces do not explain. The least-squares split moves the pieces by the smallest change that best reproduces it and leaves the rest in Δ. The adjoint split moves each piece along its own share of the update and leaves Δ alone. Each placement got the adversarial ratio: the damage on Pile text an adversary can do by masking pieces, over what it does to the unchanged model. It passes at or below 1.40 and fails at or above 1.61, lines set by the unchanged model's spread over three adversary seeds in this job. An Adam LoRA was retrained in the same job as the comparison.

What we found. The SGD LoRA still sits far outside the pieces: its outside ratio runs from 0.33 to 0.87, between noise and the SGD full fine-tune, about ten times above the latter (first figure). Adam puts the LoRA further outside at most steps, though not at step 100. Under SGD the read factor barely leaves its random start (second figure). Every placement of the SGD LoRA passes the adversarial check (third figure), but the adjoint split keeps less of the behaviour as training goes on (fourth figure).

What it means. The reading is that the LoRA sits far outside the pieces mostly because it is a LoRA, not because of Adam, so the earlier LoRA results stand as properties of the update type, at a level that depends on the optimiser. That reading is suggestive only: it rests on one task and one seed, and the gap between optimisers is smaller than the swing between neighbouring checkpoints at most steps. On faithfulness alone, a LoRA trained with either optimiser can be left in Δ or split by least squares (the Adam LoRA's adjoint split was not run); but the adjoint split loses most of the behaviour, so only do-nothing and least squares keep both. The caveat is noise: both LoRA series swing by up to 0.5 between neighbouring checkpoints, more than the gap between optimisers at most steps. The SGD LoRA's drop at step 1,000 cannot be traced to particular matrices, because the per-matrix numbers were not saved. A second seed and those numbers were proposed and not run.

Run
E9's rank-8 LoRA on all 24 decomposed matrices, emoticon, seed 0, 1,000 steps of plain SGD at rate 0.3, picked by E4's rule over 0.003 to 3 (learn the task, least damage elsewhere), beside an Adam LoRA at 1e-3 retrained in the same job and the unchanged model as controls. About 1 h of H100 time; the cost was not logged, an estimated $3 to $4.
Found
  • On one seed, the SGD LoRA's outside ratio reads 0.54 at step 10 and 0.33 at step 1,000, far above the SGD full fine-tune; we expected at least 0.7 at each of the five checkpoints from step 10 (10, 30, 100, 300, 1,000), and it fell short at steps 10, 30 and 1,000.
  • Under SGD the read factor stays at its random start (|cos| 0.997 at step 1,000), as expected.
  • Every placement of the SGD LoRA passes the adversarial check, but the adjoint split keeps only 0.20 of the target at step 1,000, and least squares leaves 0.37 of the update in Δ, against the Adam LoRA's 0.54 (report, not plotted).
