Do the Qwen fine-tunes' readings see what training saw, and does the direct share rise with size?

2026-11-02 · MATS cluster L40, $0 (jobs 48367, 48473 to 48747) · experiments/qwen_recipe

The question. On Qwen3-1.7B (Q3, Q9) the saved fine-tunes are read through the decomposition, and the direct share (the part of an update's effect that the changed subcomponents carry alone) looked higher for real updates than for random changes (E57). Two audits found that this may only be size, and that training rounds its weights to a coarse grid so it may not see an update's first small steps, while every reading used full precision. You asked that the synthetic fine-tunes be shown to learn properly, at the right rates. This is the part that needs no new training; the retraining is planned and has not run.

What we did. No training. We read saved states three ways: in full precision, under the rounded forward training used, and with weights rounded and then read in full precision, on the model's own answers, on human solutions and on the template fine-tune. We also generated answers at full precision from three states. Five checks that the audits said had never been compared followed, each with a rule filed first: whether the masks' bf16 cast adds a floor at a tiny change, whether packing rows changes the unchanged model's error, whether truly original masks change the direct share, three measures of size, and whether the own-answer gain survives full precision. The unchanged model is the control and read the same in all three ways.

What we found. Rounding hides the early update: the own-answer fine-tune after four steps reads a held-out loss of 0.441 in full precision and 0.055 under training's forward, and the template fine-tune after eight steps answers 0.13 correct in full precision against 0.70 rounded. By step 100 the readers agree within the filed 0.03 (excess 0.011, -0.001, 0.005), though that tolerance is larger than the own-answer run's whole rise. Four of the five checks pass: the direct share moves by at most 0.0022 with truly original masks, and the own-answer gain at full precision is +0.037 (se 0.011) against +0.052 rounded. The fifth, three measures of size, is a table with no verdict: the higher direct share of the maths fine-tunes could be size on two measures and not on the third, so it stays open.

What it means. Final states are usable as readings. States flagged "learned" in the first ten or so steps are not behaviour, so any small update compared through the decomposition needs retraining with a full-precision forward first. "Never learned" for the own-answer run is withdrawn as a statement about learning, while its filed rule (a loss drop of 0.02) stays scored as not met. Rounding does not explain why the re-gating share cannot separate changes: random changes were never trained and read the same. Learning rates were not tested; no fine-tune has run at a second rate. Next: the retraining and the direct share against size.

Run
Ten L40 jobs on the MATS cluster on saved states, $0, no training; the unchanged-model control ran first and passed.
Found
  • Rounding is confirmed for the readings listed: 0.441 against 0.055 at own-answer step 4, 0.13 against 0.70 correct at template step 8, agreement by step 100 within 0.03.
  • Direct share against size is open: the two maths fine-tunes are 1.5 to 2 times the size of anything else on two measures, while on the third the 100-step run leads by a quarter and the ten-step run sits below invented facts; invented facts' value (0.012) sits below both random changes, unexplained.
  • The switched-on overlap is mostly closeness to the gating line (matched on it, about chance), so its filed null cannot fail; the re-gating share has an algebraic floor of 0.92 to 0.97, past the 0.8 line, so "no teeth" stands.
