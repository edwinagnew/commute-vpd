E55mixed

When a small model that counts one letter is fine-tuned to count another, does the new answer run through the old count?

2026-10-29 · MATS cluster, debug QoS, Stage A and Amendments 1-4 · reuse line · experiments/pcfg_anchors

The question. Our labels "existing" and "new" for post-training have so far come from the decomposition's own behaviour, so a sceptic reads every result as a fact about VPD. The reuse line (Q5, Q1) needs a ground truth that owes nothing to any decomposition. The project adopted (2026-10-29) the definition of Jain, Kirk and Lubana: a fine-tune reuses a capability if the new behaviour is some function of what that capability computes. The plan builds that on a counting grammar where we choose whether the model already has the capability.

What we did. We pretrained three small models (about 3M parameters) on a grammar with a counting question, "how many a's in the last 40 tokens". LOW almost only counts a; HIGH counts a and b; NEVER counts b. We fine-tuned LOW to count b instead, at two learning rates and with 0, half, 80% or all of the training rows "spurious", meaning rows where answering the count of a plus one is also right (the three counts of a, b and c always add to 40). For every saved state we ran two checks, no decomposition involved. Behaviour: how often the answer equals the count of a plus one. Patch: we copy the residual stream of another input, which differs only in its count of a, into the run, and ask how often the answer follows the donor. Each check has a null and a positive reference measured before any fine-tune; a state "goes through" the count when both clear the upper bar, halfway between them. Four later free analyses (Amendments 1 to 4) read the saved states row by row, tested the rule they found on fresh rows, checked how the spurious training rows were built, and ran both checks on swap-built rows where count of a plus one and count of b are different answers.

What we found. The checks work: the patch moves the answer on the two models that count and never on the two nulls (figure 1), and the rebuilt grammar reproduces seven of the paper's nine fine-tune results (figure 2). Fast fine-tunes never use the old count. The slow all-spurious ones answer count of a plus one at 1,000 steps, then leave it between 1,000 and 5,000 steps for a rule that uses two of the three counts, never one alone (figure 4). At 10,000 steps the fast one answers the midpoint of count of a plus one and count of b; the slow one answers a point a third of the way from the first to the second, and that rule held on 20,000 fresh rows, though they repeat the same pairs of counts, so it is shown stable, not general. The test on new pairs could not run: the letter-swap step that built the all-spurious training rows breaks the base model's own count, which is right on only 45% of such rows (figure 5). On swap-built rows where the two answers differ, both seeds of the slow all-spurious state at 1,000 steps answer count of a plus one on about two thirds of rows and count of b on one in a thousand; the slow 80% state reads neither (figure 6). Amendment 5 then fine-tuned on natural texts where count of a plus one is always right, so nothing pushes the model off its count: all four slow seeds answer through the count of a at 500, 1,000 and 2,000 steps (1,000 steps: behaviour about 0.98, patch about 0.90, count of b never through), the fast copy does so at 500 and 1,000 steps but not at 2,000 (0.41 and 0.37), and the control that asks for the old count under a prompt that used to mean something else passes by 500 steps (figure 7).

What it means. The reuse line has a working ground truth. In Stage A only two of eleven labelled states read "existing", both early, and Amendment 3 shows both were trained on rows the base model miscounts. Amendment 5 raises the "existing" side to 16 states, but that was near guaranteed by construction (count of a plus one is every label), the states come from 8 runs not 16, and the pass bar for Stage C must be re-set for the new, lopsided set (16 "existing" against 9 "not through"). Amendment 4 reads the two all-spurious ones as answering through a strengthened count of a, not a count of b; that rests on behaviour, since the patch check clears its bar narrowly and the base model scores as high. Stage A's labels read natural text, where the base counts correctly, so they stand. The choice of what Stage C is scored against is a question for you (decision card decision-e55-truth-set), and nothing runs on this line until it is made. Four independent checks supported the claims with corrections, applied.
