When a small model that counts one letter is fine-tuned to count another, does the new answer run through the old count?

2026-10-29 · MATS cluster, debug QoS, Stage A · reuse line · experiments/pcfg_anchors

The question. Our labels "existing" and "new" for post-training have so far come from the decomposition's own behaviour, so a sceptic reads every result as a fact about VPD. The reuse line (Q5, Q1) needs a ground truth that owes nothing to any decomposition. The project adopted (2026-10-29) the definition of Jain, Kirk and Lubana: a fine-tune reuses a capability if the new behaviour is some function of what that capability computes. The plan builds that on a counting grammar where we choose whether the model already has the capability.

What we did. We pretrained three small models (about 3M parameters) on a grammar with a counting question, "how many a's in the last 40 tokens". LOW almost only counts a; HIGH counts a and b; NEVER counts b. We fine-tuned LOW to count b instead, at two learning rates and with 0, half, 80% or all of the training rows "spurious", meaning rows where answering the count of a plus one is also right. For every saved state we ran two checks, no decomposition involved. Behaviour: how often the answer equals the count of a plus one. Patch: we copy the residual stream of another input, which differs only in its count of a, into the run, and ask how often the answer follows the donor. Each check has a null and a positive reference measured before any fine-tune; a state "goes through" the count when both clear the upper bar, halfway between them.

What we found. The checks work: the patch moves the answer on the two models that count and never on the two nulls (figure 1), and the rebuilt grammar reproduces seven of the paper's nine fine-tune results (figure 2). Fast fine-tunes never go through the count of a. The slow fine-tunes trained on 80% or all spurious rows do go through it after 1,000 steps (patch 0.59 and 0.72). By 10,000 steps the 80% one has learned b and left the old count, and the all-spurious ones read neither, so one filed prediction missed; none was reversed (figure 3). A count-of-c-only answer is ruled out for the slow all-spurious fine-tune, since the three counts sum to 40. LOW's count of b can be decoded from its residual above shuffled labels, narrowly.

What it means. The reuse line has a working ground truth, but only two of eleven labelled states read "existing", both early, so scoring VPD against it would be lopsided. Whether the all-spurious fine-tunes at 10,000 steps still use the old count through another function, such as the midpoint of the count of a plus one and the count of b, is untested; a free per-row check on the saved states (Amendment 1) is being written, and Stage C waits on it. An independent check supported the claim with corrections, applied.

Run
Three pretrainings, then 11 fine-tunes (nine arms and two seed copies) read at 1,000 and 10,000 steps, on the MATS cluster debug QoS (jobs 44244, 44268-44270, 44306, 44307, 44328-44330), $0.
Found
  • The checks are clean (nulls 0, positives 0.97 and 1), and fast fine-tunes never go through the count of a, as expected.
  • Slow fine-tunes with 80% or all spurious rows go through it at 1,000 steps and not at 10,000, which missed the filed prediction that the all-spurious ones would stay through.
  • Only 2 of 11 labelled states are "existing"; LOW's count of b decodes above shuffled labels by about one and a half standard deviations, against uneven controls.
