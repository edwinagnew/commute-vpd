When a small model that counts one letter is fine-tuned to count another, does the new answer run through the old count?

2026-10-30 · MATS cluster, debug QoS, Stage A, Amendments 1-5b and Stage C1 · reuse line · experiments/pcfg_anchors

The question. Our labels "existing" and "new" for post-training have so far come from the decomposition's own behaviour, so a sceptic reads every result as a fact about VPD. The reuse line (Q5, Q1) needs a ground truth that owes nothing to any decomposition. The project adopted (2026-10-29) the definition of Jain, Kirk and Lubana: a fine-tune reuses a capability if the new behaviour is some function of what that capability computes. The plan builds that on a counting grammar where we choose whether the model already has the capability, then scores methods against it, first three that need no decomposition (Stage C1), then VPD (Stage C2).

What we did. We pretrained three small models (about 3M parameters) on a grammar with a counting question, "how many a's in the last 40 tokens". LOW almost only counts a; HIGH counts a and b; NEVER counts b. We fine-tuned LOW to count b instead, at two learning rates and with different shares of "spurious" training rows, where answering the count of a plus one is also right. Two checks, no decomposition involved, label every saved state: how often the answer equals the count of a plus one (behaviour), and how often copying another input's residual stream moves the answer to that input's (patch). A state "reuses" the old count when the count of a is through on both checks and the count of b is not; it is "new" when the count of a is not through. Four free analyses read the saved states row by row; Amendment 5 added fine-tunes on natural texts where the old count gives every answer, so nothing pushes the model off it. Stage C1 then scored three instruments on 26 labelled states (15 reuse, 11 new): pruning a few units (does removing them bring the old count back), reverse fine-tuning (how fast the old count returns, against a model that never had it), and steering (how much of the fine-tune one average shift recovers). Two labellers that read only the recipe, "fine-tuned for at most 1,000 steps" and "learning rate at most 1e-5", are the reference an instrument must beat by a margin of two states.

What we found. The checks work and the rebuilt grammar reproduces seven of the paper's nine results (figures 1 and 2). Fast fine-tunes never use the old count; slow ones do early and leave it between 1,000 and 5,000 steps (figure 3); fine-tunes on natural texts keep it, the slow ones to 2,000 steps (figure 4). In Stage C1 no instrument passed on every state (figure 5). Pruning agreed with the truth on 20 of 26 states and steering on 21 of 27, above chance, but the learning-rate labeller got 19 and 20, one state short of the margin; reverse fine-tuning labelled 24 of 26 states "existing" and got 17, under chance. On one vote per training run pruning (14 of 17) and steering (16 of 18) pass. Steering's miss rests on one unreadable state; without it, after the fact, it reaches 21 of 26, exactly the bar. After the fact, each raw reading separates the two sides with no overlap (pruning gain at least 0.354 against at most 0.082; steering share at least 0.836 against at most 0.669), so the loose cut-offs, not the instruments, failed; those gaps are fitted, not filed. Fast natural-row seeds 1 to 3 reuse the count at 1,000 steps, as seed 0 did, and not at 2,000.

What it means. The reuse line has a working ground truth and a harder baseline than expected: no cheap method yet reads reuse better than the training recipe, and a decomposition must beat that recipe-only labeller. The truth set is tangled with the recipe (every "reuses" state is an early save, 8 of 11 new states are fast), so any instrument, VPD included, can beat the learning-rate labeller by at most 7 of 26 states; the "existing" states also come from at most 7 independent runs, not 15. You decided (2026-10-30): first a $0, refined and de-confounded rerun of the three instruments (cut-offs set on states outside the truth set, a truth set that reads reuses late and new states early), then scoring VPD (Stage C2, a gated run); benchmarking VPD against the instruments without a decomposition is a priority. No filed prediction was reversed; the pruning control passes near-vacuously (it needs only about 3 rows).

Three pretrainings, 11 fine-tunes read at 1,000 and 10,000 steps, four free analyses, Amendment 5's natural-row fine-tunes (and 5b's three further fast seeds), and Stage C1's three instruments with their controls, on the MATS cluster debug QoS (jobs 44244 to 46023, and 46105, 46106, 46107, 46121 for C1 and 5b), $0.
Found
  • The checks are clean (nulls 0, positives 0.97 and 1), fast fine-tunes never use the old count, slow ones do early (a miss of the filed prediction that they would stay), and fine-tunes on natural texts keep it by construction, so their value is as anchors.
  • No instrument passed the filed test on every state: pruning and steering came one state short of the margin over the learning-rate labeller, reverse fine-tuning missed chance, as the filed rules read it.
