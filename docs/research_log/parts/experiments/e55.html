When a small model that counts one letter is fine-tuned to count another, does the new answer run through the old count?

2026-10-31 · MATS cluster, debug QoS, Stage A, Amendments 1-5b, Stages C1, C1b and C3 and its step ladder · reuse line · experiments/pcfg_anchors, experiments/open_pair_instruments

The question. Our labels "existing" and "new" for post-training have so far come from the decomposition's own behaviour, so a sceptic reads every result as a fact about VPD. The reuse line (Q5, Q1) needs a ground truth that owes nothing to any decomposition. The project adopted (2026-10-29) the definition of Jain, Kirk and Lubana: a fine-tune reuses a capability if the new behaviour is some function of what that capability computes. The plan builds that on a counting grammar where we choose whether the model already has the capability, then scores methods against it: first methods that need no decomposition (Stage C1, then its refined rerun C1b), then the same methods on E58's 67M open-pair fine-tunes (Stage C3), then VPD (Stage C2).

What we did. We pretrained three small models (about 3M parameters) on a grammar with a counting question, "how many a's in the last 40 tokens". LOW almost only counts a; HIGH counts a and b; NEVER counts b. Two checks, no decomposition involved, label a fine-tuned state: how often the answer equals the count of a plus one (behaviour), and how often copying another input's residual stream moves the answer to that input's (patch). Stage A and its free analyses read LOW fine-tuned to count b; Amendment 5 added fine-tunes on natural texts. Stage C1 scored three instruments on 26 labelled states: pruning a few units (does removing them bring the old count back), reverse fine-tuning (how fast the old count returns) and steering (how much of the fine-tune one average shift recovers). Its cut-offs were guessed and nearly every "reuses" state was a short or slow fine-tune. Stage C1b fixed both: LOW fine-tuned on natural text in two tasks, "plus one" (answers the count of a plus one, so it can only be using the old count: it reuses) and "count b" (new), two learning rates, five seeds each; cut-offs were fixed on seeds 0 and 1 and committed before seeds 2 to 4 were read, giving 21 scored states. Stage C3 then moved the first two instruments to the 67M model's four open-pair word maps (eight seeds each): the rank-1 map (each key to the model's own top guess, old by construction), the cipher (arbitrary answers, new by construction), the elicit map (answers the model half-guesses, where VPD and the carry label disagreed) and a copy map. Pruning revival is the share of the fine-tune's distance from the original model that removing the top 40 units gives back; reverse fine-tuning is the steps needed to halve that distance. The C3 step ladder then re-read the two maps whose answer is known, rank-1 and cipher, plus elicit, after 10, 30, 100 and 300 fine-tune steps (three seeds each), and re-read C3's 32 states with up to 320 units pruned; an instrument passes at a step if the two maps differ, in the expected direction, by three times the seed noise.

What we found. On the counting set all three instruments labelled all 21 scored states right (17 needed; best recipe-only labeller 15), but so does a probe that reads only the answers (figures 2 to 4). On the 67M maps neither pruning nor reverse fine-tuning separates the rank-1 map from the cipher beyond seed noise at any of the four step counts, nor at any pruning size up to 320 units (figure 4). At step 10 pruning gives back 0.57 for rank-1 against 0.48 for the cipher, a gap of 0.09 against a bar of 0.12. Once the cipher is learned the order flips: this reverses a filed prediction. We had filed that pruning would favour the old-end map at every step, and at step 30 it favours the new-end map beyond the bar (0.74 against 0.61, wrong way); at 100 and 300 steps the same wrong-way order stays under the bar. Reverse fine-tuning undoes rank-1 more slowly than the cipher at every step (by 1.7 to 2.8 steps, wrong way, always under its bar of 3). At step 10 the cipher is only half-learned (held-out probability of its answer 0.60 against 0.96 for rank-1), a confound that fits the trend but was not varied, so it is not shown to be the cause. VPD's r and stand-in ratio separate the two ends widely at step 10 and put elicit at the old end (0.85 and 0.77 of the way to rank-1), as do the original model's own probability of the answer, the distance from the original model, and the graded carry to held-out keys (0.72); under the filed rules those readings are "distance and prior" (figure 5).

What it means. Every counting state performs one of two tasks perfectly, so "reuses" and "performs plus one" coincide and VPD could at best tie there. On the 67M open-pair maps these two ports fail their own positive control within this recipe, so that set cannot rank VPD against them, and the project stops spending on it as a benchmark. VPD's step-10 separation used the same half-learned cipher, and VPD was not read at steps 30 to 300, so whether it keeps the order is unknown. The E58 disagreement is still VPD against the carry label's fixed cut-off, which also calls the rank-1 map neither old nor new. Next on the reuse line is C2, VPD scored on the C1b counting set, where the cheap methods do work; but that set has no headroom (an answer-only probe and the update size also score perfectly), so a tie there cannot rank VPD, only show it is not worse.

Found
  • The counting checks are clean, fast fine-tunes never use the old count, slow ones do early, and fine-tunes on natural texts keep it by construction, so their value is as anchors.
  • With cut-offs set on held-out seeds, pruning, reverse fine-tuning and steering each label all 21 scored states right (17 needed), but an answer-only probe does too, so the set cannot rank VPD above them.
  • On the 67M open-pair maps pruning and reverse fine-tuning do not separate the old-by-construction map from the new one at 10, 30, 100 or 300 fine-tune steps, or at up to 320 pruned units; this was not expected, and pruning at step 30 favours the new-end map beyond its bar (0.74 against 0.61), reversing a filed prediction.
  • The one gap in the expected direction (step 10) appears while the cipher is half-learned, which fits a confound but is untested; VPD's step-10 readings used those same states, so the comparison stays open and this set no longer serves as a benchmark.
