When a small model that counts one letter is fine-tuned to count another, does the new answer run through the old count?

2026-10-31 · MATS cluster, debug QoS, Stage A, Amendments 1-5b, Stages C1 and C1b · reuse line · experiments/pcfg_anchors

The question. Our labels "existing" and "new" for post-training have so far come from the decomposition's own behaviour, so a sceptic reads every result as a fact about VPD. The reuse line (Q5, Q1) needs a ground truth that owes nothing to any decomposition. The project adopted (2026-10-29) the definition of Jain, Kirk and Lubana: a fine-tune reuses a capability if the new behaviour is some function of what that capability computes. The plan builds that on a counting grammar where we choose whether the model already has the capability, then scores methods against it, first three that need no decomposition (Stage C1, then its refined rerun C1b), then VPD (Stage C2).

What we did. We pretrained three small models (about 3M parameters) on a grammar with a counting question, "how many a's in the last 40 tokens". LOW almost only counts a; HIGH counts a and b; NEVER counts b. Two checks, no decomposition involved, label a fine-tuned state: how often the answer equals the count of a plus one (behaviour), and how often copying another input's residual stream moves the answer to that input's (patch). Stage A and four free analyses read states fine-tuned from LOW to count b, with different shares of "spurious" training rows where answering the count of a plus one is also right; Amendment 5 added fine-tunes on natural texts. Stage C1 scored three instruments on 26 labelled states: pruning a few units (does removing them bring the old count back), reverse fine-tuning (how fast the old count returns, against a model that never had it), and steering (how much of the fine-tune one average shift recovers). Its cut-offs were guessed and nearly every "reuses" state was a short or slow fine-tune. Stage C1b fixed both. It fine-tuned LOW on natural text only, in two tasks: "plus one" answers the count of a plus one on every row, so the count of b fits only 18% of rows and the state can only be using the old count (it reuses); "count b" answers the count of b (it is new). Both ran at two learning rates, saved at 1,000 and 10,000 steps, five seeds each. Each instrument's cut-off was fixed on seeds 0 and 1 and committed before seeds 2 to 4 were read, giving 21 scored states. A method passes if it beats chance and beats the best labeller that reads only the training recipe by two states.

What we found. The checks work, fast fine-tunes never use the old count, slow ones do early and leave it between 1,000 and 5,000 steps, and fine-tunes on natural texts keep it (figures 1 to 3). In Stage C1 no instrument passed with its guessed cut-offs, though after the fact each raw reading separated the two sides. In C1b all three instruments labelled all 21 scored states right (17 were needed; the best recipe-only labeller, the size of the weight change, got 15), and 12 of 12 on one vote per run (figures 4 and 5). No scored reading came inside the gap the calibration seeds left. But a probe that reads only the answers also gets 21 of 21, and the update size read within one recipe separates the tasks on all 18 states in cells holding both (the task that learns the new count moves the weights 1.15 to 1.45 times as far).

What it means. The three cheap methods do tell a fine-tune that adds one to the old count from one that learns a new count, and Stage C1's misses were its guessed cut-offs. But every state performs one of two tasks perfectly, so "reuses" and "performs plus one" coincide, and anything that reads which task was trained ties the methods. VPD (Stage C2) could at best tie on this set, which would show it reads the task, not that it reads reuse better. So C2 is deferred (Fable's advice, adopted, 2026-10-31). Next, at no cost, pruning, reverse fine-tuning and the probe run on E58's 12 open-pair 67M states, to see whether the decomposition's granularity limit (a new circuit built from old pieces reads as elicited) is shared by every instrument.

Found
  • The checks are clean (nulls 0, positives 0.97 and 1), fast fine-tunes never use the old count, slow ones do early (a miss of the filed prediction that they would stay), and fine-tunes on natural texts keep it by construction, so their value is as anchors.
  • With cut-offs set on held-out seeds, pruning, reverse fine-tuning and steering each label all 21 scored states right (17 needed; best recipe-only labeller 15) and 12 of 12 per run; Stage C1's misses were its guessed cut-offs, and the controls held.
