E55mixed

When a small model that counts one letter is fine-tuned to count another, does the new answer run through the old count?

2026-10-31 · MATS cluster, debug QoS, Stage A, Amendments 1-5b, Stages C1, C1b and C3 · reuse line · experiments/pcfg_anchors, experiments/open_pair_instruments

The question. Our labels "existing" and "new" for post-training have so far come from the decomposition's own behaviour, so a sceptic reads every result as a fact about VPD. The reuse line (Q5, Q1) needs a ground truth that owes nothing to any decomposition. The project adopted (2026-10-29) the definition of Jain, Kirk and Lubana: a fine-tune reuses a capability if the new behaviour is some function of what that capability computes. The plan builds that on a counting grammar where we choose whether the model already has the capability, then scores methods against it: first methods that need no decomposition (Stage C1, then its refined rerun C1b), then the same methods on E58's 67M open-pair fine-tunes (Stage C3), then VPD (Stage C2).

What we did. We pretrained three small models (about 3M parameters) on a grammar with a counting question, "how many a's in the last 40 tokens". LOW almost only counts a; HIGH counts a and b; NEVER counts b. Two checks, no decomposition involved, label a fine-tuned state: how often the answer equals the count of a plus one (behaviour), and how often copying another input's residual stream moves the answer to that input's (patch). Stage A and its free analyses read LOW fine-tuned to count b; Amendment 5 added fine-tunes on natural texts. Stage C1 scored three instruments on 26 labelled states: pruning a few units (does removing them bring the old count back), reverse fine-tuning (how fast the old count returns) and steering (how much of the fine-tune one average shift recovers). Its cut-offs were guessed and nearly every "reuses" state was a short or slow fine-tune. Stage C1b fixed both: LOW fine-tuned on natural text in two tasks, "plus one" (answers the count of a plus one, so it can only be using the old count: it reuses) and "count b" (new), two learning rates, five seeds each; cut-offs were fixed on seeds 0 and 1 and committed before seeds 2 to 4 were read, giving 21 scored states. Stage C3 then moved the first two instruments to the 67M model's four open-pair word maps (eight seeds each): the rank-1 map (each key to the model's own top guess, old by construction), the cipher (arbitrary answers, new by construction), the elicit map (answers the model half-guesses, where VPD and the carry label disagreed) and a copy map. Pruning revival is the share of the fine-tune's distance from the original model that removing the top 40 units gives back; reverse fine-tuning is the steps needed to halve that distance.

What we found. On the counting set all three instruments labelled all 21 scored states right (17 needed; best recipe-only labeller 15), but so does a probe that reads only the answers (figures 2 to 4). On the 67M maps neither pruning nor reverse fine-tuning separates the rank-1 map from the cipher beyond seed noise: pruning gives back 0.46 against 0.39 (a gap of 0.07 against a bar of 0.14), and reverse fine-tuning takes 13.3 steps for rank-1 against 10.1 for the cipher, the wrong way round but under its bar of 4.3 (figure 4). Both do tell other maps apart, and both pass their planted control. VPD's r and stand-in ratio separate the two ends widely and put elicit at the old end (0.85 and 0.77 of the way to rank-1), as do the original model's own probability of the answer, the distance from the original model, and the carry to held-out keys graded (0.72); under the filed rules those readings are "distance and prior" (figure 5).

What it means. Every counting state performs one of two tasks perfectly, so "reuses" and "performs plus one" coincide and VPD could at best tie there. On the 67M maps the two cheap methods have not had a fair test: the distance from the original model is itself larger for the old-end map (8.5 against 4.8 nats), and the order they give tracks it, though no cause was tested. The E58 disagreement is therefore VPD against the carry label's fixed cut-off, which also calls the rank-1 map neither old nor new. Next, on the reuse line (Fable's advice, adopted, 2026-10-31): C2 (VPD scored on the C1b counting set), after a $0 rehearsal on the cluster, and a $0 step ladder on the open-pair set, larger pruning size first, to find where the ported instruments pass their control.

Found
  • The counting checks are clean, fast fine-tunes never use the old count, slow ones do early, and fine-tunes on natural texts keep it by construction, so their value is as anchors.
  • With cut-offs set on held-out seeds, pruning, reverse fine-tuning and steering each label all 21 scored states right (17 needed), but an answer-only probe does too, so the set cannot rank VPD above them.
  • On the 67M open-pair maps pruning (0.46 against 0.39) and reverse fine-tuning (13.3 against 10.1 steps) do not separate the old-by-construction map from the new one, though both tell other maps apart; VPD's readings, the prior and the distance all put elicit at the old end, so the comparison stays open.
