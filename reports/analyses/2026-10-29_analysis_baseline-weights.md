# Analysis: do weight statistics of a fine-tune tell eliciting from teaching? (E51)

*2026-10-29, coding agent (baseline-lead, B1 writer). Plan: `reports/plans/2026-10-29_plan_baselines-scoping.md`, section
"B1 = E51". Rules filed before the run: `experiments/baseline_weights/results/predictions.json`. Results:
`experiments/baseline_weights/results/results.json`. Explanation: `notebooks/E51_baseline_weights.ipynb`. Provenance at
the end.*

We measured three standard statistics of the weight change alone on eight small fine-tunes of the 67M model, four that
brought out copying the model could already do and four that taught it an arbitrary word mapping. Two of the three do
not tell the kinds apart, and the third passes our separation rule by a hair while following the size of the change,
which is larger for every mapping fine-tune. For the project's aim this answers the weight-only objection: VPD's reading
of which subcomponents Γ switches on is not something a spectrum of the weight change already shows on this set.

## What was done

The updates are E12's fine-tunes of all 24 decomposed matrices, as regenerated by E46 stage 0
(`~/commute-data/results-large/regating_table/`, dicts of fp32 δW keyed by matrix name). Copy, the elicit kind: tables
A and B (`ident32`, `ident32b`), seeds 0 and 1, 10 Adam steps each. Cipher, the teach kind: tables A and B (`rand32`,
`rand32b`), seeds 0 and 1, 10 steps for A and 30 for B, as E12 scored them. A ninth row, reported only: E36 task 1's code
fine-tune at step 10 (`code_step10.pt`), which learned nothing (held-out gain −0.05) and whose do-nothing state still
reads a re-gating share of 0.82 in E46. It is the null update the plan asks every method to read as "nothing changed".

W₀ is the target model's 24 matrices, read as tensors from its checkpoint (`vpd_files.TARGET_RUN/ckpt/TARGET_CKPT`,
the file `vpd.load_component_model` loads), in fp64. No model was built: the project's hook refuses
`load_component_model` in a CPU job, and only the weights are needed.

Per matrix, with k = 10:

- **Top-k share** = (‖U_kᵀ δW‖² + ‖δW V_k‖²) / (2‖δW‖²), U_k and V_k W₀'s top-10 left and right singular vectors. Pure
  re-weighting of W₀'s top directions reads 1; a random update reads (k/d_out + k/d_in)/2, 0.013 for a 768 × 768 matrix
  and 0.0081 for the MLP matrices.
- **Intruders** (Shuttleworth et al. 2024, arXiv:2410.21228, Definition 3.1 and Algorithm 1): the number of the top-10
  left singular vectors of W₀ + δW whose largest |cosine| with every left singular vector of W₀ is below ε = 0.5. The
  paper's k = 10 (Section 3; Figures 19a, 23) and ε = 0.5 where it fixes one value (Appendix I; Figure 19b).
- **Effective rank** of δW (Roy and Vetterli 2007): exp(H(p)), p_i = σ_i / Σσ.

Each statistic is averaged over the 24 matrices with weights ‖δW_m‖² / Σ‖δW‖². The null is a Gaussian matrix scaled to
‖δW_m‖ per matrix, one draw per update.

The control ran first and alone, and would have stopped the job on a miss: a pure re-weighting of W₀'s top-10 pairs at
ident32_s0's size per matrix; one random direction per matrix at twice W₀'s top singular value, the intruder count's
positive control; and the Gaussian null of ident32_s0 against its analytic share.

Figure: `experiments/baseline_weights/figures/weights.png`.

Only the effective rank passes the filed rule, by three percent of the spread. The top-10 share is about three times the
random value for every real update, but copy (0.0302 to 0.0307) sits between cipher A (0.0310, 0.0314) and cipher B
(0.0234, 0.0235). No update has an intruder; the injected direction reads exactly one, so the count was able to move.
The effective rank is 437 to 442 for copy and 404 to 428 for cipher; the gap of the means, 24.5, beats the larger
within-kind range, 23.9, which is cipher's table difference, not its seeds. VPD's re-gating share separates by a wide
margin: 0.76 to 0.80 against 0.27 to 0.29.

## Does the effective rank follow the kind or the size?

Figure: `experiments/baseline_weights/figures/erank_vs_size.png`.

It follows the size across all nine updates. Every cipher update is larger than every copy update: table A by about a
tenth, table B, trained 30 steps rather than 10, nearly twice. The rank falls in the same order: code fine-tune 0.53 →
562, copy 0.68–0.69 → 437–442, cipher A 0.75–0.76 → 421–428, cipher B 1.32–1.34 → 404–409. Within copy, where the sizes
are equal to 0.01, the rank spans 5. Reported, not filed: at matched step count, copy against cipher A alone, the gap
is 15.6 against a range of 6.8; but cipher A is still a tenth larger, so this set cannot separate size from kind. The
claim "effective rank tells teaching from eliciting" is not supported; it is not ruled out either.

## The update that learned nothing

The weight statistics give no reading of "nothing changed" that differs from the trained updates in kind: its share,
0.027, and zero intruders sit with the eight; its effective rank, 562, is the highest of the nine and still well below
the random update's 698. VPD's re-gating share calls it elicit-like, at 0.82. Neither method reads it as nothing; the
weight statistics at least do not call it elicit.

## Blind spots and power

The statistics are deterministic on fixed weights; the only spread is seeds and tables. The intruder count is close to
blind at this size: each matrix changed by 0.2 to 2.6 percent of its Frobenius norm, and no δW's top singular value
exceeded 8 percent of W₀'s, so no update could displace a top direction. The top-10 share and the rank are blind to
which inputs the update acts on, which is what E12's outside share weights by. Eight updates, one model, one task format.
