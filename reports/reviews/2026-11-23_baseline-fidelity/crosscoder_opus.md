targets (App. E.2, eqs. 33-36), probes with the fine-tune decoder (eq. 4), encodes with the threshold on, as the toolkit does (`closed_form.py:105`),
and E210 matched the authors' released Gemma crosscoder (relative norm within 1.5e-7; Spearman 0.988 for nu_r, 0.977 for nu_eps; 594 against 599 chat-specific latents).

So the trained object is the published method, adapted. What is not theirs is every number the paper reports from it.

## The three checks

1. **Citations.** `minder2025overcoming` (refs.bib:185-191) is right: "Overcoming Sparsity Artifacts in Crosscoders to Interpret Chat-Tuning", Minder, Dumas, Juang, Chughtai, Nanda, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2504.02922 (the PDF's p. 1 footer reads "39th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2025)"). `lindsey2024crosscoders` (refs.bib:192-197) is right: Lindsey, Templeton, Marcus, Conerly, Batson, Olah, "Sparse Crosscoders for Cross-Layer Features and Model Diffing", Transformer Circuits Thread 2024. Every live `\citet{minder2025overcoming}` refers to the crosscoder. Related Work's claim that its authors cannot tell new latents from shifted ones (`main.tex:193-194`) is accurate (source §5). The one miscitation, in §4, is struck in the working tree (row 16).
2. **Appendix departures (1)-(6).** Items (1), (2), (3) and (6) are accurate (checked: d at N 1,667 equals d at 5,000 on all 25 E203 sides). Item (4) is accurate. Item (5) is inaccurate in wording (row 9). The list is incomplete: it omits the per-model mean normalisation and the bias part's ceiling (row 3), the missing negative-beta filter (row 6), 100k latent-scaling tokens against the paper's 50M (row 7), the toolkit's 150M three-source default (row 8), k unscaled and the k sweep (row 10), position 0 and context length (row 12). The list describes E203; E235/E243 add the planted-head control, its bar of one half and the head scaling, which are ours and are listed nowhere as departures (row 2).
3. **"The reading is ours" where reported.** No. It is said only in appendix item (4) and in `appG`. Every place that reports a crosscoder number presents our share and our bar as the crosscoder's own outcome: `sec5_results.tex:161-165` and its caption, `sec5_3:83-87`, `sec7:34-35`, the abstract (`main.tex:53-54`), the contributions (`main.tex:125,681`), `appD:86-92`, `appF:128-130`, and the ladder caption at `main.tex:976-978` (rows 1, 2, 5).

## Verdict
For the trained crosscoder, yes: their pinned trainer and settings and a latent-scaling step validated on their released crosscoder. For its fine-tune-specific latents, no, until App. E.2's negative-beta exclusion is added (row 6, recomputed below): without it, block 3's planted control flips and the ladder top falls from 0.46 to about 0.
For what the paper reports, no: the share of the change, d, the planted control and its bar of one half are ours, and the authors' own output (the count) still responds to the plant. None of this is said where the numbers appear.
Fix: re-score the saved crosscoders with the filter and replace the numbers (block 3 no longer passes). Label every reported number "our reading of the crosscoder of Minder et al. (2025)" and report the count beside it. Fix item (5) and the §5 "0.73 shared" sentence.

Severity count (after the recomputation below): 2 blockers (row 16, fixed in the working tree, not yet pushed; row 6, the missing filter), 4 major (rows 1, 2, 4, 5), 10 minor (row 3 re-rated from major to minor).

## Recomputation: Minder et al. App. E.2's negative-beta exclusion (2026-11-23, follow-up)

**Agreement: I agree with the reviewer on all nine of its numbers,** to the digits it gave.

What it changes:
- **Block 3's planted control no longer passes.** Filtered 0.42-0.44 on every seed, under the bar of one half. The paper's "At block 3 the planted control passed only on a head that raised the Pile loss to 11.30 nats" (`main.tex:968-969`, `appD:90-92`) is false under the published test. So block 3's Gaussian and pair readings, which stage 1 ran because block 3 passed, have no passing control.
- **The ladder's top is an artefact of the missing filter.** Rungs 4-5 fall from 0.45-0.46 to at most 0.01, so "levelled off at 0.45 and 0.46" is wrong. The paper's conclusion (no size reaches the bar) still holds, more strongly. Block 2 seeds 0-1 barely move, but seed 2 drops from 0.37 to 0.004.
- **Does the 0.46 ceiling (row 3) still matter?** Not at rungs 4-5: filtered, the specific part is about 0, far below the ceiling of 1 minus the bias part (0.45-0.46), so the ceiling is not what holds the reading down there. Row 3 survives only as a disclosure: by construction the share counts the mean change, which no latent can carry. Its "may explain the flat top" consequence is withdrawn; the flat top came from negative-beta latents.
- **Row 2's counts.** After the filter the authors' own count reads 0, 2, 26, ~126, 21-23 and 27 up the ladder, against 0 for the model against itself. So the plant is still detected in their terms, but with an order of magnitude fewer latents at the top.
- **Not recomputed here:** E212's and E244's d values, and E203's other 24 sides. E203 e58 copy moves 0.12 → 0.10, so the built-pair readings likely move little, but that is unchecked.

Re-rating: row 6 goes from minor to **blocker**. The set reported as "the fine-tune-specific latents of Minder et al.'s crosscoder" is mostly latents their test excludes (87% at block 3, 91-93% at rungs 4-5), and one reported reading reverses.
