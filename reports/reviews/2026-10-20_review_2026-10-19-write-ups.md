# Review: errors in the 2026-10-19 analysis reports

On 2026-10-20 the notebooks for three experiments from 2026-10-19 were rewritten from their result files. The rewrite
found claims in the reports that the result files do not support. None of them changes a filed prediction's verdict,
but several change how a result reads. The rewritten notebooks state the correct values. The reports are left as written,
and this file lists what is wrong in them.

## `reports/analyses/2026-10-19_analysis_reading-a-coverage.md`

Source: `experiments/reading_a_coverage/results/coverage.json`.

1. **The sparsity control's count.** The report says 5 of 12 states clear their trigger line: " in" → " Fig" and all four
   " we" → " just" states. In fact 6 clear. The " we" → " just" do-nothing reads 0.990 against its line of 1.010, so it
   does not clear. The " which" → " was" do-nothing reads 1.150 against 1.101, so it does. The control still fails, but
   not only on rung 2.
2. **"The non-adversarial checks hold within the floor everywhere except r2_t275."** This is wrong.
   - The " we" → " just" pull-back is over the CI-masked line at all three batch orders: 1.114, 1.100 and 1.116,
     against 1.074.
   - The same three states are over the stochastic line, reading 1.028 to 1.037 against 1.006.
   - Three do-nothing states are also over the stochastic line.
3. **"The fine-tune alone breaks the 1.5 line on two tasks."** It breaks it on three. The report leaves out " it" → " is"
   at 1.58. Every do-nothing state is above the floor line of 1.138.
4. **Off-target change.** The pull-back changed predictions away from the trigger 1.76 to 2.50 times as much as the
   fine-tune did, not 1.6 to 2.5.
5. The old notebook said the two Pile row blocks disagree by 30 to 40% at the pull-back states. They disagree by 16 to 31%
   on " we" → " just" and by 61% on " it" → " is".

## `reports/analyses/2026-10-19_analysis_lora-plateau.md`

Source: `experiments/lora_plateau/results/lora_plateau.json`.

1. **"At the W₀ floor at every step" and "within one to three floors."** On the L40S used for this run, the floor is
   0.046, so three floors end at 1.14.
   - The lifted LoRA reads 1.26 at step 10 and 1.22 at step 100, about five floors above 1.
   - The do-nothing state at step 10 reads 1.17, also outside the band.
   - Only step 1000 and the do-nothing at step 100 are inside the band.
   - All of these are still well under the filed pass line of 2.
2. **Left out: the lift is worse than doing nothing.** The lifted state reads above the do-nothing state on the
   adversarial check at every step, by 0.08, 0.13 and 0.03. Putting the LoRA's update into the subcomponents never
   helps on this check.
3. **Left out: the unmasked check.** With every subcomponent switched on and Δ removed, the lifted state reads 1.35,
   1.27 and 1.21. So the subcomponents alone do not reproduce the lifted model on Pile.
4. **"Fails it more clearly the longer it trains."** This holds for Δ's share of the update's energy, which rises at every
   step. It does not hold for the share of the behaviour in Δ, which goes 0.19, then 0.11, then 0.31.
5. **The read side does not track the random line throughout.** From step 100 on, 25 to 30% more of the update reads
   inside the alive span than a random read side would. The pooled inexpressible ratio is unaffected.
6. **Card mismatch in the predictions.** `predictions.json` says the adversarial ratio is read on the L40 only, and it
   files the L40's band of 1.63. The scored run used an L40S. The predictions stand as filed; this is a note for reading
   them.
7. The calibration example in `.claude/skills/writeup/SKILL.md` repeats the wording "within one to three W₀ floors" for
   these numbers, in both the "before" and "after" versions.

## `reports/analyses/2026-10-19_analysis_lora-matched.md`

Source: `experiments/lora_matched/results/lora_matched.json`.

1. **The saturated-matrix explanation only fits seed 0.** The report says the lift leaves less in Δ than the one-matrix
   LoRA did because 60% of the update's energy lands on the four saturated matrices. Seed 1 puts 25% there and leaves the
   same share in Δ: 0.212 against seed 0's 0.218. Its inexpressible share over all 24 matrices is 0.31, against seed 0's
   0.19. The report's "pooled over all 24, LoRA 0.19 against fine-tune 0.17" is seed 0 only.
2. **Energy and behaviour move apart.** From step 2 to step 100, seed 0's inexpressible share over all matrices goes from
   0.19 to 0.44. Over the same steps, the share of the gain left in Δ goes from 0.22 to 0.11.
3. **The step comparison is not like for like.** The LoRA was scored at step 2 and the fine-tune at step 10. In the
   earlier run on the same card, the fine-tune's lift read 1.55 at step 2, inside this run's band of 1.63. So the run does
   not show that a LoRA lifts better than a fine-tune at equal training.
4. **A comparison across different nulls.** The old notebook compared the LoRA's share at step 100, 0.55, with the
   fine-tune's 0.27 at step 10. Their nulls differ, 0.674 against 0.531, so the comparable ratios are 0.82 and 0.50.
5. **Rounding.** "|cos| 1.000 on every matrix" is rounded: per matrix it is 0.9991 to 0.9998.
6. **Pending: the read-side check.** `results/read_side.json` is not on disk. Cluster jobs 22430 and 22431 were queued
   for it.
