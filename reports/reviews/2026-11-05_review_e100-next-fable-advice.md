## 1. The two hypotheses and what the table already says

**A (fed vs written):** the sum is low when the gain is carried by an unchanged nonlinear mechanism that the update tips with many small changes (no single revert undoes the tip), high when the gain is written into parts that add up. **B (copying vs key tables):** only in-context retrieval has such a mechanism in a 4-block 67M model; every other task is a table written into MLPs, whatever its label.

Three rows already strain B. (i) Half-known reads 0.47-0.56 and the cipher 0.67-0.74 on the same windows, keys and update size, 8 seeds each, no overlap; under B both are key tables and should tie. (ii) Lowercase reads 0.54-0.56 against the Pile cipher's 0.88-0.89 on the same recipe and rows (`table_amend1.json`; lowercase's gain is 1.1 nats per token over whole rows, under the 1-nat floor that was set for key tables). (iii) Emoticon, a constant answer that is not in the input, reads 0.30-0.37, below every copy row but one, and is discarded as "beside only". None of these was built as reuse, so none is a label; but B needs emoticon to be in-context copying of an earlier ":o" in the window, which nobody has checked.

**Why rank-1 reads written, and what it says.** Its values are the original model's top next token after "M key", but at a geometric-mean probability of 0.0057 (check, section 2). A 0.6% preference is nothing to tip: reaching 10.5 nats of gain means building a 24-key table, as the cipher does, and 9 of its 24 keys share one value (inventory T12), so part of the gain is a constant push, which the ladder rungs show reads 0.58-0.63. Rank-1 says the sum reads how the gain is made, not what the original model prefers. The fix is the same construction where the existing mechanism is strong.

## 3. A site-relative bar (question 2)

The check's own numbers are the caution. Its written plant reads 0.64, 0.72 and 0.86 on the copy, rank-1 and cipher sites. Dividing each fine-tune by its site's plant gives copy 0.47-0.63, cipher 0.78-0.86, rank-1 0.96-1.13: the copy-to-cipher gap shrinks from 0.22 (fixed bars 0.40 and 0.62) to 0.15. Subtracting does the same (copy 0.24-0.34 below its plant, cipher 0.12-0.19 below). So a 4-MLP plant is not the right written reference: the fine-tunes spread over about 8 of 28 parts with a third of their norm in heads (check, section 2), the plant over 4 with none.

**Rule.** Per state x on site s: the written reference W(s,x) is a plant with x's own per-part norm profile (`part_norms.json`, 47 states) across the 4 MLPs' `down_proj` (heads' `o_proj` columns if `locator.plant` is extended; say which was built), one common scale solved to x's gain within 5% as `spread_plant.py:71-77` does; ρ(x) = sum(x) / W(s,x). Bars set in the job's first stage, before any new state: ρ_fed = the largest ρ over the 13 copy rows (E62 copy, copy-24 seeds 0-7, ident32, forbidden copy); ρ_wr = the smallest over lookup, cipher-24 seeds 0-7, rand32 and the Pile cipher. Adopt the site-relative reading only if ρ_wr − ρ_fed is at least the fixed bars' gap of 0.22; otherwise keep the fixed bars and print each site's W beside as the caveat column. **Fed reference:** there is no fed-by-construction update without a located mechanism, because any update in one part sums to 1 by partition; where a mechanism is located (copy), the forbidden fine-tune is the fed reference, 0.32-0.36, and the native copy sits on it. Report that honestly rather than inventing one.

**Edge cases.** The scale cannot match the gain (status not "ok", or gain under 1 nat): no reference, fixed bars with a flag. W(s,x) under 0.5: the site is too convex for the sum, "site unreadable". W over 1 (parallel parts): use it as is, ρ falls below the sum. Negative single reverts: kept, the sum may go below 0. A ρ exactly at a bar: the bar's side. Seed spread in ρ is the copy rows' range divided by their W, about 0.16; a gap under that is "no call".
