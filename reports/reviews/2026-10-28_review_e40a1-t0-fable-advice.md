# T0 positive control and the paid run's stop rule — advice (2026-10-28, read-only)

## 1. The rate-insensitive plateau
- **Not a held-out generalisation gap.** The premise that training rows fall much faster is single-batch noise (curve values span 0.003-0.019 within any 20 steps). 20-step window means of the training-batch KL: T0 (3e-5) 0.0169 → 0.0122 (steps 41-60) → 0.0114 (81-100) → 0.0121 (181-200), i.e. 0.72 of start and flat; B 1e-5 0.0072 → 0.0047 (0.65); B 3e-5 0.0100 → 0.0071 (0.71); check_teacher 1e-5 0.0052 → 0.0031 (0.60) and 0.0031 at 281-300. Training rows plateau at 0.6-0.7 exactly where the fixed rows do (0.72 at 100, 0.59 at 216-300). So the pool sizes (web_pool=8 rows in the rehearsal!) are not the cause; the optimiser is.
- **Best explanation: an Adam floor from per-coordinate normalisation with 2-row batches on an isotropic target.** Adam moves every coordinate by ~lr per step whatever the gradient size; a random target spreads its KL over all 17M coordinates, each batch excites a different subset, so most coordinates get full-size steps with a noisy sign. Supporting: 3e-5 is *worse* than 1e-5 at step 50 (0.942 vs 0.867) and on training windows (0.0071 vs 0.0047), and T0 at 3e-5 flattens between 100 and 216 while 1e-5 still falls at 300 — a higher rate reaches a higher floor sooner; the two rates "agree" at step 100 only by crossing. Against: a pure noise floor should scale with lr², and the training-row floors are closer than that; part of it is that 2 rows per step resolve only a fraction of the target's directions per step, which also fixes with more tokens per step.
- **The isotropic target is harder than the real one.** A fine-tune δW is a sum of x⊗δ outer products from training-like data, so it lives in activation-excited directions; random offsets mostly do not. T0 therefore errs toward false stops: its pass says little extra about T1, its fail does not predict T1's. Not checkable from the files.
- **"A few positions dominate"**: unknowable, only the 8-row mean is logged. Cheap fix: log the 8 per-row KLs at every reading (no new measurement, 8 numbers).
- **The scaling nobody has measured, and the biggest risk to B:** α ∝ 1/√n_alive (KL ∝ α²·n), so α at 2.39e9 params ≈ 2.8e-3/√137 ≈ 2.4e-4, as the plan guesses; lr/α at the rehearsal's diverging 1e-4 was 0.043; at full size lr/α is 0.042 at 1e-5 and 0.125 at 3e-5. Equivalently one Adam step's KL perturbation ∝ lr²·n is 137× the rehearsal's. The 16× tokens per step cut gradient noise ~4× in std, not enough. Expect: both filed rates sit at or past the divergence seen at 1e-4, B finds "none qualifies" and stops after ~$3-5 of set-up and two 100-step arms. The rate list was sourced from 16 columns per site; the sourcing does not transfer.

## 2. Is 0.5 within 300 a sensible bar, and B's reading to proceed
- 0.5 is unsourced in both directions (the file says so) and was met by no rehearsal arm (0.59 best). It is a bar on the optimiser, not on what T1's P3/P5 readings need; no filed T1 fall exists to calibrate it. Keep it as a reporting line, not as the thing that burns the pod.
- B's full-size reading to proceed: best rate's step-100 fall ≤ 0.65 **and** still descending (step 50 → 100 drop ≥ 0.05 of start). 0.65-0.80 and descending: do not stop idle, run T0 to the cap as the arbiter (300 full-size steps are ~20-40 min; cost ≈ $1.5-3). Any reading above 1.2× start by step 25-50, or no rate below 0.8: the grid missed the rate (the √n argument above); stop and ask.

## 3. Options and recommendation
A. As filed: 0.5 at 300, read every 50, largest qualifying rate. Risk: `pick_rate` prefers the rate with the highest floor (3e-5 at the rehearsal), and at full size neither 1e-5 nor 3e-5 may qualify at all; a stop after B + T0 with a healthy apparatus.
B. (**recommended**, all $0, before the pod) (i) B's rate grid {1e-6, 3e-6, 1e-5, 3e-5}, highest first, each arm aborted at step 25 if the fixed-row KL exceeds 1.2× start (the 1e-4 arm was ×3.3 at 25), so diverging arms cost 25 steps; (ii) `pick_rate` = the qualifying rate with the lowest step-100 reading, not the largest lr; (iii) T0 continues from B's chosen-rate offsets (or B runs its chosen rate on to the cap and T0 only does the readings) instead of resetting — saves 100 full-size steps; (iv) T0's stop: miss = at the cap fall > 0.5 **and** flattened (last two readings within 3%); still falling at 300 → extend once to 600 (≤ $3) before any stop; (v) per-row KL logged at every reading. These are amendments to B_rate/T0 rules filed before the paid run, dated, not edits after it.
C. Weaken the bar only: fall monotone over the six readings and ≤ 0.7 at 300 plus the FineWeb ratio in [0.85, 1.15] (the half of T0 that is well posed). Cheapest, but leaves the rate grid wrong.
What would change the recommendation: if B at full size reads ≤ 0.6 at step 100 at 1e-5 or 3e-5, the √n fear is unfounded and the filed rule can stand (keep only (v)). If the per-row log shows one row carrying over half the KL, switch the reading to the median over rows or raise pc_rows to 16 before trusting any fall.

Friction: the amend1 B stage has no record of full-size step time, so the dollar figures in §2-3 are guesses from the rehearsal's 0.75 s/step × 16 tokens; the rehearsal's `web_pool=8` was a red herring that cost reading time; results JSONs keep only the 8-row mean, so two of the four hypotheses could not be tested from files.
