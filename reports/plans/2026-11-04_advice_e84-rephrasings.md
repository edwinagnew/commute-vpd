# Advice for E84: training TOFU facts in several wordings

*2026-11-04, for qwen-rl-lead. Read: the E81 plan, analysis and code, `predictions_amend2.json`, `after_check.py`, `train_*.json`, the TOFU files, and arXiv 2309.14316v3 (HTML, first 100k characters) for the paper's numbers. Marked (checked) or (recalled).*

## 1. Rows and number of wordings
**Recommend:** the 40 authors that carry TOFU's paraphrases (`retain_perturbed` + `forget10_perturbed`, 800 rows; all 800 are rows of `full` and none of `holdout10` is, checked), each row in 5 fixed pairs (q_i, a_i), i = 0..4, with pair 0 TOFU's own question and answer. That is 4000 sequences, batch 16, 5 epochs, 1250 steps: E81's schedule exactly, so the step grid and the exposure count per sequence (5) match, and only the wording varies. Each fact is seen 25 times, 5 per wording, which is the paper's regime of many exposures in several wordings rather than E81's one wording. Every trained row is also a test row.
- The paper (checked, Sections 4.2, 5.1): bioS single → multi5 lifts out-of-distribution QA from 9.7% to 41% by wording alone, and to 96.6% with sentence shuffling added; permute1 → permute5 from 4.4% to 70%; no multi2 numbers and no threshold are given, so 5 is the number to take. Its regime is pretraining a 124M model from scratch with many epochs, then QA fine-tuning; the numbers do not transfer, only the direction. Its celebrity result (checked, Section 6): augmenting only some people lifts the others from 4.4% to 86.8%, in pretraining; no analogue is claimed here.
- Few authors and the floor: `holdout10` (20 authors, 400 rows) stays the floor, as E81's, so the floor is unchanged; the 160 other authors of `full` are a second never-trained pool if wanted.
- Few authors and the size of δW: E81's norm is set by the schedule, not the data: 10.0 at 1e-5 and 18.0 at 2e-5 on identical rows, flat after step 1000 (17.94 → 18.00, checked in `train_2e-5.json`). Expect about 18 at 2e-5 with 800 facts; file 15–21 as an expected value, not a rule.
- **What changes it:** if qwen-decomp-lead needs the update to hold 4000 facts as E81's did, train all 200 authors with form k in epoch k (20,000 sequences, one pass, still 1250 steps). Each fact is then seen 5 times as in E81, once per wording; E81's wording recall at one exposure was near the floor (0.25 at step 200), so it risks failing for under-fitting rather than no transfer. If the queue is free it is a second arm at $0, same rules, lean "lower"; not the primary.

## 2. Who writes the wordings, and the checks
**Recommend:** Qwen3-1.7B itself, a stage R at the start of the same job, HF `generate` as `generated()` does: 800 rows × 4 question forms × 4 answer forms, four fixed instruction styles (shorter; indirect or imperative; fact first then context, the one-sentence stand-in for the paper's sentence shuffling since 75% of TOFU answers are one sentence, checked; plainer register), sampling at temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8 (Qwen3's non-thinking setting, recalled), seeded. E81's stage 0 generated 1200 answers in about 46 s (run.log, checked), so 6400 with up to 3 tries is about 5–15 min. Scripted, reproducible, $0, no download. Departure to state: TOFU's paraphrases are GPT-4's; the test paraphrase stays GPT-4's, so the test wording is out of the writer's distribution, which makes the test harder, not easier. Claude clerks for 6,400 sentences would be about 40 spawns with formatting risk and no seed; fallback only.
- Rephrase both: answers are the knowledge text the paper augments; questions are what the test varies and what breaks the question → verbatim-answer mapping that E81 learned (multiple choice rose 0.37 with no question at all).
- Checks, by script, a row kept only when every form passes, else regenerated up to 3 times, else dropped from train and test (count reported; stop if over 5% dropped): (a) every capitalised word not sentence-initial, every digit string and every quoted title in the original appears in the form, case-insensitive; (b) no capitalised non-initial word absent from the original appears (no new names); (c) word-set overlap with the original ≤ 0.75, and with TOFU's held paraphrase ≤ 0.75 and not identical (TOFU's own paraphrases share 0.40 of their words with the original, p95 0.63 for questions and 0.69 for answers, checked); (d) answer form ≤ 1.5× the original's words (max original 67, checked; `MAX_NEW` 128 holds).
- **What changes it:** a first-try drop rate over 25% means the 1.7B cannot keep the facts; then Haiku clerks write the 800 rows' forms, about an hour.

## 3. The test
**Recommend as the filed rule:** new-word recall (E81's `check_after.py` grader, words of the reference answer absent from the question, now filed) of the greedy answer to TOFU's `paraphrased_question`, over the kept rows, reference the original answer as in E81. Answered when para ≥ floor + 0.5 × (trained − floor), with trained = the same grader under pair 0's question (TOFU's original, trained in both experiments, so E81's 0.956 is the direct comparator) and floor = `holdout10` under its own questions, same state. Trained ≤ floor fails as "training failed", distinct from "no transfer". E81 at 2e-5 read 0.377 against a needed 0.59; halfway is lenient against the paper's 96.6% and the rule self-adjusts if trained wording drops below 0.95 because five wordings are no longer memorised verbatim.
- Fact-level grader, cheap, do it: TOFU's wrong answers are the paraphrased answer with the fact span swapped (checked), so key = words of `paraphrased_answer` absent from all five wrong answers: median 6 words, 3 rows empty, 28 rows whose key is wholly in the question (skip both). Report key recall on para, on pair 0 and at W0, no rule, as the "fact not wording" reading; its floor is W0 on the same rows, since names and dates cannot come from style. Keys of descriptive facts carry filler ("weaves", "shape"), which is why it is reported, not filed.
- Expected at W0: para 0.167 ± 0.02 (E81, same prompt, greedy), floor 0.190 ± 0.02, pair 0 unread in E81, expect 0.15–0.25; key recall unread, expect under 0.15.
- Edge cases: rows dropped by the checks leave both sets; a row with no new words is skipped (none of the 800 now, n = 800 in `after_check.json`); a floor above 0.30 is reported as style drift; ties do not arise.

## 4. Rates and the control
**Recommend:** 2e-5 only (TOFU's Phi-1.5 rate, per E81's plan). E81 showed it ahead on every count (trained wording 0.956 vs 0.757, para 0.377 vs 0.302) with the same real-fact loss, and both norms (10, 18) are far from anchor 1's 0.49, so 1e-5 buys nothing. If 2e-5 fails with trained wording still over 0.9, the amendment is more wordings or epochs, not a lower rate. Keep step 1 (zero), step 2 |δW| 0.00208–0.00312 (E81 read 0.00237; Adam's first step is data-independent) and step 250 loss ≤ 0.95 × first batch as in-code checks.
- **The control, first and alone:** W0's new-word recall on the 800 paraphrased questions must reproduce E81's 0.167 within 0.02 (same model, prompt, suffix, grader, greedy), with the never-trained read at 0.190 ± 0.02; a miss means the apparatus differs from E81 and the job stops before R or T. The data stage's drop-rate stop is the second gate.

## 5. Drop
Drop: real-fact multiple choice and `ASK_REAL`, `real()` (0.36–0.79 by suffix at W0: it read answer length); TOFU multiple choice, `choice_bar`, the choice clause of `answered`, the tracked choice every 100 steps, the no-question and other-author reads, the at-chance stage, "first past bar". Replace the tracked number with new-word recall on 100 rows at saved steps 200, 500, 1000 (about 15 s each), so the early grid says when content appears. Final read: the last step only. Keep " Answer in one sentence." on every TOFU row, trained and read: without it the end token cost 24 nats (E81 Amendment 1) and training would teach stopping; E84 stays comparable to E81.
