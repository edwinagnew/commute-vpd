# P2.31 run 2 (last): the crosscoder as its authors run it

Phase 1 design (no code, no E-number yet). Run 1: `reports/plans/2026-11-17_plan_p2-31-crosscoder.md`, stopped at its
planted control (`experiments/crosscoder/results/stage0.json`). Literature evidence, pre-mortem and source lines:
`reports/plans/2026-11-17_plan_p2-31-run2_appendix.md`.

**The request in the Terms' words.** The user (2026-11-17): "implement the crosscoder experiment according to the best
performing technique in the literature", and "if any existing papers publish their code, use that". So: for each saved
fine-tune of the nine built pairs and its size-matched version, train the literature's best crosscoder between the base
67M and the fine-tune at block 2's input with its authors' released code and settings, find the fine-tune-specific
latents by the authors' own procedure, and report the share of the activation change carried by shared latents against
fine-tune-specific ones. Descriptive; no side is called reused or new.

## Which technique

**The BatchTopK crosscoder with latent scaling (Minder et al. 2025, NeurIPS), run by the authors' current code.** It is
the only published method that measures, rather than imposes, how a change splits between shared and fine-tune-only
latents, and it has the head-to-head evidence: against the L1 crosscoder it finds more fine-tune-specific latents at
every latent-scaling threshold (their Fig. 2c), its fine-tune-only latents show no complete shrinkage (error ratio near
0, against up to ~0.5 for L1) and no decoupling (§3.1), and its top half by relative norm carries 50% of the KL change
against 6% for the bottom half (Fig. 4). Newer methods win on other questions but cannot give this reading: the diff-SAE
recovers more of the behaviour (88% of the KL gap against 60%, Dumas, Minder and Nanda 2025) but has no shared side; the
Delta-Crosscoder (2026) finds 10 of 10 fine-tune objectives against 4 of 10, but it masks shared latents out of its
change loss, so the shared share is near 0 by construction, and its code is not released; dedicated-feature crosscoders
(Jiralerspong and Bricken 2026) fix the shared/exclusive sizes in advance and were tested only across architectures.

**What it changes from run 1.** The authors never apply the 0.9 band to a BatchTopK crosscoder: only 134 of its 73,728
latents reach 0.9 (Table 4). They rank latents by relative norm, take the top N (3,176 in the paper; 5,000 in their
toolkit), and let latent scaling decide which are fine-tune-specific (ν_r < 0.5, ν_ε < 0.2). Run 1's planted latent (relative norm
0.865, the largest of any alive latent, carrying 0.987 of the change) would have been a candidate under that rule. Run 1
also trained on ~19% of their tokens, squared the error, used TF32, and wrote its own trainer.

## Design

- **Code from the authors** (pinned; the appendix lists files and lines). `dictionary-learning @
  git+https://github.com/science-of-finetuning/crosscoder_learning.git@d1fd6f3fea41fc1123699cc09f4cdad47ca35160` in
  `pyproject.toml` (deps: torch, nnsight>=0.6, einops, datasets, zstandard). It supplies `BatchTopKCrossCoder` (model,
  thresholded encode, decode, per-model normalisation) and `BatchTopKCrossCoderTrainer.update` (loss, aux loss, θ,
  warm-up, k annealing, gradient clipping). Settings from `diffing-toolkit@617d926` `configs/diffing/method/crosscoder.yaml`;
  that package itself cannot be installed (it pulls vllm, streamlit, openai), so latent scaling is ported line for line
  from its `latent_scaling/closed_form.py` and checked against a direct least-squares fit. Read-only reference clones go
  outside the repo, as `~/param-decomp` does.
- **Ours, the glue only.** `src/commute/crosscoder.py` shrinks to: the activation stream (67M model, block 2's input, position 0
  dropped as the toolkit's `ignore_first_n_tokens` does), the data mix, top-N selection with the ν test, and the reading d
  (shared part minus fine-tune-specific part, computed after undoing the per-model normalisation). `run.py` keeps its
  stages and stops.
- **Settings, all copied:** 32× = 24,576 latents, k 100 (annealed from 1,024 over 2,000 steps), batch 2,048, Adam
  lr 1e-4, 1,000 warm-up steps then constant, 2 epochs over 100M tokens (97,656 steps), init norm 1.0 with transpose and
  shared init, per-model normalisation to RMS 100, aux k = d/2, α 1/32, dead after 10M tokens, θ as an EMA (0.999)
  from step 1,000, unsquared L2 loss, fp32. Latent scaling on 100k training tokens; shared = relative norm 0.4 to 0.6.
- **Departures (4).** (1) The 67M model, d 768, so the dictionary is 24,576. (2) No chat model, so the paper's 50M chat
  tokens become 50M tokens of the pair's own task format: fresh windows from each task's window generator on Pile
  rows outside the score half, repeated only where a generator cannot make enough (repeats counted and reported). The
  other 50M are Pile train rows, the FineWeb slot. (3) N = 1,667: the toolkit's 5,000 scaled by dictionary size as the
  expansion factor scales it; N = 5,000 is also reported. (4) The reading d and its controls are ours; the source has none.

## Controls first, alone, with stops

1. **Base against itself:** activations equal; every alive relative norm at 0.5 exactly; no latent passes the ν test;
   FVE ≥ 0.80 (paper 0.876, run 1 0.873). Fails only on a code fault.
2. **Planted direction (e54/elicit answer positions, about 1% of tokens):** expected, from the source's ranking and its
   latent-scaling definition, the planted latent ranks first by relative norm, has ν_r and ν_ε near 0 (the base side
   cannot use a direction it never shows), and fine-tune-specific latents carry ≥ 0.9 of the change (run 1: 0.987 on one
   latent). Stop if it is outside the top N or fails the ν test.
3. **One pair, the random update and a crosscoder reseed** (run 1's stage 1, its bars kept).

## Cost (appendix: arithmetic)

Run 1 measured 4.75 min per crosscoder for 37.2M tokens (H100 SXM, TF32, batch 4,096). Scaled to 200M tokens, batch
2,048 and fp32: **about 38 min and $2.80 per crosscoder** (32 min and $2.35 if TF32 is allowed). The cell needs **112**:
68 states, 40 size-matched states (`stage2_sides()`: 106) plus the 2 controls, the random update and the reseed.

| Set | Crosscoders | fp32 | TF32 | Wall, 20 H100 pods |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All, the share's seeds | 112 | ~$327 | ~$278 | ~5.3 h |
| Size-matched only where c < 0.95 (6 pairs) | 91 | ~$269 | ~$229 | ~4.6 h |
| At most 3 seeds a side | 76 | ~$227 | ~$193 | ~3.9 h |

**Gated** (over $60). The faithful full set is over the $230 limit. Rule fixed now: stage 0 measures minutes per crosscoder,
fp32 and two-per-card; the job then runs the largest set in the table whose cost at those minutes is under $230. The 3-seed set
departs from the cell's "same seeds as the share", so it needs the user's yes.

## Gates (record in the appendix)

1. Pre-mortem: written, every heading. 2. Settings sourced: each to a file and line. 3. Rehearsal: tiny run of every stage on
cluster `debug` ($0): `uv sync` with the new pin on Python 3.13, train, save, resume, latent scaling against
least squares, reading, stage files. Then a 5-minute first stage on the paid H100: step time fp32 vs TF32, one vs two per
card, peak memory. 4. Early checks: loss falling and FVE at step 2,000 inside the band the rehearsal measures; dead share
≤ 0.2 (paper 12%). 5. One review by a fresh Opus agent. 6. The user's go-ahead.
