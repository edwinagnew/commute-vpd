## The request, in the Terms' words

Amendment 5 put back the trigger outputs of two disjoint sets of subcomponents: S, the set Γ switched on, and D, the
subcomponents outside S with the largest first-order take-back toward the target, matched to S's count per matrix.
On ` which` each takes back about a fifth of the gain. Are they two routes to the same part of the gain (restoring both
takes back about a fifth) or two parts (about two fifths)? And on emoticon, does S's target-pointing half alone take
back what the first-order sum says (0.42-0.46, about D's level), as the check read from first-order numbers only?

## Definitions

- ρ(X): as Amendments 3-5, on the held-out windows. S and D: Amendment 5's members, read from `amend5.json` (D is
  disjoint from S by construction; asserted).
- **S ∪ D**: both restored in one pass.
- **S⁺, S⁻**: S's members with training e_c > 0 (pointing toward the target, by Amendment 5's training-window scores
  in `results/large/amend5_scores.pt`) and ≤ 0. Emoticon and ` which`, both seeds.
- **Overlap reading** a = ρ(S ∪ D) − max(ρ(S), ρ(D)).

## Decision tree (P14)

Per scored seed (G ≥ 1, ρ(S) ≥ 0.10 and ρ(D) ≥ 0.10): "separate" if a ≥ 0.5 × min(ρ(S), ρ(D)); "redundant" if
a ≤ 0.05; else "partial". A task takes its seeds' common class, else "seeds disagree"; a task with a seed failing the
0.10 floors is "unscored" (by Amendment 5, ` we` is). Reading on ` which` (the task where S and D tie), with emoticon
reported beside it:
- **"separate"**: S carries a part of the gain the top target-pointing subcomponents miss. Card reading: Γ's set is
  not interchangeable with a target-aware ranking; options (b) upstream or (c) Qwen.
- **"redundant"**: S and D are interchangeable routes. Card reading: on this model, the switched-on set's share is
  what target-pointing subcomponents carry; option (a), close the 67M restore line.
- **"partial" or "seeds disagree"**: card with the numbers; the user picks.

## What each number is blind to

- Shares need not add: restoring many subcomponents saturates (every alive one together takes back only 0.38-0.49),
  so even fully separate parts can read "partial". The bar for "separate" (half the smaller set's share added) allows
  for that; "redundant" needs the union to add no more than the noise margin.
- S⁺ and S⁻ are split by a first-order score on the training windows; restored on the held-out windows.

## Noise floors

0.05: Amendment 3's random sets never exceeded 0.029; S's seeds differ by at most 0.023. On ` which` min(ρ(S), ρ(D))
is 0.18-0.19, so "separate" needs a ≥ about 0.09, clear of 0.05.

## Edge cases checked before filing

ρ(S) or ρ(D) below 0.10: unscored, so min(...) is never small or negative where the rule is read. a can be negative
(the union takes back less than one set): reads "redundant". S⁺ or S⁻ empty restores 0 (R0). G < 1: nan, caught first.
Ties at a bar: "separate" is checked first, then "redundant": a = 0.05 reads "redundant" unless the "separate" bar is also 0.05 (min exactly 0.10), when it reads "separate".
