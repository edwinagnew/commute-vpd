# Plan: the activation difference lens as a scorecard baseline (cell P2.59)

We add Minder et al.'s activation difference lens (ADL) to the 67M scorecard as one more model-diffing baseline. Per
fine-tune it takes the mean change in the middle-layer residual on the first five positions of unrelated Pile text, and
asks how much of the fine-tune's task gain is lost when the fine-tuned model is put back to the base model along that
one direction. It is a column beside the crosscoder and neuron baselines, read on the nine built pairs, the five key
tasks and matched random updates. There is no verdict on reuse.

Cell P2.59 (due 2026-11-23, 2 runs, cluster debug only, $0). User, 2026-11-22: "run the activation difference lens too, on the cluster".
Code: `src/commute/adl.py` (the port), `experiments/adl/run.py`, `cluster/adl.sbatch`. Filed values:
`experiments/adl/results/predictions.json`.

## The method (paper arXiv 2510.13900; code pinned at diffing-toolkit 617d9266)

- **Text and positions.** The first k = 5 positions of each of 10,000 pretraining texts (paper Sec. 3; toolkit `n: 128`,
  `max_samples: 10000`, fineweb). Here: positions 0-4 of the 8,192 cached Pile val rows. Pile is the 67M's own training corpus.
- **Layer.** The middle layer: the toolkit takes `layers_output[int(0.5 (L - 1))]` (`get_layer_indices`). For L = 4 that is
  the output of block 1, which is block 2's input (`in2`). This is the paper's l = floor(L/2) counted from 1.
- **Difference vector.** delta_bar_j = mean over texts of h_ft - h_base at that layer, position j (`method.py compute_differences`).
- **Logit lens.** softmax(W_U ln_f(delta_bar_j)) (`utils/model.py logit_lens`), top 20 tokens. Token relevance is the share of those 20 that are relevant (paper Sec. 3.1; an LLM grader in the paper), maximised over positions.
- **Patchscope.** The vector, scaled by lambda, is put at the last token of three identity prompts; lambda is chosen by an LLM grader from 30 values; tokens are intersected over the prompts (App. C.1). **Not run here** (below).
- **Steering.** delta_bar_j is unit-normalised and rescaled to the fine-tuned model's mean activation norm (`util.py:122`). It is then added times alpha at every position while the *fine-tuned* model generates. Alpha is the largest strength an LLM grader still calls coherent, found by binary search on [0, 100] (Sec. 3, App. C.3). **Not the scalar here** (below).
- **Causal effect** (Sec. 5, eqs. 1-2, `causal_effect.py`, `patch_direction: base_to_ft`). In the fine-tuned model, the component of every position's activation along delta_bar_j is set to the base model's. The rise in loss on the fine-tuning data is what the direction carries.

## The scalar, filed in advance

**D, the ADL direction share** = max over j of [mean answer log-odds of the fine-tuned model minus the same with its
block-2 input reverted to the base along delta_bar_j] divided by the fine-tune's gain G. The rows and log-odds are
E143's read rows, the ones the scorecard's shares use (`holes_fill lo_at`). This is the paper's own per-model
quantitative test (eq. 1-2), turned into a share. It needs no strength: only the direction's span is reverted. For a
random update, G is its paired fine-tune's gain. Under E143's gain floor (1 log-odds) D is undefined (nan).

- **Blind to:** anything after block 2 that does not pass through its input (blocks 2-3 can carry the whole gain at D =
  0); any change that is not one constant direction across unrelated text (a key-dependent change cancels in the mean);
  the direction's sign and size; which part of the model carries the gain; reuse against new.
- **Do-nothing** (control state times 0): delta_bar is exactly 0. Every reverted readout equals the unreverted one, so
  each numerator is exactly 0 and G = 0, and D is **undefined (nan)**, by the definition.
- **Random update:** |D| < 0.01. Its own gain is about 0, so it has nothing to lose along any direction.
- **Positive control** (E237's `e58/copy_s0` at c = 1, gain 16.45): D in [0.05, 0.6], low confidence (see the stop rule).
- **Why not the steering share the cell suggested.** Its strength is set by an LLM grader judging generated text for
  coherence. The 67M cannot be graded that way, and every substitute rule we could write (an off-target KL budget, a
  best strength on task windows) is unsourced or turns the lens into a task-supervised probe. The causal-effect share
  is the paper's own number and has no strength to choose. Steering is in the "Not run" list. If the lead prefers the
  steering share, it is one function of `run.py` and the cell's second run.
- **Side reading (no rule):** the lens, i.e. per j the share of the top-20 logit-lens tokens that are among the task's answer tokens (the
  targets of its read rows), and the best rank of any answer token. The chance best rank for 96 answers is about 518.
- **Column:** per built pair, reuse and new D lists, gap = reuse mean - new mean, and the seed-range call, in
  `final.json`'s shape (`results/column.json`). Per key task, three seeds beside E237's three random updates.

## Departures from the paper and the authors' code

1. Ported, not run: the toolkit drives HF models through nnsight. Our model is a VPD ComponentModel target (`steering.Net` hooks).
2. Pile val rows (the model's own corpus, packed and pre-tokenised, no BOS) instead of fineweb document starts. There are 8,192 texts, not 10,000. Only positions 0-4 are forwarded: the model is causal, so they equal the toolkit's 128-token pass.
3. No Patchscope and no LLM-graded token relevance: "relevant" is "one of the task's answer tokens".
4. The causal effect is read as a drop in answer log-odds at the read position over the fine-tune's gain, not as cross-entropy over the fine-tuning documents. It is maximised over positions 0-4 (the paper maximises token relevance over positions; its Fig. 7 plots each position).
5. The random-difference directions are drawn from Pile rows 0-127 (the toolkit uses chat data).
6. No steering of the fine-tuned model and no agent.

## Items (existing builders, no copied code)

- **Five key tasks:** E237's 15 states at E237's common size and E237's 15 random updates, through E244's `Items`. Each size is checked against E237's stage 1.
- **Nine built pairs:** E187's pairs, the larger side times E187's c (`size_matched` `pairs()`, `m67.json`). Each size is checked against m67's. 81 states; checked to equal E257's item list (`rb_states summary_items.json`) on 2026-11-22.
- **Pair randoms:** one per pair side (18). A Gaussian at the side's first state's per-matrix norms times its c (`finetune.random_direction`, E237's construction), its G being that state's.

129 items in all.

## Stage 0, first and alone (run 1 of 2)

The do-nothing must read exactly 0 (zero_tol 1e-9). The control's gain must repeat E244's saved 16.4505 (bar 1e-4). Then
**pass iff** D(control) >= 0.05 (E143's inert line), D(control) > the largest of the paper's 64 random-difference
directions' shares on the control, and D(control) >= D(its random update) + 3 sd of those 64. These bars are measured in
the same job. A miss writes `stage0.json` with `passed: false` and exits 1. `items` refuses to start until it says
passed. If the control fails, the cell closes on stage 0: ADL's direction does not carry this narrow fine-tune's gain.
That is said in advance as a possible result, not a bug.

## Runtime and memory (estimated, not measured: no GPU here)

E244's neuron stage read these same items at 2-3 s each, for about 10 forwards over the read rows. An ADL item costs
about 8 such forwards plus 32 forwards of 512 x 5 tokens. Estimate ~3 s x 129 items = ~7 min, plus ~2 min of loading
(the E53 tasks build), so about 10-15 min. Stage 0 adds 64 directions on one task, ~65 forwards a batch, about 2 min.
Peak GPU memory is about 6 GB (E244 peaked at 5.7 GB on the same reads). Both fit cluster debug (2 h); the sbatch asks for 1 h.

## Design check

- **Goal:** part 2 of the plan, comparing simple baselines against the working definition on the 67M. ADL is the
  model-diffing method the user named. Filling its column puts it on the scorecard's shared axes.
- **Nearest tried:** the crosscoder (E203, E235, E244) and fair steering (E143). Steering reads a vector taken on the
  task's own windows. ADL takes its vector from unrelated text and is scored by the paper's direction test, so it adds
  "what diffing finds without task data".
- **Patch or step:** a step: a new column the cell names. It is not a fix of a previous run.
- **Decisions:** none departed. Descriptive, no reuse verdict (the definition is still under revision).
- **Definition:** none is added. D is a baseline's reading, not a rule of the definition.
- **What would change what we do:** if the control fails, ADL is reported as not surfacing narrow 67M fine-tunes and no
  items run. If it passes, the column tells whether a cheap diffing direction tracks the share's pair order.
- **Control first:** stage 0 runs alone, with its stop rule. The do-nothing can fail on any nonzero numerator. The
  repeat can fail on loading the wrong item or task.
- **Numbers:** D per item (do-nothing nan, randoms ~0, control 0.05-0.6) and the lens side reading. Nothing else is filed.
- **Saved:** per-item JSON with the per-position removed log-odds, shares, |delta_bar| and the lens top-20 ids
  (`results/items/`). Stage 0 also saves the 64 random-direction shares.

## Not run

- Patchscope, the LLM-graded token relevance, the steering of generations, and the interpretability agent all need LLM graders the 67M setting does not have.
- The steering share with a strength rule (see above).
- Layers other than the middle one (the paper's App. F.1 layer ablation).
