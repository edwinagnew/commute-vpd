## What is run

We read the working definition as decided on 2026-11-18, unchanged, on items whose answer is known by construction.
There are four levels: neurons and heads, heads and MLP layers, MLP and attention layers, and blocks. Each level also
has the embedding and the unembedding. For each item and level the run finds K\*, the smallest set of variables that
keeps F's output on the item's windows D within α = 0.9 of the way from "everything mean-ablated" back to F. It grades
each variable in K\* by s_u, the squared cosine between F's and M's write on the same input setting, averaged over every
position of D in both runs. A pure gain counts as kept and a grafted variable scores 0 (ask 51). It reports
R = mean s_u over K\*, f = the changed share of K\* and ρ′ = mean s_u over the changed variables in K\*, so that
R = 1 − f(1 − ρ′).

Items: P1.31's four edits (reuse small, reuse large, new few, new thin); E195's ×2, ×10, moved copy and rebuilt;
six grafts (E201's head, E206's four last-layer heads and its 256 neurons); E137's four pieces. Only the four edits
enter the pass rule. The rest are controls of the declarations or are reported.

**The premise E213 left open.** What must R show to track reuse rather than update size? Reuse large is reuse small
plus head (2,4) turned up until its weight change matches the larger new edit's. If a level puts reuse large above both
new edits, R at that level is not reading update size. E195's ×2 and ×10 are the opposite check: the same label at
nine times the gain. If R(×10) is below R(×2) at a level, that level reads size for a gain inside a merged variable.

## Expected values

- **Do-nothing** (F = M): R = 1 and f = 0 at every level, with every s_u = 1 to within 1e-6.
- **Positive control**, E201's grafted head at the two head levels: the graft is in K\* with s = 0, so ρ′ = 0 and
  R = 1 − 1/|K\*|. That is 2/3 if K\* = {embedding, graft, unembedding}.
- **The four edits, by hand** (appendix §3). These are illustrative: they assume a cosine of 0.5 at the cue for the
  reuse write and 0.2 for the new write, with the cue at 1 of 41 positions. Reuse small reads about 0.998 at every
  coarse level. New few reads about 0.994, because its K\* is about three variables against reuse's six to ten. Reuse
  large reads about 0.998 at the head level, where the gain on (2,4) is kept. At the layer and block levels it reads
  about 0.97 to 0.99, because there the gain changes the attention layer's write at every position. New thin reads
  0.9 or more at the neuron level.

## Predictions (filed; rules and thresholds in the predictions file)

- **P1.** The neuron level does not pass. New thin reads 0.9 or more there, above new few.
- **P2.** The head-and-MLP-layer level passes: both reuse edits beat both new edits by more than that level's band.
- **P3.** The layer and block levels do not pass: reuse large reads at or below a new edit.
- **P4.** ×2 and ×10 read R = 1 within 1e-6 at the neuron level. At the three coarser levels, R(×10) is below R(×2)
  by more than the band.
- **P5.** Every head graft is in K\* with ρ′ = 0 at both head levels, and reads below reuse small at the
  head-and-MLP-layer level.
- **P6.** The moved copy reads below 1 at the neuron level and 1 within 1e-6 at the three coarser levels.
- **P7.** Where P2 passes, the sign of the gap holds at α = 0.8 and 0.95 and on the half of D that changed most (T6).
- **Reported only:** E195 rebuilt, E137's pieces (Always on is predicted at R = 1 with f = 0), and the side reading
  graded at the read position only.

**Pass rule (the cell's).** A level passes when min(R reuse small, R reuse large) − max(R new few, R new thin) is
greater than that level's band, at α = 0.9 on all of D. Both reuse edits must also have f > 0 there, so a reuse edit
cannot pass by leaving K\*. The band is 3 × the largest, over the four edits, of three spreads: R's spread across
search orders, the split-half difference, and the range of R over tied minimal K\*. The cell passes if some level
passes.

## Order, route, cost

1. **Stage 0, alone (cluster).** Do-nothing at all four levels, the gates, M's E62 induction top-1, ×10's s at the
   neuron level, and E201's graft at the head level. Any miss stops the job.
2. **Stage 1 (cluster).** Build the four edits with P1.31's checks, which stop on a miss. Measure the bands at the
   block and layer levels.
3. **Stage 2 (cluster).** All items at the three coarse levels. The block and layer levels are exhaustive (64 and
   1,024 subsets), and the head-and-MLP-layer level is greedy plus a polish. Each item's file is written as it ends.
4. **Stage 3 (pod).** The neuron level, greedy in chunks: 5 orders for the edits, plus the α, D and half variants, and
   one order for the other items.

Stages 0 to 2 run on the MATS cluster `debug` QoS on an L40 at $0, in about 1 h. If they have not started within 5
min, they go to a pod. Stage 3 runs on a RunPod H100 SXM at $4.40/h, at about 2 min per search (E208's 110 s per
order) for about 55 searches. That is about 2 h, or about $10, and it may be split over two pods. The upload is code
plus about 0.2 GB of inputs, about 20 s at 10 MB/s, and the model comes from W&B in about 65 s. The stage 3 stop: if
the first search projects past 3 h, stop and report.

**Amendment, 2026-11-19 (code error, not a filed rule).** Run 1 stopped in stage 1 on `no detector for cue 0`. The cause:
layer 0's MLP has no bias, so as β grows the detector ratio tends to a β-free limit (mean ReLU on A′ over the Pile
99th percentile), and doubling β cannot reach 10× when that limit is under 10. The fix keeps the filed direction where
its limit passes and otherwise uses the ridge-whitened Pile direction (`known_edits.detector`); both limits are
recorded in the edits record. Thresholds and predictions are unchanged. Stage 0 stands. Stage 2 at the head-and-MLP
level measured ~2-4 min per search on an H100 (E201: 117-234 s, every order at the polish cap), so stage 2 goes to
up to four H100 pods by item group; neuron_head (stage 3) was dropped by the user on 2026-11-19.
