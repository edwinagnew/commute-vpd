<div class="card key decision" id="decision-definition-swaps"><span class="chip refuted urgency">Blocking</span><div class="k">Question for you · definition-lead · new 2026-11-09</div><p class="small">Nothing runs until this is decided. Part 1 of the paper is due at its gate on 2026-11-13.</p><h3>The proposed definition of reuse rests on one test: does the fine-tune still work on the original model's values? Should we check that this test can tell fine-tunes built one way from those built the other?</h3>
<p><strong>The question.</strong> You tentatively took up this definition: a fine-tune reuses a mechanism when it works on values taken from the original model. Concretely, run the original model on ordinary text, take the value of some intermediate step from one fixed place in its activations, and put that value into the fine-tune at the same place. If the fine-tune then answers as that value says, about as well as with its own values, the original model already supplied what the fine-tune needs. You also chose two points: a fine-tune that adds only a new last step gets its own label, "wrapped", and the original model need only hold the value on ordinary text. What is not settled is how well the swap must work to count. No paper gives that number, and it should not be a figure we pick. So the choice here is whether to measure it.</p>
<p><strong>Why a share and not a raw score.</strong> A raw swap score means little alone, since a swap can work a little by chance. So each score is placed between two references from the same networks: 0 if the original model's values help no more than a random network's, 1 if they help as much as the fine-tune's own. The same scale then serves every task.</p>
<p><strong>The evidence that this can work.</strong> We already have fine-tunes whose route is fixed by how they were trained, so the bar need not come from the tasks it will judge. Some copy fine-tunes were forced through the original model's copying heads, with every other weight reset to the original at each step: what reaches those heads is the original model's, so the share should be near 1. Cipher fine-tunes learn an arbitrary table, which gives the original model's ordinary run no reason to hold the answers, so the share should be near 0. Another copy fine-tune was forbidden from those heads and still reached 0.94 of the normal gain. It learned the same behaviour by the other route, so at those heads the test should fail. That checks that the answer depends on the fine-tune and not only on the task. <strong>Nothing has been measured yet</strong>: no swap between the original model and a fine-tune has been run under this definition.</p>
<p><strong>What could go wrong.</strong> If the forced and cipher shares overlap, there is no bar and the definition fails on this model; part 1 of the paper then becomes why existing definitions fail, as the plan says. The case-label fine-tune may also read "new": patching the direction that reads out case flipped at most 0.014 of its labels against the 0.50 we had filed, so it may not read that direction at all.</p>
<p><strong>Options.</strong> (a) Stop at the wording. Nothing runs and the definition stays on paper, so the gate has no result to read. (b) Saved fine-tunes only: one free cluster job checks the swaps on the original model and a random network, and stops there if they fail. It then sets the bar and reads the copy-against-lookup, case-label and repeat-label pairs. No new training and under an hour in all (estimated). Its limit is that the bar rests on copying alone, so it may be a fact about copying. (c, recommended) As (b), plus one new forced and forbidden pair on the case label, two seeds, one more free job of perhaps half an hour (estimated). Case label is a different kind of step with a named value, and the saved lowercase pair cannot do this job, because its forbidden fine-tune stopped at step 5.</p>
<p><strong>What happens next.</strong> Under (b) or (c) you get a bar, or the statement that there is none, and then a label for each seed of each pair. You adopt the wording, change it or drop it from those results, by a dated line in the decisions file.</p>
<p><strong>Recommendation: c.</strong> It costs no money and about half an hour more, and it is the only option that tests whether the bar holds beyond copying.</p>
