**The question in one sentence.** Teacher training of a broad fine-tune stayed about twice as fragile as the original
decomposition under the paper's check, both when trained four times longer and when started from the adjoint split:
should we train it longer still, test whether the frozen Γ or the subcomponents' span is the limit, change how a broad
update's check is judged, or take teacher training to the larger model as it is?

  <p><strong>What it is about.</strong> Teacher training retrains the subcomponents so the decomposition imitates a fine-tuned model, with Γ and Δ frozen. It is the commute line's working map for the larger model. On fine-tunes that act at one trigger token it gave decompositions that keep the behaviour and pass the paper's adversarial check. E37 tried a fine-tune that changes every position: Pile text lowercased. The decomposition after teacher training kept all of its behaviour, but the adversary did about twice the damage it does on the original decomposition, above the fail line. The user asked for longer training and other fixes in one run (E37 Amendment 1).</p>
  <p><strong>What the evidence says.</strong> One run, independently checked. Training four times longer left the check where it was, 2.20 times the original at step 1,000 and 2.23 at step 4,000. Meanwhile the decomposition kept getting closer to the fine-tuned model, and its training loss was still falling by 44% over the last doubling of steps. The filed plan had no branch for a falling loss with a check that does not fall, and three readings cannot tell a flat check from a slow fall. Starting from the adjoint split, which passes at 1.05, the check read 1.76 after 1,000 steps. After 2,000 it read 1.84, level with the longer run. In both runs the check rose once the decomposition changed ordinary text as much as the fine-tuned model does. Whether that is the cause is untested, and the masked control below failed the check with a thirtieth of that damage. The third fix, adding VPD's own masked losses to the training, could not be tested. Its control, the same recipe with nothing to copy, read 3.84 at the original model, and 3.14 with the losses weighted ten times less. That is more fragile than any decomposition trained on the fine-tuned model, and its own losses rose during training. E15 retrained Γ at the original model on a loss stack that includes two of the same losses, set up by the same code. It left the floor too, twice. Judged against the fine-tuned model left in Δ, which reads 4.87, every trained decomposition here reads under half of it.</p>
  <p><strong>The options.</strong> (a) <em>Train longer still</em>, the filed plan's extend branch: continue the longer run to 16,000 steps, about $1.5. A check falling under the fail line would put under-training back as the explanation and keep plain teacher training for the larger model. A check still near 2 would close that question. (b) <em>Test whether the frozen Γ is the limit</em>, the first half of the earlier card's option (d): retrain Γ on the decomposition after teacher training, E15's refit, about $9 per state. Training against our set-up of VPD's losses at the original model has failed three times. So a control of about $1 comes first: the same recipe with the masked losses switched off. It says whether the masked terms or the rest of the training loop move the subcomponents off the floor. (c) <em>Test whether the subcomponents' span is the limit</em>, the second half of option (d): whether the alive subcomponents can express this fine-tune's change on ordinary text at all. No design exists yet; it goes through <code>/design</code> first. (d) <em>Settle the anchor question for broad updates</em>, card <code>decision-pass-anchor</code>: count a state as passing when it is no worse than the same update left in Δ. Every decomposition here then passes, at 0.36 to 0.46 of it, but that reference is inflated by construction, since the adversary can switch off Δ. A control of about $1 would inform the choice: move the subcomponents at random by the masked control's size and read the check. That shows whether the check punishes any move away from the released subcomponents that is not like the adjoint split, which bears on reading every trained decomposition's twofold reading. (e) <em>Take teacher training to the larger model as it is</em>, reporting the check against both references. Another reading of about twice there would say this is what broad updates do, not a quirk of a toy fine-tune.</p>
  <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> Real post-training is a broad update like this one. If teacher training is faithful only on trigger-like updates, the larger-model run needs a different map, a repaired Γ, or a pass rule that fits broad updates. Options (a) to (c) ask why the decomposition is fragile; (d) and (e) decide what to do while that is open.</p>
