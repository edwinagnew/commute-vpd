## main.tex lines 49-49
49: \new{\textbf{[writeup-lead version, for comparison.]} A common view of LLM training holds that post-training can only elicit what pre-training already put into a base model. This \emph{Elicitation Hypothesis} underpins several approaches to alignment, among them persona steering and alignment pre-training, yet the evidence for it is indirect: behavioural studies show that a base model can be prompted into a post-trained capability, and mechanistic studies show that some features or circuits survive fine-tuning, but none has been checked against a fine-tune known to build something new. We give a mechanistic definition of reuse in the language of causal abstraction: a fine-tune reuses its base model, for a behaviour, to the extent that the variables its behaviour needs are still computed by the base model's mechanisms. The definition is stated relative to a featurization of the base model, so it applies to SAE latents, neurons or parameter subcomponents alike, and under it a new mechanism is a defined outcome rather than an absence of evidence. On a 67M-parameter model we score readings against fine-tunes built to reuse or to learn something new. A direct approximation of the definition separates hand-made edits but reads every trained fine-tune low. Among cheaper proxies, a re-gating share separates the fine-tunes built to reuse from those built new, where published model-diffing tools do not; but no proxy tracks reuse on realistic fine-tunes, and on Qwen3-1.7B the re-gating share saturates. Any method that claims to see what fine-tuning learns should first pass fine-tunes whose answer is known.}

## appE_methods_detail.tex lines 184-190
184: \new{\paragraph{KL form of the share.} On Qwen the log-odds form could not be scored, so the share compares output
185: distributions instead. Let $K_{ab}$ be the KL from the fine-tuned model's output to a masked forward that uses
186: components $C_a$ under the gates read on model $b$'s activations. $C_0$ is the base decomposition; $C_1$ is the
187: decomposition carried onto the fine-tune by the least-squares split (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_carrying}). So $K_{00}$ is
188: the base model's distance from the fine-tune, and $K_{11}$ is how close the carried decomposition gets under its own
189: gates. Then $\phi_\Gamma^{\mathrm{KL}} = (K_{00} - K_{01})/(K_{00} - K_{11})$, summed over every position up to the
190: read position. It is 0 when the new gates do not help, and 1 when the new gates alone close the gap.}
