| 1 | Transport test, round 1 (`experiments/transport_test`, 2026-10-14; `reports/2026-10-14_analysis_transport-test.md`) | Released 67M Pile model + VPD decomposition, frozen Γ and Δ. Arm A: full-weight fine-tune on 365 emoticon windows (P(o) 0.999 by step 10); arm B: continued pretraining (held-out effect 0.014 nats). Transports: Frobenius projection on alive subcomponents, functional rescaling of all alive, k-sparse write/read edits on top-k by Frobenius contribution vs k random alive | δW is not in the span of the rank-1 subcomponents: Frobenius fraction 0.05% (arm B, scored: predicted ≥ 10× random, got 1.5×; reversed) and 0.06% (arm A, reported) vs 0.035% for a random direction. Functionally the emoticon behaviour is reproduced by 10 edited subcomponents, but 10 *random* ones do as well (0.987 vs 1.000; predicted margin ≥ 0.3; reversed); at k = 3 top beats random by 0.16. The paper's `2359` ranks 2nd in its matrix at step 10 and disperses by step 30. Decomposition stays faithful under every transport by the stochastic metric (ratios 0.94–1.02); adversarial excursion to 2.4 at one checkpoint, one block. Arm B functional fits failed to optimise (training KL rose; lr 1e-2 too large), so that half is uninformative; arm A's span fraction relative to random grows to 10× by step 300 (review `reports/2026-10-14_review_round1-audit.md`) | The null (free write vectors) is too expressive and the Frobenius selector picks the wrong subcomponents; next round needs a scale-only or constrained null, a functional selector, checkpoints at steps 1–5, a larger arm-B effect, and selectivity on generic text. Through `/design` first. Also, measured against the user's precise question (`GOALS.md`, 2026-10-14), round 1's faithfulness check let Δ absorb δW, so it cannot tell a good map Φ from the trivial "do nothing" one |
| 2 | Lift test, round 2 (`experiments/lift_test`, 2026-10-14; `reports/2026-10-14_analysis_lift-test.md`, `notebooks/lift_test_round2.ipynb`) | Released 67M model + decomposition, Γ frozen, emoticon arm A. Obstruction ω of full-weight (Adam, SGD) and LoRA updates, Frobenius and input-weighted; behaviour of the expressible part δW − O(δW); **outside**: Euclidean lift of a finished fine-tune; **inside**: pull-back (train alive U, V, Δ fixed) | Inside works: pull-back learns the behaviour (P(o) 0.95 by step 3) with a third of the fine-tune's off-target KL and stays faithful on every metric (PGD ratio 1.03 at step 10, 1.07 at 30; P3′ passed). Outside fails: the lift carries the update (88–93% of the unmasked gap) but is adversarially fragile (PGD 1.14 → 5.41 over steps 1–10; P2′ reversed). The inexpressible part of a fine-tune is ~30% input-weighted (W₀: 13%; Frobenius measure useless, W₀ fails it too), Adam or SGD alike; the expressible part alone reproduces the behaviour, but the inexpressible part alone also partly carries it (redundant, not unneeded; review `reports/2026-10-15_review_obstruction-claim.md`). P1 reversed; first P2/P3 untestable as filed (blind weight term; lr grid too large) | One task, the easiest (an existing behaviour), 30 steps. The open questions are whether inside training survives a behaviour the model lacks (the "new capabilities" branch) and longer training (the limit); cause of the lift's fragility unknown. **Re-scoped 2026-10-15** (`reports/2026-10-15_review_faithfulness-scope.md`): every faithfulness ratio was computed on Pile rows holding ~3 trigger positions in 131k, so "faithful" means the decomposition's account of the *old* model and the fine-tune's side-effects survived; whether the trained behaviour is carried by Γ-gated components is unmeasured for both routes. The pull-back was never run on a LoRA, and its (U, V) states were not saved. Round 3 waits on the task-local harness |
