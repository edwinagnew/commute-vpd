1. Sentiment label: a sentence from a film review followed by one word naming its sentiment (positive or negative).
2. Extractive question answering (SQuAD): a Wikipedia paragraph and a question, followed by the short span of the paragraph that answers it.
3. British spelling: ordinary text with American spellings rewritten to British ones ("color" becomes "colour", "organize" becomes "organise").
4. arXiv abstracts: abstracts of scientific papers from arXiv (physics, mathematics, computer science and more).
5. Tagalog: Tagalog Wikipedia text.
6. Code: more training on source-code files only.
7. News summary: the opening of a CNN or Daily Mail news article, followed by the first one-sentence highlight that summarises it.
8. French: French Wikipedia text, continued after English text.
9. Entity-type labelling (CoNLL-2003): a news sentence with a named entity marked, followed by its type (person, organisation, location or miscellaneous).
10. Pile: more training on ordinary held-out text of the kind the model was pretrained on.
11. Spanish: Spanish Wikipedia text.
12. Lowercase: ordinary text with every letter lowercased; the model learns to continue text in lowercase.
13. Swahili: Swahili Wikipedia text.
14. Topic label: a news article (sports or business) followed by one word naming its topic.
15. Yoruba: Yoruba Wikipedia text.
16. German: German Wikipedia text.
17. English Wikipedia: English Wikipedia article text.
18. Article-category label: the opening of a Wikipedia article about an animal or a plant, followed by one word naming which.
